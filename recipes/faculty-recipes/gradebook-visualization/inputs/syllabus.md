# MATH 21a: Multivariable Calculus — Syllabus (excerpt)

_Harvard University, Faculty of Arts and Sciences. Synthetic sample syllabus prepared for the Summer of Claude "Gradebook Visualization" recipe. Course content and policies are illustrative._

## Course description

A study of the calculus of functions of several variables: vectors and curves, partial derivatives, multiple integrals, vector fields, and the theorems of Green, Stokes, and Gauss. Three section meetings per week plus a weekly problem-solving section.

## Meeting times

- **Lectures:** Mon / Wed / Fri
- **Section:** one weekly small-group meeting (attendance contributes to participation)
- **Term:** Fall semester (~14 instructional weeks + December exam period)

## How your grade is calculated

Your **course percentage** is a weighted average of six components. The weights sum to 100%:

| Component        | Weight | What it is                                                        |
|------------------|:------:|-------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Mini-Exam        |   5%   | An early diagnostic in Week 2 — your first timed assessment.       |
| Problem Sets     |  30%   | Ten weekly problem sets (3% each).                                |
| Midterm 1        |  15%   | Week 5, covering Units 1–2.                                        |
| Midterm 2        |  15%   | Week 10, covering Units 3–4.                                       |
| Participation    |   5%   | Section attendance and in-class polling.                          |
| Final Exam       |  30%   | Cumulative, during the December exam period.                      |

**Formula:**

```
Course_Pct = 0.05 * MiniExam
           + 0.30 * mean(Problem Sets)
           + 0.15 * Midterm1
           + 0.15 * Midterm2
           + 0.05 * Participation
           + 0.30 * Final
```

> Note on the data files: in the accompanying `gradebook_*.csv` files, all ten problem sets count equally at 3% each, so the `Course_Pct` column is a straightforward weighted average of the listed columns.

## A note on early assessments (the point of the visualization)

The Mini-Exam and Midterm 1 happen early and are worth relatively little on their own (5% and 15%). They are best read as **calibration signals**, not verdicts. Every term, students who stumble on the first assessment recover with a change in study strategy, and some strong starters drift. The grade-trajectory animation built from this data is meant to show that range of realistic outcomes — and where a well-timed intervention (office hours, the Math Question Center, a study-group change) tends to matter most.
