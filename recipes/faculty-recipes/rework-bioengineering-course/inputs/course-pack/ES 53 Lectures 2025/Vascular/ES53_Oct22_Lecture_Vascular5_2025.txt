Circulatory Shock
• Occurs when there is inadequate blood flow (or O2) to
  tissues
    • CV system undergoes compensatory changes
    • Sometimes shock becomes irreversible  death
• Hypovolemic Shock is circulatory shock caused by
  low blood volume
• Septic Shock refers to very low blood pressure
  resulting from sepsis (infection)
• Other types of circulatory shock:
  • Severe allergic reaction can cause a rapid fall in BP called
    anaphylactic shock (due to generalized release of histamine
    causing vasodilation)
  • Cardiogenic shock is common following cardiac failure
    resulting from infarction

Hypertension
• Definition: BP > 140/90 mmHg (either / or)
• Afflicts about 20% of adults
• Caused by complex and poorly understood
  processes
   • Main Features:
       • Increase in peripheral resistance is universal
       • BP and HR are elevated in many
       • Prolonged high BP causes thickening of arterial
         walls  arteriosclerosis
       • Increases workload of the heart leading to
         ventricular hypertrophy and heart failure
   Usually treated with drugs (treatments include
   diuretics to reduce fluid volume, beta-blockers to
   decrease HR, and others)
Arrhythmias Detected on ECG
• Arrhythmias are abnormal heart rhythms
• In fibrillation, contraction of myocardial cells is
  uncoordinated and pumping ineffective
   • Electrical defibrillation resynchronizes heart by
     depolarizing all cells at same time
