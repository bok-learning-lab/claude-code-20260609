Total Peripheral Resistance
• Sum of all vascular resistances within the systemic circulation is the total
  peripheral resistance (TPR); TPR relates to afterload
    • Sum of all Levels: arteries + arterioles + capillaries + veins
• Arteries supply tissues and organs in parallel circuits
    • Changes in resistance in these circuits determines overall & relative
      blood flow

Physical Laws Describing Blood Flow - Pressure
• Blood flows through vascular system when there is pressure difference
  (∆P) between its two ends
    • Flow rate is directly proportional to difference (∆P = P1 - P2)
    • Flow = ∆P/R analogous to current-voltage relationship: V=IR 
      I=V/R
    • Q = ∆P/R

Physical Laws Describing Blood Flow - Resistance
• Flow rate is proportional to the pressure drop (∆P) & inversely
  proportional to resistance

   • Flow = ∆P / R  ∆P = Flow ∙ R  R = ∆P / Flow
   • Resistance is directly proportional to length of vessel (L) and
     viscosity of blood (η)
       • Inversely proportional to 4th power of radius (r4)
           • So diameter of vessel is very important for resistance

• Poiseuille's Law describes factors affecting blood flow:
   • Blood flow = π ∆Pr4     Resistance = ∆P = 8ηL
                  8ηL                                Flow   πr4
   • Vasodilation dramatically decreases resistance, increases
     blood flow
   • Vasoconstriction does opposite

Reynolds Number
• For flow in a pipe or tube, the Reynolds
  number is defined as:
        ρvD                  ρQD
   Re =                 Re =
         µ                    µA
 D is the diameter of the pipe
 v is the mean velocity
 ρ is the fluid density
 µ is the viscosity
 Q is the flow rate
 A is the pipe cross-sectional area

                                Laminar when Re<2300
                                Transitional when 2300<Re<4000
                                Turbulent when 4000<Re
