Structure of Blood Vessels
• Innermost layer of all vessels is the endothelium
• Capillaries are made of only endothelial cells
• Arteries and veins have 3 layers  Media is mostly smooth muscle
• Although they have same basic elements, arteries and veins are quite
  different

Comparison of Blood Vessels
Arteries
• Large arteries are muscular and elastic
• Small arteries and arterioles are muscular and provide the
  most resistance in the CV system
Capillaries
• Provide extensive surface area for exchange
• Blood flow through a capillary bed is determined by state
  of precapillary sphincters of arteriole supplying it.
Veins
 Contain majority of blood in
  circulatory system (70%)
    Have thin walls and are
     very compliant, thus they
     stretch easily to
     accommodate more
     blood without (much)
     increased pressure
 Contain very low pressure
  (about 2mm Hg)
    Insufficient to return
     blood to heart
    Blood return depends on
     valves, muscle, and
     breathing.

Total Peripheral Resistance
• Sum of all vascular resistances within the systemic circulation is the total
  peripheral resistance (TPR); TPR relates to afterload
    • Sum of all Levels: arteries + arterioles + capillaries + veins
• Arteries supply tissues and organs in parallel circuits
    • Changes in resistance in these circuits determines overall & relative
      blood flow

Physical Laws Describing Blood Flow - Resistance
• Mean arterial pressure (MAP) and vascular
  resistance (R) are the 2 major factors regulating
  blood flow

• Flow rate is proportional to the pressure drop (∆P)
  & inversely proportional to resistance
