Calculation of GFR using Starling equation
It is possible to calculate glomerular filtration rate
(GFR) using the Starling equation:
    GFR = Kf([Pc − Pi] − [πc − πi])
The equation is used both in a general sense for all capillary flow, and in a specific sense for
the glomerulus:
Note that ([Pc − Pi] − [πc − πi]) is the net driving pressure, and therefore the net filtration is
proportional to the net driving pressure.

Glomerular Filtration Rate (GFR)
• Is volume of filtrate produced by both
  kidneys/min
  • Averages 115 ml/min in “females”; 125 ml/min in
    “males”
  • ~20% of plasma passes through the glomerulus
  • Totals about 180L/day (~45 gallons)
    • So most filtered water must be reabsorbed or death
      would ensue from water lost through urination
Reabsorption of Salt and H2O

• PCT returns most molecules and H2O from
  filtrate back to peritubular capillaries
  • About 180 L/day of ultrafiltrate produced; only
    1–2 L of urine excreted/24 hours
  • Urine volume varies according to needs of
    body
  • Minimum of 400 ml/day urine necessary to
    excrete metabolic wastes (obligatory water
    loss)
Reabsorption of Salt and H2O continued

• Return of filtered
  molecules is called
  reabsorption
• Water is never
  transported
• Other molecules are
  transported and water
  follows by osmosis
