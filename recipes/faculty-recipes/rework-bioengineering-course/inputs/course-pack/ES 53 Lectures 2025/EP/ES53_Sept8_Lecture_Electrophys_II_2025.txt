    Resting Membrane Potential (Vmem)
1. Each ion has its own equilibrium (Nernst) potential, which is typically
   different from all of the other ions.
2. The membrane potential is the weighted average of the Nernst potentials
   of the ions present in the cell. The weights are directly related to the
   permeability of each ion and the absolute concentration of the ion.
3. In the resting state K+ usually dominates the membrane potential as can
   be seen in the GHK equation. Sodium permeability is low so it makes a
   small contribution. Chloride is intermediate, but its equilibrium potential is
   usually close to the K+ equilibrium potential.
4. There will be a flux across the membrane of any ion for which the
   membrane potential is not at the equilibrium potential (this difference in
   potentials leads to a driving force or driving potential which in turn causes
   a current).
5. If membrane potential is stable, then the total net flow of ionic charge
   across the membrane is zero even if the individual ion flows are not zero.

                  Clinical Case: Hypokalemia
•   Hypokalemia is caused by abnormally low levels of potassium in the
    body.
•   Almost 98% of potassium is found inside the cells. Small changes in
    the level of potassium that is present outside the cells can have
    severe effects on the heart, nerves, and muscles.
•   Potassium is important to maintain muscle contraction.
•   The kidney is the main organ that controls the balance of potassium
    by removing excess potassium into the urine.
