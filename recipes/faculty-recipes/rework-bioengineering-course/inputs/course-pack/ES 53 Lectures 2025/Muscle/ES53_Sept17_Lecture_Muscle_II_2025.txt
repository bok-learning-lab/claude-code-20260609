Prolonged stimulation can lead to fatigue = reduction in force
Muscle fatigue is complex and can have many causes including:
1) Increased extracellular K+ concentration
2) Decrease in cellular “energy” stores: ATP, glycogen, . . .
3) Build up of byproducts: Phosphate, ADP,. . .
Muscle fatigue is the reduced ability to generate
force due to:
 1. Accumulation of extracellular K+, reducing membrane
    potential
 2. Depletion of stored glycogen
 3. Reduced SR calcium release
 4. Lactic acid accumulation and lower pH
 5. Increased concentration of PO4 due to phosphocreatine
    breakdown
 6. Lack of ATP
 7. Buildup of ADP
 8. Fatigue of upper motor neurons (in the CNS), called central
    fatigue

Summation
Increase force from multiple twitches “summing up”
This is about what happens within individual muscle cells




Recruitment
Increase force from multiple motor units being “recruited” to contract at the same time
This is about multiple muscle cells working together


Large motor units are innervated by large motor neurons
Small motor units are innervated by small motor neurons

Small motor units are recruited first

                                            Summary




                                                                                 Increasing afterload
                                                                                 •   Longer isometric phase
                                                                                 •   Higher isotonic force
                                                                                 •   Lower isotonic velocity




Muscle shortening against a fixed weight (i.e. “afterload”)
1) After the start of stimulation force exerted by the muscle begins to increase
• If the force from the muscle is insufficient to lift the weight then the length of the muscle stays constant
• isometric contraction = active force generation with no change in muscle length
2) When the force becomes high enough to lift the weight, the muscle gets shorter
• Initially, the contraction occurs at a constant velocity
• Isotonic contraction = muscle contraction with constant velocity
