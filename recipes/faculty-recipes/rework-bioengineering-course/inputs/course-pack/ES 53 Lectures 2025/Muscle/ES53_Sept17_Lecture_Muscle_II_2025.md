## Slide 9
Stimulating a muscle fiber
1) Action potential in the neuron causes release of acetylcholine at the neuromuscular junction
Acetylcholine binds to nicotinic acetylcholine receptors (ligand-gated ion channels), which induces a depolarization. . .
2) . . . voltage gated ion channels in the muscle cell are activated, inducing an action potential. . .
3) . . . the action potential travels down the t-tubule, which stimulates the opening of voltage-gated Ca
2+
channels. . .
4) . . . these channels are coupled to Ca
2+
release channels in the sarcoplasmic reticulum, which causes Ca
2+
to be released to the cytoplasm. . .
5) . . . Ca
2+
binds to troponin, which causes a readjustment of the troponin-tropomyosin complex, which allows myosin to bind to actin
Low Ca
2+
, myosin can't bind actin
High Ca
2+
, myosin can bind actin

## Slide 26
Summation vs Recruitment
Summation
Increase force from multiple twitches “summing up”
This is about what happens within individual muscle cells
Recruitment
Increase force from multiple motor units being “recruited” to contract at the same time
This is about multiple muscle cells working together
Large motor units are innervated by large motor neurons
Small motor units are innervated by small motor neurons
Small motor units are recruited first

## Slide 31
Summary
Muscle shortening against a fixed weight (i.e. “afterload”)
After the start of stimulation force exerted by the muscle begins to increase
If the force from the muscle is insufficient to lift the weight then the length of the muscle stays constant
isometric contraction
= active force generation with no change in muscle length
2) When the force becomes high enough to lift the weight, the muscle gets shorter
Initially, the contraction occurs at a constant velocity
Isotonic contraction
= muscle contraction with constant velocity
Increasing afterload
Longer isometric phase
Higher isotonic force
Lower isotonic velocity
