## Slide 3
Learning Objectives
Calculate and estimate the partial pressures of gases outside the body, inside the lungs and in the blood. Are there any differences in how these are calculated?
Describe the normal hematocrit levels in men, women, and anemic people. What does hemoglobin do? Why is iron (Fe) essential? Why does iron deficiency cause anemia?
What is the O2 carrying capacity of hemoglobin (Hb), and what are the units?
Determine the O2 content in the blood based on blood volume, PO2 throughout the vascular system, and the hematocrit levels.
Apply Fick’s principle and determine the rate of O2 consumption under normal conditions, exercise, and/or extreme conditions. What are the units?
Describe and numerically estimate the change in oxy-hemoglobin saturation when carbon monoxide (CO) is present, or when tissue is acidic/basic/cold/hot. What affects Hb-O2 saturation?
Describe the role of cells in the alveolar sacs and the function of each type, specifically what is the role of surfactant and where does it come from?
Describe the structures and functions of the conducting and respiratory zone of the lungs.
Apply knowledge of laminar flow to the tubes of the lungs and apply knowledge of flow to the pulmonary vascular system. Use the derivation of Poiseuille’s law to calculate flow in vessels/airways.
Describe the processes of inhalation and exhalation. What is the role of the diaphragm and intercostal muscles? What is the difference between quiet and forced breathing? What pressure changes (and where) occur to drive this exchange of air? Describe the location and significance of the pleural membranes, and how the intrapleural and intrapulmonary pressures change during breathing.
Understand the four primary lung volumes (TV, IRV, ERV, RV) and four primary lung capacities (sum of two or more lung volumes). Which of these can be estimated from simple laboratory equipment?
Describe several pulmonary disorders and what happens to the flow/partial pressures, etc., in each case.
