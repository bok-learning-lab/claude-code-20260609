     Transport of Oxygen in Blood

• There are only minor amounts of dissolved
  oxygen in blood
  • Solubility of oxygen (0.003 ml O2/dl blood) / mmHg
      • Normal blood (PO2=100 mmHg)  0.3 ml O2 / dl blood
  • Normal resting oxygen consumption 250 ml O2/min
  • Would require 83 L/min blood flow! This is why hemoglobin (and
    red blood cells) are important


• The vast majority of oxygen in blood is bound to
  hemoglobin (Hb) in red blood cells
  • >97% O2 transported by Hb: O2 + Hb        HbO2
  • O2 Carrying capacity: 1.34 ml O2 / g Hb
  • Normal [Hb]: ~12-15 g Hb/dl blood  16-20 ml O2 /dl blood
Transport of Carbon Dioxide
• The CO2 in blood is mostly dissolved.
  • solubility in plasma is about 20X that of oxygen
  • venous blood: 2.7 ml/100 ml blood
  • arterial blood: 2.4 ml/100 ml blood
  • transported : 0.3 ml/100 ml blood
  • Also transported as carbonic acid

Respiration & Oxygen Consumption
• Respiration is comprised of 3 related functions: ventilation, gas
  exchange, and O2 utilization (cellular respiration)
   • Ventilation moves air in and out of lungs for gas exchange
     with blood (external respiration)
   • Gas exchange between blood and tissues occurs via
     passive diffusion which rely on spatial concentration
     gradients.
   • O2 is consumed (and CO2 created) by aerobic metabolism in
     tissues throughout the body - this is called internal respiration
                              ·
  Rate of O2 consumption = O2 = Q * (CAO2 - CVO2)
                                   Q = rate of blood flow = cardiac output
                         Where     CAO2 = O2 concentration in arterial blood
                                   CVO2 = O2 concentration in venous blood
