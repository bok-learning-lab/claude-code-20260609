Blood Pressure Parameters you need to know

• Systolic pressure = arterial pressure during ventricular
 contraction
• Diastolic pressure = arterial pressure during ventricular
relaxation
• Pulse pressure = (systolic pressure) – (diastolic pressure)
• Mean arterial pressure (MAP) represents average arterial
pressure during the entire cardiac cycle
   • MAP ≈ 2/3 diastolic pressure + 1/3 systolic pressure
      • Why is MAP closer to the diastolic pressure?
   • MAP ≈ diastolic pressure + 1/3 pulse pressure

                                                         3
Cardiac Cycle – Volume parameters you need
to know

• End-diastolic volume is volume of blood in
  ventricles at end of diastole (EDV)
• End-systolic volume is amount of blood left in
  ventricles at end of systole (ESV)
• Stroke volume is amount of blood ejected from
  ventricles during systole (SV = EDV-ESV)
• Ejection fraction is the portion of blood ejected
  from the LV compared to the total volume of blood
  at the end of diastole. (EF = SV/EDV)
• Cardiac output is the volume of blood ejected from
  the LV per minute. (CO = HR x SV)

Stroke Work
SW refers to the work done by the ventricle to eject a
volume of blood (i.e., stroke volume) into the aorta. It is
the area within the ventricular P-V loop.




 SW ~= Stroke Volume X Mean Aortic (arterial) Pressure15
Recall from the reading assignment:

Stroke Volume is determined by 3 variables

 End diastolic volume (EDV) = volume of blood in
  ventricles at end of diastole = Preload
 Contractility = strength of ventricular contraction
 Total peripheral resistance (TPR) = impedance to blood
  flow in arteries = Afterload
