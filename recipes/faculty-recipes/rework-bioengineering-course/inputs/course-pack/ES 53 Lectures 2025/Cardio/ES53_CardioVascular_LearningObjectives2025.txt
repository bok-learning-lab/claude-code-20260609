 ES 53 - Quantitative Physiology                                                            Fall 2025
                                           Module 3:
                                       Cardiac Physiology


Outline for Cardiac Module (~4 lectures):
   • Part I: Cardiac anatomy, electrical properties of the heart, and ECG
   • Part II: Cardiac mechanics, cardiac cycle
   • Part III: Pressure volume dynamics


Learning Objectives - By the end of this module you should be able to do the following (or answer
the following questions). Use the learning objectives to guide your reading & studying for this module.

   1) Describe the purpose of all four chambers of the heart, the valves within the heart, the
       pulmonary vein, and the aorta; how do defects in these affect flow and circulation?
   2) Map the flow of blood throughout the systemic and pulmonary vascular systems starting at the
       left ventricle. How does pressure vary along this path?
   3) How do electrical signals move through the heart and why is the order important?
   4) Draw an ECG and label the waves. Indicate the electrical and mechanical events that produce
       these waves. Differentiate atrial vs. ventricular abnormalities based on an ECG. How do the
       waves of the ECG change with heart rate and breathing rate?
   5) What is the primary pacemaker of the heart (SA node), and where is it located?
   6) What is a refractory period and why is it essential to cardiac contraction?
   7) Re-create a Wigger’s diagram showing the phases of contraction and relaxation in the left
       atrium and left ventricle. Correlate the mechanical pressure and volume cycles with the
       electrical (ECG) cycle of the heart. You should be able to recall approximate pressures,
       volumes and the timing. You should be able to read the EDV and ESV, EDP and ESP of the left
       ventricle.
   8) Recreate a pressure-volume loop from a Wigger’s diagram and estimate the work done by the
       left ventricle.
   9) What is pre-load and what is after-load? How do changes in them change the stroke work done
       by the heart, and how do they change the pressure-volume loop of the work done by the heart?
   10) What might cause increased resistance (afterload)? What might cause increased pre-load
       (filling)?
   11) What is the Frank-Starling Mechanism, and how does it relate to cardiac output and/or stroke
       volume?
   12) Calculate mean arterial pressure (MAP), ejection fraction (EF), stroke volume (SV), stroke work
       (SW), cardiac output (CO), heart rate (HR in units of beats per min (BPM)), pulse pressure (PP),
       total peripheral resistance (TPR).
   13) What are a few biomedical interventions that can alleviate heart dysfunction? In other words,
       what are some biomedical engineering devices that assist cardiac function (e.g. LVAD,
       pacemaker), and what do they change about cardiac function?

                                          Module 4:
                                      Vascular Physiology


Outline for Vascular (~4-5 lectures):
   • Part I: Vascular anatomy
   • Part II: Modeling the cardiovascular system as a circuit
   • Part III: Blood pressure and blood flow; turbulent and laminar flow; Poiseuille flow
   • Part IV: Regulation of blood flow and blood pressure
   • Part V: Cardiovascular problems and treatments/devices


Learning Objectives - By the end of this module you should be able to do the following (or answer
the following questions). Use the learning objectives to guide your reading & studying for this module.

   1) Map blood pressure throughout the systemic and pulmonary vascular systems starting at the left
      ventricle. How does pressure vary along this path?
   2) How do the vessels change in size, function, and anatomy along the systemic circulatory
      system, and how do you determine the resistance along the path?
   3) Which vessels are more compliant and why?
   4) How is resistance controlled, and what are some factors that cause it to change?
   5) Calculate the resistance to blood flow if given vessel size, and a diagram of the interconnectivity
      of the vessels. Generate a corresponding circuit diagram that represents the resistive network of
      vessels in a circulatory system.
   6) What is Reynold’s number, how does it compare to laminar flow, and where might you find flow
      with a high Reynold’s number within the body?
   7) Calculate changes in cardiac output given changes in TPR, blood pressure as well as exercise.
   8) How does atherosclerosis affect vessels? How can build up of fatty plaque in an artery lead to a
      heart attack?
   9) What are a few biomedical interventions that can alleviate cardiovascular dysfunction? In
      other words, what are some biomedical engineering devices that assist cardiac function (e.g.
      LVAD, pacemaker), and what do they change about cardiac function?
