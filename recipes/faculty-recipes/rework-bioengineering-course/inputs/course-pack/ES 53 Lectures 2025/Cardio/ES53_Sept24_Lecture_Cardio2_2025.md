## Slide 6
Arrhythmias
Abnormal heart rhythms
Bradycardia: slow heart rate, below 60 bpm
Tachycardia: fast heart rate, above 100 bpm
-
These heart rhythms are normal if the person is active, but not normal at rest.)
-Abnormal tachycardia can occur due to drugs or fast ectopic pacemakers.
-Ventricular tachycardia occurs when pacemakers in the ventricles make them contract out of synch with the atria. This condition is very dangerous and can lead to ventricular fibrillation and sudden death.
2. Flutter and Fibrillation
Flutter: extremely fast (200 to 300 bpm) but coordinated contractions
Fibrillation: uncoordinated pumping between the atria and ventricles (or uncoordinated contraction of a chamber)

Cardiac Cycle – Pressure changes
Ventricles begin contraction, pressure rises, and AV valves close (
lub
); isovolumetric contraction
Pressure builds, semilunar valves open, and blood is ejected into arteries.
Pressure in ventricles falls; semilunar valves close (
dub
); isovolumetric relaxation
Pressure in ventricles falls below that of atria, and AV valve opens. Ventricles fill.
Atria contract, sending last of blood to ventricles

## Slide 21
Cardiac Cycle –
Volume
parameters you need to know
End-diastolic volume
is volume of blood in ventricles at end of diastole (EDV)
End-systolic volume
is amount of blood left in ventricles at end of systole (ESV)
Stroke volume
is amount of blood ejected from ventricles during systole (SV = EDV-ESV)
Ejection fraction
is the portion of blood ejected from the LV compared to the total volume of blood at the end of diastole. (EF = SV/EDV)
Cardiac output
is the volume of blood ejected from the
LV per minute.
(CO = HR x SV)
