Goal: Throughout ES53, we learn about our familiar but mysterious, intricate but simple, delicate but robust body. Now it’s time to dig deeper into one specific area. Throughout ES53, we have explored the complexities and remarkable adaptability of the human body, it’s elegant organization, and how it is both delicate and robust. As we conclude the course, you are now invited to investigate one specific physiological system or process in greater depth. You will use some engineering and creativity to try to design a new medical intervention. For this assignment, you will work in teams of four to:

Understand the pathophysiology of a particular disease/condition

Research available treatment options and identify gaps (shortcomings of current medical interventions)

Propose a medical solution to treat the disease or address complication(s)

Write up your proposed idea with supporting background information

Pitch your idea to a group of engineers - your classmates!

Part 1: Written Report with Proposed New Solution

Choose a disease that affects a body system we will study - nervous, muscular, cardiac, vascular, pulmonary, renal, or visual. Learn about the pathophysiology, symptoms, and complications of the disease using print resources (e.g. textbooks) and reputable websites (do not use Wikipedia!! use PubMed, Google Scholar, journal articles, etc., but avoid non-verified websites). Be sure to address the following prompts: 

What are the causes of this disease and what demographic is primarily affected? 

How does this disease impact one or more of the physiological systems that we studied? You can use graphs, diagrams, and/or equations.

What are the available treatment options? 

How can the current treatment methods be improved? What problems remain unaddressed?

Next, you and your group members should brainstorm possible solutions that could treat the disease or address a complication. Think through some typical design considerations, such as feasibility, practicality, expense, and possible side effects. Choose your top solution and describe how you would design it.

Write up your background research and proposed new solution in a 5-6 page report (single or double spaced, 12 point). Include figures if they help describe your design. If you would like feedback on your written work prior to the deadline, submit a draft by the November deadline above - this draft will be ungraded.

Part 2: Oral Pitch of New Solution

Once you have developed your idea, it is now time to pitch your idea to the class and defend your design! Create a 6-8 minute presentation, which should include an overview of the disease (1 slide), background information on current treatments (1 slide), and a detailed explanation of your proposed solution (2-3 slides). It is helpful to use diagrams and schematics to illustrate your idea and new solution. It’s possible that your new solution might be an algorithm as opposed to a physical device (feel free to use MATLAB, but this is not required). Be prepared for a Q&A session!  

Optional: Prototyping 

You can create a prototype to illustrate your solution. This prototype can manifest in several different ways, such as a physical model, an computer-aided design model (such as SolidWorks or other CAD software), an animation, a computer program, etc. Note that this step is not required but may enhance the effectiveness of your pitch/proposal. If you create a prototype, include a description of it in your written report.
