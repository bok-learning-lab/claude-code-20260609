Answers to parts a-c:

From 2011 to 2014 the overall prevalence of chronic kidney disease was 14.8% in the United States1. As of 2014 there were 678,383 cases of end stage renal disease which amounts to about 0.2% of the U.S. population1. Each year over 120,000 new cases of end stage renal disease cases are identified and about 88% go onto hemodialysis1. For Medicare costs alone over 32.8 billion dollars were spent on fees associated with care for patients with end stage renal disease1. In the U.S. in 2014 a total of 17,914 kidney transplants were performed, but by the end of that year there were still over 88,000 patients waiting for a kidney transplant1. There aren’t enough kidney donors to keep up with the need for kidney transplants, so most patients wait for years before getting a transplant.
