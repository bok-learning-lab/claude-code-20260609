The clearances are all calculated as Ux* / Px,      where  = rate of urine flow in mL/min

Plugging in, we get

Cinulin = 1 mg/mL * (1.4 L/1440 min)  / .01 mg/mL = 97.2 mL/min

Curea = 200 mmol/L * (1.4 L/1440 min) / 5 mmol/L = 38.9 mL/min

CPAH = 70 mg/mL * (1.4 L/1440 min ) / 0.2 mg/L = 340.3 mL/min

b. [1] Renal plasma flow (RPF) in mL/min

RPF, the effective renal plasma flow, is the clearance of PAH, given above as 340.3 mL/min because PAH is completely secreted.

c. [1] Glomerular filtration rate (GFR) in mL/min

GFR is the clearance of inulin, given above as 97.2 mL/min because inulin is freely filtered, but not reabsorbed or secreted.

d. [1] The rate of urea filtration in mmol/min Note: This is the rate at which urea is filtered from the glomerulus into Bowman’s space, not the amount excreted in urine. Note: urea is freely filtered out of the glomerulus.

The rate of urea filtration is just Cinulin * Purea = 97.2 mL/min x 5 mmol/L = 0.486 mmol min-1. This is the rate at which urea is filtered from the plasma, but not the rate at which it is excreted. 

e. [1] The rate of urea excretion in mmol/min

The rate of urea excretion is just  * Uurea = 1.4 L/1440 min * 200 mmol/L = 0.194 mmol/min

f. [1] The rate of urea reabsorption in mmol/min  Note: The rate of urea reabsorption is the difference between the rate filtered and the rate excreted.

Rate of urea reabsorption = 0.486 mmol/min - 0.194 mmol/min = 0.292 mmol/min

g. [1] The rate of PAH filtration in mg/min

The rate of PAH filtration is Cinulin * PPAH  or GFR * PPAH = 97.2 mL/min * 0.2 mg/m= 19.44 mg/min

h. [1] The rate of PAH excretion in mg/min

The rate of PAH excretion is  * UPAH = 0.972 mL m in-1 x 70 mg mL-1 = 68.06 mg min-1

i. [1] Renal blood flow (RBF) in mL/min

Renal blood flow is linearly related to the renal plasma flow by a simple factor of the hematocrit. Since the hematocrit here is 0.4, the plasma is 1-0.4, therefore RPF = (1- 0.4)*RBF = (0.6)*RBF

Using what you calculated above, RBF = RPF/0.6 = 340.3 mL/min/0.6 = 567 mL/min (Note that this is lower than “normal” which we discussed in class. Recall that RBF at rest is typically 20% of total cardiac output.)
