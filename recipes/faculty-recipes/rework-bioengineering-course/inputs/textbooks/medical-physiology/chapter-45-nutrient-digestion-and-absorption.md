## Section 0 — Overview: the five patterns of digestion-absorption (pp. 914–916)

### Subsection headings
- **General mechanisms of digestion and absorption** (Fig. 45-1, p. 914)
- **Sites of nutrient absorption along the small intestine** (Fig. 45-2, p. 916)

### Core claims
- The small intestine absorbs **monomers but not polymers**; dietary food is mostly ingested in a non-absorbable form and must be enzymatically converted first. Digestion occurs either in the **lumen** (pancreatic enzymes), at the **brush border** ("membrane digestion," brush-border enzymes), or in the **cytosol** of the enterocyte.
- **Five patterns** (Fig. 45-1): (1) no digestion needed (e.g., glucose absorbed as-is); (2) polymer → monomers in lumen, then absorbed (e.g., protein → amino acids); (3) oligomer → monomers at the brush border, then absorbed (e.g., sucrose → glucose + fructose); (4) oligomer absorbed intact, then digested **inside** the cell (e.g., oligopeptides); (5) substance disassembled before absorption then **re-synthesized** in the cell (e.g., triacylglycerol, TAG).
- **Segmental distribution** (Fig. 45-2): carbohydrate, protein, and lipid absorption is greatest in the **duodenum**, less in the jejunum, least in the ileum. Some substances (Ca²⁺, iron, folate) are actively absorbed only in the **duodenum**; bile acids are actively absorbed only in the **ileum**; cobalamin is absorbed only in the **ileum**.
- Digestion-absorption is highly efficient overall (~95% of dietary lipid absorbed; <4% of ingested nitrogen lost in stool). Material escaping small-intestinal absorption is fermented by colonic bacteria to **short-chain fatty acids (SCFAs)** absorbed in the colon.

### Citation-anchor quotes
- > "The small intestine can directly absorb the monomers but not the polymers." (p. 914)
- > "Digestion within the small intestine occurs either in the lumen, mediated by pancreatic enzymes, or at the small-intestinal brush-border membrane (membrane digestion), mediated by brush-border enzymes." (p. 914)

## Section 1 — Carbohydrate digestion and absorption (pp. 914–920)

### Subsection headings
- **Carbohydrates require hydrolysis to monosaccharides before absorption** (pp. 914–916)
- **Luminal digestion: salivary and pancreatic α-amylase** (pp. 916–917)
- **"Membrane digestion": brush-border disaccharidases** (pp. 916–919)
- **Carbohydrate absorption: SGLT1, GLUT5, GLUT2** (pp. 919–920)

### Core claims
- Carbohydrate provides **~45%** of energy in Western diets. Composition: **~45–60% starch** (amylose, α-1,4 straight chain; amylopectin, α-1,4 + branching α-1,6); **30–40% disaccharides** (sucrose = glucose+fructose; lactose = glucose+galactose); **5–10% monosaccharides** (glucose, fructose). Glycogen ("animal starch") has α-1,4 + α-1,6 like amylopectin but is more highly branched.
- **Fiber** (cellulose β-1,4 glucose, pectins, lignins) is not digestible by mammalian enzymes; colonic bacteria ferment some of it.
- **Luminal step.** Salivary and pancreatic **α-amylase** are secreted in **active** form (not as proenzymes; 94% sequence-identical). α-Amylase is an **endoenzyme** hydrolyzing only **internal α-1,4** links; it cannot cleave **terminal α-1,4**, **α-1,6** branch points, or α-1,4 links adjacent to branch points. Products: **maltose, maltotriose, and α-limit dextrins** — never free glucose. CCK stimulates pancreatic amylase secretion. Salivary amylase is inactivated by gastric acid; its role in adults is minor.
- **Brush-border step.** Four oligosaccharidase entities on three integral membrane proteins with lumen-facing catalytic domains: **lactase** (lactose → glucose + galactose); **glucoamylase/maltase** (terminal α-1,4 of maltose, maltotriose, dextrins; chains up to 9 residues); **sucrase-isomaltase** (two enzymes — **sucrase** splits sucrose → glucose + fructose, **isomaltase/α-dextrinase** is the **only** enzyme that splits the branching **α-1,6** links of α-limit dextrins).
- **Rate-limiting steps.** For maltose/sucrose, **transport uptake** (not hydrolysis) is rate-limiting. For **lactose, lactase activity is rate-limiting** — lactase activity is the lowest of the disaccharidases.
- Oligosaccharidase abundance peaks in the **proximal jejunum** (ligament of Treitz), absent in the colon; distribution parallels active glucose transport. **Lactase declines after weaning** in most non-white populations (genetically determined); other oligosaccharidases do not. Lactase is most vulnerable to enterocyte injury (e.g., viral enteritis) and slowest to recover.
- **Absorption is a two-step transcellular process.** Apical uptake of glucose/galactose by **SGLT1** (Na⁺-coupled, secondary active transport, against the glucose gradient, energized by the basolateral Na-K pump). **Fructose** enters apically by **facilitated diffusion via GLUT5** (Na⁺-independent, mainly jejunum). All three monosaccharides exit basolaterally by **facilitated diffusion via GLUT2**.
- **SGLT1 stereospecificity:** transports only **D-hexoses** able to form a **six-membered pyranose ring**; rejects L-glucose (wrong stereochemistry) and D-fructose (forms a 5-membered furanose ring). GLUT2 has no homology to SGLT1 but is 41% identical to GLUT5.

### Citation-anchor quotes
- > "Because the small intestine can absorb only monosaccharides, all dietary carbohydrate must be digested to monosaccharides before absorption." (p. 914)
- > "α-amylase is an endoenzyme that hydrolyzes internal α-1,4 linkages … As a result, starch hydrolysis products are maltose, maltotriose, and α-limit dextrins." (pp. 916–917)
- > "The human small intestine has three brush-border proteins with oligosaccharidase activity: lactase, glucoamylase (most often called maltase), and sucrase-isomaltase." (p. 916)
- > "Thus, uptake, not hydrolysis, is the rate-limiting step. In contrast, lactase activity … is rate limiting for overall lactose digestion-absorption." (p. 918)
- > "The uptake of glucose across the apical membrane via SGLT1 … represents active transport, because the glucose influx occurs against the glucose concentration gradient." (p. 919)
- > "SGLT1 has two structural requirements for monosaccharides: (1) a hexose in a D configuration, and (2) a hexose that can form a six-membered pyranose ring." (p. 919)

### Transport relations
- **SGLT1 (apical glucose/galactose):** Na⁺-coupled symport — flux driven by the inwardly directed electrochemical Na⁺ gradient $\Delta\mu_{Na}$ set by the basolateral Na-K pump:
  $$\text{glucose}_{\text{out}} + n\,\text{Na}^{+}_{\text{out}} \;\xrightarrow{\;\text{SGLT1}\;}\; \text{glucose}_{\text{in}} + n\,\text{Na}^{+}_{\text{in}}$$
  Inhibiting the Na-K pump collapses the apical Na⁺ gradient and abolishes active glucose absorption.
- **GLUT5 (apical fructose) and GLUT2 (basolateral, all three sugars):** carrier-mediated **facilitated diffusion** down the concentration gradient, Na⁺-independent.

### Clinical boxes (Section 1)
- **Box 45-1 — Lactase deficiency.** Primary lactase deficiency = isolated post-weaning fall in lactase, normal other enzymes, normal histology. Symptoms (diarrhea, cramps, flatus) depend on gastric emptying, transit time, and colonic bacterial fermentation. Treatment: reduce lactose or use lactase-treated products.
- **Box 45-2 — Glucose-galactose malabsorption.** Hereditary **SGLT1** mutations (single amino-acid substitutions) → osmotic diarrhea on glucose/galactose/lactose; **fructose (GLUT5) is tolerated**. No glycosuria (renal SGLT2 compensates).

## Section 2 — Protein, peptide, and amino-acid digestion-absorption (pp. 920–925)

### Subsection headings
- **Proteins require hydrolysis to oligopeptides or amino acids; four absorptive pathways** (pp. 920–921)
- **Luminal digestion: gastric pepsin + 5 pancreatic proteases (activation cascade)** (pp. 921–922)
- **Brush-border and cytosolic peptidases** (pp. 922)
- **Whole-protein absorption by apical endocytosis (neonatal; M cells)** (pp. 922–923)
- **PepT1: H⁺-driven di/tri/tetrapeptide uptake** (pp. 923)
- **Apical and basolateral amino-acid transporters** (pp. 923–925)

### Core claims
- Western dietary protein **70–100 g/day**; **~50% of protein entering the small intestine is endogenous** (secreted enzymes, hormones, immunoglobulins, desquamated cells, plasma proteins). Protein assimilation is very efficient (<4% of nitrogen lost in stool).
- **Four pathways** (Fig. 45-6, 45-7): (1) luminal proteases → AA, absorbed; (2) luminal → peptides, **brush-border peptidases** → AA, absorbed; (3) luminal → peptides, **peptides taken up intact** then digested by **cytosolic** peptidases; (4) intact protein taken up by **endocytosis** (neonatal/antigenic).
- **Gastric pepsin.** Chief cells secrete **pepsinogen**; activated at low pH; pepsin is an **endopeptidase** (aromatic/large neutral residues), optimal pH 1.8–3.5, irreversibly inactivated above pH 7.2. Pepsin digests ~10–15% of dietary protein but is **not essential** (gastrectomy/pernicious-anemia patients have normal fecal nitrogen).
- **Pancreatic proteases (Table 45-2).** Five secreted as **proenzymes**. **Enterokinase (enteropeptidase)** of the jejunal brush border activates **trypsinogen → trypsin** by cleaving a hexapeptide; **trypsin then auto-activates trypsinogen and activates all the others** (chymotrypsinogen, proelastase, procarboxypeptidase A and B). Secretion as proenzymes + luminal activation **prevents pancreatic autodigestion**.
- **Endopeptidases** (trypsin, chymotrypsin, elastase) → oligopeptides of 2–6 AA; **exopeptidases** (carboxypeptidase A, B) cleave C-terminal residues → single AA. Net: ~70% of luminal amino nitrogen as oligopeptides, ~30% free AA.
- **Brush-border peptidases** (endo-, exo-, dipeptidases) prefer **larger oligopeptides (3–8 AA)**; **cytosolic peptidases** prefer **di- and tripeptides**. They are distinct, independently regulated molecules (unlike oligosaccharidases, which are brush-border only).
- **Whole-protein uptake** (Fig. 45-7): enterocytes endocytose intact protein, mostly degraded in lysosomes (~90%); **M cells** over Peyer's patches package antigens in clathrin-coated vesicles for the lamina propria → immune sampling. Neonatal absorption of intact colostral protein (passive immunity) ceases by **~6 months** ("closure," hormonally/corticosteroid mediated).
- **PepT1 (SLC15A1):** apical **H⁺/oligopeptide cotransporter** moving **di-, tri-, and tetrapeptides** into the enterocyte; **proton-driven** (not Na⁺-driven). Also carries dipeptide-like antibiotics (amino-substituted cephalosporins). The **"kinetic advantage"**: glycine delivered as the dipeptide glycylglycine gives **~2× higher** blood glycine than free glycine (Fig. 45-8). Intracellular peptides are hydrolyzed to AA before basolateral exit; few peptides reach portal blood (mainly proline-containing, hydrolysis-resistant).
- **Apical amino-acid transporters (≥7 systems, Table 36-1).** Predominant: **system B⁰ (SLC6A19, SLC6A15)** — Na⁺-dependent, electrogenic uptake of **neutral** L-amino acids (secondary active transport, analogous to SGLT1). **B⁰⁺ (SLC6A14)** broader; **b⁰⁺ (SLC7A9/SLC3A1 dimer)** Na⁺-independent, cationic + cystine. Separate carriers for anionic, cationic, β-amino acids, and imino acids.
- **Basolateral (≥6 systems):** three **Na⁺-independent** transporters export AA to blood; two **Na⁺-dependent** transporters import AA for crypt-cell nutrition (crypt cells lack apical AA uptake).

### Citation-anchor quotes
- > "Both gastric and pancreatic proteases, unlike the digestive enzymes for carbohydrates and lipids, are secreted as proenzymes that require conversion to their active form." (p. 921)
- > "Trypsin not only autoactivates trypsinogen but also activates the other pancreatic proteolytic proenzymes." (p. 921)
- > "The coordinated action of these pancreatic proteases converts ~70% of luminal amino nitrogen to oligopeptides and ~30% to free amino acids." (p. 922)
- > "Oligopeptide uptake is an active process driven not by an Na⁺ gradient, but by a proton gradient." (p. 923)
- > "Administering an amino acid as a peptide … results in a higher blood level of the amino acid than administering an equivalent amount of the same amino acid as a monomer." (p. 923)

### Transport relations
- **PepT1 (apical di/tri/tetrapeptides):** H⁺-coupled symport, energized by the apical H⁺ gradient (acid microclimate maintained by Na-H exchange):
  $$\text{peptide}_{\text{out}} + \text{H}^{+}_{\text{out}} \;\xrightarrow{\;\text{PepT1}\;}\; \text{peptide}_{\text{in}} + \text{H}^{+}_{\text{in}}$$
- **System B⁰ (apical neutral AA):** Na⁺-coupled, electrogenic secondary active transport (same driving logic as SGLT1).

- **Box 45-4 — Lysinuric protein intolerance** (basolateral cationic-AA exit defect; present in intestine, liver, kidney).

## Section 3 — Lipid digestion and absorption (pp. 925–933)

### Subsection headings
- **Natural lipids are sparingly water-soluble; physicochemical classification** (pp. 925–926)
- **Dietary lipids are predominantly TAGs** (pp. 926–927)
- **Endogenous lipids: biliary phospholipid and cholesterol** (p. 927)
- **Mechanical disruption → emulsion** (p. 927)
- **Lingual and gastric (acid) lipase initiate digestion** (pp. 927–928)
- **Pancreatic lipase, colipase, milk lipase, PLA₂, cholesterol esterase complete digestion** (pp. 928–929)
- **Products enter the bulk phase as vesicles, mixed micelles, monomers** (pp. 929–930)
- **Diffusion through unstirred layers; apical uptake** (pp. 930–931)
- **Re-esterification and chylomicron assembly** (pp. 931–932)
- **Chylomicrons → lymph (fed); VLDLs → lymph (fasted)** (pp. 932–933)

### Core claims
- Western diet **~140 g fat/day (~60% of energy)**; **>90% is TAG** (long-chain fatty-acyl esters of glycerol at the sn1/sn2/sn3 positions); ~5% phospholipid (mostly **phosphatidylcholine/lecithin**); ~0.5 g unesterified cholesterol. **Biliary lipid exceeds dietary** by 2–4× (phospholipid 10–15 g/day; cholesterol 1–2 g/day).
- **Emulsification** (mechanical: chewing, antral churning against a closed pylorus, retropulsion) increases surface-area-to-volume; droplets are stabilized by a surface coat of phospholipids, cholesterol, denatured protein, and digestion products (fatty acids, MAGs) that prevents coalescence.
- **Acid lipases (lingual + gastric).** Human gastric lipase (42 kDa, secreted by chief cells, gastrin-stimulated) is a **serine hydrolase with acidic pH optimum (~pH 4)**, acid-stable, pepsin-resistant, not inhibited by the emulsifier surface; it prefers the **sn3** position, leaving sn1,2-DAG. **Important in neonates** (pancreatic lipase secretion is low at birth) and in pancreatic insufficiency. Long-chain fatty acids (LCFAs) released in the stomach stay protonated/insoluble in the TAG core; SCFAs/MCFAs are absorbed across the gastric mucosa into portal blood. Acid lipases are inactivated at neutral pH and by pancreatic proteases in the small intestine.
- **Pancreatic (alkaline) lipase** ("colipase-dependent pancreatic lipase," 48 kDa): the major lipolytic enzyme, secreted **1000× in excess** of need; requires **alkaline pH, Ca²⁺, bile salts, fatty acids, and colipase**. It is active only at the **oil-water interface**; surface emulsifiers and **bile-salt micelles inhibit** it by displacing it from the droplet. **Colipase** (secreted as procolipase, activated by trypsin → colipase + **enterostatin** pentapeptide) is a cofactor that anchors lipase to the interface and reverses bile-salt inhibition. A lipase **lid** (peptide loop over the active site) opens on interfacial binding. Pancreatic lipase hydrolyzes the **sn1 and sn3** ester bonds → **2 fatty acids + one sn2-monoacylglycerol (2-MAG)**.
- **Other pancreatic esterases.** **Carboxyl ester hydrolase** (= bile salt-stimulated milk lipase; aka cholesterol esterase, pancreatic esterase, lysophospholipase) — broad specificity, yields free cholesterol and glycerol. **Phospholipase A₂ (PLA₂)** — trypsin-activated proenzyme, alkaline pH, requires bile salts + Ca²⁺; removes the sn2-fatty acid from glycerophospholipids → **sn1-lysophospholipid**. **Bile salt-stimulated milk lipase** digests DAGs, MAGs, cholesteryl esters, and fat-soluble-vitamin esters — important for breast-fed infants.
- **CCK and GIP** are released by duodenal mucosa when gastric fatty acids arrive; **CCK** contracts the gallbladder, relaxes the sphincter of Oddi (bile flow), and stimulates pancreatic enzyme secretion.
- **Bulk-phase products** (Fig. 45-12): TAG-droplet hydrolysis builds a surface multilamellar liquid-crystalline layer (fatty acids, MAGs, lysolecithin, cholesterol, bile salts) that buds off as **multilamellar vesicles → unilamellar vesicles → mixed micelles** (bile salts + mixed lipids). Centrifuging fat-digestion contents gives three phases: oily layer, an aqueous layer of vesicles/micelles/monomers, and a calcium-soap pellet. **Most fat absorption is from the micellar phase**; when intraluminal bile salt is low (newborns, obstructive jaundice) absorption proceeds from vesicles.
- **Diffusion barriers:** mucous gel + **unstirred water layer** (now estimated **~40 µm**, not a major barrier) + apical membrane. Mixed micelles act as a high-capacity **reservoir**, raising the effective concentration of LCFAs near the brush border by ~10⁵–10⁶ vs. monomers in water. At the brush border, an **acidic microclimate** (apical Na-H exchange) protonates fatty acids, which leave the micelle to enter the cell.
- **Apical uptake** (Fig. 45-13): traditionally simple/nonionic diffusion; at least three membrane proteins facilitate fatty-acid entry — **FAT/CD36**, plasma-membrane **FABPpm**, and **FATPs (SLC27)**. **Cholesterol uptake** uses the **NPC1L1** protein — the target of the cholesterol-lowering drug **ezetimibe**, which blocks internalization of the cholesterol–NPC1L1 complex. Bile salts mostly stay in the lumen (those entering are pumped back out by **ABCG5/ABCG8**); bile salts are actively reabsorbed by **ASBT** in the distal ileum.
- **Re-esterification & chylomicron assembly** (Fig. 45-14). LCFAs bind cytosolic **FABP2** (intestinal FABP, highest in proximal jejunal villi) → carried to the **smooth ER (SER)**. Postprandially the **2-MAG (monoacylglycerol) pathway** re-esterifies fatty acids onto absorbed 2-MAG; during fasting the **phosphatidic-acid pathway** dominates. Both require activation of fatty acid to **acyl-CoA** by long-chain acyl-CoA synthetase. **Apolipoproteins** synthesized in RER associate with TAG in the SER (apoA-I joins in the Golgi). Chylomicrons (~250 nm; mostly TAG; **apoB-48**) move SER → Golgi (glycosylation of apolipoproteins) → basolateral membrane in transport vesicles; **basolateral exocytosis is rate-limiting**.
- **Export.** Chylomicrons are too large for blood-capillary fenestrae and enter the **lymph (lacteals)** via large interendothelial channels → cisterna chyli → thoracic duct → left subclavian vein. VLDLs (30–80 nm) are also secreted into lymph, more during fasting and carrying endogenous lipid. **SCFAs, MCFAs, and glycerol** are water-soluble, **bypass re-esterification, and pass directly into portal blood** — making medium-chain TAG a useful fat substitute in malabsorption. Most dietary lipid is absorbed **within 60 cm of the pylorus**; the process has large reserve and redundancy.

### Clinical box (Section 3)
- **Box 45-5 — Celiac disease.** Autoimmune response to the gluten peptide **gliadin** (wheat/rye/barley); anti-tissue-transglutaminase antibodies; duodenal villous atrophy with crypt elongation ("flat biopsy") → malabsorption + steatorrhea; lifelong gluten-free diet. Steatorrhea = the marker of fat malabsorption. Fat malabsorption is classified by the impaired step: intraluminal digestion, intraluminal dispersion, mucosal penetration, chylomicron assembly, or transport to blood.

## Section 4 — Vitamins and minerals (pp. 933–941)

### Subsection headings
- **Fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, K) follow the lipid pathway** (pp. 933–934)
- **Folate: deconjugation by brush-border conjugase, then anion-exchange uptake** (pp. 933–936)
- **Cobalamin (B₁₂): haptocorrin → intrinsic factor → ileal endocytosis** (pp. 935–938)
- **Ca²⁺: active duodenal (TRPV6/calbindin/vitamin D) + passive paracellular** (pp. 938–939)
- **Mg²⁺: active in the ileum** (p. 939)
- **Iron: heme and nonheme, duodenum; DMT1, ferroportin, hephaestin, hepcidin** (pp. 939–941)

### Core claims — fat-soluble vitamins
- A, D, E, K follow the **lipid-absorption pathway**: freed from food protein by gastric acidity/proteolysis and from esters by carboxyl ester hydrolases; incorporated into emulsion droplets/vesicles/mixed micelles; taken up by diffusion or transporters; loaded into chylomicrons/VLDLs in the SER → lymph → liver (receptor-mediated endocytosis). Absorption efficiency **50–80% for A, D, K; only 20–30% for E**. High vitamin A impairs E and K absorption; high E enhances A.
- Deficiency arises in fat-malabsorption states (bariatric surgery, **orlistat** [lipase inhibitor], **cholestyramine** [bile-acid binder], hepatobiliary disease): A → blindness; D → bone demineralization; E → neuro/erythrocyte defects; K → bleeding/hypercoagulable disorders.

### Core claims — folate
- Dietary folate is mostly **polyglutamate (PteGlu₇)**; the medicinal/absorbable form is **monoglutamate (PteGlu₁)**. A brush-border **folate conjugase** (zinc-activated exopeptidase) sequentially removes glutamates — this **deconjugation is rate-limiting**, occurs only in the proximal small intestine, optimal at **pH 5**.
- Apical uptake of PteGlu₁ is a saturable **anion exchange** (folate–OH⁻ exchange) via **SLC46A1**, enhanced at acid pH; basolateral exit mechanism unknown. Intracellular dihydrofolate reductase reduces PteGlu₁ → DHF → **THF** (active), then to **5,10-methylene-THF** (DNA synthesis) and **N⁵-methyl-THF** (methyl donor for methionine synthesis). Folate deficiency → **megaloblastic anemia**; supplementation in pregnancy reduces neural-tube defects.

### Core claims — cobalamin (B₁₂)
- Synthesized only by microorganisms; sourced from animal products (strict vegetarians at risk). Coenzyme for **homocysteine:methionine methyltransferase** (transfers a methyl from N⁵-methyl-THF → homocysteine → methionine + THF) and for methylmalonyl-CoA mutase. Cobalamin and folate deficiencies cause **identical megaloblastic anemia** (the "methyl-folate trap"); cobalamin deficiency additionally causes neurological abnormalities.
- **Sequential handling** (Fig. 45-16C/D): (1) gastric pepsin + acid release cobalamin from food protein; (2) freed cobalamin binds **haptocorrin** (R-binder, from salivary/gastric glands) — acid favors this binding, **not** intrinsic-factor binding; (3) parietal cells secrete **intrinsic factor (IF)**, a 45-kDa glycoprotein; (4) in the alkaline duodenum, **pancreatic proteases degrade haptocorrin**, freeing cobalamin to bind **IF**; (5) the cobalamin–IF complex is enzyme-resistant and binds specific **apical receptors on ileal enterocytes** (requires Ca²⁺, not energy-dependent for binding); (6) endocytosis (energy-dependent); (7) intracellular dissociation; cobalamin binds **transcobalamin II** for basolateral exit into portal blood → liver. **IF secretion parallels acid secretion** but is unaffected by omeprazole (H-K pump inhibitor); H₂-blockers do reduce it.
- Total-body cobalamin **~5 mg**; daily requirement only **~2 µg**. The intestinal load is ~equal parts diet and **bile (enterohepatic circulation)**. Deficiency causes: vegetarian diet, **pernicious anemia** (loss of parietal cells → no acid/IF), bacterial overgrowth, ileal Crohn/resection (loss of ileal receptors).

### Core claims — calcium
- Dietary Ca²⁺ **~1000 mg/day** (mostly milk); ~500 mg absorbed, ~325 secreted → **net ~175 mg/day**. Oxalate (leafy vegetables) lowers bioavailability.
- **Two routes** (Fig. 45-17): (1) **active transcellular** absorption, **duodenum only**, vitamin-D-regulated; (2) **passive paracellular** absorption throughout the small intestine, **not** vitamin-D-regulated — quantitatively the larger route (jejunum + ileum have more surface area/slower flow). Transcellular steps: apical entry via **TRPV6** Ca²⁺ channels (down the electrochemical gradient); cytosolic buffering by **calbindin**; basolateral extrusion by a **Ca pump (PMCA)** and **Na-Ca exchanger (NCX1)**. **1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D** stimulates all three steps, chiefly by inducing **calbindin** synthesis (genomic effect). Absorption is enhanced by low plasma [Ca²⁺], pregnancy, and lactation; declines with age.

### Core claims — magnesium and iron
- **Mg²⁺**: active absorption in the **ileum** (contrast Ca²⁺ in duodenum); **not** consistently vitamin-D-dependent; passive absorption elsewhere. RDA ~350 mg (men)/~280 mg (women).
- **Iron**: only **10–20%** of dietary iron absorbed (heme > nonheme); **no regulated excretory pathway**, so body stores depend on absorption. Both forms absorbed in the **duodenum** (Fig. 45-18).
  - **Nonheme iron**: **Fe³⁺ → Fe²⁺** reduced at the apical surface by **Dcytb** (duodenal cytochrome b); ascorbate reduces/solubilizes iron (enhances), tannins chelate (inhibit). Apical uptake of Fe²⁺ via **DMT1 (SLC11A2)**, an **H⁺-coupled** divalent-metal cotransporter (also carries Cd²⁺, Pb²⁺). Cytosolic **mobilferrin** ferries Fe²⁺ to the basolateral membrane; export via **ferroportin (FPN1, SLC40A1)**; the ferroxidase **hephaestin** (ceruloplasmin homolog) oxidizes Fe²⁺ → Fe³⁺ for binding to plasma **transferrin**.
  - **Heme iron**: heme enters by an unknown apical mechanism; intracellular **heme oxygenase** splits it, releasing **Fe²⁺ (+ CO + biliverdin)**, then handled like nonheme iron.
  - **Regulation**: iron stores tightly control absorption. **Hepcidin** (25-AA hepatic peptide) is the master negative regulator — it binds basolateral **FPN1 → internalization/degradation → reduced absorption**. Iron deficiency upregulates DMT1 and FPN1; FPN1 mRNA has an **iron-response element** in its 5′ UTR. IL-6 raises hepcidin → **anemia of inflammation**.

### Citation-anchor quotes
- > "The absorption efficiency of fat-soluble vitamins varies from 50% to 80% for A, D, and K to only 20% to 30% for vitamin E." (p. 933)
- > "This stepwise hydrolysis of the polyglutamate chain of PteGlu7 is the rate-limiting step in folate digestion-absorption." (p. 934)
- > "Cobalamin without IF neither binds to ileal receptors nor is absorbed." (p. 937)
- > "The active form of vitamin D—1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D—stimulates all three steps of the transcellular pathway, but its most important effect is to enhance the second step by increasing the synthesis of calbindin." (p. 938)
- > "The enterocyte takes up nonheme iron across the apical membrane via the divalent metal cotransporter DMT1 (SLC11A2), which cotransports Fe²⁺ and H⁺ into the cell." (p. 939)
- > "Hepcidin, a 25-amino-acid peptide secreted by hepatocytes, reduces duodenal iron absorption by downregulating the iron exporter ferroportin (FPN1)." (p. 940)

### Transport relations
- **DMT1 (apical Fe²⁺):** H⁺-coupled symport, energized by the apical H⁺ gradient (Na-H exchange), analogous to PepT1:
  $$\text{Fe}^{2+}_{\text{out}} + \text{H}^{+}_{\text{out}} \;\xrightarrow{\;\text{DMT1}\;}\; \text{Fe}^{2+}_{\text{in}} + \text{H}^{+}_{\text{in}}$$
- **Hepcidin–FPN1 control loop:** ↑ iron stores (or IL-6) → ↑ hepcidin → FPN1 internalization/degradation → ↓ basolateral Fe export → ↓ net iron absorption (negative feedback).
- **Folate (apical PteGlu₁):** anion antiport — $\text{folate}^{-}_{\text{out}} / \text{OH}^{-}_{\text{in}}$ exchange via SLC46A1, acid-pH-enhanced.

Basolateral **hepcidin** binding triggers FPN1 internalization/degradation (regulatory input).

### Clinical box (Section 4)
- **Box 45-6 — Hemochromatosis.** Hereditary hemochromatosis (HFE C282Y, autosomal recessive) → excess iron absorption, hepatic iron overload, cirrhosis, diabetes ("bronze diabetes"), cardiomyopathy. Mechanism likely **inappropriately low hepcidin** → unrestrained FPN1 → excess absorption. Treatment: phlebotomy. The same hepcidin–FPN1 axis underlies **anemia of inflammation** (IL-6 → ↑ hepcidin → ↓ absorption).

## Section 5 — Nutritional requirements (pp. 941–943)

### Core claims
- **No absolute requirement for carbohydrate or fat** per se. Typical Western caloric split ~55–60% carbohydrate, 25–30% fat, 10–15% protein. Energy need is set chiefly by activity level and lean-body-mass fraction (adipose has low metabolic rate). 1 kg fat ≈ 9400 kcal.
- **Essential fatty acids**: mammals cannot insert double bonds beyond C9, so dietary **linoleate (18:2)** and **linolenate (18:3)** are required (precursors of arachidonic acid → prostaglandins/leukotrienes/thromboxanes). Omega-3 PUFAs appear cardioprotective.
- **Protein**: ~0.6 g/kg/day to maintain nitrogen balance; RDA **0.8 g/kg** (adults), ~1 g/kg (adolescents), ~2 g/kg (first 6 months), higher in pregnancy/lactation/athletes/burns/post-surgery. Diet must supply the **nine essential amino acids**; protein "scored" against egg (=100) by limiting essential AA. Amino acids also serve roles beyond protein (arginine → NO; glutamate/glycine neurotransmitters; glutamine → renal NH₃, muscle protein turnover) and underpin immune competence.
- **Minerals and vitamins** are not energy sources but are needed as enzyme cofactors, in protein complexes, or as biomolecule precursors. Of 13 vitamins, RDAs exist for 11 (biotin, pantothenate have "safe ranges"). Water-soluble vitamins are renally cleared (toxicity uncommon); **fat-soluble vitamins accumulate and can be toxic** (e.g., hypervitaminosis A from polar-bear liver). Excess minerals interact (excess Ca²⁺ impairs iron/zinc/Mg²⁺ use; excess copper affects zinc).

### Citation-anchor quotes
- > "Because mammals cannot insert double bonds beyond carbon 9, they need two fatty acids in the diet: linoleate … and linolenate." (p. 941)
- > "The average human needs ~0.6 g of protein per kilogram of body weight per day to maintain nitrogen balance. The RDA for protein is ~0.8 g/kg." (p. 942)

## Glossary

- **Membrane (brush-border) digestion** — final oligomer hydrolysis at the apical membrane by integral enzymes.
- **α-Amylase** — endoenzyme; internal α-1,4 only → maltose, maltotriose, α-limit dextrins.
- **Sucrase-isomaltase / lactase / glucoamylase (maltase)** — the four brush-border oligosaccharidase activities.
- **SGLT1 (SLC5A1)** — apical Na⁺/glucose-galactose cotransporter (secondary active).
- **GLUT5 / GLUT2** — apical fructose / basolateral all-sugars facilitated transporters.
- **Lactase deficiency / hydrogen breath test** — post-weaning lactase decline; unabsorbed lactose → colonic H₂.
- **Enterokinase (enteropeptidase)** — activates trypsinogen → trypsin (the cascade trigger).
- **Endopeptidase vs. exopeptidase** — internal-bond vs. terminal-residue cleavage.
- **PepT1 (SLC15A1)** — apical H⁺/di-tri-tetrapeptide cotransporter; "kinetic advantage."
- **System B⁰ / b⁰⁺ / y⁺L** — neutral (apical, Na⁺) / cationic-cystine (apical, Na⁺-indep) / cationic (basolateral) AA transporters (Hartnup / cystinuria / lysinuric protein intolerance).
- **Pancreatic lipase + colipase** — sn1,3 hydrolysis of TAG → 2 FA + 2-MAG; colipase reverses bile-salt inhibition.
- **PLA₂ / carboxyl ester hydrolase (cholesterol esterase)** — phospholipid and broad-ester hydrolysis.
- **Mixed micelle / unstirred water layer** — micellar reservoir delivering lipolytic products to the brush border.
- **NPC1L1 / ezetimibe** — enterocyte cholesterol uptake protein and its inhibitor.
- **FABP2, acyl-CoA, 2-MAG pathway** — intracellular re-esterification machinery.
- **Chylomicron (apoB-48) / VLDL** — TAG-rich lipoproteins exported to **lymph**.
- **SCFA/MCFA portal route** — short/medium-chain fatty acids bypass lymph → portal blood.
- **Steatorrhea** — fecal fat; marker of fat malabsorption.
- **Folate conjugase / PteGlu₇ → PteGlu₁ / SLC46A1** — deconjugation (rate-limiting) + folate-OH⁻ exchange.
- **Haptocorrin / intrinsic factor / transcobalamin II** — sequential cobalamin carriers; ileal receptor uptake.
- **Pernicious anemia** — parietal-cell loss → no IF → B₁₂ malabsorption.
- **TRPV6 / calbindin / NCX1 / 1,25-(OH)₂-vitamin D** — active duodenal Ca²⁺ transcellular machinery.
- **DMT1 (SLC11A2) / Dcytb / mobilferrin / ferroportin (FPN1) / hephaestin / transferrin** — iron-absorption chain.
- **Hepcidin** — master negative regulator of iron absorption via FPN1 degradation.
