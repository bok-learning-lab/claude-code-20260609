## Section 1 — Transport of gametes and fertilization (pp. 1129–1132)

### Subsection headings
- **Cilia and smooth muscle transport the egg and sperm within the female genital tract** (p. 1129)
- **The "capacitation" of the spermatozoa that occurs in the female genital tract enhances the ability of the sperm cell to fertilize the ovum** (p. 1129)
- **Fertilization begins as the sperm cell attaches to the zona pellucida and undergoes the acrosomal reaction, and it ends with the fusion of the male and female pronuclei** (pp. 1129–1132)

### Core claims
- A man deposits **150–600 million sperm** at ejaculation; only **50–100** reach the ampulla of the fallopian tube, where fertilization normally occurs. Sperm arrive within **~5 min** — too fast for swimming alone; forceful contractions of uterus, cervix, and tubes (aided by seminal prostaglandins and female orgasm) propel them.
- The fimbriae sweep the ovulated **oocyte–cumulus complex** (oocyte + cumulus oophorus + corona radiata) into the tube; cilia and tubal smooth muscle move it toward the uterus.
- **Capacitation** is a poorly understood maturation that sperm must undergo in the female tract (or in vitro) before they can penetrate the zona pellucida; removal/modification of a protective surface protein coat appears central. It is **not organ-specific** — GIFT, intraperitoneal insemination, and IVF all work without cervical/uterine transit.
- The **zona pellucida** is a latticework of three glycoproteins: **ZP1** cross-links filamentous **ZP2** and **ZP3**. Sperm plasma-membrane receptors bind **ZP3**, initiating signal transduction.
- **The eight steps of fertilization** (Fig. 56-1):
  1. Sperm head weaves past follicular cells and **binds the zona pellucida** (receptor → ZP3).
  2. **Acrosomal reaction**: a rise in sperm intracellular [Ca²⁺]ᵢ triggers fusion of the outer acrosomal membrane with the sperm plasma membrane → exocytosis of hydrolytic acrosomal enzymes.
  3. Sperm **penetrates the zona pellucida** by enzymatic digestion (protease inhibitors block it) **and** mechanical action — the head oscillates about a neck fulcrum at **~6–8 Hz**, cutting a tangential cleavage slit.
  4. **Sperm–oocyte membrane fusion**: oocyte microvilli envelop the sperm; only the cytoplasmic head and tail enter, leaving the sperm plasma membrane behind ("like a snake crawling out of its skin").
  5. **Cortical reaction**: sperm entry generates IP₃ → Ca²⁺ release from internal stores → [Ca²⁺]ᵢ waves; cortical granules fuse with the oocyte membrane, releasing enzymes that **harden the zona pellucida** and destroy ZP receptors → **block to polyspermy**.
  6. Oocyte **completes meiosis II** (arrested in metaphase since just before ovulation) → extrudes the **second polar body**; nucleus becomes the **female pronucleus**.
  7. Sperm nucleus decondenses → **male pronucleus**; the cytoplasmic tail degenerates.
  8. **Syngamy**: male + female pronuclei fuse → **zygote** with 46 chromosomes (23 maternal + 23 paternal). Chromosomal sex is set here (X-sperm → XX female; Y-sperm → XY male).
- **Polyspermy is lethal**: polyspermic embryos are polyploid and arrest at early cleavage. Hence two blocks — the cortical reaction hardens the zona and destroys ZP receptors.

## Section 2 — Implantation of the developing embryo (pp. 1132–1136)

### Subsection headings
- **The presence of an embryo leads to decidualization of the endometrium** (pp. 1133–1134)
- **Uterine secretions nourish the preimplantation embryo, promote growth, and prepare it for implantation** (pp. 1133–1134)
- **The blastocyst secretes substances that facilitate implantation** (pp. 1134–1135)
- **During implantation, the blastocyst apposes itself to the endometrium, adheres to epithelial cells, and finally invades the stroma** (pp. 1135–1136)

### Core claims
- **Timeline.** Fertilization in the ampulla → conceptus remains in the tube **~72 h**, reaching the **morula** (≥12-cell mulberry mass), nourished by tubal secretions; isthmic smooth-muscle contractions hold it back while the endometrium prepares. Then it passes to the uterus and becomes a **blastocyst** (fluid-filled cavity, outer **trophoblast/trophoectoderm** + **inner cell mass**). It floats free **~72 h** more, so **implantation occurs 6–7 days after ovulation**.
- **Decidualization.** Progesterone from the corpus luteum drives the secretory endometrium to a vascularized, secretory state; **predecidualization** begins 9–10 days post-ovulation near spiral arteries (stromal cells → rounded decidual cells). With pregnancy these changes are sustained and extended → the **decidua** (the specialized endometrium of pregnancy). Three zones: **decidua basalis** (under the embryo), **decidua capsularis** (over it), **decidua parietalis** (the rest). The hypersecretory glands give the **Arias-Stella phenomenon**.
- **Uterine nourishment.** Before establishing the placental lifeline the embryo lives on uterine secretions — steroid-dependent proteins (Table 56-1), cholesterol, iron, fat-soluble vitamins, matrix and adhesion molecules. **Pinopodes** (progesterone-dependent, estrogen-inhibited finger-like protrusions, days ~19–21) endocytose luminal fluid, bringing embryo and epithelium closer and defining the **implantation window** (days ~20–24).
- **hCG as a long-range signal.** The early blastocyst secretes **human chorionic gonadotropin (hCG)** — closely related to LH — which **rescues the corpus luteum** as maternal LH falls, and also acts as an **autocrine trophoblast growth factor** with protease activity (Table 56-2).
- **Hatching.** ~6–7 days post-ovulation the zona pellucida degenerates ("hatching"), aided by endometrial lytic factors; **plasmin** (from plasminogen) is a candidate uterine lytic factor.
- **Three stages of implantation:**
  - **Apposition** — loose contact between trophoectoderm and endometrial epithelium, usually in a crypt where the zona is lysed; the inner cell mass rotates to point toward the endometrium.
  - **Adhesion** — ligand–receptor binding via trophoblast microvilli; **integrins** bind matrix proteins (laminin, fibronectin, collagen IV, vitronectin); endometrial integrin expression (days 20–24) marks receptivity; heparan-sulfate proteoglycans also participate.
  - **Invasion** — trophoblast proliferates and differentiates into inner **cytotrophoblast** and outer **syncytiotrophoblast** (a multinucleated giant cell, the principal maternal-fetal interface). Syncytiotrophoblast protrusions secrete **TNF-α** (disrupting cadherins/β-catenin) and **proteases** (metalloproteases, serine proteases) to penetrate basement membrane and reach stroma. Decidual cells fill with lipid/glycogen and, on degenerating near the invading trophoblast, nourish the embryo.
- **Immune evasion.** The blastocyst is genetically foreign. Cytotrophoblasts at the interface express the non-classical MHC molecule **HLA-G**, blocking recognition by **decidual NK cells**, and the **Fas ligand**, inducing apoptosis in Fas-bearing maternal lymphocytes.

- > "The decidua is the specialized endometrium of pregnancy." (p. 1132)
- > "A long-range signal that is secreted by the early blastocyst is human chorionic gonadotropin (hCG), which is closely related to LH … and sustains the corpus luteum in the face of rapidly falling levels of maternal LH." (p. 1134)

## Section 3 — Physiology of the placenta (pp. 1136–1139)

### Subsection headings
- **At the placenta, the space between the fetus's chorionic villi and the mother's endometrial wall contains a continuously renewed pool of extravasated maternal blood** (pp. 1136–1138)
- **Gases and other solutes move across the placenta** (pp. 1138–1139)
- **The placenta makes a variety of peptide hormones, including hCG and human chorionic somatomammotropin** (p. 1139)

### Core claims
- **Villus development.** Syncytiotrophoblast **lacunae** form 8–9 days post-fertilization; by 12–15 days they tap endometrial **veins then spiral arteries**, opening free communication with maternal blood. Cytotrophoblasts grow into the syncytium → **primary chorionic villi**; mesenchyme invades → **secondary villi**; de novo fetal vessels form → **tertiary villi**. Mature villi present a thin syncytiotrophoblast brush border to maternal blood, beneath which lie cytotrophoblasts, mesenchyme, and fetal vessels.
- **The intervillous space is the "capillary."** Lacunae merge into one large **intervillous space**; ~120 maternal **spiral arteries** spurt blood (pulsatile, perpendicular to the wall) toward the chorionic plate; villi (a "forest of trees" rooted in the chorionic plate "soil") are bathed; blood drains through **basal-plate venous orifices** parallel to the wall. No true capillaries connect the maternal arterioles and venules. Uterine contractions attenuate inflow and interrupt venous drainage, so intervillous volume actually **rises** during contractions, sustaining (reduced) exchange.
- **Fetal blood flow.** Two **umbilical arteries** carry **deoxygenated** blood to the placenta, branch through the chorionic plate into villus capillaries, and return oxygenated/nutrient-rich blood via the single **umbilical vein**.
- **Amniotic fluid** buffers mechanically and carries waste; water turns over ≥1×/day. After 10–12 wk fetal kidneys provide ~75% of production (lungs the rest); removal is by fetal GI tract (~55%), amnion (~30%), lungs (~15%).
- **Gas exchange.** Maternal intervillous blood starts near arterial values (Pᴏ₂ ~100, Pᴄᴏ₂ ~40 mm Hg, pH 7.40) but falls to **Pᴏ₂ 30–35 mm Hg** (Hb sat ~65%) as O₂ diffuses to the fetus. Despite this low Pᴏ₂, the fetus is well oxygenated because **fetal Hb has higher O₂ affinity** (extracts O₂ from maternal Hb), the fetus has high cardiac output per body weight, and late fetal [Hb] is ~50% above adult. **CO₂** moves fetus→mother down a ~5 mm Hg gradient (umbilical-artery Pᴄᴏ₂ ~48 vs. intervillous ~43) aided by fetal blood's lower CO₂ affinity.
- **Solute transport.** Urea/creatinine diffuse passively (fetus→mother); steroids shuttle by diffusion; **glucose by facilitated diffusion**; **amino acids by secondary active transport**; vitamins/minerals often pumped against gradients (ATP from glycolysis, citric-acid cycle, pentose-phosphate pathway). Large molecules (LDL, transferrin, insulin, IgG) enter by **receptor-mediated endocytosis**, increasing through gestation.
- **Placental hormones (Table 56-4).** The placenta makes amines, neuropeptides, releasing hormones (placental variants of all hypothalamic releasing hormones), proteins, glycoproteins, and steroids. Chief peptide is **hCG**. It also makes two **human chorionic somatomammotropins (hCS1, hCS2 = human placental lactogens hPL1, hPL2)** — GH/PRL-related polypeptides that shift fuel toward fatty acids and ketones (the fetoplacental fuel economy) and promote maternal mammary development.

#### Placental O₂/CO₂ gradients (mass-balance picture)

$$P_{O_2}^{\text{intervillous}} \approx 30\text{–}35 \text{ mm Hg} \;\Rightarrow\; S_{O_2}^{\text{maternal}} \approx 65\%, \qquad S_{O_2}^{\text{umbilical vein}} \approx 85\%$$

Higher umbilical-vein saturation at *lower* Pᴏ₂ reflects the leftward-shifted fetal-Hb dissociation curve.

$$\Delta P_{CO_2} = P_{CO_2}^{\text{umb. artery}} - P_{CO_2}^{\text{intervillous}} \approx 48 - 43 = 5 \text{ mm Hg (fetus} \to \text{mother)}$$

### Citation-anchor quotes
- > "In the mature placenta, fetal blood is separated from maternal blood only by the fetal capillary endothelium, some mesenchyme and cytotrophoblasts, and syncytiotrophoblast." (p. 1136)
- > "No capillaries are present between the maternal arterioles and venules; the intervillous space is the functional capillary." (pp. 1137–1138)
- > "Unlike systemic arteries after birth, umbilical arteries carry deoxygenated blood." (p. 1137)
- > "Because fetal Hb has a much higher affinity for O₂ than does maternal Hb … the fetal Hb can extract O₂ from the maternal Hb." (p. 1138)
- > "Glucose moves from the mother to the fetus by facilitated diffusion … and amino acids move by secondary active transport." (p. 1138)
- > "The most important placental peptide hormone is hCG." (p. 1139)

## Section 4 — The maternal-placental-fetal unit (pp. 1139–1142)

### Subsection headings
- **During pregnancy, progesterone and estrogens rise to levels that are substantially higher than their peaks in a normal cycle** (pp. 1139–1140)
- **After 8 weeks of gestation, the maternal-placental-fetal unit maintains high levels of progesterone and estrogens** (pp. 1140–1142)

### Core claims
- **Why the corpus luteum must be rescued.** A normal corpus luteum lives **~12 days**, then regresses as LH falls, dropping progesterone/estrogen. In pregnancy, **hCG from the syncytiotrophoblast (LH-like) rescues the corpus luteum**, sustaining steroid output through early pregnancy.
- **The luteal-placental shift.** The corpus luteum alone cannot generate late-pregnancy steroid levels. The placenta augments its own production until, **by ~8 weeks, the placenta becomes the major source** of progesterone and estrogens. **Ovariectomy before the shift → miscarriage; after the shift → pregnancy continues.**
- **The placenta is an incomplete steroidogenic organ.** It cannot make adequate **cholesterol**, and it **lacks 17α-hydroxylase, 17,20-desmolase (both P-450c17), and 16α-hydroxylase** (Table 56-5). It *does* have **3β-hydroxysteroid dehydrogenase, aromatase (P-450arom), and sulfatase**.
- **The division of labor (the fetoplacental unit).**
  - **Mother** supplies **LDL cholesterol**; the placenta converts it to **progesterone** and exports it (solving maternal progesterone supply once the corpus luteum is inadequate).
  - **Fetal adrenal + liver** supply the enzymes the placenta lacks (17α-hydroxylase and 17,20-desmolase in the adrenal; **16α-hydroxylase in the liver**). The fetus makes weak androgens only as far as **DHEA and 16α-hydroxy-DHEA**, and **sulfates** them (→ **DHEA-S, 16α-OH-DHEA-S**) to blunt their activity and protect itself.
  - The **placental sulfatase** strips the sulfate when these conjugates return, letting the placenta aromatize them to **estrone/estradiol** (from DHEA-S) and **estriol** (from 16α-OH-DHEA-S) and export them to the mother. The placenta is also a **sink** for fetal androgens, preventing masculinization of female fetuses.
- **Estriol** is unimportant in nonpregnant women but, by circulating level, is a **major pregnancy estrogen** — and a readout of the fetoplacental unit because its precursor is fetal-adrenal **16α-OH-DHEA-S**.

#### Hormonal logic in LaTeX

hCG rescue of the corpus luteum (early progesterone source):

$$\text{Syncytiotrophoblast} \xrightarrow{\text{hCG (LH-like)}} \text{Corpus luteum} \xrightarrow{} P_4,\,E_2 \quad (\text{weeks } 0\text{–}\sim8)$$

The luteal-placental shift (source crossover at ~8 weeks):

$$\text{Source}(P_4) : \; \text{Corpus luteum} \xrightarrow[\sim 8\,\text{wk}]{} \text{Placenta} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \text{ovariectomy after the shift does not end pregnancy}$$

Fetoplacental estrogen synthesis (DHEA-S precursor pathway):

$$\underbrace{\text{LDL-chol}}_{\text{mother}} \to \underbrace{\text{pregnenolone(-S)}}_{\text{placenta}\to\text{fetus}} \to \underbrace{\text{DHEA-S},\;16\alpha\text{-OH-DHEA-S}}_{\text{fetal adrenal/liver}} \xrightarrow[\text{aromatase}]{\text{placental sulfatase}} \underbrace{E_1,\,E_2,\;E_3\,(\text{estriol})}_{\text{exported to mother}}$$

### Citation-anchor quotes
- > "Early in the first trimester, hCG that is manufactured by the syncytiotrophoblast rescues the corpus luteum, which is the major source of progesterone and estrogens." (pp. 1139–1140)
- > "By 8 weeks of gestation the placenta has become the major source of these steroids—the luteal-placental shift." (p. 1140)
- > "Removal of the ovaries (with the corpus luteum) before the luteal-placental shift leads to miscarriage, whereas pregnancy continues normally if the ovaries are removed after the luteal-placental shift." (p. 1140)
- > "The human placenta cannot synthesize these hormones by itself; it requires the assistance of both mother and fetus. This joint effort … has led to the concept of the maternal-placental-fetal unit." (p. 1140)
- > "The fetus conjugates the necessary steroid intermediates to sulfate, which greatly reduces their biological activity." (p. 1140)
- > "It is only when DHEAS and 16α-hydroxy-DHEAS finally move to the placenta that a sulfatase removes the sulfate groups, and thus the placenta can complete the process of steroidogenesis and can export the hormones to the mother." (p. 1142)

## Section 5 — Response of the mother to pregnancy (pp. 1142–1144)

### Subsection headings
- **Both maternal cardiac output and blood volume increase during pregnancy** (pp. 1142–1143)
- **Increased levels of progesterone during pregnancy increase alveolar ventilation** (p. 1143)
- **Pregnancy increases the demand for dietary protein, iron, and folic acid** (p. 1143)
- **Less than one third of the total maternal weight gain during pregnancy represents the fetus** (pp. 1143–1144)

### Core claims
- **Duration.** ~**266 days (38 wk) from ovulation** or **280 days (40 wk) from the last menstrual period**.
- **Cardiovascular / volume.** Blood volume rises through the 2nd trimester, plateauing late — up to **~45% in singletons** (75–100% in twins/triplets). Plasma volume rises earlier and more (~50%) than red-cell volume (~33%) → physiologic **dilutional "anemia."** Mechanism: progesterone/estrogen vasodilation → ↓ peripheral resistance and renal perfusion → **renin-angiotensin-aldosterone** activation (↑ aldosterone, salt/water retention), plus a **leftward shift of AVP-vs-osmolality** and **refractoriness to angiotensin II**'s pressor effect. **Cardiac output** rises **35–40%** in the 1st trimester (~45% at term), mainly ↑ stroke volume (also ↑ HR); flow is targeted (renal +40%, uterine 1%→15% of CO, plus heart/skin/breast), unchanged to brain/gut/skeleton. **MAP falls mid-pregnancy** (↓ peripheral resistance) then rises in the 3rd trimester, staying ≤ normal. **Supine hypotension**: the term uterus compresses the **inferior vena cava near L5**, so CO is higher in lateral recumbency than supine.
- **Respiratory.** Diaphragm rises ~4 cm (progesterone relaxation) and the costovertebral angle widens (~+2 cm transverse) → net **↓ RV and FRC**; VC and compliance ≈ unchanged; total pulmonary resistance falls. **Tidal volume rises ~40%** (respiratory rate little changed) → **↑ alveolar ventilation** — one of the earliest changes (by 6 wk), reflecting **progesterone (± estrogen) stimulation of medullary respiratory centers**. Result: maternal **Pᴄᴏ₂ falls from ~40 to ~32 mm Hg** → mild **respiratory alkalosis** compensated by modestly lowered plasma [HCO₃⁻].
- **Nutrition.** +**30 g protein/day**; iron need rises to **~7 mg/day absorbed** (vs. ~1.5 mg/day nonpregnant) → **60 mg/day elemental-iron** supplement recommended (net ~800 mg circulating iron gained); **folate** demand rises (deficiency → maternal megaloblastic anemia and fetal **neural-tube defects**) → **400–800 µg/day folic acid**.
- **Weight gain.** Recommended **11.5–16 kg** (normal BMI). Of a 14-kg gain, only ~5 kg is intrauterine — **fetus 3.3 kg**, placenta+membranes 0.7 kg, amniotic fluid 1 kg; the other ~9 kg is maternal (uterus 0.7, blood 1.3, breasts 2.0, adipose+interstitial fluid 5.0). **Birth weight parallels maternal weight gain**; restriction is rarely advisable.

- > "Maternal blood volume may have increased by as much as 45% near term in singleton pregnancies and up to 75% to 100% in twin or triplet pregnancies." (p. 1142)
- > "Cardiac output increases appreciably during the first trimester of pregnancy (by 35% to 40%)." (pp. 1142–1143)
- > "In the supine position, the fundus of the enlarged uterus rests on the inferior vena cava near L5, thereby impeding venous return to the heart." (p. 1143)
- > "These increases in V_T and V_A are some of the earliest physiological changes during pregnancy, beginning 6 weeks after fertilization." (p. 1143)

## Section 6 — Parturition (pp. 1144–1146)

### Subsection headings
- **Human birth usually occurs at around the 40th week of gestation** (p. 1144)
- **Parturition occurs in distinct stages, numbered 0 to 3** (pp. 1144)
- **Reciprocal decreases in progesterone receptors and increases in estrogen receptors are critical for the onset of labor** (pp. 1144–1145)
- **Signals from the fetus may initiate labor** (p. 1145)
- **PGs initiate uterine contractions, and both PGs and OT sustain labor** (pp. 1145–1146)

### Core claims
- **The five components of parturition**: (1) myometrium quiescent → highly contractile; (2) cervical remodeling — **effacement** (softening) and dilation; (3) rupture of fetal membranes; (4) expulsion of uterine contents; (5) uterine **involution**.
- **Stages 0–3.**
  - **Stage 0 — Quiescence**: uterus relaxed, insensitive to uterotonins (PGs, OT); **Braxton Hicks** contractions are weak/irregular and do not induce labor.
  - **Stage 1 — Transformation/Activation**: myometrium expresses **contraction-associated proteins (CAPs)** — uterotonin receptors (PGF FP receptor, OT receptor), PG-synthesis enzymes (COX-2), and **gap-junction connexin-43** (synchronizes contractions). Cervix expresses collagen-degrading enzymes.
  - **Stage 2 — Active labor**: forceful rhythmic contractions driven by ↑ **PGs (esp. PGE₂)**, ↑ myometrial interconnectivity, and ↑ responsiveness to PGs/OT; each contraction drives the head against the softening cervix until dilation allows expulsion of fetus then placenta.
  - **Stage 3 — Involution**: **OT-driven** sustained contraction constricts spiral arterioles for **postpartum hemostasis** (preventing lethal hemorrhage from the placental bed); falling estrogen → myometrial atrophy and uterine involution over 3–6 wk; menses resume in 3–5 months.
- **Functional progesterone withdrawal (humans).** In most viviparous species labor is triggered by systemic **progesterone withdrawal + estrogen activation**. In **women, systemic progesterone stays high until the placenta is delivered**. Instead labor begins by **functional progesterone withdrawal** — desensitization of uterine cells to progesterone. Because progesterone normally suppresses estrogen-receptor expression, desensitization → ↑ **estrogen receptors** → ↑ responsiveness to estrogen → CAP induction → laboring phenotype. **Mifepristone (RU486)**, a progesterone-receptor antagonist, increases contractility and can induce labor at any stage.
- **Estrogen's role.** Augments contractility and cervical dilation; ↑ myometrial responsiveness to OT/PGs; promotes gap junctions; ↑ fetal-membrane PG release; induces cervical collagenase for effacement.
- **Fetal signals.** In **sheep**, a surge in **fetal-adrenal cortisol** (driven by the fetal HPA axis) induces placental enzymes converting progesterone → estrogens, flipping to estrogen dominance → labor. In **humans** the fetal HPA axis is **not** the dominant timer; the human initiating signals remain unclear.
- **Prostaglandins.** Uterus, placenta, fetal membranes synthesize PGs from arachidonic acid (high in term membranes). **PGF₂α and PGE₂** from decidua act in paracrine fashion on myometrium. Three effects: strongly stimulate contraction; **PGF₂α** potentiates OT by promoting gap junctions; cause cervical effacement (an inflammatory-type softening). PGs are used clinically to induce labor; **aspirin (COX inhibition)** reduces PGs and can prolong gestation. PGs are thought to **physiologically initiate** labor; OT and PGs **sustain** it.
- **Oxytocin.** Nonapeptide closely related to AVP (Fig. 56-10), made in supraoptic/paraventricular nuclei, stored in posterior pituitary. **OT → Gq-coupled OTR → PLC → IP₃ → ↑[Ca²⁺]ᵢ → calmodulin → MLCK → contraction**; OT also stimulates decidual **PGF₂α** production. **Estrogen ↑ OTR number**: the uterus is OT-insensitive until ~20 wk, then OTRs climb to **80×** baseline by ~36 wk and **200×** during early labor — explaining spontaneous contractions even without rising plasma OT. Maternal plasma OT rises gradually then in **bursts** during active labor; primary release stimulus is **cervical distention** = the **Ferguson reflex**.
- **Positive feedback.** Two self-reinforcing loops: (1) contractions → PG release → stronger contractions; (2) contractions → cervical stretch → OT release (Ferguson reflex) → more contractions. **Relaxin** (insulin-related, from corpus luteum/placenta/decidua) keeps the uterus quiet and may soften/dilate the cervix at labor. **Mechanical stretch** of the uterus to a critical size may also trigger rhythmic contractions.

#### Oxytocin signaling and feedback (LaTeX)

$$\text{OT} \to \text{OTR}\,(G_{q}) \to \text{PLC} \to \text{IP}_3 \to \uparrow[\text{Ca}^{2+}]_i \to \text{calmodulin} \to \text{MLCK} \to \text{contraction}$$

OTR upregulation by estrogen:

$$\text{OTR density:}\quad \sim20\,\text{wk (onset)} \;\to\; 80\times \text{ baseline at } \sim36\,\text{wk} \;\to\; 200\times \text{ in early labor}$$

Positive-feedback loops sustaining labor:

$$\text{contraction} \to \uparrow\text{PG} \to \text{contraction}, \qquad \text{contraction} \to \text{cervical stretch} \xrightarrow{\text{Ferguson reflex}} \uparrow\text{OT} \to \text{contraction}$$

### Citation-anchor quotes
- > "Human parturition is not associated with systemic progesterone withdrawal. Instead, labor in women is thought to be initiated by desensitization of uterine cells to the progestational actions of progesterone (i.e., functional progesterone withdrawal)." (p. 1145)
- > "Treatment of women with nuclear progesterone receptor antagonists, such as mifepristone (RU486), increases myometrial contractility and excitability and in most cases induces labor at any stage of pregnancy." (pp. 1144–1145)
- > "A surge in fetal adrenal cortisol production triggers normal parturition in sheep. … In humans, the fetal hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal axis does not play as critical a role in controlling the timing of birth." (p. 1145)
- > "Whereas OT, produced by the maternal posterior pituitary, and PGs, produced locally within the gestational tissues, play an important role in stimulating the contractions that sustain labor, only PGs are believed to have a key role in initiating labor." (pp. 1145–1146)
- > "The number of OTRs increases progressively to 80-fold higher than baseline values by ~36 weeks … and then rises again to 200-fold higher than baseline values during early labor." (p. 1146)
- > "The primary stimulus for the release of maternal OT appears to be distention of the cervix; this effect is known as the Ferguson reflex." (p. 1146)

## Section 7 — Lactation (pp. 1146–1150)

### Subsection headings
- **(Mammary structure and development)** (p. 1146)
- **The epithelial alveolar cells of the mammary gland secrete the complex mixture of sugars, proteins, lipids, and other substances that constitute milk** (pp. 1146–1148)
- **PRL is essential for milk production, and suckling is a powerful stimulus for PRL secretion** (pp. 1148–1150)
- **OT and psychic stimuli initiate milk ejection ("let-down")** (p. 1150)
- **Suckling inhibits the ovarian cycle** (p. 1150)

### Core claims
- **Mammary structure.** The secretory unit is the **alveolus** (secretory epithelial/alveolar cells + contractile **myoepithelial cells** + adipose). Alveoli → **lobules** → **ductules**; 15–20 ductules → a **lactiferous duct** widening at the **ampulla** (reservoir) → exterior.
- **Development.** Puberty: estrogens + progesterone. Pregnancy: rising **PRL** and **hCS** plus very high **estrogen/progesterone** → full breast development. Hormone roles (Table 56-6) are **mammogenic** (proliferation), **lactogenic** (initiate milk), **galactokinetic** (myoepithelial contraction → ejection), **galactopoietic** (maintain established milk).
- **Milk synthesis — five routes** (Fig. 56-11D): (1) **secretory pathway** — lactalbumin and casein via ER→Golgi, where Ca²⁺/phosphate are added and **lactose synthetase** (galactosyltransferase + lactalbumin) makes **lactose** (~200 mM, the main osmotic driver); exocytosis to lumen. (2) **Transcytosis of IgA** — basolateral receptor-mediated endocytosis of maternal Ig → apical exocytosis (mainly **IgA**), taken up by the infant gut for passive immunity. (3) **Lipid pathway** — short-chain fatty acids synthesized in-cell; long-chain (>16C) from diet/stores; droplets bud off the apical membrane as **membrane-bound milk-fat globules**. (4) **Transcellular salt/water transport** — apical/basolateral transporters move electrolytes; water follows osmotically (chiefly lactose-driven). (5) **Paracellular pathway** — salt, water, and leukocytes pass through tight junctions.
- **Composition (Table 56-7).** Human milk vs. colostrum vs. cow's milk: **cow's milk has >3× the protein** (mostly casein) and higher electrolytes — too concentrated for the neonate's GI tract. **Colostrum** (first days postpartum) is thin, yellowish, immunoglobulin- and cell-rich.
- **Prolactin.** Anterior-pituitary **lactotroph** hormone (GH/hCS family). Uniquely, its dominant hypothalamic control is **tonic inhibition by dopamine (DA = PRL-inhibitory factor, PIF)** — so disinhibition releases PRL. **PRL is the classic lactogenic and primary galactopoietic hormone**; initiation also requires the **abrupt postpartum fall in estrogen/progesterone**. PRL binds a **tyrosine-kinase-associated (JAK/STAT-type) receptor** and transcribes milk-protein genes (lactalbumin, casein). **PRL-releasing factors**: TRH, angiotensin II, substance P, β-endorphin, AVP; **estradiol** sensitizes lactotrophs to TRH and desensitizes them to DA. DA-receptor agonists rapidly shut off PRL and milk.
- **The suckling reflex.** Suckling is the most powerful PRL stimulus: nipple afferents → spinal cord → hypothalamus → **inhibit arcuate dopaminergic neurons** → ↓ DA to lactotrophs → **PRL surge** (episodic peaks on an elevated baseline, maintained as long as suckling continues). Non-nursing mothers' PRL falls to nonpregnant levels in 1–2 wk.
- **Milk ejection / let-down (OT).** Suckling also triggers bursts of **OT** from paraventricular/supraoptic neurons → posterior pituitary → systemic blood → contracts **myoepithelial cells** around alveoli/ducts → **let-down after 40–60 s** (galactokinetic). **Psychic stimuli** (sight/sound of infant) can trigger let-down; **fear/anger/stress suppress** it. AVP is 1%–20% as potent as OT.
- **Lactational amenorrhea.** Suckling also **inhibits GnRH** (arcuate/preoptic) → ↓ FSH/LH → suppressed ovarian cycle. **Breast-feeding delays ovulation and menses**; duration depends on suckling intensity/frequency (anovulation averages 18–24 months in Bangladeshi breast-feeders; non-nursing mothers resume ~8–10 wk postpartum).

### Citation-anchor quotes
- > "Milk is an emulsion of fats in an aqueous solution containing sugar (lactose), proteins (lactalbumin and casein), and several cations … and anions." (p. 1146)
- > "Cow's milk has more than three times more protein than human milk, almost exclusively a result of its much higher casein concentration." (p. 1146)
- > "Dopamine (DA) inhibits the release of PRL from lactotrophs. Thus, the removal of inhibition promotes PRL release." (p. 1148)
- > "Suckling is the most powerful physiological stimulus for PRL release." (p. 1149)
- > "OT … enhances milk ejection by stimulating the contraction of the network of myoepithelial cells … The result is to promote the release of pre-existing milk after 40 to 60 seconds, a process known as the let-down reflex." (p. 1150)
- > "Breast-feeding delays ovulation and normal menstrual cycles." (p. 1150)

Suckling at the nipple sends sensory signals up the **spinal cord** to the hypothalamus, producing **four** effects:

4. In the **preoptic area/arcuate nucleus**, it **inhibits GnRH** → ↓ FSH/LH from gonadotrophs → **inhibits the ovarian cycle** (lactational amenorrhea).

## Glossary

- **Capacitation** — final sperm maturation in the female tract (or in vitro) enabling zona penetration.
- **Acrosomal reaction** — Ca²⁺-triggered exocytosis of acrosomal hydrolases after ZP3 binding.
- **Zona pellucida (ZP1/ZP2/ZP3)** — glycoprotein coat; ZP3 is the sperm receptor.
- **Cortical reaction** — oocyte cortical-granule exocytosis hardening the zona; the block to polyspermy.
- **Syngamy** — fusion of male and female pronuclei → zygote (end of fertilization).
- **Morula / blastocyst** — solid ≥12-cell mass / hollow ball (trophoblast + inner cell mass).
- **Decidua / decidualization** — pregnancy endometrium (basalis, capsularis, parietalis).
- **Cytotrophoblast / syncytiotrophoblast** — inner mononuclear vs. outer multinucleate trophoblast; the maternal-fetal interface.
- **hCG** — LH-like placental glycoprotein; rescues the corpus luteum; basis of pregnancy tests.
- **Luteal-placental shift** — ~8 wk crossover of progesterone source from corpus luteum to placenta.
- **Maternal-placental-fetal unit** — division of steroidogenic labor (maternal LDL-cholesterol; fetal 17α-hydroxylase/17,20-desmolase/16α-hydroxylase; placental 3β-HSD/aromatase/sulfatase).
- **DHEA-S / 16α-OH-DHEA-S** — sulfated fetal-adrenal androgens; precursors for placental estradiol and estriol.
- **Estriol (E₃)** — major pregnancy estrogen; readout of the fetoplacental unit.
- **hCS / hPL** — human chorionic somatomammotropin / placental lactogen; fetoplacental fuel economy + mammary development.
- **Intervillous space** — pooled maternal blood; the placenta's functional capillary.
- **Functional progesterone withdrawal** — uterine desensitization to progesterone initiating human labor (no systemic fall).
- **Contraction-associated proteins (CAPs)** — connexin-43, OTR, PGF receptor, COX-2.
- **Ferguson reflex** — cervical distention → OT release; a labor positive-feedback loop.
- **PGF₂α / PGE₂** — decidual prostaglandins; initiate labor, potentiate OT, efface cervix.
- **Oxytocin (OT)** — Gq/PLC contraction signal; let-down; AVP-related nonapeptide.
- **Relaxin** — insulin-related; uterine quiescence and cervical softening.
- **Prolactin (PRL)** — lactogenic/galactopoietic; under tonic dopamine inhibition.
- **Let-down reflex** — OT-driven myoepithelial contraction ejecting milk (~40–60 s).
- **Lactational amenorrhea** — suckling-induced GnRH suppression → anovulation.
- **Colostrum** — early, thin, IgA/cell-rich pre-milk secretion.
