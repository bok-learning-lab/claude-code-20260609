# Chapter 4 — Regulation of Gene Expression

> Section II · Physiology of Cells and Molecules · pp. 73–101 · Author: Peter Igarashi

## Chapter map (top-level)

1. **From genes to proteins** (pp. 73–76) — central dogma, the eight steps of gene expression, what each step gives the cell to regulate.
2. **Structure of the eukaryotic gene** (pp. 76–78) — promoter, regulatory elements, introns/exons, 3′ UTR, polyadenylation signal.
3. **Mechanisms of transcriptional regulation** (pp. 78–93) — RNA pol II machinery, general transcription factors, specific transcription factors and their structural classes, coactivators/corepressors, chromatin modifications (epigenetics), DNA methylation.

## Section 1 — From genes to proteins (pp. 73–76)

### Subsection headings
- **The pathway from genes to proteins involves three steps: transcription, processing, and translation** (p. 73)
- **The gene consists of a transcription unit and flanking regulatory regions** (pp. 74–75)
- **Gene expression is regulated at multiple steps** (pp. 75–76)

### Core claims
- **Central dogma (Fig. 4-1):** DNA → (transcription, in the nucleus) → primary RNA transcript → (processing) → mRNA → (export to cytoplasm) → (translation, on ribosomes) → protein.
- **Eight regulatable steps** in eukaryotic gene expression, each with a corresponding regulation method (Fig. 4-4):

| # | Step | Principal regulation methods |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Alteration in chromatin structure | Histone acetylation; DNA methylation |
| 2 | Initiation of transcription | Transcriptional activation / repression |
| 3 | Transcript elongation | Premature termination, pausing release |
| 4 | Termination of transcription | Alternative polyadenylation |
| 5 | RNA processing (capping, splicing, cleavage, polyadenylation) | Alternative splicing |
| 6 | Nucleocytoplasmic transport | Blockade of nuclear export |
| 7 | Translation | Control of translation initiation (eIFs); IRES; uORFs |
| 8 | mRNA degradation | mRNA stability elements (AREs); miRNAs |

- **Transcription unit** extends from the **transcription start site (TSS)** to a **transcription termination site** and produces the primary transcript. **5′ flanking region** = upstream of TSS (contains promoter and most regulatory elements); **3′ flanking region** = downstream (often contains polyadenylation signal `AAUAAA` and additional regulatory elements).
- **Primary transcript** is co- and post-transcriptionally processed: 5′ 7-methylguanosine cap, splicing of introns, 3′ cleavage and poly-A addition.
- **Cap** is required at position +7 with a `5′–5′ triphosphate` linkage; it is required for both export and translation initiation.

### Citation-anchor quotes
- > "the central dogma of molecular biology applies to most protein-coding genes, exceptions exist. For example, RNA viruses (such as the human immunodeficiency virus [HIV]) that cause acquired immunodeficiency syndrome) contain their genetic information in the sequence of an RNA genome." (p. 74)
- > "Each of these eight steps is potentially a target for regulation." (p. 75)
- > "The gene consists of a transcription unit and flanking regulatory regions." (p. 74)
- > "The RNA that is initially transcribed from a gene is called the primary transcript … Before it can be translated into protein, the primary transcript must be processed into a mature mRNA in the nucleus." (p. 74)
- > "The 5′ flanking region, which is immediately upstream of the transcription initiation site, is called the 5′ flanking region." (p. 74)
- > "An important related alteration in chromatin structure is the state of methylation of the DNA." (p. 75)

## Section 2 — Structure of the eukaryotic gene (pp. 76–78)

### Subsection headings
- **The promoter contains a core element and proximal regulatory elements that bind general and gene-specific transcription factors** (p. 76)
- **Enhancers and silencers act over long distances to activate or repress transcription** (pp. 77–78)

### Core claims
- **Core promoter** (Fig. 4-6): the minimal element required for accurate transcription initiation. Includes the **TATA box** (consensus `TATAAA`, ~25 bp upstream of TSS) and the **initiator (Inr)** sequence around TSS.
- **Proximal promoter elements** (within ~200 bp upstream): CAAT box, GC box (binds Sp1).
- **Enhancers**: cis-acting DNA sequences that can activate transcription from a distance (kb to Mb) and from either orientation. Work by DNA looping to bring transcription factors into the promoter vicinity.
- **Silencers**: enhancer analogs that repress.
- **Insulators / boundary elements**: separate independent transcription domains; prevent enhancer "leakage" across topological domains.
- **Locus control regions (LCRs)**: globin gene cluster prototype; integrate multiple enhancers and developmental switching.

### Citation-anchor quotes
- > "The promoter is the region of DNA that determines the site of transcription initiation. TFIID itself … binds to the TATA box." (p. 76)
- > "Enhancers are DNA sequences that increase the rate of transcription, often from a distance, and may be located either upstream or downstream of the gene that they activate." (p. 77)

## Section 3 — Transcriptional regulation (pp. 78–93)

### Subsection headings (verbatim major beats)
- **The general transcription factors and RNA polymerase II assemble into a preinitiation complex on the core promoter** (pp. 78–80)
- **Specific transcription factors bind sequence-specific DNA elements and modulate the preinitiation complex** (pp. 80–82)
- **Specific transcription factors share a small number of structural motifs** (pp. 82–86) — zinc finger, basic leucine zipper (bZIP), basic helix-loop-helix (bHLH), helix-turn-helix (HTH/homeodomain), winged-helix, Rel/NF-κB.
- **Coactivators and corepressors are transcription factors that do not bind DNA** (pp. 86–87)
- **Chromatin structure regulates transcription: histone modifications and chromatin remodeling complexes** (pp. 87–90)
- **DNA methylation silences gene expression at CpG islands** (pp. 90–93)

### Core claims — general transcription machinery
- **RNA polymerase II** transcribes all protein-coding mRNAs.
- **General transcription factors (GTFs)**: TFIIA, TFIIB, TFIID (contains TBP, TATA-binding protein), TFIIE, TFIIF, TFIIH (contains the CDK7 kinase that phosphorylates the Pol II CTD on Ser5 to initiate transcription; also has DNA helicase activity for promoter melting).
- **Pre-initiation complex (PIC)** assembly: TBP/TFIID binds TATA → TFIIB → TFIIF + Pol II → TFIIE → TFIIH → ATP-dependent promoter melting → CTD Ser5 phosphorylation → escape from promoter → productive elongation (CTD Ser2 phosphorylation marks elongating Pol II).
- **Mediator complex**: large multisubunit bridge that couples enhancer-bound specific TFs to the PIC.

### Core claims — specific transcription factor architectures (Fig. 4-8)
Three highly conserved DNA-binding modules dominate the eukaryotic catalogue (the chapter teaches each as a distinct geometric trick to read DNA bases through major-groove insertion):

- **Zinc finger.** ~30-aa motif folded around a Zn²⁺ ion (Cys₂His₂ classic; Cys₂Cys₂ in nuclear receptors). Each finger inserts an α helix into the major groove and reads ~3 base pairs. Multiple tandem fingers tile a longer DNA target. Examples: Sp1, TFIIIA, GR/MR/ER/AR ligand-binding nuclear receptors.
- **Basic leucine zipper (bZIP).** Two α helices dimerize via a leucine-rich "zipper" coiled-coil; basic regions at the N-terminal end of each helix splay into the major groove on opposite sides. Bind palindromic / pseudo-palindromic sites. Examples: CREB, AP-1 (Fos/Jun), C/EBP, ATF.
- **Basic helix-loop-helix (bHLH).** Two helices joined by a loop; HLH region dimerizes, basic region binds DNA. E-box recognition (`CANNTG`). Examples: MyoD, Myc-Max, achaete-scute.
- **Helix-turn-helix (HTH) / homeodomain.** Three-helix bundle, with helix 3 (the "recognition helix") inserting into the major groove. Homeobox-encoded TFs (Pax, Hox, Pou, Oct, Lhx) regulate development.
- **Winged helix (forkhead/FOXO).** HTH variant with β-strand "wings" flanking the helix bundle. FOXO TFs are downstream of Akt.
- **Rel/NF-κB**. β-barrel DNA-binding fold; activated by cytoplasmic-to-nuclear translocation after IκB phosphorylation/degradation.

### Core claims — coactivators / corepressors / chromatin
- **Coactivators** (no DNA binding of their own) bridge specific TFs to Pol II. Examples: CBP/p300, Mediator. CBP/p300 also has **histone acetyltransferase (HAT)** activity → opens chromatin.
- **Corepressors**: N-CoR, SMRT recruit **histone deacetylases (HDACs)** → close chromatin.
- **Chromatin remodeling complexes**: SWI/SNF, ISWI, CHD families use ATP to slide or evict nucleosomes — making cis-elements accessible to TFs.
- **Histone code (Table 4-1 territory)**: H3K4me3 → active promoters; H3K27me3 → polycomb-mediated silencing; H3K9me3 → heterochromatin; H3K36me3 → gene bodies of active genes; H3K27ac → active enhancers; H4K16ac → euchromatin.
- **DNA methylation**: 5-methylcytosine at CpG dinucleotides. Maintained by DNMT1; established de novo by DNMT3A/B. Heavily methylated CpG islands at promoters → transcriptional silencing (via MBD-protein recruitment of HDACs and chromatin remodelers). Genomic imprinting and X-inactivation use this.
- **Post-translational modifications of TFs** (Table 4-2): phosphorylation, acetylation, methylation, sumoylation, ubiquitination, glycosylation — each can enable, disable, relocalize, or destabilize the TF.

### Citation-anchor quotes
- > "Specific transcription factors share a small number of structural motifs." (p. 82)
- > "Zinc fingers are a class of DNA-binding motifs containing one or more zinc ions … Each zinc finger contacts 3 base pairs of DNA." (p. 82)
- > "The crystal structure reveals that these transcription factors resemble scissors in which the blades represent the leucine zipper domains and the handles represent the DNA-binding domains (see Fig. 4-8B)." (p. 83)
- > "Homeodomain proteins are a family of eukaryotic transcription factors that bind to a DNA sequence called the homeobox." (p. 83)
- > "Coactivators and corepressors are transcription factors that do not bind to DNA." (p. 83)
- > "Histone acetylation and chromatin remodeling are important mechanisms for regulating gene transcription." (p. 87)
- > "DNA methylation usually silences gene expression." (p. 90)

## Section 4 — Regulation of inducible gene expression (pp. 93–100)

### Subsection headings
- **Signal-responsive transcription factors couple receptors at the cell surface to gene-expression programs in the nucleus** (p. 93)
- **CREB integrates cAMP and Ca²⁺ signals through Ser133 phosphorylation** (pp. 94–95)
- **NF-κB couples inflammatory and stress signals to inducible transcription via IκB degradation** (pp. 95–96)
- **JAK-STATs are dimeric transcription factors phosphorylated directly by receptor-associated tyrosine kinases** (pp. 96–97)
- **Nuclear receptors as ligand-activated transcription factors** (pp. 97–98)
- **HIF couples cellular oxygen sensing to transcription** (pp. 98–99) — clinical anchor: erythropoietin in anemia of chronic kidney disease; tumor HIF-driven VEGF expression.
- **SREBP couples sterol sensing in the ER to transcription** (pp. 99–100)
- **NFAT, p53, c-Myc, MEF2** (briefer treatments).

### Core claims — CREB
- Phosphorylated on **Ser133** by PKA (cAMP), CaMKIV (Ca²⁺), MAPKAP-K2 (stress), Akt. Phospho-CREB recruits **CBP** coactivator.
- Binds **CRE** elements (`TGACGTCA`) in target promoters (somatostatin, c-fos, BDNF, tyrosine hydroxylase, PEPCK, many others).
- A canonical convergence point for hormonal (Gs/cAMP), neuronal (depolarization → Ca²⁺), and growth-factor (RTK) signals.
- Clinical / cognitive anchor: CREB-mediated transcription is required for **long-term memory** in many species (Box 4-1 territory).

### Core claims — NF-κB
- Resting state: NF-κB (p50/p65 heterodimer) is held in the cytoplasm by **IκB** masking the nuclear localization signal.
- Inflammatory stimulus (TNF-α, IL-1, LPS via TLRs) → IKK kinase phosphorylates IκB → IκB ubiquitinated → proteasomal degradation → NF-κB exposes NLS → translocates → activates target genes (cytokines, adhesion molecules, anti-apoptotic Bcl-xL, COX-2, iNOS).
- Negative feedback: NF-κB drives expression of new IκB → re-sequestration.

### Core claims — STATs
Reviewed under JAK-STAT in Ch 3; here the nuclear arm is detailed. STATs are activated by receptor-associated JAK phosphorylation, dimerize through SH2-pTyr interactions, translocate to nucleus, and bind **GAS** (gamma-activated sequence) or **ISRE** elements. Negative regulators: **SOCS** proteins (induced by STATs themselves; feedback shut-off), **PIAS** proteins, tyrosine phosphatases (SHP-1, SHP-2).

### Core claims — HIF (Fig. 4-16)
- HIF-1 = αβ heterodimer (bHLH-PAS family). HIF-1β (ARNT) is constitutive. **HIF-1α** is regulated by O₂.
- **Normoxia**: HIF-1α is hydroxylated at two prolines by **PHD (prolyl hydroxylase)** in an O₂-dependent reaction; hydroxylated HIF-1α is recognized by **VHL** (von Hippel–Lindau) E3 ubiquitin ligase → ubiquitinated → degraded by proteasome. A second hydroxylation at Asn by **FIH (factor inhibiting HIF)** blocks CBP/p300 recruitment.
- **Hypoxia**: PHD and FIH cannot hydroxylate → HIF-1α accumulates → dimerizes with HIF-1β → binds **hypoxia response elements (HRE)** in target genes → drives EPO, VEGF, glycolytic enzymes, glucose transporters, NOS.
- Clinical anchors: anemia of CKD (low EPO; HIF-PHD inhibitor class drugs); tumor angiogenesis (VEGF); von Hippel–Lindau syndrome (constitutive HIF-1α, hemangioblastomas, renal cell carcinoma).

### Core claims — SREBP
- ER membrane-anchored transcription factor escorted by **SCAP**. When ER membrane cholesterol falls, SCAP escapes Insig retention and chaperones SREBP to the Golgi, where **S1P** and **S2P** proteases sequentially cleave SREBP to release its soluble bHLH-Zip transcription-factor domain. Nuclear SREBP activates LDLR, HMG-CoA reductase, fatty acid synthase. Statins exploit this homeostatic loop.

### Citation-anchor quotes
- > "CREB is a transcription factor that is activated by phosphorylation at serine 133." (p. 94)
- > "The PKA-mediated phosphorylation of CREB at Ser-133 creates a binding site for the coactivator CBP." (p. 94)
- > "HIF-1α is degraded when oxygen is present and accumulates when oxygen is low." (p. 99)
- > "The combination of HIF-1α and HIF-1β binds to a hypoxia response element [HRE] in the upstream regions of genes encoding glycolytic enzymes, VEGF, and erythropoietin." (p. 99)

### Box references
- **Box 4-1 — CREB and memory** (pp. 94–95).
- **Box 4-2 — Role of a chimeric transcription factor in acute promyelocytic leukemia (APL)** (p. 95): the `t(15;17)` translocation fuses PML to RARα, producing a fusion receptor that aberrantly retains corepressor complexes at RA-responsive genes; pharmacologic doses of all-trans retinoic acid (ATRA) dissociate the corepressors and force differentiation of the leukemic cells. A classic clinical example of a chimeric TF and a rationally targeted therapy.

## Section 5 — Post-transcriptional regulation (pp. 100–101)

### Core claims
- **Alternative splicing** produces tissue-specific protein isoforms from a single primary transcript (e.g., calcitonin/CGRP from the calcitonin gene — calcitonin in thyroid C cells, CGRP in neurons).
- **Nucleocytoplasmic export** is regulated (e.g., HIV Rev-RRE for unspliced viral RNA).
- **mRNA stability** controlled by **AU-rich elements (AREs)** in 3′ UTR; recognized by destabilizing (TTP, AUF1) or stabilizing (HuR) RBPs. **Iron response elements (IREs)** in transferrin receptor mRNA mediate iron-dependent stabilization.
- **Translational control**:
  - Cap-dependent initiation gated by **eIF4E** (sequestered by 4E-BPs; phosphorylation by mTOR releases).
  - **uORFs** delay or repress initiation of the main ORF (GCN4 / ATF4 paradigm; integrated stress response).
  - **IRES** elements allow cap-independent initiation (picornaviruses; some cellular mRNAs during stress).
  - **eIF2α phosphorylation** (by PKR, PERK, GCN2, HRI) inhibits global translation in viral infection, ER stress, amino-acid starvation, heme deprivation.
- **microRNAs (miRNAs)** bind 3′ UTR sites with imperfect complementarity → RISC complex → translational repression and mRNA destabilization. Each miRNA targets dozens to hundreds of mRNAs.

### Citation-anchor quotes
- > "A single gene can give rise to multiple mRNAs by alternative splicing." (p. 100)
- > "Different cell types may splice the primary transcript of a given gene differently, and the proteins encoded by the differentially spliced mRNAs may have very different functions." (p. 100)

## Equations / quantitative anchors

The chapter is qualitative. Quantitative content lives in companion chapters (kinetics of binding in Ch 3; thermodynamics of repression/derepression in advanced texts). The principal quantitative idea worth flagging for the AI application:

- A transcription factor's effective concentration at a promoter is the product of (nuclear concentration) × (TF activity state) × (TF affinity for the cis-element) × (chromatin accessibility). Activation can target any of these. This four-axis decomposition is the chapter's hidden conceptual model.

## Glossary (downstream-chunkable)

- **TSS** — transcription start site.
- **5′ / 3′ flanking region** — sequence upstream / downstream of the transcription unit.
- **Promoter / core promoter** — minimal sequence directing accurate initiation; includes TATA + Inr.
- **TATA box, CAAT box, GC box, Inr** — core/proximal promoter elements.
- **Enhancer / silencer / insulator / LCR** — distal regulatory elements.
- **TBP / TFIID / TFIIA / B / E / F / H** — general transcription factors.
- **CTD (Pol II)** — Ser5-P at initiation, Ser2-P at elongation.
- **Mediator** — bridge between specific TFs and Pol II PIC.
- **bZIP, bHLH, HTH/homeodomain, zinc finger, winged helix, Rel/NF-κB** — DNA-binding motifs.
- **CBP / p300** — coactivators with HAT activity.
- **HDAC, HAT, SWI/SNF, ISWI** — chromatin-modifying enzymes.
- **5-methylcytosine, CpG island, DNMT1/3A/3B, MBD proteins** — DNA methylation machinery.
- **Histone code** — combinatorial PTM marks on histone tails (H3K4me3, H3K27me3, H3K9me3, H3K27ac, etc.).
- **CRE, CREB, CBP/p300** — cAMP/Ca²⁺-responsive transcription module.
- **NF-κB / IκB / IKK** — inflammatory-signal transcription module.
- **STAT / GAS / ISRE / SOCS / PIAS** — cytokine-signal transcription module.
- **HRE / HIF-1α / HIF-1β / PHD / FIH / VHL** — O₂-sensing transcription module.
- **SREBP / SCAP / Insig / S1P / S2P** — sterol-sensing transcription module.
- **NFAT / calcineurin** — Ca²⁺-sustained transcription module; cyclosporine target.
- **p53 / MDM2** — DNA-damage transcription module.
- **Alternative splicing** — generation of protein isoforms.
- **ARE / IRE** — mRNA-stability cis-elements.
- **eIF4E / 4E-BP / mTOR / uORF / IRES / eIF2α-P** — translational-control nodes.
- **miRNA / RISC** — small-RNA gene silencing.
