## Section 1 — Electrical and chemical synapses (pp. 204–207)

### Subsection headings
- **Electrical synapses transmit signals through gap junctions** (pp. 204–205)
- **Chemical synapses use neurotransmitters to provide electrical continuity between adjacent cells** (pp. 205–207)
- **The two synapse types have distinct properties and physiological roles** (p. 207)

### Core claims
- **Electrical synapse**: direct cytoplasmic continuity via **gap-junction channels** (connexons of six connexin subunits each, paired across two cells). Currents and small (<1 kDa) molecules pass; the synapse is **bidirectional**, **fast** (essentially instantaneous), and **non-rectifying** in most cases.
- **Chemical synapse**: pre- and postsynaptic cells are separated by a **synaptic cleft** (~20–40 nm). Signal transmission is **unidirectional**, with **synaptic delay** (~0.3–0.5 ms minimum at the NMJ), **amplification** (one AP releases enough neurotransmitter to depolarize the postsynaptic cell), and high regulatability.
- **Table 8-1** in source contrasts the two systems on speed, modulability, fidelity, plasticity, energy cost.

- > "An electrical synapse consists of one or more gap junction channels permeable to small ions and molecules." (Fig. 8-1 caption, p. 204)
- > "Chemical synapses use neurotransmitters to provide electrical continuity between adjacent cells." (p. 205)
- > "By their very nature, chemical synapses are inherently rectifying or polarized." (p. 205)
- > "The process of chemical transmission can be summarized by the following series of steps." (p. 205)

## Section 2 — The NMJ as the prototype chemical synapse (pp. 207–214)

### Subsection headings
- **Anatomy of the NMJ: motor nerve terminal, junctional folds, basal lamina** (p. 207)
- **A single nerve action potential triggers a large endplate potential and a muscle twitch** (pp. 208–210)
- **The endplate current is carried by a nonselective cation channel — the AChR** (pp. 210–211)
- **Miniature endplate potentials reveal quantal release of acetylcholine** (pp. 211–213)
- **Synaptic delay** (p. 213)
- **Statistical properties of release follow Poisson statistics** (pp. 213–214)

### Core claims
- **NMJ anatomy**: a motor axon terminates as multiple boutons opposite the **endplate** region of the muscle fiber. The muscle membrane is thrown into **junctional folds**; nicotinic AChRs are concentrated at the crests of these folds, opposite the **active zones** of the nerve terminal. **AChE** (acetylcholinesterase) is anchored in the basal lamina of the synaptic cleft.
- **Endplate potential (EPP)**: a single motor-nerve AP releases ~100–300 vesicles → cleft ACh rises briefly to mM concentrations → ~3 × 10⁷ AChRs open at the crests of junctional folds → large, brief inward current → **EPP** of >40 mV, well above muscle-fiber threshold → propagated muscle AP and twitch.
- **Endplate current (EPC)** under voltage clamp: faster than the EPP; rise time ~1 ms, decay ~3 ms; **reversal potential ≈ 0 mV** because the AChR is a nonselective cation channel (Na⁺ and K⁺ are the major permeant cations; some Ca²⁺).
- **Miniature endplate potentials (mEPPs)** (Fatt & Katz, 1952): spontaneous random ~0.5 mV depolarizations at the resting NMJ, occurring at irregular intervals. They have **uniform amplitude** (the "quantum"), and the evoked EPP amplitude is an integer multiple of the mEPP — direct evidence that the EPP arises from the synchronous release of **quanta of ACh**, each quantum corresponding to the contents of a single synaptic vesicle.
- **Quantal content (m)** = mean number of quanta released per AP. At the NMJ in vivo, m ≈ 100–300; in low-Ca²⁺ / high-Mg²⁺ saline, m is reduced and individual quanta can be counted. The number of quanta released per AP follows **Poisson statistics**:

  $$P(n) = \frac{m^n e^{-m}}{n!}$$

  where $P(n)$ is the probability of releasing $n$ quanta and $m$ the average release. The fraction of failures ($n=0$) equals $e^{-m}$ — measuring failures gives $m$ even without resolving individual quanta.

### Citation-anchor quotes
- > "Step 1: Neurotransmitter molecules are packaged into synaptic vesicles." (p. 205, summarizing the chemical synapse cycle)
- > "Typically, an axon makes a single point of synaptic contact with a skeletal muscle fiber, midway along the length of the muscle fiber." (p. 209)
- > "miniature endplate potentials … occur randomly at unstimulated nerve processes that are referred to as terminal arborizations." (p. 210)
- > "Each mEPP results from the release of a single packet (quantum) of neurotransmitter, presumably from the fusion of a single synaptic vesicle." (p. 211)
- > "The full-sized EPP is the sum of multiple quanta released synchronously by a presynaptic action potential." (p. 211)

## Section 3 — Synaptic vesicle exocytosis (pp. 214–219)

### Subsection headings
- **Synaptic vesicles are loaded with neurotransmitter by an H⁺/transmitter antiporter driven by a vesicular V-ATPase** (p. 214)
- **Vesicles dock and prime at active zones** (pp. 214–216)
- **An AP-triggered Ca²⁺ influx through presynaptic voltage-gated Ca²⁺ channels triggers vesicle fusion** (pp. 216–217)
- **SNARE proteins drive vesicle fusion; synaptotagmin is the Ca²⁺ sensor** (pp. 217–218)
- **After fusion, vesicles are retrieved by clathrin-mediated endocytosis and refilled** (pp. 218–219)
- **Neurotoxins that block fusion: tetanus and botulinum toxins** (Table 8-2)

### Core claims
- **Vesicle loading**: V-ATPase pumps H⁺ into the vesicle → H⁺ gradient drives **vesicular transmitter transporters (VAChT, VMAT1/2, VGAT, VGLUT1–3)** to load ACh, monoamines, GABA/glycine, and glutamate respectively.
- **Active zone**: dense projection on presynaptic membrane where docked vesicles sit; **voltage-gated Ca²⁺ channels** (P/Q-type at fast central synapses; N-type at autonomic and many peripheral synapses) cluster within tens of nm of the docked vesicles.
- **Fusion machinery**:
  - **v-SNARE**: synaptobrevin (VAMP) on the vesicle.
  - **t-SNAREs**: syntaxin and SNAP-25 on the plasma membrane.
  - The four-helix trans-SNARE complex zippers, pulling the membranes into apposition.
  - **Synaptotagmin** is the Ca²⁺ sensor: Ca²⁺ binding to its C2 domains triggers conformational change that completes fusion within sub-ms of Ca²⁺ rise.
  - **Munc13/Munc18** are priming factors; **complexin** is a clamp that holds the SNARE complex assembled-but-arrested until Ca²⁺ release.
- **Endocytic retrieval**: after fusion the vesicle membrane is retrieved either rapidly by **kiss-and-run** or more slowly by **clathrin-mediated endocytosis**, then refilled and recycled.
- **Vesicle pool dynamics**: readily releasable pool (~1% of vesicles, immediately available) → recycling pool → reserve pool. Use-dependent depression and facilitation arise from pool dynamics and residual Ca²⁺.

### Table 8-2 — Neurotoxins that block fusion
- **Botulinum toxins A–G** (Clostridium botulinum) — Zn²⁺-protease light chains cleave specific SNAREs at peripheral cholinergic terminals:
  - BoNT/A, /C, /E cleave SNAP-25.
  - BoNT/B, /D, /F, /G cleave synaptobrevin (VAMP).
  - BoNT/C also cleaves syntaxin.
  - Clinical: **flaccid paralysis** (NMJ blockade). Therapeutic use in dystonia, spasticity, blepharospasm, cosmetic Botox.
- **Tetanus toxin** (Clostridium tetani) — cleaves synaptobrevin (VAMP). Travels retrograde up motor axons and trans-synaptically into inhibitory interneurons of the spinal cord → blocks GABA/glycine release → **spastic paralysis** ("lockjaw," opisthotonos).

### Citation-anchor quotes
- > "The protein machinery that mediates vesicle fusion involves a complex of proteins, including the SNAREs (soluble N-ethylmaleimide-sensitive factor attachment protein receptors)." (p. 217)
- > "Synaptotagmin is the calcium sensor that triggers vesicle fusion." (p. 218)
- > "Botulinum toxins cleave SNARE proteins and cause flaccid paralysis." (p. 219)
- > "Tetanus toxin causes spastic paralysis because it cleaves synaptobrevin in inhibitory interneurons of the spinal cord." (p. 219)

## Section 4 — The nicotinic acetylcholine receptor (pp. 219–222)

### Subsection headings
- **AChs activate nicotinic AChRs to produce an excitatory endplate current** (p. 219)
- **The muscle nAChR is a pentamer of 2α + β + γ (fetal) or ε (adult) + δ subunits** (pp. 219–220)
- **Single-channel openings of the AChR are stochastic** (pp. 220–221)
- **The fetal AChR has lower conductance and longer mean open time than the adult AChR** (p. 221)
- **AChR pharmacology: agonists, antagonists, depolarizing and non-depolarizing blockers** (pp. 221–222)

### Core claims
- **Muscle nAChR**: pentameric Cys-loop-family ligand-gated cation channel; two α subunits each bind one ACh molecule; binding of both is required (cooperative) to open the channel.
- **Fetal vs. adult composition** (Fig. 8-8): fetal nAChR has a γ subunit; adult has ε in its place. The γ → ε switch occurs around birth and changes single-channel conductance (γ-AChR ≈ 32 pS, ε-AChR ≈ 50 pS) and mean open time (γ longer, ε shorter — better suited to fast adult NMJ).
- **Channel properties**: nonselective for monovalent cations; reversal potential ≈ 0 mV; single-channel current ~2–4 pA at typical voltages; rectangular openings with mean open time of ~1–10 ms.
- **Pharmacology**:
  - **Agonists**: ACh, nicotine, carbachol, succinylcholine (depolarizing NMJ blocker — agonist that doesn't desensitize and causes sustained depolarization → inactivation of voltage-gated Na⁺ channels → flaccid paralysis after initial fasciculations).
  - **Competitive antagonists**: **d-tubocurarine** (curare; the original NMJ blocker), pancuronium, rocuronium, vecuronium, cisatracurium — non-depolarizing NMJ blockers. Reversed by **neostigmine / edrophonium** (AChE inhibitors that raise cleft ACh).
  - **α-bungarotoxin** (krait venom): irreversible AChR antagonist; used as a radioligand to label muscle AChRs.
  - **Local anesthetics** (e.g., procaine): also block the AChR pore at high concentrations (Fig. 8-10).

### Citation-anchor quotes
- > "ACh activates nicotinic AChRs to produce an excitatory endplate current." (p. 219)
- > "The muscle nicotinic AChR is a pentameric protein composed of four types of subunit." (p. 219)
- > "The fetal AChR (which contains γ) has a smaller single-channel conductance and a longer mean open time than the adult AChR (which contains ε)." (p. 221)
- > "Succinylcholine causes flaccid paralysis by acting as an agonist of the AChR; sustained depolarization inactivates the muscle voltage-gated Na⁺ channels." (p. 222)

## Section 5 — Neurotransmitters: synthesis, release, inactivation (pp. 222–225)

### Subsection headings
- **Classical low-molecular-weight neurotransmitters: ACh, glutamate, GABA, glycine, monoamines (NE, DA, 5-HT, histamine)** (pp. 222–223)
- **Neuropeptides: enkephalins, endorphins, substance P, VIP, NPY, CCK, somatostatin** (pp. 223–224)
- **Termination of action: degradation, reuptake, diffusion** (pp. 224–225)
- **Co-release of classical transmitters and neuropeptides** (p. 225)

### Core claims
- **Synthesis**:
  - **ACh**: choline + acetyl-CoA → ACh, by **choline acetyltransferase (ChAT)**; choline is taken up by a high-affinity Na⁺-coupled choline transporter (ChT/SLC5A7) from the cleft.
  - **Glutamate, GABA, glycine, monoamines**: derived from amino-acid precursors in the cytoplasm, then loaded into vesicles by transmitter-specific vesicular transporters.
  - **Neuropeptides**: synthesized as larger precursors in the cell body, processed in dense-core vesicles, transported anterogradely to the terminal.
- **Termination of action**:
  - **Hydrolysis** in the cleft: ACh by **AChE** (extremely fast — turnover ~25,000 s⁻¹).
  - **Reuptake** into the presynaptic terminal or surrounding glia by Na⁺-coupled SLC transporters: **SERT** (5-HT), **DAT** (dopamine), **NET** (norepinephrine), **EAAT1–5** (glutamate), **GAT** (GABA), **GlyT** (glycine).
  - **Diffusion** out of the cleft for unhydrolyzed transmitters.
- **Pharmacological targets at this layer:**
  - **AChE inhibitors**: physostigmine, neostigmine, donepezil (Alzheimer); organophosphates (irreversible — sarin, parathion, malathion); reactivators (pralidoxime / 2-PAM).
  - **Monoamine reuptake inhibitors**: SSRIs (fluoxetine etc.), SNRIs, TCAs, cocaine (DAT/NET inhibitor), methylphenidate.
  - **Vesicular monoamine transporter inhibitors**: reserpine (irreversibly depletes monoamine stores).
  - **MAO and COMT inhibitors**: degrade monoamines after reuptake (Parkinson and depression therapy).

### Citation-anchor quotes
- > "Acetylcholinesterase rapidly hydrolyzes ACh in the synaptic cleft." (p. 224)
- > "Reuptake of neurotransmitter by sodium-coupled symporters terminates the action of monoamines, GABA, glycine, and glutamate." (p. 224)
- > "Many synapses release both a classical low-molecular-weight neurotransmitter and one or more neuropeptide co-transmitters." (p. 225)

## Section 6 — NMJ pharmacology and pathology (pp. 225–227)

### Core claims — clinical anchors
- **Myasthenia gravis**: autoantibodies to muscle nAChR (most common) or to MuSK (a kinase required for AChR clustering at the endplate). Reduced AChR density → smaller EPP, fatigable weakness. Treatment: AChE inhibitors (pyridostigmine), thymectomy, immunosuppression.
- **Lambert–Eaton myasthenic syndrome**: autoantibodies to presynaptic P/Q-type Cav2.1; reduced ACh release per AP → weakness that *improves* with sustained activity (because residual Ca²⁺ accumulates). Often paraneoplastic (small-cell lung carcinoma).
- **Organophosphate poisoning**: irreversible AChE inhibition → cleft ACh accumulates → muscle depolarization-induced inactivation of voltage-gated Na⁺ channels → flaccid paralysis. SLUDGE syndrome at muscarinic synapses (salivation, lacrimation, urination, defecation, GI, emesis). Treat with atropine (muscarinic) + 2-PAM (reactivates AChE).
- **Curare and modern non-depolarizing NMJ blockers**: competitive AChR antagonists; produce relaxation for surgery. Reversed by neostigmine/sugammadex (encapsulates rocuronium).
- **Succinylcholine** (depolarizing block): used for rapid-sequence intubation; risk of malignant hyperthermia in *RYR1* / *CACNA1S* mutant individuals.
- **Congenital myasthenic syndromes**: mutations in AChR subunits (slow-channel and fast-channel syndromes), AChE deficiency, MuSK/agrin/rapsyn — see Ch 9 for muscle context.

### Citation-anchor quotes
- > "Myasthenia gravis is an autoimmune disease in which antibodies are directed against the AChR." (p. 226)
- > "Lambert–Eaton myasthenic syndrome is caused by antibodies directed against presynaptic voltage-gated Ca²⁺ channels." (p. 226)
- > "Organophosphate compounds are irreversible inhibitors of AChE." (p. 226)

## Equations and quantitative anchors

- **Quantal hypothesis** (del Castillo & Katz): EPP amplitude $= m \cdot q$, where $m$ = quantal content (number of vesicles released) and $q$ = mEPP amplitude (single-quantum size).
- **Poisson statistics** at low release probability:

  $$P(n) = \frac{m^n e^{-m}}{n!}, \qquad P(\text{failure}) = e^{-m}$$

  Variance of quantal content equals the mean for a pure Poisson process: $\sigma^2 = m$.

- **EPC reversal potential** (mixed Na⁺/K⁺ channel):

  $$E_{\text{rev}} \approx \frac{P_{Na} E_{Na} + P_K E_K}{P_{Na} + P_K}$$

  ≈ 0 mV for AChR.

- **Synaptic delay** (~0.3–0.5 ms at fast synapses) is dominated by the Ca²⁺-influx-to-fusion step, not by diffusion of ACh across the cleft.

## Glossary

- **Electrical synapse / gap junction / connexon / connexin** — direct cytoplasmic coupling.
- **Chemical synapse / cleft / active zone / postsynaptic density** — separated cells, neurotransmitter-mediated.
- **Endplate / endplate potential (EPP) / endplate current (EPC)** — postsynaptic responses at the NMJ.
- **Miniature EPP (mEPP)** — single-quantum spontaneous event.
- **Quantum / quantal content (m) / quantal size (q)** — the building blocks of evoked response.
- **Synaptic vesicle / readily releasable / recycling / reserve pool** — vesicle populations.
- **V-ATPase / VAChT / VMAT / VGAT / VGLUT** — vesicular loading machinery.
- **Active zone / SNAP-25 / syntaxin / synaptobrevin / SNAREs / synaptotagmin / Munc13 / Munc18 / complexin** — fusion machinery.
- **Cav2.1 (P/Q-type) / Cav2.2 (N-type)** — presynaptic Ca²⁺ channels.
- **Clathrin-mediated endocytosis / kiss-and-run** — vesicle retrieval modes.
- **nAChR / Cys-loop family / α₂βγδ (fetal) / α₂βεδ (adult)** — muscle nicotinic receptor.
- **AChE / ChAT / ChT/SLC5A7** — ACh metabolism.
- **d-Tubocurarine, succinylcholine, pancuronium, rocuronium** — NMJ blockers.
- **Pyridostigmine, neostigmine, edrophonium, donepezil** — AChE inhibitors.
- **α-Bungarotoxin** — AChR irreversible probe.
- **Botulinum toxin / tetanus toxin** — SNARE proteases.
- **Myasthenia gravis / LEMS / congenital myasthenic syndromes** — clinical anchors.
- **Synaptic delay** — Ca²⁺-influx-to-fusion limit.
