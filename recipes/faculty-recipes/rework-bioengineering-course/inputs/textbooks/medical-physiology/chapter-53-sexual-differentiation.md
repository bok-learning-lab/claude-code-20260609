# Chapter 53 — Sexual Differentiation

> Section IX · The Reproductive System · pp. 1072–1091 · Authors: Sam Mesiano, Ervin E. Jones

## Chapter map (top-level)

1. **Genetic aspects of sexual differentiation** (pp. 1072–1076) — meiosis and gametes; genotypic sex set at fertilization (X- vs. Y-bearing sperm); the SRY/TDF gene on the Y; Barr body / lyonization; Turner (45,X) and Klinefelter (47,XXY).
2. **Differentiation of the gonads** (pp. 1076–1078) — the bipotential indifferent gonad (cortex + medulla); PGC migration from yolk sac; medulla → testis (Sertoli, Leydig), cortex → ovary.
3. **Development of the accessory sex organs — internal ducts** (pp. 1078–1081) — Wolffian (mesonephric) → male ducts; Müllerian (paramesonephric) → female ducts; the Jost experiments; testosterone vs. AMH.
4. **Differentiation of the external genitalia** (pp. 1081–1083) — urogenital sinus partition; common anlagen; DHT-driven male development.
5. **Endocrine and paracrine control** (pp. 1084–1087) — the three fetal testicular secretions (testosterone, DHT, AMH); androgen receptor; brain sexual dimorphism; clinical DSDs.
6. **Puberty** (pp. 1087–1091) — gonadarche vs. adrenarche; reactivation of the hypothalamic GnRH pulse generator; falling feedback sensitivity; Tanner stages.

> **The organizing paradigm.** Sexual differentiation proceeds at three nested levels: **chromosomal/genetic sex** (XX vs. XY, fixed at fertilization) → **gonadal sex** (indifferent gonad → testis or ovary, decided by SRY) → **phenotypic sex** (internal ducts, urogenital sinus, external genitalia, brain). The **female pattern is the default program**; the **male pattern is actively imposed** by three testicular hormones (testosterone, DHT, AMH). "Genetic determination of sexual differentiation is not irrevocable" (p. 1076) — disrupting the hormonal signals reroutes phenotype regardless of genotype.

## Section 1 — Genetic aspects of sexual differentiation (pp. 1072–1076)

### Subsection headings
- **Meiosis occurs only in germ cells and gives rise to male and female gametes** (pp. 1073–1074)
- **Fertilization of an oocyte by an X- or Y-bearing sperm establishes the zygote's genotypic sex** (pp. 1074–1075)
- **Genotypic sex determines differentiation of the indifferent gonad into either an ovary or a testis** (pp. 1075–1076)
- **The testis-determining gene is located on the Y chromosome** (p. 1076)
- **Endocrine and paracrine messengers modulate phenotypic differentiation** (p. 1076)

### Core claims
- **Karyotype**: somatic cells are diploid (2N), 22 autosome pairs + one sex-chromosome pair. Normal female = **46,XX**, normal male = **46,XY** (Fig. 53-1). Gametes are haploid (N), carrying either an X or a Y.
- **Meiosis** (germ-cell-only) is a reduction division: meiosis I separates homologous chromosomes (with recombination / crossing-over at chiasmata), meiosis II separates chromatids. One **spermatogonium → 4 spermatids**; one **oogonium → 1 mature oocyte + 2–3 polar bodies** (Fig. 53-2).
- **The male is the heterogametic (XY) sex.** The ovum always supplies an X; the sperm supplies X or Y. Therefore **the sperm determines zygotic sex**: X-bearing → 46,XX (female); Y-bearing → 46,XY (male).
- **Causal chain**: genotypic sex → gonadal sex → phenotypic sex (fully established at puberty).
- **The Y chromosome is the master switch.** Present → male; absent → female. The number of X chromosomes is "of little significance" for the male/female switch.
- **SRY = the testis-determining factor (TDF)**, mapped to the short (p) arm of the Y. It encodes an HMG-box transcription factor; the 80-amino-acid HMG box binds DNA and is highly conserved. SRY acts **before week 9**; if absent or too late, an ovary forms.
- **Two X chromosomes are needed for a normal ovary**, even though one X is silenced. In **45,X (Turner)** the ovaries fail and appear as fibrous **streak gonads** (Box 53-1). The resolution: genes on the "inactivated" X escape silencing and are required for ovarian development.
- **Lyonization / Barr body**: to dose-compensate the double X, each 46,XX somatic cell at the blastocyst stage randomly and permanently inactivates one X (maternal or paternal). The condensed inactive X is the **Barr body**, usable to read genotypic sex from a cell.
- **47,XXY (Klinefelter)** individuals are **male**, not female — confirming that a single Y drives maleness despite two X's. Together, 45,X and 47,XXY show that *absence of a Y produces female phenotype*.
- **Translocation of TDF** (Fig. 53-3B): aberrant X–Y crossing-over in the father can put SRY on an X chromatid (→ a **46,XX male**) or strip it from a Y (→ a **46,XY female**). "A 'normal' testis in the absence of a Y chromosome has never been reported" (Box 53-2).
- **DSD terminology** (Box 53-2): genotype–phenotype discordance is now termed **disorders of sex development (DSD)**, replacing "hermaphrodite / pseudohermaphrodite / intersex."

### Citation-anchor quotes
- > "When a Y chromosome is present, the individual develops as a male; when the Y chromosome is absent, the individual develops as a female. In embryos with abnormal sex chromosome complexes, the number of X chromosomes is apparently of little significance." (p. 1075)
- > "This so-called testis-determining factor (TDF) has been mapped to the short arm of the Y chromosome and indeed turns out to be a single gene called SRY (for sex-determining region Y)." (p. 1075)
- > "Individuals with the karyotype 47,XXY—Klinefelter syndrome—are not phenotypic females (based on the presence of two X chromosomes) but males." (p. 1075)
- > "Once inactivated, that X chromosome remains inactivated for the life of the cell and all of its descendants." (p. 1075)
- > "Genetic determination of sexual differentiation is not irrevocable; numerous internal and external influences during development may modify or completely reverse the phenotype of the individual, whatever the genotypic sex." (p. 1076)

## Section 2 — Differentiation of the gonads (pp. 1076–1078)

### Subsection headings
- **Primordial germ cells migrate from the yolk sac to the primordial gonad** (pp. 1076–1078)
- **The primitive testis develops from the medulla of the primordial gonad** (p. 1078)
- **The primitive ovary develops from the cortex of the primordial gonad** (p. 1078)

### Core claims
- The **indifferent (bipotential) gonad** arises ~week 5 as paired **gonadal ridges** on the intermediate mesoderm beside the developing kidneys. It has an outer **cortex** and inner **medulla** and can become either gonad (Fig. 53-4).
- **Primordial germ cells (PGCs)** do not originate in the gonad. They form in the **yolk-sac endoderm** and migrate along the hindgut mesentery to the gonadal ridge (Fig. 53-4A,B). **Gonadal development fails without germ cells** — any block to PGC migration causes abnormal gonadal differentiation.
- **Medulla → testis, cortex regresses** (46,XY): PGCs move into the medullary **primitive sex cords**, which hollow out into **seminiferous tubules** containing **Sertoli cells**; PGCs become **spermatogonia**. Remnant cords form the **rete testis**, draining via **efferent ductules** (from mesonephric tubules) into the mesonephric duct. Mesenchyme between the cords becomes the **Leydig cells**, which by ~week 10 respond to **hCG / fetal LH** and secrete **testosterone**.
- **Cortex → ovary, medulla regresses** (46,XX): medulla resorbs; cortex thickens with mitotically dividing PGCs → **oogonia** → surrounded by granulosa cells as **primordial follicles** → **primary oocytes** arrested at prophase I. The ovary holds ~600,000 oogonia at week 8, ~6–7 million by week 20; by birth ~1 million primordial follicles remain — the **entire lifetime supply** (no postnatal renewal, unlike continuous male spermatogonial replenishment).

### Citation-anchor quotes
- > "These gonadal ridges are identical in the two sexes and are referred to as the indifferent gonads. They are composed of an outer cortex and an inner medulla and capable of becoming either testis or ovary." (p. 1076)
- > "Gonadal development fails to progress normally in the absence of germ cells. Thus, any event that interferes with germ cell migration may cause abnormal gonadal differentiation." (pp. 1076–1077)
- > "In an embryo with a 46,XY karyotype, the medullary portion of the gonad develops to become a testis and the cortex regresses. Conversely, 46,XX germ cells stimulate development of the cortex of the early gonad to become an ovary and the medulla regresses." (pp. 1077–1078)
- > "Unlike spermatogonia, the entire investment of oogonia … in the developing ovary is irreversibly committed to meiosis by the 20th week of development." (p. 1078)

## Section 3 — Development of the internal genital ducts (pp. 1078–1081)

### Subsection headings
- **Embryos of both sexes have a double set of embryonic genital ducts** (pp. 1078–1079)
- **In males, the wolffian ducts become the epididymis, vas deferens, seminal vesicles, and ejaculatory duct** (pp. 1079–1080)
- **In females, the müllerian ducts become the fallopian tubes, the uterus, and the upper third of the vagina** (p. 1080)
- **In males, development of the wolffian ducts requires testosterone** (pp. 1080–1081) — the Jost experiments.
- **In males, antimüllerian hormone causes regression of the müllerian ducts** (p. 1081)

### Core claims
- Both sexes begin with **two parallel duct systems** (Fig. 53-5A): the **Wolffian (mesonephric) ducts** and the **Müllerian (paramesonephric) ducts**, both draining into the **urogenital sinus**. Before ~7 weeks both are present; by ~week 10 only one survives per sex — **Wolffian in males, Müllerian in females**.
- **Male (Wolffian) derivatives**: mesonephric duct → **epididymis → vas deferens → (lateral bud) seminal vesicle → ejaculatory duct**; urethral outgrowths form the **prostate**. Müllerian ducts regress.
- **Female (Müllerian) derivatives**: cranial → **fallopian tubes (+ fimbriae)**; fused midportions → **uterus (fundus + corpus) + cervix**; fused caudal **uterovaginal primordium** → **upper one-third of the vagina**. Wolffian ducts and mesonephros degenerate. (Lower two-thirds of vagina + external female genitalia come from the urogenital sinus.)
- **The Jost experiments (1953, fetal-rabbit castration)** established the controlling logic (Fig. 53-6):
  - **Castrate a male early** → no testis → Wolffian ducts regress, Müllerian ducts persist → **female internal + external genitalia** (the default program).
  - **Castrate a female early** → no effect; Müllerian development proceeds normally → **the ovary is not required for female duct development.**
  - **Unilateral male castration** → ipsilateral female ducts; contralateral (testis-bearing) side male; external virilization proceeds — duct effects are **local/paracrine**.
  - **Bilateral castrate + exogenous testosterone** → Wolffian ducts preserved, **but no Müllerian regression** → testosterone alone cannot regress the Müllerian ducts.
  - **Normal female + testosterone** → both duct sets persist (Wolffian preserved, Müllerian intact) — confirms a *second*, non-steroid testicular factor.
- **The second factor is AMH.** **Sertoli cells** secrete **antimüllerian hormone (AMH)** / **müllerian-inhibiting substance (MIS)** — a TGF-β–superfamily glycoprotein (gene on chromosome 19; 140-kDa disulfide-linked dimer; bioactivity in its C-terminal domain). AMH drives **apoptotic, craniocaudal regression** of the Müllerian ducts (complete within ~1 week of exposure, ~week 8). SRY likely activates AMH transcription.
- **Two-hormone division of labor in the male fetus**: **testosterone (Leydig)** *builds* the Wolffian ducts; **AMH (Sertoli)** *destroys* the Müllerian ducts. In the female, **Müllerian ducts develop spontaneously without AMH**, and **Wolffian ducts involute spontaneously without testosterone**.
- Defects in AMH or its receptor → **persistent müllerian duct syndrome**: a 46,XY male with undescended testes plus a rudimentary uterus / Müllerian remnants.

### Citation-anchor quotes
- > "Later in gestation (by the 10th week) only one of the duct systems survives in each sex: wolffian ducts in males and müllerian ducts in females." (p. 1079)
- > "Although testosterone can support wolffian development, it is unable to cause müllerian regression … a testicular product other than testosterone is necessary for regression of the müllerian ducts." (p. 1081)
- > "The Sertoli cells of the testis produce a nonsteroid macromolecule—antimüllerian hormone (AMH) or müllerian-inhibiting substance (MIS)—that causes müllerian degeneration in the male fetus." (p. 1081)
- > "In females, the müllerian ducts differentiate spontaneously in the absence of AMH, and the wolffian ducts involute spontaneously in the absence of testosterone." (p. 1081)

## Section 4 — Differentiation of the external genitalia (pp. 1081–1083)

### Subsection headings
- **The urogenital sinus develops into the urinary bladder, the urethra, and, in females, the vestibule of the vagina** (pp. 1081–1083)
- **The external genitalia of both sexes develop from common anlagen** (pp. 1083–1084)

### Core claims
- The **cloaca** (common urogenital + GI termination) is partitioned by the **urorectal septum** into a dorsal **rectum** and a ventral **urogenital sinus** (Fig. 53-7A,B). The sinus has three regions — **vesicle, pelvic, phallic**:
  - **Male**: vesicle → bladder; pelvic → prostatic urethra; phallic → initial penile urethra.
  - **Female**: vesicle → bladder; pelvic → entire female urethra; phallic → **vestibule of the vagina**.
- The **vagina** forms from the **vaginal plate** (sinovaginal bulbs at the uterovaginal-primordium/sinus junction); central resorption creates the lumen; the **hymen** separates vaginal lumen from sinus. In males the vaginal anlage regresses, leaving the **prostatic utricle** remnant.
- **External genitalia share common anlagen** (Fig. 53-8) — the **genital tubercle**, paired **urogenital folds**, and paired **labioscrotal (genital) swellings** — anatomically indistinguishable until ~end of the first trimester.

| Common anlage | Male derivative (DHT-driven) | Female derivative (default) |
|---|---|---|
| Genital tubercle | Glans penis (+ corpus spongiosum, corpora cavernosa) | Clitoris |
| Urogenital folds | Fuse → ventral shaft of penis / spongy (penile) urethra (penile raphé) | Remain separate → labia minora |
| Labioscrotal swellings | Fuse → scrotum (scrotal raphé) | Remain unfused → labia majora; fuse anteriorly → mons pubis, posteriorly → posterior labial commissure |
| Urogenital sinus (phallic) | Penile urethra | Vestibule of vagina |

- **DHT is the master signal for external virilization.** Male fusion (urethral groove closure, scrotal fusion, penile growth) is complete by ~weeks 13–14. **Without androgen**, the anlagen stay unfused and follow the **female pattern** — regardless of genotype.

### Citation-anchor quotes
- > "Although anatomically separate precursors give rise to the internal sex organs, common anlagen give rise to the external genitalia of the two sexes." (p. 1083)
- > "The genital tubercle of the primitive embryo develops into the glans penis in males and the clitoris in females." (p. 1083)
- > "In males, the genital or labioscrotal swellings fuse to give rise to the scrotum. In females, however, the labioscrotal swellings fuse anteriorly to give rise to the mons pubis and posteriorly to form the posterior labial commissure." (p. 1084)

## Section 5 — Endocrine and paracrine control of sexual differentiation (pp. 1084–1087)

### Subsection headings
- **The SRY gene triggers development of the testis, which makes the androgens and AMH necessary for male sexual differentiation** (pp. 1084–1086)
- **Androgens direct the male pattern of sexual differentiation of the internal ducts, the urogenital sinus, and the external genitalia** (pp. 1086–1087)
- **Androgens and estrogens influence sexual differentiation of the brain** (pp. 1086–1087)

### Core claims — the three fetal testicular secretions
The female pattern is the default; the male pattern is imposed by **three hormones** (Fig. 53-9):

| Hormone | Source cell | Target / action | Tissue requirement |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Testosterone** | Leydig cells | Builds **Wolffian ducts** → epididymis, vas deferens, seminal vesicles, ejaculatory duct; masculinizes brain (via aromatization) | Acts **as testosterone itself** — Wolffian cells lack 5α-reductase |
| **Dihydrotestosterone (DHT)** | Peripheral conversion of testosterone by **5α-reductase** | Virilizes **urogenital sinus + external genitalia** → prostate, penile urethra, penis, scrotum | Requires **5α-reductase** in target tissue |
| **AMH** | Sertoli cells | Regresses **Müllerian ducts** | Acts via AMH receptor on Müllerian mesenchyme |

- **Androgen receptor (AR)**: testosterone and DHT diffuse into target cells and bind the **AR**, a ligand-activated transcription factor. **DHT binds AR ~100-fold more tightly** than testosterone, and the DHT–AR complex binds DNA more tightly — so even a **low circulating testosterone** profoundly affects 5α-reductase–expressing tissues.
- **Two androgen-dependent steps differ in their requirement**:
  - **Wolffian ducts** respond to **testosterone per se** (no DHT needed; they lack 5α-reductase). Sertoli **androgen-binding protein (ABP)** keeps local testosterone high.
  - **Urogenital sinus + external genitalia** require **DHT** (they express 5α-reductase) — both in embryogenesis and at pubertal maturation.
- **Brain sexual dimorphism**: androgens do **not** masculinize the brain directly — **aromatase converts androgens to estrogens** in the brain, and these estrogens masculinize sexually dimorphic hypothalamic nuclei. Functional dimorphism example: **cyclic (female) vs. tonic (male)** gonadotropin release.

### Clinical DSDs (the diagnostic payoff of the cascade)

| Disorder | Karyotype | Defect | Phenotype |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Androgen insensitivity syndrome (AIS)** (Box 53-3) | 46,XY | Absent / abnormal **androgen receptor** | Testes present (AMH still acts → no Müllerian structures), but **female external genitalia**; Wolffian + Müllerian ducts regress ("testicular feminization") |
| **5α-reductase deficiency** ("guevedoces") (Box 53-3) | 46,XY | Cannot convert testosterone → DHT | **Female-appearing external genitalia at birth** with normal Wolffian ducts + no Müllerian structures; **virilization at puberty** when high testosterone overcomes the deficit |
| **Congenital adrenal hyperplasia (CAH)** (Box 53-4) | 46,XX | Steroidogenic enzyme deficiency (**21α-hydroxylase ~95%**, also 11β-OHase, 3β-HSD) shunts precursors into adrenal androgens | **Virilized (ambiguous) external genitalia** in a genotypic female (clitoromegaly, fused labioscrotal folds) — the "adrenogenital syndrome" |
| **Persistent Müllerian duct syndrome** | 46,XY | AMH or AMH-receptor mutation | Male with undescended testes + rudimentary uterus / Müllerian remnants |
| **Turner syndrome** (Box 53-1) | **45,X** | Monosomy X | Female phenotype, **streak gonads**, short stature, primary amenorrhea, sexual infantilism |
| **Klinefelter syndrome** | **47,XXY** | Extra X | **Male** phenotype (a Y is present) |

- **Testicular descent is androgen-dependent** (Box 53-5): the gubernaculum guides the testis through the processus vaginalis / inguinal canal into the scrotum, completed by ~month 7 (~97.5% of full-term infants descended at birth). Failure (cryptorchidism) accompanies 5α-reductase deficiency and complete androgen resistance.

### Citation-anchor quotes
- > "The female pattern of sexual differentiation is the default program and the male pattern of sexual differentiation is directed by endocrine and paracrine factors produced by the testes." (p. 1084)
- > "Further male-pattern sexual differentiation depends on the presence of three hormones: testosterone, dihydrotestosterone (DHT), and AMH. The testis directly produces both testosterone and AMH. Peripheral tissues convert testosterone to DHT." (p. 1084)
- > "DHT binds to AR with an affinity that is ~100-fold greater than the binding of testosterone." (p. 1085)
- > "The wolffian phase of male sexual differentiation is regulated by testosterone itself and does not require conversion of testosterone to DHT. In contrast, virilization of the urogenital sinus, the prostate, the penile urethra, and the external genitalia during embryogenesis requires DHT." (pp. 1085–1086)
- > "Androgens in the brain serve as prohormones for estrogens. Therefore, estrogens are derived from androgens that appear to masculinize sexually dimorphic nuclei directly in the brain." (p. 1086)

## Section 6 — Puberty (pp. 1087–1091)

### Subsection headings
- **Puberty involves steroid hormones produced by the gonads and the adrenals** (pp. 1087–1088)
- **Hypothalamic gonadotropin-releasing hormone secretion controls puberty** (p. 1088)
- **Multiple factors control the timing of puberty** (p. 1088)
- **Androgens and estrogens influence secondary sex characteristics at puberty** (pp. 1088–1091)
- **The appearance of secondary sex characteristics at puberty completes sexual differentiation and development** (p. 1091)

### Core claims
- Puberty = two processes:
  - **Gonadarche** — functional maturation of the gonads (gamete + sex-steroid production), driven by rising pituitary **FSH and LH**. In males → **spermarche**; in females → **folliculogenesis, menarche, thelarche**.
  - **Adrenarche** — rising adrenal androgens (**DHEA, DHEAS, androstenedione**) at ages ~6–8 yr, driving **pubarche** (pubic hair). Adrenarche is **peculiar to humans and great apes**, occurs in both sexes, and is **independent of gonadarche** (its absence does not prevent reproductive competence).
- **The GnRH pulse generator is the master clock.** Hypothalamic **GnRH** drives pituitary FSH/LH. One of the earliest pubertal events is the onset of **pulsatile GnRH release** during REM sleep, later spreading across the day (Fig. 53-11A). Once established, the pulsatile pattern "continues throughout reproductive life."
- **Falling feedback sensitivity** explains pubertal timing (Fig. 53-11B,C): prepubertally the hypothalamic-pituitary axis is **exquisitely sensitive** to negative feedback — even tiny sex-steroid levels suppress gonadotropins. At puberty the **set-point rises**: much higher steroid levels are now needed to suppress release, so GnRH/FSH/LH climb. The trigger for this reset is unknown (likely neuronal maturation).
- **Lifelong gonadotropin trajectory** (Fig. 53-11A): peaks in fetal life and early infancy → falls by ~4 months → low through childhood → rises at puberty (female levels then oscillate monthly) → rises sharply at menopause.
- **Timing determinants**: largely **genetic**, plus nutrition, geography (closer to equator / lower altitude → earlier), light exposure, body fat. Secular **decline in menarcheal age**. Severe obesity and heavy exercise **delay** puberty.
- **Tanner stages** (Table 53-1, Fig. 53-12): 5 stages scored on **pubic hair + genital (male) or breast (female)** development. **Stage 1 = prepubertal; Stage 5 = adult.** First sign of male puberty = **testicular enlargement (>2.5 cm)** (Tanner 2; range 9–14 yr); first sign of female puberty = **thelarche** (~10–11 yr); menarche ~2 yr after thelarche (mean 12.5 yr).
- **Sex-steroid effects at puberty**: testosterone → external-genital + accessory-organ maturation, secondary characteristics (voice, hair), and **anabolic** growth (men ~150% of women's lean/skeletal mass; ~10 cm taller). Estrogen/progesterone → breast development (estrogen = duct, progesterone = alveoli), uterine/endometrial growth, female fat distribution. The growth spurt comes from combined GH + sex steroids.

## Glossary

- **Genotypic / gonadal / phenotypic sex** — the three nested levels of differentiation.
- **SRY / TDF** — sex-determining region Y; testis-determining factor; HMG-box transcription factor on the Yp arm.
- **Indifferent (bipotential) gonad** — cortex + medulla; → ovary (cortex) or testis (medulla).
- **Primordial germ cells (PGCs)** — yolk-sac-derived; migrate to gonadal ridge.
- **Wolffian (mesonephric) duct** — male internal ducts; testosterone-driven.
- **Müllerian (paramesonephric) duct** — female internal ducts; regressed by AMH in males.
- **AMH / MIS** — antimüllerian hormone; Sertoli; TGF-β superfamily; Müllerian regression.
- **Testosterone / DHT / 5α-reductase** — testosterone builds Wolffian ducts; DHT (made by 5α-reductase) virilizes urogenital sinus + external genitalia.
- **Androgen receptor (AR)** — ligand-activated TF; DHT binds ~100× tighter than testosterone.
- **Default-female / active-male paradigm** — female is the baseline; three testicular hormones impose maleness.
- **Lyonization / Barr body** — random permanent X-inactivation; condensed inactive X.
- **Turner (45,X)** — streak gonads, female phenotype. **Klinefelter (47,XXY)** — male phenotype.
- **AIS / 5α-reductase deficiency / CAH** — the canonical DSDs.
- **Gonadarche vs. adrenarche** — gonadal maturation (FSH/LH) vs. adrenal androgen rise (DHEA); pubarche, thelarche, menarche, spermarche.
- **GnRH pulse generator** — hypothalamic clock reactivated at puberty; falling feedback sensitivity.
- **Tanner stages** — 1 (prepubertal) → 5 (adult); pubic hair + genital/breast.
