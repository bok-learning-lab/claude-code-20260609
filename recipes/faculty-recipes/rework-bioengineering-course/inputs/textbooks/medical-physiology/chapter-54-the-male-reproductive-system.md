## Section 1 — Hypothalamic-pituitary-gonadal axis (pp. 1092–1097)

### Subsection headings
- **The hypothalamus secretes GnRH, which acts on gonadotrophs in the anterior pituitary** (pp. 1092–1094)
- **Under the control of GnRH, gonadotrophs in the anterior pituitary secrete LH and FSH** (pp. 1094–1095)
- **LH stimulates the Leydig cells of the testis to produce testosterone** (pp. 1095–1096)
- **FSH stimulates Sertoli cells to synthesize hormones that influence Leydig cells and spermatogenesis** (pp. 1095–1096)
- **The hypothalamic-pituitary-testicular axis is under feedback inhibition by testicular steroids and inhibins** (pp. 1096–1097)

### Core claims
- The male reproductive system = **gonads (testes)** + **sex accessory organs** (epididymides, vas deferens, seminal vesicles, ejaculatory ducts, prostate, bulbourethral/Cowper's glands, urethra, penis). The testes do two jobs: produce haploid **spermatozoa** and synthesize/secrete hormones, chiefly **testosterone**.
- Testis interior = **seminiferous tubules** (Sertoli-cell epithelium + germ cells; site of spermatogenesis) plus **interstitial Leydig cells** between the tubules (site of testosterone synthesis).
- **GnRH** is a decapeptide made by peptidergic neurons in the **arcuate nucleus and preoptic area**; these neurons originate in the **olfactory placode** embryonically and migrate to the hypothalamus (relevant to Kallmann syndrome). Synthesized as a 69-aa prohormone; cleavage yields GnRH (residues 1–10) + the 56-aa **GnRH-associated peptide (GAP)**, role unknown.
- GnRH neurons project to the **median eminence**, releasing GnRH into the **hypothalamic-pituitary portal** vessels, which deliver it directly to anterior-pituitary gonadotrophs.
- **GnRH signaling**: GnRH receptor is a **GPCR coupled to G_q** → phospholipase C → IP₃ (releases Ca²⁺ → exocytosis of LH/FSH) + DAG (activates PKC → ↑transcription of LH/FSH genes).
- **Pulsatility is essential.** Because portal GnRH is pulsatile, LH/FSH release is **episodic** (~8–14 LH pulses per 24 h in men). **Continuous** GnRH or high-dose GnRH analogs paradoxically **suppress** gonadotropins via receptor internalization exceeding "receptor replenishment" → clinical **chemical castration in prostate cancer**.
- **LH and FSH** are glycoprotein heterodimers (α + β) of the same family as **hCG** and **TSH**. The **α subunit (92 aa)** is identical across all four; the **β subunit** confers specificity. hCG's β is LH's β plus an extra C-terminal 24 aa; hCG often substitutes for LH clinically.
- **LH → Leydig cell** (G_s → adenylyl cyclase → cAMP → PKA): drives testosterone biosynthesis (mnemonic: **L** for **L**H and **L**eydig).
- **FSH → Sertoli cell** (same G_s/cAMP/PKA cascade; mnemonic **S** for **F**SH/**S**ertoli) → synthesis of:
  1. **Androgen-binding protein (ABP)** — secreted into tubule lumen; keeps local testosterone high.
  2. **P-450 aromatase** — converts testosterone (diffusing from Leydig cells) to estradiol.
  3. **Growth factors** supporting spermatogonia, spermatocytes, spermatids. FSH does **not** act directly on germ cells — it acts on Sertoli cells.
  4. **Inhibins** (TGF-β superfamily, α/β heterodimer) — negative feedback that selectively suppresses **FSH** at the pituitary.
- **Leydig–Sertoli crosstalk**: Leydig testosterone acts on Sertoli cells; Sertoli aromatase converts it to estradiol, which feeds back to **decrease** Leydig testosterone output in response to LH; Sertoli growth factors can **increase** Leydig LH-receptor number.
- Optimal spermatogenesis requires **two cell types** (Leydig + Sertoli), **two gonadotropins** (LH + FSH), and **one androgen** (testosterone).
- **Negative feedback (two routes, see Fig. 54-2):**
  - **Testosterone** inhibits **GnRH** pulse frequency/amplitude at the hypothalamus **and** inhibits LH secretion at the pituitary gonadotrophs.
  - **Inhibin** inhibits **FSH** at the anterior pituitary (not the hypothalamus). Plasma FSH rises in proportion to loss of germinal elements.

### Citation-anchor quotes
- > "In the male, LH and FSH control, respectively, the Leydig and Sertoli cells of the testes." (p. 1092)
- > "Because secretion of GnRH into the portal system is pulsatile, secretion of LH and FSH by the gonadotrophs is also episodic." (p. 1094)
- > "Continuous administration of GnRH—or intermittent administration of high doses of GnRH analogs—suppresses the release of gonadotropins." (p. 1094)
- > "The α subunits of LH and FSH, as well as the α subunits of hCG and TSH, are identical." (p. 1094)
- > "The interstitial Leydig cells are the principal targets for LH and the primary source of testosterone production in the male." (p. 1095)
- > "The stimulatory effect of FSH on spermatogenesis is not a direct action of FSH on the spermatogonia; instead, stimulation of spermatogenesis occurs via the action of FSH on Sertoli cells." (p. 1095)
- > "Normal circulating levels of testosterone inhibit the pulsatile release of GnRH by the hypothalamus." (p. 1096)
- > "Inhibin appears to diminish FSH secretion by acting at the level of the anterior pituitary rather than the hypothalamus." (p. 1097)

### Equations

- **GnRH signal transduction** (gonadotroph):

  $$\text{GnRH} \rightarrow \text{GPCR}/G_q \rightarrow \text{PLC} \rightarrow \text{PIP}_2 \rightarrow \text{IP}_3 + \text{DAG}$$
  $$\text{IP}_3 \rightarrow \uparrow[\text{Ca}^{2+}]_i \rightarrow \text{exocytosis of LH, FSH}; \quad \text{DAG} \rightarrow \text{PKC} \rightarrow \uparrow\text{LH/FSH gene expression}$$

- **LH and FSH signal transduction** (Leydig and Sertoli cells, respectively):

  $$\text{LH (or FSH)} \rightarrow \text{GPCR}/G_s \rightarrow \text{adenylyl cyclase} \rightarrow \uparrow[\text{cAMP}]_i \rightarrow \text{PKA} \rightarrow \uparrow\text{gene transcription}$$

- **Feedback loop (qualitative):**

  $$\text{Testosterone} \dashv \text{GnRH (hypothalamus)} \ \text{and} \ \dashv \text{LH (pituitary)}; \qquad \text{Inhibin B} \dashv \text{FSH (pituitary)}$$

## Section 2 — Testosterone (pp. 1097–1100)

### Subsection headings
- **Leydig cells convert cholesterol to testosterone** (pp. 1097–1098)
- **Adipose tissue, skin, and the adrenal cortex also produce testosterone and other androgens** (pp. 1097–1099)
- **Testosterone acts on target organs by binding to a nuclear receptor** (pp. 1099–1100)
- **Metabolism of testosterone occurs primarily in the liver and prostate** (pp. 1099–1100)

### Core claims
- **Cholesterol** is the obligate androgen precursor; the Leydig cell makes it de novo from acetyl-CoA or takes up LDL by receptor-mediated endocytosis (roughly equal in humans).
- **Cholesterol translocation to the inner mitochondrial membrane** is gated by two proteins: **SCP-2** (sterol-carrier protein 2, 13.5 kDa) and **StAR** (steroidogenic acute regulatory protein; 37-kDa pro-StAR → 30-kDa mature StAR in the intermembrane space).
- **Five enzymes** convert cholesterol to testosterone; **three are P-450 enzymes**. The **"preferred" (delta-5) pathway** in human Leydig cells:
  1. **Cholesterol → pregnenolone** by **P-450scc** (side-chain cleavage / 20,22-desmolase) on the inner mitochondrial membrane — the **rate-limiting step**. LH stimulates it two ways: ↑P-450scc affinity for cholesterol (fast) and ↑levels of SCP-2/StAR/P-450scc via PKA-driven transcription (slow).
  2. **Pregnenolone → 17α-hydroxypregnenolone** by **17α-hydroxylase (P-450c17)** in the SER.
  3. **17α-hydroxypregnenolone → DHEA** by **17,20-desmolase** (same P-450c17 enzyme, different activity).
  4. **DHEA → androstenediol** by **17β-HSD** (not a P-450).
  5. **Androstenediol → testosterone** by **3β-HSD** (not a P-450).
- **Branch points**: **5α-reductase** (SER) → **DHT**; **aromatase** → **estradiol**. Most DHT is made **extratesticularly** (in target cells). Leydig cells make **~95%** of circulating testosterone.
- **Other androgen sources**: adipose tissue, skin, adrenal cortex, brain, muscle. The **adrenal** makes the weak androgens **DHEA, DHEA-sulfate, androstenedione**; essentially all plasma DHEA is adrenal, but <1% of plasma testosterone derives from DHEA. Androstenedione → testosterone (and → estrogens) in peripheral tissues.
- **Transport** (key for SHBG focus): ~**45%** of plasma testosterone is bound to **sex hormone-binding globulin (SHBG)**, ~**55%** to **albumin + CBG**, and only ~**2%** circulates **free**. Free testosterone diffuses into target cells.
- **Androgen receptor (AR)**: a nuclear-receptor-family transcription factor; gene on the **X chromosome**; functions as a **homodimer (AR/AR)**; binds testosterone **or** DHT. AR presence determines androgen responsiveness.
- **DHT vs. testosterone**: in tissues expressing 5α-reductase, testosterone is amplified to **DHT**, whose biological activity is **30–50×** higher (higher AR affinity) — important in hair follicles, sebaceous glands, sex accessory organs, prostate. In the brain, testosterone is **aromatized to estradiol** acting via the estrogen receptor. Some androgen effects are **nongenomic**.
- **Metabolism**: <2% of daily testosterone enters urine unmetabolized; the rest is converted in the **liver** to 17-ketosteroids and in the **prostate** to DHT, then excreted in urine/feces as sulfate or glucuronide conjugates.

### Citation-anchor quotes
- > "Cholesterol is the obligate precursor for androgens and other testicular steroids." (p. 1097)
- > "This reaction is the rate-limiting step in the biosynthesis of testosterone." (p. 1097)
- > "The Leydig cells of the testes make ~95% of the circulating testosterone." (p. 1098)
- > "About 45% of plasma testosterone binds to sex hormone-binding globulin (SHBG) and ~55% binds to serum albumin and corticosteroid-binding globulin (CBG)." (p. 1099)
- > "The biological activity of DHT is 30 to 50 times higher than that of testosterone." (p. 1099)
- > "Whether the active compound in any tissue is DHT or testosterone depends on the presence or absence in that tissue of the microsomal enzyme 5α-reductase." (p. 1099)
- > "The gene coding for the AR is located on the X chromosome." (p. 1099)

### Equations

- **Rate-limiting step (delta-5 preferred pathway):**

  $$\text{Cholesterol} \xrightarrow{\text{P-450scc (20,22-desmolase)}} \text{Pregnenolone} \xrightarrow{\text{P-450c17}} 17\alpha\text{-OH-pregnenolone} \xrightarrow{\text{17,20-desmolase}} \text{DHEA}$$
  $$\text{DHEA} \xrightarrow{\text{17}\beta\text{-HSD}} \text{Androstenediol} \xrightarrow{\text{3}\beta\text{-HSD}} \text{Testosterone}$$

- **Peripheral conversions:**

  $$\text{Testosterone} \xrightarrow{\text{5}\alpha\text{-reductase}} \text{DHT}; \qquad \text{Testosterone} \xrightarrow{\text{aromatase (P-450}_\text{arom}\text{)}} \text{Estradiol (E}_2\text{)}$$

- **Plasma partition:**

  $$\text{Testosterone}_{\text{plasma}} \approx 45\%\ \text{SHBG-bound} + 55\%\ (\text{albumin}+\text{CBG}) + 2\%\ \text{free}$$

## Section 3 — Biology of spermatogenesis and semen (pp. 1100–1104)

### Subsection headings
- **Spermatogenesis includes mitotic divisions of spermatogonia, meiotic divisions of spermatocytes to spermatids, and maturation to spermatozoa** (pp. 1100–1101)
- **The Sertoli cells support spermatogenesis** (pp. 1100–1102)
- **Sperm maturation occurs in the epididymis** (pp. 1102–1103)
- **Spermatozoa are the only independently motile cells in the human body** (p. 1103)
- **The accessory male sex glands—the seminal vesicles, prostate, and bulbourethral glands—produce the seminal plasma** (pp. 1103–1104)

### Core claims
- **Lineage**: primordial germ cells → **spermatogonia** (diploid, 2N, 46 chromosomes) on the inner basal lamina. **Type A** spermatogonia are stem cells (self-renew); a daughter becomes **type B**, which after several mitoses gives **primary spermatocytes**.
- **Meiosis bookkeeping**:
  - **Primary spermatocyte** — after S-phase/crossing-over: duplicated 46 chromosomes (**4N** DNA).
  - **Secondary spermatocyte** — after meiosis I: haploid set of duplicated chromosomes (**2N** DNA).
  - **Spermatid** — after meiosis II: haploid, unduplicated (**1N** DNA).
  - Each primary spermatocyte → **four spermatozoa** (two X-bearing, two Y-bearing).
- **Spermiogenesis**: spermatids transform into spermatozoa (cytoplasmic reduction, head/tail differentiation); cells **decrease in volume**. **Spermiation** = release from the Sertoli cell.
- **Timing**: type-B-spermatogonium-to-mature-sperm ≈ **64 days**; per-stage life spans (type B ≈16–18 d, primary spermatocyte ≈23 d, secondary ≈1 d, spermatid ≈23 d). Rate is **constant, hormonally non-accelerable**; failed cells undergo **apoptosis**. Healthy 20-yr-olds make ~**6.5 million sperm/g testis/day** (~**1000 sperm/second**), falling to ~3.8 million/g/day at 50–90 yr.
- **Spermatogenesis is asynchronous** along the tubule (different segments at different stages) — the basis of the cycle/wave of the seminiferous epithelium.
- **vs. oogenesis**: (1) male mitotic proliferation occurs **after puberty, lifelong** (female: entirely before birth); (2) one primary spermatocyte → **four** gametes with minimal cytoplasm (female: one ovum + polar bodies); (3) male meiosis completes pre-fertilization with substantial further differentiation (female: meiosis II completes only at fertilization).
- **Sertoli ("nurse"/sustentacular) cells** (6 jobs): (1) maintain a spermatogenesis-conducive environment; (2) under FSH, secrete substances promoting spermatogonial proliferation and meiosis; (3) secrete **ABP**; (4) secrete **inhibin** (controls pituitary FSH); (5) **phagocytose** excess gamete cytoplasm; (6) make **antimüllerian hormone**.
- **Blood-testis barrier**: tight junctions between adjacent Sertoli cells partition the epithelium into **basal** and **adluminal** compartments — analogous to the blood-brain barrier — protecting developing germ cells (and immunologically isolating haploid cells).
- **Epididymis**: a single convoluted duct 4–5 m long (head/caput, body/corpus, tail/cauda). Sperm transit ≈ **12–26 days**; sperm gain **forward motility and fertilizing capacity** here (Table 54-2). Caput sperm often cannot fertilize; corpus more fertile; cauda/vas-deferens sperm almost always fertile. Transport is largely **passive** (cilia + smooth-muscle/efferent-duct contraction).
- **Spermatozoon anatomy** (Fig. 54-9): **head** (nucleus + acrosome — a Golgi-derived enzyme vesicle for ovum penetration); **midpiece** (mitochondrial spiral = the engine; energy from **fructose** in seminal fluid); **tail** (flagellum/axoneme). The only independently motile human cell.
- **Semen / seminal plasma**: only **~10%** of semen volume is sperm cells; **~90%** is seminal plasma. **Seminal vesicles ≈70%** of volume; prostate + bulbourethral + epididymal fluids the rest. Seminal plasma is **isotonic**; epididymal lumen is acidic (pH 6.5–6.8), but alkaline seminal-vesicle secretion raises final semen pH to **7.3–7.7** (optimal for sperm).
- **Composition**: **fructose** (from seminal vesicles — absent fructose with low volume suggests seminal-vesicle obstruction); **citric acid**; high **Zn²⁺** and **Ca²⁺**; **acid phosphatase** (prostate); **hyaluronidase** (from sperm cytoplasm). Semen **coagulates** immediately then **liquefies** via prostatic proteolytic enzymes.

### Citation-anchor quotes
- > "In a continuous process, a young man produces sperm at a rate of ~1000 per second." (p. 1100)
- > "The rate of spermatogenesis is constant and cannot be accelerated by hormones such as gonadotropins or androgens." (p. 1100)
- > "In the male, the meiotic divisions of a primary spermatocyte produce four mature spermatozoa with a minimal amount of cytoplasm." (p. 1100)
- > "Tight junctions connect the adjacent Sertoli cells, forming a blood-testis barrier—analogous to the blood-brain barrier." (p. 1101)
- > "Sperm are stored in the epididymis, where they undergo a process of maturation before they are capable of progressive motility and fertilization." (p. 1102)
- > "The energy for sperm motility comes from the metabolism of fructose present in the seminal fluid." (p. 1103)
- > "Only 10% of the volume of semen (i.e., seminal fluid) is sperm cells." (p. 1103)

## Section 4 — Male sex act (pp. 1104–1107)

### Subsection headings
- **The sympathetic and parasympathetic divisions of the autonomic nervous system control the male genital system** (pp. 1104–1105)
- **Erection is primarily under parasympathetic control** (pp. 1105–1106)
- **Emission is primarily under sympathetic control** (pp. 1106–1107)
- **Ejaculation is under the control of a spinal reflex** (p. 1107)

### Core claims
- **Dual ANS innervation** plus somatic and afferent supply. **Sympathetic** preganglionics: thoracolumbar (T1–T12, L1–L3) → five prevertebral plexuses (celiac, superior mesenteric, inferior mesenteric, superior hypogastric, inferior hypogastric/pelvic) + spermatic and hypogastric ganglia. **Parasympathetic** preganglionics: sacral (S2–S4) → pelvic nerve → pelvic plexus. **Somatic + afferent**: **pudendal nerve (S2–S4)**; the **dorsal nerve of the penis** is the sole tactile sensory root.
- **Erection — three motor pathways + one sensory** (Fig. 54-10B):
  1. **Parasympathetic (pelvic nerve) — first and most important.** Causes **vasodilation** of penile vessels, ↑inflow to corpora cavernosa/spongiosum → engorgement. Terminals release **ACh** and **NO**. ACh → M₃ muscarinic receptors on endothelium → G_q → PLC → ↑Ca²⁺ → NO synthase → NO; nerves may also release **NO** directly. **NO → guanylyl cyclase → cGMP → smooth-muscle relaxation → erection.**
  2. **Sympathetic (hypogastric nerve)** — tonic sympathetic tone keeps the penis **flaccid**; **decreased** tone during arousal permits tumescence.
  3. **Somatic (pudendal nerve)** — contraction of **ischiocavernosus** and **bulbospongiosus** striated muscles raises intracorporal pressure (can exceed arterial pressure) in the final phase.
- **PDE5 / erectile dysfunction (Box 54-3)**: **cGMP-specific phosphodiesterase type 5 (PDE5)** degrades cGMP, limiting erection. **Sildenafil (Viagra), vardenafil (Levitra), tadalafil (Cialis)** are selective PDE5 inhibitors → raise [cGMP] → improve erection, but only **during sexual arousal** (they amplify, not initiate, NO signaling). Side effects: "blue vision" (retinal PDE inhibition); **dangerous with other vasodilators/nitrates** (can cause sudden death).
- **Emission (sympathetic, hypogastric nerve, norepinephrine)**: peristaltic contraction of vas deferens ampulla, seminal vesicles, prostatic smooth muscle moves ejaculate into the prostatic/proximal urethra; simultaneous **internal bladder-sphincter constriction** prevents **retrograde ejaculation** (Box 54-4). The accessory glands are **multiunit smooth muscle** (individually innervated, minimal coupling) → fast, coordinated response. IV epinephrine/norepinephrine can induce emission; sympathectomy/α-blockade inhibits it.
- **Ejaculation (spinal reflex, S2–S4)**: triggered by semen entering the bulbous urethra; afferents → sacral cord → **pudendal** efferents → rhythmic contraction of perineal striated muscles (pelvic floor, ischiocavernosus, bulbospongiosus) forcefully expel semen. **Orgasm** is the cognitive correlate — central as much as peripheral.

### Equations

- **NO–cGMP erection cascade:**

  $$\text{Parasympathetic} \rightarrow \text{ACh} \rightarrow M_3/G_q \rightarrow \uparrow[\text{Ca}^{2+}]_i \rightarrow \text{NOS} \rightarrow \text{NO} \xrightarrow{} \text{guanylyl cyclase} \rightarrow \uparrow\text{cGMP} \rightarrow \text{smooth-muscle relaxation} \rightarrow \text{erection}$$

- **PDE5 inhibition (pharmacology of ED drugs):**

  $$\text{cGMP} \xrightarrow{\text{PDE5}} \text{GMP (terminates signal)}; \qquad \text{sildenafil} \dashv \text{PDE5} \Rightarrow \uparrow[\text{cGMP}] \Rightarrow \text{sustained erection}$$

## Glossary

- **GnRH** — hypothalamic decapeptide; pulsatile; G_q/PLC signaling at gonadotrophs.
- **LH / FSH** — anterior-pituitary glycoprotein gonadotropins (shared α, distinct β); G_s/cAMP/PKA signaling.
- **Leydig cell** — interstitial; LH target; testosterone synthesis ("**L**H/**L**eydig").
- **Sertoli cell** — seminiferous epithelium; FSH target; ABP, inhibin, aromatase, AMH, growth factors ("**F**SH/**S**ertoli").
- **Inhibin** — Sertoli-secreted TGF-β-family heterodimer; selective FSH negative feedback at pituitary.
- **ABP (androgen-binding protein)** — keeps intratubular testosterone high.
- **Steroidogenesis** — cholesterol → (P-450scc, rate-limiting) → … → testosterone; delta-5 preferred pathway.
- **StAR / SCP-2** — cholesterol delivery to inner mitochondrial membrane.
- **5α-reductase** — testosterone → DHT (30–50× more potent).
- **Aromatase (P-450_arom)** — testosterone → estradiol.
- **SHBG** — sex hormone-binding globulin; binds ~45% of plasma testosterone (~2% free).
- **Androgen receptor (AR)** — X-linked nuclear receptor; homodimer; binds testosterone/DHT.
- **Spermatogenesis** — spermatogonia → spermatocytes → spermatids → spermatozoa; ~64 days; ~1000/s.
- **Spermiogenesis / spermiation** — spermatid→sperm differentiation / release from Sertoli cell.
- **Blood-testis barrier** — Sertoli tight junctions; basal vs. adluminal compartments.
- **Epididymis** — site of sperm maturation/motility; 12–26-day transit.
- **Acrosome** — Golgi-derived enzyme cap for ovum penetration.
- **Seminal plasma** — ~90% of semen; seminal vesicles ~70% of volume; fructose, Zn²⁺, citrate; pH 7.3–7.7.
- **Erection** — parasympathetic; NO–cGMP relaxation of cavernosal smooth muscle.
- **PDE5 inhibitors** — sildenafil/vardenafil/tadalafil; raise cGMP.
- **Emission** — sympathetic (hypogastric/NE); internal sphincter closes (prevents retrograde ejaculation).
- **Ejaculation** — spinal reflex (S2–S4); pudendal-driven perineal muscle contraction.
