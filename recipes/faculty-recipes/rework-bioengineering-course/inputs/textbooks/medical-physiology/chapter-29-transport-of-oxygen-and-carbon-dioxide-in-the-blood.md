# Chapter 29 — Transport of Oxygen and Carbon Dioxide in the Blood

> Section V · The Respiratory System · pp. 647–659 · Author: Walter F. Boron
> The molecular bridge between **ventilation mechanics (Chs 26–27)** and **gas exchange / V̇/Q̇ (Chs 30–31)**. The chapter develops two parallel transport stories — **O₂ carriage** (a tiny dissolved fraction obeying Henry's law plus ~98.5% bound to hemoglobin, with sigmoidal cooperative binding modulated by temperature, [H⁺], P_CO₂, 2,3-DPG, and CO) and **CO₂ carriage** (a five-form "total CO₂" pool partitioned mostly into HCO₃⁻ via erythrocyte carbonic anhydrase and the AE1 chloride shift, plus dissolved CO₂ and carbamino-Hb, with the **Haldane effect** reciprocal to the Bohr effect). The chapter culminates in the **O₂–CO₂ diagram** (Fig. 29-11) that ties the two transport systems together and sets up the regional V̇/Q̇ analysis of Ch 31.

## Chapter map (top-level)

1. **Carriage of O₂** (pp. 647–654) — dissolved O₂ obeys Henry's law and is inadequate (~0.3 mL/dL → only ~15 mL/min delivered) so >98% rides on hemoglobin; Hb tetramer (α₂β₂, heme + Fe²⁺ + globin); cooperative T → R conformational change (Monod-Wyman-Changeux); sigmoidal Hb-O₂ dissociation curve, P₅₀; a-v difference; **Bohr effect** (↑T, ↑P_CO₂, ↓pH right-shift the curve via H⁺ binding and carbamino formation); **2,3-DPG** (chronic hypoxia / altitude / anemia right-shift); fetal Hb (γ chains, low 2,3-DPG binding, left shift) for placental O₂ extraction; **CO poisoning** (~200× affinity; left-shifts the remaining curve so residual O₂ won't unload); methemoglobin (Fe³⁺, cannot bind O₂); Box 29-1 (HbA, HbA₂, HbF, HbA₁c, HbS); Box 29-2 (pulse oximetry).
2. **Carriage of CO₂** (pp. 654–659) — five "total CO₂" forms (dissolved CO₂, H₂CO₃, HCO₃⁻, CO₃²⁻, carbamino); ~90% travels as HCO₃⁻ at rest; tissue → blood incremental CO₂ partition (~10% dissolved, ~69% HCO₃⁻, ~21% carbamino) generated almost entirely inside the RBC by **carbonic anhydrase + AE1 chloride/Hamburger shift + Hb buffering**; Haldane effect (deoxyHb is a better H⁺ buffer and forms carbamino more readily → at any P_CO₂ the CO₂-carrying capacity rises as Hb desaturates); CO₂ dissociation curves much steeper than O₂'s in their physiological ranges; the integrated **O₂–CO₂ diagram** with isopleths for constant O₂ content and constant CO₂ content.

## Section 1 — Carriage of O₂ (pp. 647–654)

### Subsection headings (verbatim)
- **The amount of O₂ dissolved in blood is far too small to meet the metabolic demands of the body** (p. 647)
- **Hemoglobin consists of two α and two β subunits, each of which has an iron-containing "heme" and a polypeptide "globin"** (pp. 647–649)
- **The Hb-O₂ dissociation curve has a sigmoidal shape because of cooperativity among the four subunits of the Hb molecule** (pp. 649–652)
- **Increases in temperature, [CO₂], and [H⁺], all of which are characteristic of metabolically active tissues, cause Hb to dump O₂** (pp. 652–654)
- **2,3-Diphosphoglycerate reduces the affinity of adult, but not of fetal, Hb** (pp. 654–655)

### Core claims

#### Dissolved O₂ is essentially negligible — the role of Henry's law
- O₂ in blood exists in two pools: **dissolved** (a tiny fraction in the aqueous phases of plasma and RBC cytoplasm) and **Hb-bound** (>98% inside the erythrocyte).
- The dissolved pool obeys **Henry's law**:

$$\boxed{[O_2]_{\text{pls}} = k_{O_2} \cdot P_{O_2}} \quad (\text{Eq. 29-1})$$

  with $k_{O_2} \approx 0.003$ mL O₂ / (dL blood · mm Hg) at 37°C. Worked at alveolar P_O₂ = 100 mm Hg:

$$[O_2]_{\text{pls}} = \frac{0.003 \text{ mL } O_2}{100 \text{ mL blood} \cdot \text{mm Hg}} \cdot 100 \text{ mm Hg} = 0.3 \text{ mL } O_2/100 \text{ mL blood} \quad (\text{Eq. 29-2})$$

- The **Fick principle** test: if 5 L/min of cardiac output extracts every molecule of dissolved O₂, the delivery is just

$$\dot Q \cdot \Delta[O_2]_{\text{pls}} = 5000 \tfrac{\text{mL}}{\text{min}} \cdot \tfrac{0.3 \text{ mL } O_2}{100 \text{ mL blood}} = 15 \text{ mL } O_2/\text{min} \quad (\text{Eq. 29-3})$$

  vs. resting consumption ≈ **250 mL O₂/min**. The body would need cardiac output to climb **~17-fold** to live off dissolved O₂ alone. Hence the requirement for a bound-O₂ carrier.

#### Hemoglobin structure (Fig. 29-1)
- **Adult Hb (HbA)** is a tetramer, MW ~68 kDa, with stoichiometry $[\alpha(\text{heme})]_2[\beta(\text{heme})]_2$. Each $\alpha$ chain has 141 amino acids, each $\beta$ chain 146; both fold into the same seven-helix globin envelope wrapping a single heme.
- **Heme** is a porphyrin (four pyrrole rings linked via methine bridges) coordinating a single central iron in the **Fe²⁺ (ferrous)** state. The conjugated double-bond system absorbs visible light → **red when O₂-bound** (arterial), **purple when deoxy** (venous). One Fe²⁺ binds one O₂; four hemes per tetramer → four O₂ max.
- **Globin is essential**: free heme + O₂ → irreversible oxidation of Fe²⁺ to Fe³⁺ (rust); only the cradle of ~20 globin residues around the heme keeps the O₂-Fe²⁺ interaction loose and reversible. The **proximal histidine** bonds Fe²⁺ directly and donates negative charge stabilizing the Fe²⁺-O₂ complex; it is also the mechanical lever that transmits the binding signal to the rest of the tetramer.
- **Methemoglobin (metHb)** = Hb with Fe³⁺ instead of Fe²⁺ — cannot bind O₂. Spontaneous oxidation is continual; nitrites and sulfonamides accelerate it. The RBC enzyme **methemoglobin reductase** (cytochrome b₅ reductase / NADH-dependent) reduces metHb back to Hb, holding the resting metHb fraction at ~1.5%. Inherited deficiency raises metHb to ≥25%, depressing O₂ capacity → tissue hypoxia. Treatment of acute methemoglobinemia: **methylene blue** (an alternative electron acceptor that activates NADPH-methemoglobin reductase).
- **Myoglobin (Mb)** is a single-chain heme-O₂ protein expressed in muscle. Arose by gene duplication from a primordial globin (the lineage that also gave rise to α and β chains of Hb). Monomeric → noncooperative → much higher O₂ affinity than Hb. Function: receives O₂ handed off from Hb at the capillary and shuttles it down a Mb-relay path to mitochondria, because dissolved O₂ alone cannot establish a sufficient intracellular gradient.

#### The T → R conformational switch and cooperativity (Fig. 29-1C)
- **Tense (T) state**: deoxy. The four globin subunits are interlinked by salt bridges + steric constraints "as if by a snugly fitting system of levers and joints, that no one subunit can leave this tensed (T) state unless they all leave it together." Each proximal histidine pulls its Fe²⁺ ~0.06 nm above the porphyrin plane, doming the ring; the dome **sterically hinders O₂ approach** → very low O₂ affinity.
- **Relaxed (R) state**: oxy. When O₂ binds an Fe²⁺, the iron drops into the porphyrin plane, flattening the heme and pulling the proximal histidine and the F-helix down with it. Enough binding events accumulate energy and all four subunits **snap simultaneously** to the planar / open / R conformation (whether or not all four hemes are bound). In R state, O₂ affinity is **~150-fold greater** than T. This concerted (all-or-none) transition is the Monod-Wyman-Changeux paradigm.
- **Hill cooperativity** is the phenomenological description of this two-state switch:

$$\boxed{Y = \frac{(P_{O_2})^n}{P_{50}^n + (P_{O_2})^n}} \quad (\text{Hill equation})$$

  with $Y$ = fractional saturation, $P_{50}$ = half-saturation P_O₂ (~27 mm Hg for adult HbA at standard conditions), and **Hill coefficient n ≈ 2.7–3** (n = 1 would be pure Michaelis-Menten / single-site; n = 4 would be infinitely cooperative binding of all four sites).

#### The Hb-O₂ dissociation curve (Fig. 29-3)
- The textbook setup (Fig. 29-2): equilibrate whole blood with a series of gas-phase $P_{O_2}$ values, centrifuge, separate plasma from packed RBCs, measure O₂ content of each — subtract dissolved O₂ → Hb-bound O₂.
- Maximal O₂ capacity per gram of Hb is **1.39 mL O₂/g Hb** theoretical; in vivo ~**1.35 mL O₂/g Hb** because some Hb is metHb or CO-bound. Some texts use **1.34 mL O₂/g Hb** (Hüfner's number). With Hb content 15 g/dL:

$$\text{Max O}_2 \text{ bound to Hb} = 1.35 \tfrac{\text{mL } O_2}{\text{g Hb}} \cdot 15 \tfrac{\text{g Hb}}{\text{dL blood}} \approx 20.3 \tfrac{\text{mL } O_2}{\text{dL blood}} \quad (\text{Eq. 29-4})$$

- Percent saturation:

$$\boxed{\%\text{Sat (S}_{O_2}\text{)} = \frac{\text{O}_2 \text{ bound to Hb}}{\text{O}_2 \text{ capacity of Hb}} \cdot 100} \quad (\text{Eq. 29-5})$$

- The total **arterial O₂ content equation** combines bound + dissolved:

$$\boxed{C_aO_2 = 1.34 \cdot [\text{Hb}] \cdot S_aO_2 + 0.003 \cdot P_aO_2}$$

  with $C_aO_2$ in mL O₂/dL blood, $[\text{Hb}]$ in g/dL, $S_aO_2$ as fraction (0–1), $P_aO_2$ in mm Hg. Worked at $P_aO_2 = 100$, $S_aO_2 = 0.975$, $[\text{Hb}] = 15$: $C_aO_2 \approx 19.6 + 0.3 \approx 20.0$ mL O₂/dL.

- **Three regimes of the curve**:
  - **Low P_O₂ (0–20 mm Hg)**: shallow slope — Hb largely T state, low affinity.
  - **Middle P_O₂ (20–60 mm Hg)**: steep slope — cooperative T → R transition; small ΔP_O₂ yields large ΔS_O₂ (the unloading range in tissues).
  - **High P_O₂ (>70 mm Hg)**: plateau — Hb largely R state, near saturation; the arterial range is buffered against modest hypoxemia (the alveolar plateau).
- **Reference points (Table 29-3)**:

| Point | P_O₂ (mm Hg) | S_O₂ | O₂ bound to Hb (mL/dL) | O₂ dissolved (mL/dL) | Total O₂ (mL/dL) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **a** (arterial) | 100 | 97.5% | 19.7 | 0.3 | 20.0 |
| **v̄** (mixed-venous) | 40 | 75% | 15.2 | 0.1 | 15.3 |
| **a − v̄ difference** | 60 | 22.5% | 4.5 | 0.2 | 4.7 |

  Hb provides 4.5/4.7 ≈ **96%** of the O₂ that lungs add and tissues extract.

- **Fick-principle reconciliation** (Eq. 29-7): $\dot V_{O_2} = \dot Q \cdot \Delta C_{a-\bar v}O_2 \approx 5 \text{ L/min} \cdot 4.7 \text{ mL/dL} \approx 235$ mL O₂/min, close to the 250 mL O₂/min resting demand. The remaining ~15 mL/min comes from small increases in either $\dot Q$ or extraction.
- **Hyperoxia / breathing 100% O₂**: $P_aO_2$ climbs ~6-fold → dissolved O₂ ~1.8 mL/dL. Hb is already saturated, so the bound component does not increase. Useful in clinical hyperbaric therapy (and CO poisoning treatment).

#### Anemia, polycythemia, cyanosis
- **Anemia** (↓[Hb]) cuts maximal O₂ content roughly linearly. Compensation: ↑ cardiac output and/or ↑ extraction (lower mixed-venous P_O₂). Clinical signs: pallor (loss of red pigment), lethargy/fatigue (low O₂ delivery), palpitations and a systolic flow murmur (high CO), shortness of breath.
- **Polycythemia** (↑Hct) raises maximal O₂ content but raises blood viscosity (Ch 18, Fåhraeus-Lindqvist) → ↑ vascular resistance, ↑ systemic and pulmonary pressures, and V̇/Q̇ mismatch in the lung → paradoxical arterial desaturation. Optimal Hct ~45%.
- **Cyanosis** = purplish skin/mucosa caused by an absolute amount of **desaturated Hb** at the surface, not by a ratio. Severely anemic patients may have unsaturated Hb but too little of it to manifest cyanosis. Detection depends on skin pigmentation and lighting.

#### The Bohr effect — T, [H⁺], P_CO₂ right-shift the curve (Figs 29-4, 29-5)
Metabolically active tissues are warm, acidic, and CO₂-rich. All three properties **right-shift** the Hb-O₂ dissociation curve (decrease O₂ affinity → unload more O₂ at any given P_O₂).

- **Temperature (Fig. 29-4)**. Active muscle reaches ~40°C; cold extremities can go well below 37°C. Comparing curves at P_O₂ = 40 mm Hg (mixed-venous), Hb at high T carries less O₂ than at low T. Mechanism: small shifts in pK values of titratable amino-acid side chains → net charge changes → conformational shift toward T.
- **Acid (Fig. 29-5B) — the pH-Bohr effect**, the dominant component of Bohr's original observation (Christian Bohr, 1904, father of Niels). Hb is a major H⁺ buffer with pK values in the physiological range:

$$\text{Hb} + \text{H}^+ \rightleftharpoons \text{Hb-H}^+ \quad (\text{Eq. 29-8})$$

  Protonation alters Hb conformation and lowers O₂ affinity. Pushed to an extreme:

$$\text{Hb(O}_2\text{)}_4 + 2\text{H}^+ \rightleftharpoons \text{Hb(H}^+\text{)}_2 + 4\text{O}_2 \quad (\text{Eq. 29-9})$$

  Under physiological conditions, binding of ~**0.7 mol H⁺ releases 1 mol O₂**.

- **Carbon dioxide (Fig. 29-5C) — the CO₂-Bohr effect**, a smaller portion of the overall Bohr effect. CO₂ reacts with unprotonated α-amino groups (the four amino-termini of the globin chains; β-chain N-termini more reactive than α) to form **carbamino-Hb**:

$$\text{Hb-NH}_3^+ \xrightleftharpoons[\text{H}^+]{} \text{Hb-NH}_2 \xrightleftharpoons[\text{CO}_2]{\text{H}^+} \text{Hb-NH-COO}^- \quad (\text{Eq. 29-10})$$

  Net effect on the side chain: from +1 charge (protonated amine) to −1 charge (carbamate) — major conformational shift toward T:

$$(\text{O}_2)_4 \text{Hb-NH}_3^+ + \text{CO}_2 \rightleftharpoons (\text{O}_2)_3 \text{Hb-NH-COO}^- + \text{O}_2 + 2\text{H}^+ \quad (\text{Eq. 29-11})$$

- **Vasodilator coupling**. In most systemic arterioles, the same local hypercapnia + acidosis that right-shift the curve also vasodilate (Ch 24), so the blood that gets the unloading signal also gets to the metabolically active tissue.

#### 2,3-DPG and fetal Hb (Fig. 29-6, Fig. 29-7)
- **2,3-bisphosphoglycerate (2,3-DPG / 2,3-BPG)** is a glycolytic side-product (DPG mutase converts 1,3-BPG → 2,3-BPG) present at ~4–5 mM in RBC cytoplasm — roughly equimolar with Hb. Binds 1:1 in the **central cavity between the two β chains** via ~8 positive residues; carries an average of ~3.5 negative charges at physiological pH.
- **Mechanism of right shift**: 2,3-DPG stabilizes the T state. Deoxy-Hb has a ~100-fold higher affinity for 2,3-DPG than oxy-Hb. Net reaction:

$$\text{Hb(O}_2\text{)}_4 + 2,3\text{-DPG} \rightleftharpoons \text{Hb(2,3-DPG)} + 4\text{O}_2 \quad (\text{Eq. 29-12})$$

- **Adaptive value**. Hypoxia (chronic anemia, chronic lung disease, high-altitude acclimation) ↑ glycolysis → ↑ 2,3-DPG within ~24–48 h → right-shifted ODC → **better tissue unloading on the steep part of the curve**. The cost — a small reduction in alveolar loading on the flat part — is much smaller than the unloading gain.
- **"Stripped Hb" experiment (Fig. 29-7)**. Pure Hb in artificial solution without CO₂, 2,3-DPG, or other organic phosphates lies far to the left. Adding CO₂ alone, or 2,3-DPG alone, right-shifts modestly; **adding both reproduces the in-vivo whole-blood curve.**
- **Fetal Hb (HbF, α₂γ₂)**. Stripped HbF has the same intrinsic O₂ affinity as stripped HbA. The crucial difference is that the **γ chain binds 2,3-DPG poorly** → the in-vivo HbF curve is left-shifted relative to HbA. This left shift is what lets fetal blood **extract O₂ from maternal blood across the placenta** (the fetal "P₅₀" is ~19–20 mm Hg vs. ~27 for HbA). Box 29-1 (forms of Hb) covers HbA, HbA₂ (α₂δ₂; the δ chain reduces sickling), HbA₁c (glycated HbA at the β-chain N-terminus by glucose-6-phosphate; clinically used as a 120-day-average blood-glucose tracker in diabetes), and **HbS** (Glu → Val at β6; deoxy-HbS polymerizes into fibers → sickled, fragile cells → vaso-occlusion + chronic hemolytic anemia).

#### Carbon-monoxide poisoning
- CO binds Hb at the Fe²⁺ site with affinity **~200–250× that of O₂** (textbook range, often quoted as ~240×; original 1912 Douglas-Haldane number ~210; the chapter uses ~200×). At 0.1% inspired CO, half of Hb is HbCO.
- **Two-pronged toxicity**:
  1. CO **occupies** Fe²⁺ → reduces total O₂-carrying capacity.
  2. CO **snaps the remaining hemes into the R state** → strong **left shift** of the dissociation curve for the residual O₂-binding sites → bright-red blood that **cannot unload at tissue P_O₂**. This is why a CO-poisoned patient is more hypoxic than an equivalent anemic patient: it is not just a capacity problem, it is an unloading problem.
- Treatment: **100% O₂** (drives CO off by mass action, accelerates clearance from t½ ~5 h on room air to ~80 min on 100% O₂); **hyperbaric O₂** (drops t½ to ~20 min and saturates dissolved plasma O₂ as a CO-independent O₂ source).
- **Pulse oximetry cannot detect CO poisoning** (HbCO and HbO₂ have similar absorbance spectra in the red and infrared used by standard oximetry — Box 29-2). A multi-wavelength **co-oximeter** is required.
- **NO and H₂S** also bind Fe²⁺ and snap Hb into R state; physiological NO-Hb interactions are reviewed in Note N29-5.

### Equations (Section 1)

- **Henry's law for dissolved O₂:** $[O_2]_{\text{pls}} = k_{O_2} \cdot P_{O_2}$, with $k_{O_2} \approx 0.003$ mL O₂/(dL·mm Hg) (Eq. 29-1).
- **Worked dissolved-O₂ delivery:** $\dot Q \cdot \Delta[O_2]_{\text{pls}} \approx 15$ mL O₂/min (Eq. 29-3); vs. 250 mL O₂/min demand → 17-fold gap that drives the need for Hb.
- **Maximal O₂ bound to Hb:** $(O_2 \text{ capacity of Hb}) \cdot [\text{Hb}] \approx 20.3$ mL O₂/dL at 15 g Hb/dL (Eq. 29-4).
- **% saturation:** $\frac{\text{bound O}_2}{\text{capacity}} \cdot 100$ (Eq. 29-5).
- **Full arterial O₂ content:** $C_aO_2 = 1.34 \cdot [\text{Hb}] \cdot S_aO_2 + 0.003 \cdot P_aO_2$ (the clinical "CaO₂" formula used in ICU oxygen-delivery calculations).
- **Hill cooperativity (phenomenological):** $Y = \frac{P_{O_2}^n}{P_{50}^n + P_{O_2}^n}$ with $P_{50} \approx 27$ mm Hg and $n \approx 2.7$–3 for adult HbA.
- **Fick reconciliation:** $\dot V_{O_2} = \dot Q \cdot \Delta C_{a-\bar v}O_2 \approx 235$ mL O₂/min (Eq. 29-7).
- **H⁺ buffering by Hb:** Hb + H⁺ ⇌ Hb-H⁺ (Eq. 29-8); coupling of H⁺ binding to O₂ release (Eq. 29-9).
- **Carbamino formation cascade:** Hb-NH₃⁺ ⇌ Hb-NH₂ ⇌ Hb-NH-COO⁻ (Eq. 29-10); coupling to O₂ release (Eq. 29-11).
- **2,3-DPG-driven O₂ release:** Hb(O₂)₄ + 2,3-DPG ⇌ Hb(2,3-DPG) + 4 O₂ (Eq. 29-12).

### Box 29-1 — Forms of Hemoglobin (pp. 648–649)
- **Developmental forms**. Three α-like genes (ζ, 2× α) on chr 16; five β-like genes (ε, 2× γ, δ, β) on chr 11. The four prenatal hemoglobins (Table 29-1) are made first in yolk sac (embryonic Gower 1/2, Portland), then in liver + spleen from ~10 wk onward (HbF, α₂γ₂). HbF is **left-shifted relative to HbA** because γ chains poorly bind 2,3-DPG → essential for **placental O₂ extraction**.
- **HbF persistence**. Newborn blood is mixed HbA/HbF; HbF falls to <1–2% by 1 year. Severe erythroid stress (hemolysis, marrow failure, post-transplant) may transiently raise HbF in adults; hereditary persistence of HbF (HPFH) is benign.
- **HbA₂ (α₂δ₂)** — δ chains reduce sickling of HbS; clinically the HbA₂ level is used to diagnose β-thalassemia trait.
- **Glycated hemoglobins** — HbA₁a, A₁b, A₁c form **non-enzymatically and irreversibly** from intracellular glucose-6-phosphate reacting with the β-chain N-terminal amine. Because RBC lifespan ≈ 120 days, HbA₁c is a **time-averaged glucose biomarker** for diabetes management; rises to ≥10% of total Hb in poor glycemic control.
- **HbS (sickle hemoglobin)** — single point mutation, **Glu6Val** on the β chain. Oxygenated HbS has normal solubility; **deoxygenated HbS is half as soluble** as deoxy-HbA → polymerizes into long fibers in low-P_O₂ environments → sickle-shaped cells → vaso-occlusion ("sickle cell crisis": pain, renal dysfunction, retinal bleeding, aseptic necrosis), and shortened RBC lifespan (<20 days) → chronic hemolytic anemia.
- **HbC** (mentioned in scope) — Glu6Lys on β chain; milder than HbS; HbSC compound heterozygotes have a moderate hemolytic syndrome.

### Box 29-2 — Pulse oximetry (p. 651)
- HbO₂ and deoxy-Hb have **different absorbance spectra in red (660 nm) and infrared (940 nm)** wavelengths.
- The oximeter shines both wavelengths through a pulsating vascular bed (fingertip, ear, toe), measures the **pulsatile** component of transmitted light (which arises only from arterial blood; the steady component is from stationary tissue + capillary/venous blood), and computes S_aO₂ from the red/IR pulsatile-absorbance ratio.
- Limits and pitfalls: **does not work in peripheral vasoconstriction** (no pulsation); **does not detect CO poisoning** (HbCO ≈ HbO₂ optically); fails in nail polish; degraded by motion and bright ambient light. Standard ICU and outpatient monitor; widely used to screen for nocturnal hypoxemia in sleep apnea (Box 32-5) and by general-aviation pilots for altitude hypoxia.

## Section 2 — Carriage of CO₂ (pp. 654–659)

### Subsection headings (verbatim)
- **Blood carries "total CO₂" mainly as HCO₃⁻** (p. 655)
- **CO₂ transport depends critically on carbonic anhydrase, the Cl⁻-HCO₃⁻ exchanger, and Hb** (pp. 655–657)
- **The high P_O₂ in the lungs causes the blood to dump CO₂** (pp. 657–658)
- **The O₂-CO₂ diagram describes the interaction of P_O₂ and P_CO₂ in the blood** (pp. 658–659)

### Core claims

#### The five forms of "total CO₂"
The Van Slyke assay (introduced in the 1920s and still the basis of clinical HCO₃⁻ measurement) cannot distinguish among the five chemical pools below — hence the umbrella label "total CO₂."

1. **Dissolved CO₂** — obeys Henry's law (P_CO₂ in the millimolar range at physiological P_CO₂; CO₂ is ~20× more soluble than O₂, with Henry's coefficient $k_{CO_2} \approx 0.07$ mL CO₂/(dL·mm Hg) at 37°C, ≈ 0.03 mmol/L/mm Hg). Contributes **~5%** of arterial total CO₂ (gold portion of bar **a** in Fig. 29-8).
2. **Carbonic acid (H₂CO₃)** — equilibrium constant for CO₂ + H₂O ⇌ H₂CO₃ is only ~0.0025, so [H₂CO₃] is only ~1/400 of [CO₂]. **Quantitatively negligible.**
3. **Bicarbonate (HCO₃⁻)** — formed three ways: H₂CO₃ → H⁺ + HCO₃⁻; CO₂ + OH⁻ → HCO₃⁻ (carbonic-anhydrase-catalyzed); or CO₃²⁻ + H⁺. At pH 7.4 and P_CO₂ = 40, [HCO₃⁻]_arterial ≈ **24 mM** → ~**90%** of total CO₂ (purple portion of bar **a**).
4. **Carbonate (CO₃²⁻)** — pK_a of HCO₃⁻/CO₃²⁻ ≈ 10.3, so [CO₃²⁻] is ~1/1000 of [HCO₃⁻] at pH 7.4. **Quantitatively negligible.**
5. **Carbamino compounds** — by far the most important is **carbamino-Hb (Hb-NH-COO⁻)**, formed by reaction of CO₂ with unprotonated α-amino groups on globin chains (Eq. 29-10). Contributes ~**5%** of arterial total CO₂ (blue portion of bar **a**).

**Total CO₂ arterial**: ~26 mM ≈ 48 mL CO₂/dL at STP.

#### Tissue → blood CO₂ partitioning (Fig. 29-9)
Mixed-venous total CO₂ ≈ 52 mL/dL, so the **incremental** ΔCO₂ added by tissues ≈ **4 mL/dL**. The partitioning of that increment is **not the same as the static partition of total CO₂** — it is dominated by reactions inside the RBC:

| Fate of incremental CO₂ | Fraction | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Dissolved CO₂ in plasma | ~6% | plasma |
| Carbamino with plasma proteins | trace | plasma |
| HCO₃⁻ formed in plasma | ~5% | plasma |
| Dissolved CO₂ in RBC | ~4% | erythrocyte |
| **Carbamino-Hb** | **~21%** | erythrocyte |
| **HCO₃⁻ formed in RBC** | **~64%** | erythrocyte |
| **Total** | **100%** | |

Summed by chemical species: **~10% dissolved, ~21% carbamino, ~69% HCO₃⁻** of the incremental CO₂.

**Three concerted enzymatic / transport mechanisms make the RBC the dominant CO₂-handling compartment:**

- **Carbonic anhydrase (CA I and CA II)** in the RBC cytosol catalyze CO₂ + H₂O ⇌ H⁺ + HCO₃⁻ at ~10⁶ s⁻¹ (CA II is among the fastest known enzymes; Ch 18, p. 434). Without it, equilibration would be far too slow during the ~1 s transit through a systemic capillary.
- **AE1 (band 3) Cl⁻/HCO₃⁻ exchanger** — the most abundant RBC membrane protein (~10⁶ copies/cell), ~50,000 ions/s turnover. At the tissue capillary, it **exports HCO₃⁻ to plasma in exchange for Cl⁻** entering the RBC. This is the **chloride / Hamburger shift**: it relieves intracellular HCO₃⁻ accumulation so CA can keep generating more, and it shuttles the bulk of the buffered CO₂ load into the plasma compartment. Inhibited by **DIDS** (experimental probe). Reverses in the pulmonary capillary.
- **Hb as buffer**. Each HCO₃⁻ formed leaves behind an H⁺. **Deoxy-Hb is a substantially better H⁺ buffer than oxy-Hb** (because deoxygenation raises the pK of Hb's titratable groups, especially the proximal-histidine and N-terminal residues). H⁺ is absorbed into Hb-H⁺, pulling the CA reaction further to the right.

**RBC swells during tissue transit** (HCO₃⁻ out, Cl⁻ in; H₂O follows osmotically as HCO₃⁻ forms inside) and **shrinks during pulmonary transit** (reverse all of the above).

#### The Haldane effect — O₂ binding right-shifts the CO₂ dissociation curve (Fig. 29-10)
- **Definition.** At any P_CO₂, total CO₂ content is **higher** when Hb is desaturated (low S_O₂) and **lower** when Hb is saturated. In CO₂-dissociation-curve language: the curve **shifts up** as S_O₂ falls.
- **Physiological consequence.** As blood enters systemic capillaries and unloads O₂, the CO₂-carrying capacity **rises** → blood picks up extra CO₂. As blood enters pulmonary capillaries and loads O₂, the CO₂-carrying capacity **falls** → blood dumps extra CO₂. The Haldane effect is the **flip side of the Bohr effect**: the same coupling that makes H⁺ binding favor O₂ release at low P_O₂ also makes O₂ binding favor H⁺ release at high P_O₂.
- **Two mechanistic strands** (mirror images of Eqs 29-9 and 29-11):
  1. **H⁺ side**. O₂ binding destabilizes Hb-H⁺ → releases H⁺ → by mass action drives CO₂ + H₂O ← HCO₃⁻ + H⁺ to the left, releasing CO₂.
  2. **Carbamino side**. O₂ binding destabilizes Hb-NH-COO⁻ → releases CO₂ directly.
- **Magnitude (worked from Fig. 29-10 inset)**. At P_CO₂ = 46 mm Hg (mixed-venous):
  - Without the Haldane effect (staying on the arterial / S_O₂ = 97.5% red curve): CO₂ content would rise by only ~2.7 mL/dL above the arterial value.
  - With the Haldane effect (moving onto the mixed-venous / S_O₂ = 75% purple curve): CO₂ content rises by ~4 mL/dL — **~50% more carriage** at the same ΔP_CO₂.
  - Equivalently, without the Haldane effect, mixed-venous P_CO₂ would have to climb to ~49 mm Hg to carry the same 4 mL/dL.

#### Why CO₂ dissociation curves are so much steeper than O₂ (Fig. 29-10)
Three features:
1. **Near-linear** physiological range (40–50 mm Hg P_CO₂), in contrast to the highly nonlinear sigmoid of Hb-O₂.
2. **Up-shift with decreasing P_O₂** (the Haldane effect, above) — the family of three curves shown are at S_O₂ = 0% (blue, top), 75% (purple, middle), 97.5% (red, bottom).
3. **Steepness**. P_CO₂ needs to climb only **6 mm Hg** (40 → 46) to load 4 mL CO₂/dL — vs. P_O₂ falling **60 mm Hg** (100 → 40) to unload ~5 mL O₂/dL. Consequence: **arterial P_CO₂ is a much tighter marker of ventilation than arterial P_O₂** — a small ventilation deficit moves P_CO₂ a lot before P_O₂ moves enough to be detected.

CO₂ partitioning equations:

$$P_{CO_2} = k_{CO_2}^{-1} \cdot [CO_2]_{\text{dis}}$$

$$\text{pH} = \text{pK}_a + \log\frac{[HCO_3^-]}{0.03 \cdot P_{CO_2}} \quad (\text{Henderson-Hasselbalch, Ch 28})$$

$$\text{Total CO}_2 = [CO_2]_{\text{dis}} + [HCO_3^-] + [\text{carbamino-Hb}] \quad (\approx 5\% + 90\% + 5\% \text{ arterial})$$

#### Table 29-4 — Factors changing total CO₂ carriage

| Parameter ↑ | Net effect on total CO₂ carried |
|---|---|
| **P_CO₂** | ↑ dissolved (Henry); ↑ HCO₃⁻ formation; ↑ carbamino formation |
| **[Plasma protein]** | ↑ plasma buffering → ↑ HCO₃⁻ (indirect via H⁺ consumption) |
| **Plasma pH** | ↑ HCO₃⁻ in plasma (Henderson-Hasselbalch); ↑ pH inside RBC → ↑ HCO₃⁻ and carbamino-Hb |
| **[Hb]** | ↑ carbamino-Hb directly; ↑ RBC buffering → ↑ HCO₃⁻ and carbamino-Hb |
| **P_O₂** | ↓ buffering power of Hb (inverse of pH-Bohr) → ↓ HCO₃⁻ and carbamino-Hb; ↓ carbamino-Hb (inverse of CO₂-Bohr) — **this row is the Haldane effect** |

#### The O₂–CO₂ diagram (Fig. 29-11)
The integrated diagram for blood. Two coordinate axes — **x: P_O₂ (mm Hg); y: P_CO₂ (mm Hg)** — and two families of isopleths:
- **Blue curves: lines of constant O₂ content** in blood. Each blue curve sweeps from low-P_O₂/low-P_CO₂ corner up and to the right. At any given O₂ content, raising P_CO₂ requires raising P_O₂ to compensate — i.e., the Bohr effect tilts the isopleths away from being vertical lines.
- **Red curves: lines of constant CO₂ content** in blood. Each red curve runs roughly horizontally but tilts upward as P_O₂ rises — raising P_O₂ at fixed CO₂ content requires raising P_CO₂ to compensate — i.e., the Haldane effect tilts the isopleths away from being horizontal lines.

**Three reference points** define the physiological operating triangle:
- **Point a (arterial)**: P_O₂ = 100, P_CO₂ = 40 → O₂ content 20.0 mL/dL, CO₂ content 48 mL/dL.
- **Point v̄ (mixed-venous)**: P_O₂ = 40, P_CO₂ = 46 → O₂ content 15.3 mL/dL, CO₂ content 52 mL/dL.
- **Point I (inspired air, before any gas exchange)**: P_O₂ = 150, P_CO₂ = 0 → O₂ content would correspond to fully saturated Hb if the blood reached this gas, CO₂ content = 0.

The **green curve v̄–a–I** is the locus of all possible (P_O₂, P_CO₂) combinations that normal blood can occupy during the respiratory cycle — the path traversed end-to-end during each circulation. This diagram is used again in Ch 31 to explain regional V̇/Q̇ differences (high V̇/Q̇ apical alveoli sit near point I; low V̇/Q̇ basal alveoli sit near point v̄).

### Equations (Section 2)

- **Henry's law for CO₂:** $[CO_2]_{\text{pls}} = k_{CO_2} \cdot P_{CO_2}$, with $k_{CO_2} \approx 0.03$ mmol/L/mm Hg (≈ 0.07 mL CO₂/(dL·mm Hg)).
- **CO₂ hydration / carbonic-anhydrase reaction:** $CO_2 + H_2O \xrightleftharpoons[\text{CA}]{} H_2CO_3 \xrightleftharpoons{} H^+ + HCO_3^-$ ; $K_{eq}([H_2CO_3]/[CO_2]) \approx 0.0025$.
- **Carbonate equilibrium:** $HCO_3^- \rightleftharpoons CO_3^{2-} + H^+$, pK ≈ 10.3.
- **Carbamino formation (cascade Eq. 29-10):** $Hb\text{-}NH_3^+ \rightleftharpoons Hb\text{-}NH_2 \rightleftharpoons Hb\text{-}NH\text{-}COO^-$.
- **Total CO₂ partition (arterial):** $[\text{Total CO}_2] = [CO_2]_{\text{dis}} + [HCO_3^-] + [\text{carbamino}] \approx 0.05 + 0.90 + 0.05$ of ~26 mM.
- **Henderson-Hasselbalch (Ch 28):** $\text{pH} = 6.1 + \log\frac{[HCO_3^-]}{0.03 \cdot P_{CO_2}}$.
- **Chloride shift (Hamburger):** $HCO_{3,\text{RBC}}^- + Cl_{\text{plasma}}^- \xrightleftharpoons[\text{AE1}]{} HCO_{3,\text{plasma}}^- + Cl_{\text{RBC}}^-$; reverses in the lung.

## Glossary

- **O₂ content (CaO₂, CvO₂)** — total O₂ carried per dL of blood; CaO₂ ≈ 20 mL/dL, CvO₂ ≈ 15 mL/dL. Formula: $CaO_2 = 1.34 \cdot [\text{Hb}] \cdot S_aO_2 + 0.003 \cdot P_aO_2$.
- **O₂ capacity of Hb (Hüfner's number)** — theoretical 1.39 mL O₂/g Hb; in-vivo ~1.34–1.35.
- **S_O₂ (S_aO₂, S_vO₂, SpO₂)** — Hb saturation; pulse-oximetry value is SpO₂.
- **P₅₀** — P_O₂ at half-saturation; ~27 mm Hg for adult HbA, ~19–20 for HbF; right-shifted by Bohr, T, 2,3-DPG; left-shifted by HbF, HbCO, methemoglobin, hypothermia.
- **a-v̄ difference** — arterial − mixed-venous content; ~4.7 mL O₂/dL and ~4 mL CO₂/dL at rest.
- **Henry's law** — $[X]_{\text{dis}} = k_X \cdot P_X$. $k_{O_2} \approx 0.003$, $k_{CO_2} \approx 0.07$ mL/dL/mm Hg.
- **Fick principle** — $\dot V_X = \dot Q \cdot \Delta C_{a-\bar v}X$.
- **Hemoglobin (HbA, α₂β₂)** — adult tetramer, 68 kDa. α chain 141 aa, β chain 146 aa, each with one heme.
- **Heme** — Fe²⁺-coordinating porphyrin. Visible-light absorption → arterial red, venous purple.
- **Globin family** — α-like cluster (chr 16: ζ, 2× α); β-like cluster (chr 11: ε, 2× γ, δ, β); LCR-regulated developmental switching; myoglobin lineage diverged from a primordial globin.
- **T state / R state (Monod-Wyman-Changeux)** — tense (deoxy, low affinity, domed heme) and relaxed (oxy, high affinity, planar heme); concerted all-or-none switch; ~150-fold affinity difference. Anchored by the **proximal F-helix histidine** that moves with the Fe²⁺.
- **Hill coefficient n** — empirical cooperativity index; ~2.7–3 for HbA. n=1 noncooperative; n=4 maximally cooperative.
- **Hill equation** — $Y = P_{O_2}^n / (P_{50}^n + P_{O_2}^n)$.
- **Sigmoidal Hb-O₂ dissociation curve** — operating curve of Hb in blood; shallow at low P_O₂, steep 20–60 mm Hg, plateau >70 mm Hg.
- **Bohr effect** — right shift of Hb-O₂ curve with ↑T, ↓pH (pH-Bohr, dominant), ↑P_CO₂ (CO₂-Bohr, smaller). Mechanism: H⁺ binding and carbamino formation stabilize T state.
- **Haldane effect** — up-shift of CO₂ dissociation curve with ↓P_O₂ (Hb desaturation). Mechanism: deoxy-Hb is a better H⁺ buffer and binds CO₂ as carbamino more readily.
- **2,3-DPG (2,3-bisphosphoglycerate / 2,3-BPG)** — glycolytic intermediate (DPG-mutase from 1,3-BPG); RBC concentration ~4–5 mM ≈ [Hb]; binds 1:1 in the β-β central cavity; stabilizes T state → right shift. Rises in chronic hypoxia, anemia, altitude acclimation.
- **Methemoglobin (metHb)** — Fe³⁺ Hb, cannot bind O₂. Resting ~1.5%; reduced by NADH-cytochrome-b₅-reductase (methemoglobin reductase). Methemoglobinemia: congenital reductase deficiency, or acquired from nitrites / dapsone / benzocaine. Treatment: **methylene blue** (activates NADPH-metHb reductase).
- **Carboxyhemoglobin (HbCO)** — CO bound to Fe²⁺ with ~200× O₂ affinity. Reduces O₂ capacity AND left-shifts the remaining curve so residual O₂ won't unload. Treatment: 100% O₂; hyperbaric O₂.
- **HbA₂ (α₂δ₂)** — minor adult Hb (~2.5%); δ chain reduces HbS polymerization. Used to diagnose β-thalassemia trait.
- **HbF (α₂γ₂)** — fetal Hb. Intrinsic affinity = HbA's; in vivo left-shifted because γ chain binds 2,3-DPG poorly. Essential for placental O₂ extraction.
- **HbA₁c** — non-enzymatic glycation of β-N-terminus by glucose-6-phosphate; 120-day-average glucose biomarker for diabetes.
- **HbS (sickle Hb)** — β6 Glu→Val; deoxy-HbS half as soluble as deoxy-HbA → fiber polymerization → sickling → vaso-occlusion + hemolytic anemia.
- **HbC** — β6 Glu→Lys; milder; HbSC compound heterozygotes have moderate sickling syndrome.
- **Myoglobin (Mb)** — single-globin Fe²⁺-heme protein in muscle; noncooperative; high affinity; shuttle for intracellular O₂ delivery from sarcolemmal Hb to mitochondria.
- **Pulse oximeter (SpO₂)** — red (660 nm) + IR (940 nm) absorbance ratio in pulsatile (arterial) component. Does not detect CO poisoning or methemoglobinemia.
- **Co-oximeter** — multi-wavelength oximeter that distinguishes HbO₂, HbCO, metHb, deoxy-Hb.
- **Anemia / polycythemia / cyanosis** — ↓[Hb], ↑Hct, purple skin from absolute desaturated-Hb concentration.
- **Total CO₂** — sum of all CO₂-derived species: dissolved CO₂, H₂CO₃, HCO₃⁻, CO₃²⁻, carbamino. ~26 mM ≈ 48 mL/dL arterial.
- **Carbonic anhydrase (CA I, CA II)** — RBC cytosolic enzyme catalyzing CO₂ + H₂O ⇌ H⁺ + HCO₃⁻. CA II among the fastest known enzymes. Sulfonamide-inhibitable (acetazolamide).
- **AE1 (band 3, SLC4A1)** — RBC Cl⁻/HCO₃⁻ exchanger; ~10⁶ copies/cell; supports the chloride / Hamburger shift; DIDS-inhibitable; also a major cytoskeletal anchor.
- **Chloride / Hamburger shift** — HCO₃⁻ out, Cl⁻ in (via AE1) at the tissue capillary; reverses in lung. Allows CA-driven HCO₃⁻ production to continue.
- **AQP1 / Rh complex** — RBC membrane "gas channels" for CO₂ entry; >50% of RBC CO₂ permeability.
- **Carbamino-Hb (Hb-NH-COO⁻)** — CO₂ reacting with unprotonated globin α-amino termini; ~5% arterial total CO₂, ~21% of incremental CO₂.
- **Henderson-Hasselbalch** — pH = 6.1 + log[HCO₃⁻]/(0.03 · P_CO₂) (Ch 28).
- **Van Slyke assay** — 1920s gasometric method for total CO₂; basis of modern clinical HCO₃⁻ measurement.
- **O₂-CO₂ diagram** — integrated isopleth diagram (Fig. 29-11) showing Bohr and Haldane effects as the tilt of constant-O₂ and constant-CO₂ curves respectively.
