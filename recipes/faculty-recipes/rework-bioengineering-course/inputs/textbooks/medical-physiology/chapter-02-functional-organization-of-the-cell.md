## Section 1 — Structure of biological membranes (pp. 8–13)

### Subsection headings (verbatim from source)

- **The surface of the cell is defined by a membrane** (p. 8)
- **The cell membrane is composed primarily of phospholipids** (pp. 8–10)
- **Phospholipids form complex structures in aqueous solution** (pp. 10–11)
- **Phospholipid bilayer membranes are impermeable to charged molecules** (p. 11)
- **The physical characteristics of a lipid bilayer largely depend on the chemical nature of its constituent lipids** (pp. 11–12)
- **The diffusion of individual lipids within a leaflet of a bilayer is determined by the chemical interactions among the constituent lipids** (pp. 12–13)

### Core claims

- The plasma membrane is the requisite barrier that lets a cell maintain a cytoplasmic composition orders of magnitude different from the ECF. Without it, "all of the cytoplasm's hard-won compositional uniqueness would be lost by diffusion in a few seconds." (p. 8)
- The membrane must be more than a passive barrier — it must support **active transport** (uphill movement using metabolic energy) and **regulated passive permeability**. Both are developed in Chapters 5 and 7.
- Empirically, the membrane is a **bilayer** of **amphipathic phospholipids** with hydrophilic head groups outward and hydrophobic acyl-chain tails inward.
- Phospholipids spontaneously self-assemble (monolayer at air–water → micelle → bilayer) because the bilayer geometry minimizes hydrophobic-tail exposure to water.
- Bilayer **fluidity** depends on fatty-acid **chain length**, **degree of unsaturation** (cis double bonds → kinks → looser packing → more fluid), and **cholesterol content** (rigidifies fluid bilayers, fluidizes gel-phase bilayers — buffers fluidity).
- Lipids diffuse rapidly within a leaflet ("lateral diffusion"), but **flip-flop** between leaflets is rare and energetically expensive — this asymmetry is biologically maintained.

### Citation-anchor quotes (short, RAG-ready)

- > "The chemical composition of the cell interior is very different from that of its surroundings." (p. 8)
- > "the cell also expends tremendous energy to regulate the intracellular concentrations of numerous ions." (p. 8)
- > "the membrane must be endowed with special machinery that uses metabolic energy to drive the uphill movements of substances — active transport — into or out of the cell." (p. 8)
- > "Most of the lipid associated with erythrocyte plasma membranes belongs to the molecular family of phospholipids." (p. 10)
- > "Because phospholipids combine hydrophilic heads with hydrophobic tails, their interaction with water is referred to as amphipathic." (p. 10)
- > "the membrane is prohibitive for charged atoms or molecules. Pure phospholipid bilayer membranes are extremely [impermeable to ions]." (p. 11)
- > "The temperature at which the bilayer membrane converts [between gel and fluid] depends on chain length and degree of saturation." (p. 11–12)

### Phospholipid chemistry (compact reference)

A generic phospholipid molecule:

```
[ head group ] — phosphate — glycerol — [ FA chain 1 ]
                                      \— [ FA chain 2 ]
```

Major classes encountered (Figs. 2-1 and 2-2):

| Class | Head group | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Phosphatidylethanolamine (PE) | ethanolamine | shown as the worked example in Fig. 2-1 |
| Phosphatidylinositol (PI) | inositol (sugar) | signaling precursor (PIP₂, PIP₃) — developed in Ch 3 |
| Phosphatidylserine (PS) | serine | normally cytosolic leaflet; outward flip → apoptosis signal |
| Phosphatidylcholine (PC) | choline | major outer-leaflet lipid in animal cells |
| Sphingomyelin | choline on sphingosine backbone (not glycerol) | sphingolipid; raft component |
| Galactocerebroside | galactose on sphingosine | abundant in myelin |
| Cholesterol | hydroxyl + sterol ring + short tail | fluidity buffer; only one polar OH |

### Equations / derivations

This section is primarily descriptive. The bilayer's fluidity-vs-temperature behavior is treated qualitatively here; the thermodynamics of partitioning is treated quantitatively in Chapter 5. The relevant principle when it appears in later chapters is the partition coefficient $\beta$, where the membrane permeability of a neutral solute follows

$$P = \frac{D \, \beta}{\Delta x}$$

with $D$ the diffusion coefficient in the membrane, $\Delta x$ the membrane thickness, and $\beta$ the oil/water partition coefficient. (Forward reference: Ch 5.)

## Section 2 — Function of membrane proteins (pp. 13–17)

### Subsection headings

- **Membrane proteins can be integrally or peripherally associated with the plasma membrane** (p. 13)
- **The membrane-spanning portions of transmembrane proteins are usually hydrophobic α helices** (pp. 13–14)
- **Some membrane proteins are mobile in the plane of the bilayer** (p. 14)
- **The cell membrane is closely associated with a meshwork of submembranous proteins that constitutes a specialized cytoskeleton** (pp. 15–17)

### Core claims

- Two operational classes by extraction behavior:
  - **Peripheral membrane proteins** — released from the membrane by mild treatments (high salt, pH change); they associate with the cytoplasmic surface or via electrostatic interaction with head groups.
  - **Integral membrane proteins** — released only by detergents that disrupt the bilayer.
- Three topological subclasses of integral proteins (see Fig. 2-5):
  - Single-pass transmembrane (one α helix crossing).
  - Multi-pass transmembrane (multiple crossings — e.g., GPCRs with 7 transmembrane helices).
  - **GPI-anchored** — covalently tethered to a glycosylphosphatidylinositol lipid; the protein body sits on the outer leaflet, no transmembrane segment.
- Transmembrane segments are typically **20–25 residues of hydrophobic α helix** because that span matches the bilayer thickness and an α-helix neutralizes the polar backbone via internal H-bonds.
- Lateral diffusion of membrane proteins is real but **restricted**: cytoskeletal corrals, lipid rafts (cholesterol- and sphingolipid-rich domains), and direct cytoskeletal tethering all slow lateral mobility well below pure-lipid expectations.
- The **submembranous cytoskeleton** (best characterized in erythrocytes: spectrin/actin/ankyrin/band 4.1) tethers the bilayer and patterns protein localization.

### Citation-anchor quotes

- > "Membrane proteins can be integrally or peripherally associated with the plasma membrane." (p. 13)
- > "Peripherally associated membrane proteins are neither dissolved within the lipid bilayer nor covalently attached to molecules that are." (p. 13)
- > "the membrane-spanning portions of transmembrane proteins are usually hydrophobic α helices." (p. 13)
- > "A third group of membrane-associated proteins is not actually embedded in the bilayer at all. Instead, these lipid-anchored proteins are attached to the membrane by a covalent bond that links them either to a lipid component of the membrane or to a fatty acid derivative that intercalates into the membrane." (p. 13)

## Section 3 — The cytoskeleton, organelles, and the cytoplasm (pp. 17–24)

### Subsection headings

- **The cytoplasm is not amorphous but is organized by the cytoskeleton** (p. 17)
- (Subsections, in order:) **Microtubules**, **Microfilaments (actin)**, **Intermediate filaments**, **Thick filaments (myosin)**
- **The nucleus stores, replicates, and reads the cell's genetic information** (pp. 20–21)
- **Lysosomes digest material derived from the interior and exterior of the cell** (pp. 21–22)
- **The mitochondrion is the site of oxidative energy production** (pp. 22–23)
- **Peroxisomes contain enzymes involved in oxidative reactions** (p. 23)
- **The endoplasmic reticulum is the site of protein synthesis and membrane assembly** (pp. 23–24)
- **The Golgi complex processes newly synthesized proteins for delivery** (p. 24)

### Core claims

- The **cytoskeleton** is a dynamic protein scaffold giving the cytoplasm internal order and mechanical properties.
- Three principal polymer systems (Table 2-2):

| Polymer | Subunit | Diameter | Polarity | Principal roles |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Microtubules** | α/β tubulin heterodimer | ~25 nm hollow tube | yes (+ end grows faster) | mitotic spindle, ciliary axoneme, intracellular transport tracks for kinesin/dynein |
| **Microfilaments** | G-actin → F-actin | ~7 nm | yes (+/barbed end grows faster) | cortical mesh, lamellipodia, contractile rings, myosin tracks |
| **Intermediate filaments** | many tissue-specific (keratin, vimentin, desmin, neurofilaments, lamins) | ~10 nm | no | mechanical tensile strength; nuclear lamina |

- **Thick filaments** are myosin polymers (not a separate cytoskeletal class but a partner of actin), assembled antiparallel.
- The **nucleus** is enveloped by two membranes continuous at **nuclear pore complexes (NPCs)** that mediate regulated bidirectional transport. The outer nuclear membrane is continuous with the **rough ER**.
- The **mitochondrion** has an outer membrane (permeable to small solutes via porins), an inner membrane (impermeable barrier, site of the electron-transport chain and ATP synthase, folded into **cristae**), an **intermembrane space**, and a **matrix** (TCA cycle, fatty-acid β-oxidation, mtDNA). Mitochondria have their own circular DNA and ribosomes — relic of an endosymbiotic origin.
- The **MICU/MCU** Ca²⁺ uniporter on the inner membrane couples cytosolic Ca²⁺ signals to mitochondrial bioenergetics (referenced p. 22 in passing — developed in Ch 5/9).
- **Peroxisomes** house catalase and other oxidases; they handle very-long-chain fatty-acid β-oxidation and detoxify H₂O₂.
- **Lysosomes** are acidified (V-ATPase) organelles containing acid hydrolases for degrading endocytic and autophagic cargo.
- **ER**: rough ER = ribosomes attached, site of co-translational insertion of secretory and membrane proteins; smooth ER = lipid synthesis, drug metabolism, Ca²⁺ store (especially in muscle as sarcoplasmic reticulum).
- **Golgi**: cis → medial → trans cisternae carry glycoprotein maturation and sorting.

### Citation-anchor quotes

- > "The cytoplasm is not amorphous but is organized by the cytoskeleton." (p. 17)
- > "the nucleus is a cell's repository for its complement of chromosomal DNA." (p. 21)
- > "Lysosomes digest material derived from the interior and exterior of the cell." (p. 21)
- > "The mitochondrion is the site of oxidative energy production." (p. 22)
- > "the inner mitochondrial membrane contains the proteins that constitute the electron transport chain." (p. 22)

## Section 4 — Synthesis and recycling of membrane proteins (pp. 24–36)

### Subsection headings

- **Newly synthesized secretory and membrane proteins are processed in the rough ER and Golgi** (p. 24)
- **Signal peptides target proteins to the rough ER for co-translational insertion** (pp. 24–25)
- **Post-translational modifications occur in the ER and Golgi lumen** (pp. 25–26)
- **The secretory pathway moves proteins from the ER to the plasma membrane via the Golgi** (pp. 26–28)
- **Vesicle formation and fusion are mediated by coat proteins (clathrin, COPI, COPII) and SNARE proteins** (pp. 28–32)
- **Endocytosis returns membrane and cargo from the plasma membrane to internal compartments** (pp. 32–35)
- **Clinical correlation: the LDL receptor pathway** (clinical box, pp. 34–35)

### Core claims

- **Co-translational insertion (Fig. 2-16):**
  - Ribosomes initiate cytosolic synthesis.
  - An **N-terminal signal peptide** is recognized by the **signal recognition particle (SRP)**, which arrests translation.
  - SRP–ribosome docks at the **SRP receptor** on the rough ER, then the nascent chain is threaded into the **Sec61 translocon**.
  - For secreted proteins, the chain passes fully through the translocon into the ER lumen; signal peptidase cleaves the signal.
  - For membrane proteins, **stop-transfer sequences** (hydrophobic α helices) laterally release the chain into the bilayer, anchoring it.
- **Post-translational modifications (Fig. 2-17):**
  - N-linked glycosylation (preformed dolichol-linked oligosaccharide transferred to Asn-X-Ser/Thr in ER lumen).
  - Disulfide-bond formation (PDI; ER lumen).
  - O-linked glycosylation, complex sugar trimming, sialylation, sulfation — all in Golgi.
  - GPI anchor attachment in ER lumen.
- **Vesicular trafficking (Figs. 2-18, 2-19):**
  - **COPII** vesicles bud from ER → cis-Golgi (anterograde).
  - **COPI** vesicles recycle Golgi → ER and intra-Golgi (retrograde).
  - **Clathrin** mediates trans-Golgi → secretory/lysosomal vesicles and plasma-membrane endocytic vesicles.
  - **SNAREs** mediate fusion: a **v-SNARE** on the vesicle pairs with a cognate **t-SNARE** on the target membrane; the four-helix "trans-SNARE complex" pulls the two bilayers together.
  - **NSF + SNAPs** disassemble cis-SNARE complexes after fusion, recycling the components; **ATP** hydrolysis powers the disassembly.
  - **Rab GTPases** confer compartment identity and direct vesicle docking.
- **Endocytosis** flavors:
  - **Receptor-mediated** (clathrin-coated pits) — e.g., LDL receptor; **transferrin** receptor.
  - **Caveolae-mediated** — small flask-shaped pits enriched in caveolin and cholesterol, raft-like.
  - **Macropinocytosis** and **phagocytosis** — large-volume actin-driven uptake.
- **Sorting in endosomes:** early endosomes acidify → ligand-receptor dissociation → recycling endosomes return receptor to surface, late endosomes/multivesicular bodies → lysosomes for degradation.

### Citation-anchor quotes

- > "the ribosomes that are bound to the rough ER membrane are engaged in synthesizing proteins that will be either secreted from the cell or inserted into a cellular membrane." (p. 24)
- > "Clathrin mediates the formation of secretory vesicles that bud off from the trans Golgi as well as the internalization of membrane from the cell surface during the process of endocytosis." (Fig. 2-19 caption, p. 30)
- > "A complex of proteins forms a bridge between the vesicle and the target membrane. ATP provides the fuel for fusion. The Rab appears to be a molecular switch." (Fig. 2-19, p. 30)

### Equations / kinetic principles

The chapter is descriptive in this section. Quantitative treatments referenced forward:
- **Mass-action in receptor binding** $\;[R\cdot L] = R_T\,\frac{[L]}{K_d + [L]}\;$ (Hill/saturation) — full treatment in Ch 3.
- **First-order endocytic internalization** $\;\frac{d[\text{receptor on surface}]}{dt} = -k_{\text{int}}[R_s] + k_{\text{rec}}[R_i]\;$ at steady state.

The condition is the canonical demonstration that receptor cytoplasmic-tail sorting motifs are required for endocytic uptake.

## Section 5 — Specialized cell types (pp. 36–46)

### Subsection headings

- **Specialized cell types** (sectioning header, p. 43)
- **Epithelial cells form a barrier between the internal and external milieu** (pp. 43–44)
- **Junctional complexes link epithelial cells into sheets: tight junctions, adherens junctions, desmosomes, hemidesmosomes** (pp. 44–46)
- **Gap junctions provide regulated cytoplasmic continuity between cells** (p. 46)

### Core claims

- All cells share the same basic chemistry but tissue specialization is encoded by differential expression of a common gene complement.
- **Epithelial polarity:**
  - **Apical** membrane faces the lumen / external milieu — different transporter, channel, and lipid composition.
  - **Basolateral** membrane faces the ECF / internal milieu.
  - The two domains are kept compositionally distinct by the **tight junction** acting as a **fence** within the bilayer (prevents lateral diffusion across the junction) and a **gate** between cells (controls paracellular permeability).
- **Junctional repertoire:**
  - **Tight junction (zonula occludens)** — apical; claudins, occludin; sealing strand network in freeze-fracture EM.
  - **Adherens junction (zonula adherens)** — E-cadherin (homotypic Ca²⁺-dependent), linked to actin cytoskeleton via β/α catenin.
  - **Desmosome (macula adherens)** — desmoglein/desmocollin cadherins, linked to **intermediate filaments** (keratins in epithelia) via plakoglobin and desmoplakin.
  - **Hemidesmosome** — basal; α6β4 integrin engaging laminin in the basal lamina, linked to keratin filaments.
  - **Gap junction** — six **connexins** form a **connexon hemichannel**; cell-cell pairing produces a regulated aqueous pore permitting passage of ions and small (<~1 kDa) metabolites (cAMP, IP₃, glucose).
- **Polarized transport** as physiological consequence: vectorial Na⁺, glucose, and ion movement across epithelia (Ch 5) is built on this asymmetric protein distribution.

### Citation-anchor quotes

- > "Epithelial cells form a barrier between the internal and external milieu." (p. 43)
- > "the membranes that face the lumen of the duct (the apical membrane) are different from those that face the blood (the basolateral membrane)." (p. 43)
- > "Tight junctions [are] a complex structure that impedes the passage of molecules and ions between the cells of the epithelial monolayer." (p. 43)
- > "Epithelial cells are connected to one another by a variety of junctional complexes [that] also serve as organizing centers that orchestrate the formation of cell-cell contacts." (p. 43)

## Glossary (downstream-chunkable)

- **Amphipathic** — has both hydrophilic and hydrophobic regions.
- **Phospholipid** — diacylglycerol or sphingosine backbone + phosphate + head group; major bilayer constituent.
- **Bilayer** — two-leaflet phospholipid sheet, the basic plasma-membrane architecture.
- **Lipid raft** — cholesterol- and sphingolipid-enriched mobile microdomain.
- **Peripheral membrane protein** — released by non-detergent treatments; surface-associated.
- **Integral membrane protein** — released only by detergent; transmembrane or lipid-anchored.
- **GPI anchor** — glycosylphosphatidylinositol; covalent outer-leaflet lipid tether.
- **Cytoskeleton** — microtubules + microfilaments + intermediate filaments.
- **Microtubule** — α/β tubulin polymer; 25 nm; polar; tracks for kinesin/dynein.
- **Microfilament** — F-actin; 7 nm; polar; tracks for myosin.
- **Intermediate filament** — apolar 10-nm filament; tissue-specific subunit; mechanical strength.
- **Nuclear pore complex (NPC)** — gated bidirectional transporter spanning the nuclear envelope.
- **Mitochondrion** — double-membrane oxidative ATP organelle with own DNA.
- **Peroxisome** — single-membrane organelle housing catalase and very-long-chain FA β-oxidation.
- **Lysosome** — V-ATPase acidified organelle housing acid hydrolases.
- **Rough ER / smooth ER** — protein synthesis and lipid synthesis compartments.
- **Golgi (cis/medial/trans)** — glycan maturation and sorting compartments.
- **Signal peptide / signal recognition particle (SRP) / SRP receptor / Sec61 translocon** — the co-translational ER-insertion machinery.
- **Stop-transfer sequence** — hydrophobic helix that aborts translocation and anchors a membrane protein.
- **COPI / COPII / Clathrin** — three vesicle-coat systems for distinct trafficking steps.
- **SNARE (v- and t-)** — fusion zippers; trans-SNARE complex pulls membranes together.
- **NSF / α-SNAP** — ATPase machinery that disassembles cis-SNARE complexes.
- **Rab GTPase** — compartment-identity / docking molecular switch.
- **Endosome (early/recycling/late) / multivesicular body / lysosome** — endocytic sorting compartments.
- **LDL receptor** — clinical anchor for clathrin-mediated endocytosis.
- **Apical / basolateral** — opposite membrane domains of an epithelial cell.
- **Tight junction** — apical sealing junction; gate + fence; claudins/occludin.
- **Adherens junction** — E-cadherin–actin junction.
- **Desmosome / hemidesmosome** — intermediate-filament-coupled spot welds (cell-cell / cell-matrix).
- **Gap junction** — connexin connexon pair forming a regulated cytoplasmic pore.
