## Section 1 — Synthesis of thyroid hormones (pp. 1006–1009)

### Subsection headings
- **(opener) Follicular anatomy, colloid, and the unique features of thyroid hormone** (pp. 1006)
- **T₄ and T₃, made by iodination of tyrosine residues on thyroglobulin, are stored as part of thyroglobulin molecules in thyroid follicles** (pp. 1006–1008)
- **Follicular cells take up iodinated thyroglobulin, hydrolyze it, and release T₄ and T₃ into the blood for binding to plasma proteins** (pp. 1008–1009)

### Core claims
- The thyroid (~20 g in adults; left and right lobes joined by an isthmus) sits across the front of the trachea. It is the only endocrine gland routinely palpable on exam, the only one requiring a trace element (iodine) for active hormone, and the only one that stores hormone **extracellularly** — as **colloid** within follicles.
- The follicle is a sphere of **follicular cells** surrounding a central **colloid** lumen. The major colloid protein is **thyroglobulin (Tg)**, a very large (>600 kDa) glycoprotein that carries the hormones T₄ (thyroxine, tetraiodothyronine) and T₃ (triiodothyronine) as part of its primary structure. Tg has ~100 tyrosyl residues, of which <20 are iodinated.
- **C cells (parafollicular cells)** are not part of the follicle; they make **calcitonin** (discussed pp. 1067–1068), not thyroid hormone.
- Unlike peptide or single-amino-acid hormones, T₄/T₃ have **no cell-membrane receptors**; like steroids they act through **nuclear receptors** (see pp. 71–72) to regulate transcription.
- **Synthesis steps** (all follicular-cell, summarized in Fig. 49-3):
  1. **Iodide trapping**: the **Na/I cotransporter (symporter) NIS (SLC5A5)** on the **basolateral** membrane moves 2 Na⁺ + 1 I⁻ into the cell **against** the I⁻ electrochemical gradient, energized by the inward Na⁺ gradient. NIS is a 65-kDa protein with ~13 membrane-spanning segments. Competing anions: perchlorate, pertechnetate, thiocyanate.
  2. **Efflux into colloid**: I⁻ leaves apically into the lumen, probably via the **Cl-I exchanger pendrin (SLC26A4)**. Pendrin mutations → congenital goiter + hearing loss (deficient I⁻ delivery to colloid).
  3. **Organification**: secretory vesicles deliver Tg + the enzyme **thyroid peroxidase (TPO)** to the apical membrane. TPO oxidizes I⁻ → I⁰ (atomic iodine) using **H₂O₂ supplied by dual-oxidase 2 (DUOX2)**, then incorporates one or two oxidized iodines into specific tyrosyl residues of Tg → **MIT (monoiodotyrosine)** and **DIT (diiodotyrosine)**.
  4. **Coupling**: TPO (with H₂O₂) couples two iodinated tyrosyls within Tg to form one iodothyronine + a remnant **dehydroalanine**. DIT + DIT → **T₄**; MIT + DIT → **T₃**; an alternate coupling yields **rT₃**. Coupling does not occur unless the tyrosyls are iodinated.
  5. **Endocytosis**: follicular cells resorb colloid Tg by fluid-phase endocytosis; the endocytic vesicle fuses with lysosomes → **lysoendosome**.
  6. **Proteolysis**: lysosomal enzymes hydrolyze Tg, releasing T₄, T₃, and free DIT/MIT.
  7. **Secretion / deiodinative recycling**: T₄ and T₃ exit basolaterally into blood (mechanism partly via **MCT8 / SLC16A2**). ~**90% of secreted hormone is T₄, ~10% T₃** (≈10:1). Liberated MIT/DIT are **deiodinated intracellularly to recycle their iodide and tyrosine** (not released).
- **Transport in blood**: both T₄ and T₃ are >99% protein-bound. **Thyroxine-binding globulin (TBG)** (54 kDa, 450 aa, highest affinity), **transthyretin (TTR)**, and **albumin** carry them. **>99.98% of T₄** and **~99.5% of T₃** circulate bound; only the **free** fraction is active. Binding buffers minute-to-minute levels and **prolongs half-life** (T₄ ≈ 8 days, T₃ ≈ 24 h — both longer than peptide/steroid hormones). The liver makes all three binding proteins.
- TBG quantity (not free hormone) shifts with physiological state: ↑ TBG in pregnancy, oral estrogen, hepatitis, chronic heroin use; ↓ TBG with androgens and nephrotic syndrome. Free T₄/T₃ stay constant despite these total-hormone swings.

### Equations (Box 49-2 — free vs. bound T₄)
- TBG binding equilibrium:

  $$\mathrm{T_4 + TBG \rightleftharpoons T_4\!\cdot\!TBG}, \qquad K = \frac{[\mathrm{T_4\!\cdot\!TBG}]}{[\mathrm{T_4}]\,[\mathrm{TBG}]}$$

  with $K \approx 2\times10^{10}\ \mathrm{M^{-1}}$ for T₄ and $\approx 5\times10^{9}\ \mathrm{M^{-1}}$ for T₃.
- Free T₄ from the bound complex and free carrier:

  $$[\mathrm{T_4}]_{\text{free}} = \frac{[\mathrm{T_4\!\cdot\!TBG}]}{K\,[\mathrm{TBG}]}$$

  Worked example: $[\mathrm{T_4\!\cdot\!TBG}]\approx 100\ \mathrm{nM}$, $[\mathrm{TBG}]\approx 250\ \mathrm{nM}$ ⟹ $[\mathrm{T_4}]_{\text{free}} \approx 20\ \mathrm{pM}$, i.e. only **~0.02%** of total T₄ is free. (Minor albumin/TTR contributions omitted.)

- **⑤ Endocytosis** — TSH ↑ endocytosis of iodinated Tg from colloid; vesicle fuses with lysosome → **lysoendosome**.
- **⑥ Proteolysis** — TSH ↑ proteolysis of iodinated Tg in the lysoendosome, forming T₄ and T₃ (and freeing MIT/DIT). A cytoplasmic **deiodinase** converts some T₄ → T₃; freed MIT/DIT are deiodinated for **iodide recycling**.
- **⑦ Secretion** — TSH ↑ secretion of T₄ and T₃ into the circulation (basolateral; **MCT8 (SLC16A2)** transporter shown).
- **Hyperplasia** — TSH growth-factor effect on the follicular cells.

## Section 2 — Peripheral deiodination of T₄ (pp. 1009–1010)

### Subsection headings
- **Peripheral tissues deiodinate T₄ to produce T₃** (pp. 1009–1010)

### Core claims
- The thyroid secretes mostly T₄, but **~three-fourths of circulating T₃ comes from peripheral conversion of T₄**, chiefly in **liver and kidney**. Athyreotic patients on oral T₄ have normal circulating T₃.
- Three **deiodinases** govern activation/inactivation:
  - **Type 1 (D1, 5′/3′-outer-ring)** — high in **liver, kidney, skeletal muscle, thyroid**; generates **most of the circulating T₃**.
  - **Type 2 (D2, 5′/3′-outer-ring)** — **pituitary, CNS, placenta**; makes **local T₃** for those tissues. The pituitary D2-derived T₃ drives **feedback inhibition of TSH**.
  - **Type 3 (D3, 5/3-inner-ring)** — removes an inner-ring iodine, converting T₄ → inactive **rT₃** (and T₃ → inactive diiodothyronine).
- Outer-ring removal (D1/D2) on T₄ yields **T₃**; inner-ring removal (D3) yields **rT₃**. Sequential deiodination by any enzyme ultimately yields **T₂** (thyronine). DIT/MIT and the fully-deiodinated thyronine are biologically inactive; both inner-ring iodines + ≥1 outer-ring iodine, plus the amino group, are required for activity.
- **Caloric restriction / severe stress** inhibits **D1** → ↓ T₄→T₃ conversion → ↓ plasma T₃ and ↑ rT₃ (by default), with a fall in metabolic rate. Because **D2 in pituitary/CNS is unaffected** by caloric restriction, intra-pituitary T₃ stays normal, so **no compensatory TSH rise** occurs — teleologically, this restrains energy expenditure during low intake.

### Citation-anchor quotes
- > "Approximately three fourths of circulating T₃ arises from the peripheral conversion of T₄, which occurs principally in the liver and kidneys." (p. 1008)
- > "Two deiodinases are 5′/3′-deiodinases that remove an I from the outer ring and thereby convert T₄ to T₃." (p. 1010)
- > "A third 5/3-deiodinase—type 3 deiodinase—removes an I from the inner ring, thereby converting T₄ to the inactive rT₃." (p. 1010)
- > "Caloric restriction or severe stress inhibits the type 1 deiodinase; this process decreases the conversion of T₄ to T₃—and thus reduces the levels of T₃." (p. 1010)

### Figures

#### Figure 49-4 — Peripheral metabolism of T₄ *(viewed)*
Reaction map of sequential deiodination. **Green arrows** = the **5′/3′-monodeiodinases (types 1 and 2)** removing an **outer-ring** iodine (T₄ → T₃). **Orange arrows** = the **5/3-monodeiodinase (type 3)** removing an **inner-ring** iodine (T₄ → rT₃). Both T₃ and rT₃ undergo further deiodination to DIT/MIT-type intermediates and finally to **T₀ (thyronine)**.

## Section 3 — Action of thyroid hormones (pp. 1010–1013)

### Subsection headings
- **Thyroid hormones act through nuclear receptors in target tissues** (pp. 1010–1011)
- **Thyroid hormones can also act by nongenomic pathways** (p. 1011)
- **Thyroid hormones increase basal metabolic rate by stimulating futile cycles of catabolism and anabolism** (pp. 1011–1013)
- **Thyroid hormones are essential for normal growth and development** (pp. 1013)

### Core claims
- T₄/T₃ enter cells by lipid diffusion or carrier transport (e.g. **MCT8**). Inside, a cytoplasmic **5′/3′-monodeiodinase** converts much of the T₄ → T₃, so cytoplasmic T₄ and T₃ are roughly equal.
- **Nuclear thyroid hormone receptors (TRs)**: two genes — **TRα (chr 17)** and **TRβ (chr 3)** (≥2 TRβ isoforms). Expression is tissue-specific and developmentally timed: **liver → TRβ; brain → TRα**. TRs bind DNA at **thyroid response elements (TREs)**, preferentially as **heterodimers with the retinoid X receptor (RXR)** (see Table 3-6), and either activate or repress transcription.
- TR domain map (nuclear-receptor superfamily, domains A–F; Fig. 3-14): **(1) A/B amino-terminal** transactivation domain; **(2) C region** = DNA binding via two **zinc fingers** + dimerization; **(3) E region** (carboxyl) = ligand binding + dimerization.
- **Why T₃ > T₄ biologically** despite ~50-fold higher total T₄: (1) T₄ is bound more tightly (only ~0.01–0.02% free) than T₃ (~0.5% free), so free levels are comparable; (2) intracellular T₄→T₃ conversion equalizes cytoplasmic concentrations; (3) the nuclear TR has **~10-fold higher affinity for T₃**. Net: **T₃ occupies ~90% of TRs** in the euthyroid state.
- **Nongenomic actions** (heart, muscle, fat, pituitary): binding to cytosol/microsome/mitochondria; enhance mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation / O₂ consumption; targets include ion channels, second messengers, protein kinases.
- **BMR**: excess thyroid hormone raises BMR (heat production or O₂ consumption); deficiency lowers it (Fig. 49-6). Mechanism is **futile cycling** — driving both catabolic and anabolic limbs of fat/carbohydrate/protein metabolism so ATP is consumed without net useful work:
  - **Carbohydrate**: ↑ hepatic gluconeogenesis (induces PEPCK, pyruvate carboxylase, glucose-6-phosphatase) + ↑ glycogenolysis; plasma glucose stays normal if insulin compensates.
  - **Protein**: ↑ synthesis but greater ↑ proteolysis (mainly muscle) → net muscle loss, ↑ urinary urea nitrogen; muscle wasting in thyrotoxicosis.
  - **Lipid**: ↑ lipolysis (FAs + glycerol fuel gluconeogenesis) **and** ↑ lipogenesis; very high T₃ favors ketogenesis; serum cholesterol falls in hyperthyroidism (rises in hypothyroidism).
  - **Na-K pump**: T₃ ↑ transcription of α and β Na-K-ATPase subunits and stabilizes its mRNA; the extra ATP turnover is a major contributor to ↑ O₂ consumption (ouabain blockade can block the O₂ rise).
  - **Thermogenesis**: in rodents, T₃ + β-adrenergic (β₃) stimulation drive **brown-fat uncoupling protein (UCP/thermogenin)**, dissociating oxidative phosphorylation from ATP synthesis → heat without ATP. Thyroid hormone also ↑ **β-adrenergic receptor** expression (heart, muscle, fat) — raising catecholamine sensitivity though catecholamine levels are normal; hence β-blockers are an early treatment in acute thyrotoxicosis. In heart, T₃ favors the faster **α-myosin heavy chain** isoform → greater contractility.
- **Growth/development**: thyroid hormone drives amphibian metamorphosis (thyroidectomized tadpoles arrest). In humans, deficiency before epiphyseal fusion → growth retardation; **catch-up growth** follows treatment for **height/bone age but NOT mental age** unless therapy starts within ~7–14 days of birth. **Cretinism** (endemic iodine-deficiency): profound mental retardation, short stature, motor delay, coarse hair, protuberant abdomen — CNS damage is **irreversible** once clinically apparent. Unlike rats, humans have **no TRE in the GH promoter**, so plasma GH is normal in hypothyroidism; growth failure relates to long-bone/cartilage changes (delayed/fragmented ossification centers) and GH resistance, not low GH.

### Citation-anchor quotes
- > "There are actually two TR genes—α (chromosome 17) and β (chromosome 3)—and at least two isoforms of TRβ." (p. 1010)
- > "Both receptors bind to DNA response elements, predominately as heterodimers in association with the retinoid X receptor (RXR), and alter the transcription of specific genes." (p. 1011)
- > "T₃ is responsible for ~90% of the occupancy of TRs in the euthyroid state." (p. 1011)
- > "To the extent that thyroid hormones stimulate both synthesis and degradation, they promote futile cycles that contribute significantly to the increased O₂ consumption seen in thyrotoxicosis." (p. 1012)
- > "Brown fat in these animals expresses a mitochondrial uncoupling protein (UCP), or thermogenin, that dissociates oxidative phosphorylation from ATP generation." (p. 1013)
- > "Treatment can help mental development only if the therapy is begun within a few days of birth." (Fig. 49-7, p. 1014)

## Section 4 — Hypothalamic-pituitary-thyroid axis (pp. 1013–1017)

### Subsection headings
- **TRH from the hypothalamus stimulates thyrotrophs of the anterior pituitary to secrete TSH, which stimulates T₄/T₃ synthesis** (pp. 1013–1016)
- **T₃ exerts negative feedback on TSH secretion** (pp. 1016–1017)

### Core claims
- **TRH** (thyrotropin-releasing hormone) is a tripeptide, **pyro-Glu–His-Pro**, made chiefly by **arcuate-nucleus / median-eminence** neurons. It travels via the **hypophyseal portal system** to the anterior pituitary. Hypothalamic lesions interrupting TRH lower basal TSH; IV TRH raises TSH dose-dependently.
- **TRH receptor** on thyrotrophs is a **GPCR → phospholipase C** → DAG (→ PKC, protein phosphorylation) + IP₃ (→ ↑ [Ca²⁺]ᵢ) → ↑ synthesis and release of TSH. (TRH also raises plasma prolactin via lactotrophs, with no established physiological role.)
- **TSH (thyrotropin)** is a **28-kDa glycoprotein**, α + β chains. Its **α chain is shared** with LH, FSH, hCG; the **β chain is TSH-specific** and confers specificity.
- **TSH receptor** on the **basolateral** follicular-cell membrane is a **GPCR → Gₛ → adenylyl cyclase → ↑ cAMP**, which drives the **seven synthesis steps** (NIS uptake, Tg iodination, conjugation to T₄/T₃, endocytosis, proteolysis, secretion) **plus hyperplasia**. **T/S ratio ~30:1** in euthyroid; falls with low TSH (hypophysectomy), rises with high TSH (TSH-secreting adenoma).
- **Goiter** arises whenever the gland is chronically over-driven or stimulated: prolonged high TSH (iodine deficiency, Fig. 49-9), or TSH-receptor stimulation by **thyroid-stimulating immunoglobulin (TSI)** in Graves disease. By contrast, autoimmune **Hashimoto** destruction does not cause follicular hypertrophy, though immune-cell infiltration can modestly enlarge the gland.
- **Negative feedback**: free T₄/T₃ inhibit both hypothalamic **TRH** and pituitary **TSH**. A **50% fall in free T₄** can raise plasma TSH **50–100-fold**; hormone excess lowers TSH. The thyrotroph **senses intracellular T₃** (from blood T₃ or from **D2-mediated** local T₄→T₃ conversion). Two feedback arms: **indirect** (intracellular T₃ ↓ surface TRH receptors → less TRH sensitivity) and **direct** (T₃ ↓ transcription of both α and β TSH genes via **inhibitory T₃ response elements**). Because feedback is transcriptional and T₄/T₃ half-lives are long, the loop integrates hormone levels slowly over time.
- **Modulators**: hypothalamic **somatostatin and dopamine** inhibit TSH (shifting the T₃ set-point), counterbalancing TRH; their physiological role is small (compensatory override on chronic administration).
- **Neonatal feedback**: T₃ crosses the placenta. A hyperthyroid mother → low fetal TSH → transient neonatal hypothyroidism after birth; a hypothyroid mother → high maternal+fetal TSH → transient neonatal hyperthyroidism.

### Citation-anchor quotes
- > "TRH released by neurons in the hypothalamus travels to the anterior pituitary through the hypophyseal portal system." (p. 1014)
- > "The α chain of TSH is identical to that of the other glycoprotein hormones: the gonadotropins luteinizing hormone (LH), follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH), and human chorionic gonadotropin (hCG). The β chain is unique to TSH and confers the specificity of the hormone." (p. 1016)
- > "The TSH receptor, via Gₛ, activates adenylyl cyclase … The rise in [cAMP]ᵢ stimulates a diverse range of physiological processes." (p. 1016)
- > "Plasma [TSH] is very sensitive to alteration in the levels of free T₄ and T₃; a 50% decline in free T₄ levels can cause plasma [TSH] to increase 50- to 100-fold." (p. 1016)
- > "Both the α and β TSH genes have T₃ response elements in their promoter regions. These response elements, which are inhibitory, differ from those found in genes that are positively regulated by T₃ (e.g., Na-K pump)." (p. 1016)

## Glossary

- **Follicle / colloid / thyroglobulin (Tg)** — spherical follicular-cell unit; extracellular colloid store; >600-kDa prohormone glycoprotein.
- **Follicular cell vs. C (parafollicular) cell** — hormone-synthesizing vs. calcitonin-secreting.
- **NIS (SLC5A5)** — basolateral Na/I symporter; iodide trapping (2 Na⁺ : 1 I⁻).
- **Pendrin (SLC26A4)** — apical Cl-I exchanger; I⁻ efflux into colloid.
- **TPO (thyroid peroxidase)** — oxidizes/organifies iodide; couples MIT/DIT.
- **DUOX2** — apical H₂O₂ generator for TPO.
- **MIT / DIT** — mono-/di-iodotyrosine; DIT+DIT→T₄, MIT+DIT→T₃.
- **MCT8 (SLC16A2)** — monocarboxylate transporter; thyroid-hormone membrane transport.
- **TBG / TTR / albumin** — plasma carriers; TBG highest affinity; >99% bound.
- **Deiodinases D1/D2/D3** — D1/D2 outer-ring (activating, T₄→T₃); D3 inner-ring (inactivating, T₄→rT₃); D2 pituitary/CNS feedback.
- **rT₃ (reverse T₃)** — inner-ring-deiodinated; biologically inactive.
- **TR (TRα/TRβ) + RXR; TRE** — nuclear receptors as heterodimers on thyroid response elements.
- **BMR / futile cycles** — Na-K pump, gluconeogenesis/glycogenolysis, lipolysis/lipogenesis.
- **UCP / thermogenin** — brown-fat uncoupling protein; non-shivering thermogenesis.
- **TRH (pyro-Glu-His-Pro)** — hypothalamic tripeptide; GPCR → PLC.
- **TSH (thyrotropin)** — 28-kDa α/β glycoprotein (shared α); follicular GPCR → Gₛ/cAMP.
- **T/S ratio** — thyroid-to-serum iodine; ~30:1 euthyroid; TSH-dependent.
- **Goiter** — enlarged thyroid (iodine deficiency, Graves TSI, or infiltration).
- **Cretinism** — endemic iodine-deficiency developmental syndrome.
- **Hashimoto thyroiditis** — autoimmune (non-stimulating antibodies) → hypothyroidism; myxedema coma is the severe variant.
- **Graves disease** — TSI/TSH-receptor antibodies → hyperthyroidism, goiter, ophthalmopathy/exophthalmos, pretibial myxedema; thyroid storm is the crisis.
- **Wolff-Chaikoff** — (autoregulatory iodide escape; see cross-links / Box context).
