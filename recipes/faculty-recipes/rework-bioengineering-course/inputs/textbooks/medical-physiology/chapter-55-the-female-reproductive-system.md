## Section 1 — Anatomy and the cyclic nature of female reproduction (pp. 1108–1110)

### Subsection headings
- **Female reproductive organs include the ovaries and accessory sex organs** (pp. 1108–1109)
- **Reproductive function in the human female is cyclic** (pp. 1109–1110)

### Core claims
- **Functions of the system** (five): produce haploid ova; facilitate fertilization (syngamy); supply an implantation site and establish pregnancy; provide the physical/nutritional environment for the fetus and its timely birth; nurture the neonate.
- **Components**: gonads (ovaries), fallopian tubes, uterus + cervix, vagina, external genitalia, mammary glands — controlled by hormones from hypothalamus, pituitary, and ovaries. The principal female sex hormones are **estrogens (mainly estradiol)** and **progesterone**, produced cyclically by the ovaries.
- **Ovary**: outer cortex (developing follicles + corpora lutea in various stages) over an inner medulla (large vessels, stroma). Covered by mesothelium.
- **Fallopian tube**: infundibulum (with fimbriae) → ampulla → isthmus → intramural portion. Lined by ciliated cells (cilia beat toward uterus) plus secretory **peg cells**. Cilia + tubal-wall contractions transport the ovum; **fertilization occurs in the fallopian tube (ampulla)**.
- **Uterus**: pear-shaped muscular organ = fundus + corpus + cervix. Wall layers from lumen out: **endometrium** (glandular tissue + stroma) → **myometrium** (smooth muscle) → serosa. Cervix is dense fibrous connective tissue + smooth muscle; its glands secrete a sugar-rich mucus whose viscosity is conditioned by estrogen and progesterone.
- **Vagina** ~10 cm, stratified epithelium + thin muscular layer. External genitalia: clitoris (erectile, homologous to penis), labia majora/minora, Bartholin glands.
- **Breast**: development (**thelarche**) begins at puberty under ovarian steroids; ductal epithelium is steroid-sensitive and, in pregnancy, is activated for lactation.
- **Cyclic function**: in reflex ovulators (rabbits) mating triggers ovulation; in seasonal-estrus species (sheep, cattle, horses) females are receptive only near ovulation. In humans, baboons, and apes ovulation occurs in monthly **menstrual cycles** associated with regular uterine bleeding (**menstruation**).

### Citation-anchor quotes
- > "The female reproductive system functions to (1) produce haploid gametes—ova, (2) facilitate syngamy—or fertilization—between an ovum and a spermatozoon …" (p. 1108)
- > "The principal female sex hormones are estrogens (mainly estradiol) and progesterone, which are produced by the ovaries in a cyclic manner …" (p. 1108)
- > "Interspersed with ciliated cells are peg cells that secrete fluid and nutrients supporting the ovum and spermatozoa …" (p. 1108)
- > "Glands lining the cervical canal produce a sugar-rich secretion, the viscosity of which is conditioned by estrogen and progesterone." (p. 1108)
- > "In a small subset of species (e.g., humans, baboons, apes), ovulation occurs in monthly cycles—known as menstrual cycles—that are associated with regular episodes of uterine bleeding termed menstruation." (p. 1110)

## Section 2 — Hypothalamic-pituitary-ovarian axis and control of the menstrual cycle (pp. 1110–1116)

### Subsection headings
- **The human menstrual cycle coordinates changes in both the ovary and endometrium** (pp. 1110–1111)
- **The hypothalamic-pituitary-ovarian axis drives the menstrual cycle** (p. 1111)
- **Neurons in the hypothalamus release GnRH in a pulsatile fashion** (pp. 1111–1112)
- **GnRH stimulates gonadotrophs in the anterior pituitary to secrete FSH and LH** (pp. 1112–1113)
- **The ovarian steroids feed back on the hypothalamic-pituitary axis** (pp. 1113–1114)
- **Ovaries produce peptide hormones—inhibins, activins, and follistatins—that modulate FSH secretion** (pp. 1114–1115)
- **Modulation of gonadotropin secretion by positive and negative ovarian feedback produces the normal menstrual rhythm** (pp. 1115–1116)

### Core claims — the cycle skeleton
- The menstrual cycle (mean **28 days**, day 0 = first day of menses) is two parallel cycles: the **ovarian cycle** (follicular phase 12–14 d → ovulation → luteal phase 12–14 d) and the **endometrial cycle** (menstrual → proliferative → secretory).
- **Ovarian cycle four key events**: (1) folliculogenesis, (2) ovulation, (3) corpus luteum formation, (4) atresia (death) of the corpus luteum. The **follicular/proliferative phase** is the most variable in length.
- **Phase coupling**: follicular phase = proliferative phase (estradiol-driven endometrial growth); luteal phase = secretory phase (progesterone + estradiol → secretory glands). If no implantation by ~day 20–22, the corpus luteum regresses, progesterone/estradiol fall, and the functional layer is shed as **menses**. If pregnancy occurs, **hCG** from the placenta rescues the corpus luteum.

### Core claims — pulsatile GnRH
- GnRH is synthesized in the **arcuate nucleus** and **preoptic area**, transported to nerve terminals in the **median eminence**, and released near portal vessels that carry it to anterior-pituitary gonadotrophs. Before puberty GnRH neurons are quiescent.
- After puberty GnRH is released in **pulses ~once per hour**. GnRH half-life is only **2–4 min**, so hourly bursts cause discernible oscillations and hourly LH/FSH surges.
- **Pulse frequency sets the gonadal response.** Pulses 60–90 min apart upregulate GnRH receptors and stimulate gonadotropin release; **continuous** GnRH (or analog) downregulates GnRH receptors and *suppresses* gonadotropin output (basis of GnRH-agonist therapy).
- **Two rhythms**: the hourly rhythm plus a monthly rhythm — a massive midcycle increase in GnRH secretion from preoptic-area neurons that helps drive the LH surge.
- Schematically, gonadal activation requires a pulsatile (not tonic) signal:

  $$\text{pulsatile GnRH (}\sim\!1/\text{hr)} \;\xrightarrow{\text{GnRHR upregulation}}\; \uparrow\text{LH, FSH}$$

  $$\text{continuous GnRH} \;\xrightarrow{\text{GnRHR downregulation}}\; \downarrow\text{LH, FSH}$$

- **Gonadotroph signaling**: GnRH binds the Gq-coupled GnRH receptor → PLC → IP₃ + DAG. IP₃ raises [Ca²⁺]ᵢ → exocytosis of LH/FSH; DAG → PKC → gene transcription. **LH and FSH are heterodimers** sharing an identical α subunit; the **β subunit confers specificity** (same family as TSH and hCG). Theca cells have **LH receptors**; granulosa cells have **both LH and FSH receptors**; both receptors are **Gαs → adenylyl cyclase → cAMP → PKA**.

### Core claims — the feedback switch (the keystone concept)
- Throughout **most** of the cycle, ovarian steroids exert **negative feedback** on hypothalamus + gonadotrophs (↓ LH, FSH). **Estradiol acts negatively at both low and high concentrations; progesterone only at high concentrations.** Estradiol's inhibition of GnRH neurons is **indirect** — via interneurons (opiates in the arcuate nucleus; GABA in the preoptic area).
- At the **end of the follicular phase**, when estradiol is high and rising steeply, the axis **reverses** sensitivity: estradiol exerts **positive feedback**. The switch requires estradiol above a threshold for **≥2 days** (and perhaps the rapid *rate* of rise). Positive feedback increases gonadotroph sensitivity to GnRH and, with rising progesterone, drives the **LH surge** → ovulation.

  $$\text{E}_2 \;\text{(most of cycle)} \;\Rightarrow\; \text{negative feedback} \;\Rightarrow\; \downarrow\text{LH/FSH}$$

  $$\text{E}_2 \uparrow\uparrow \;\text{above threshold} \times \ge 2\,\text{days} \;\Rightarrow\; \text{positive feedback} \;\Rightarrow\; \text{LH surge}$$

### Core claims — inhibins, activins, follistatins
- Gonadal peptides (TGF-β superfamily) that selectively modulate **FSH** (not LH): **inhibins inhibit** FSH, **activins activate** FSH, **follistatin inhibits** FSH by binding and neutralizing activin.
- **Structure**: a glycosylated 20-kDa **α** subunit + nonglycosylated 12-kDa **β** subunits (βA, βB). **Inhibins** = α + β (α-βA = inhibin A; α-βB = inhibin B). **Activins** = two β subunits (βA-βA, βB-βB, βA-βB). Follistatin is an unrelated monomeric peptide.
- **FSH** stimulates granulosa cells to make inhibins; estradiol and (just before ovulation) LH also stimulate inhibin. Inhibin acts beyond the Ca²⁺-mobilization step, lowering mRNA for the α and βFSH subunits. Activins promote βFSH mRNA + FSH release and augment hypothalamic GnRH. Activin receptors are serine/threonine kinases → SMAD cascade.

### Core claims — the integrated monthly rhythm
- **Follicular phase**: relatively high GnRH/LH pulse frequency; early on, LH pulse amplitude rises gradually (frequency constant); later, high estradiol increases both frequency and amplitude — positive feedback begins.
- **LH surge**: abrupt rise ~day 13–14; peaks ~12 h after initiation; lasts ~48 h; peak ~3× presurge LH. A smaller FSH surge accompanies it. Triggered by estradiol crossing a high threshold (sensitizes gonadotrophs + induces a GnRH surge); rising progesterone synergizes; rising activins augment the FSH surge.
- **Luteal phase**: LH/FSH fall sharply (negative feedback from estradiol + progesterone + inhibin). Progesterone stimulates inhibitory opioidergic interneurons → ↓ LH pulse frequency (amplitude stays high). By ~48 h before menses, LH pulses slow to one every 3–4 h. Demise of the corpus luteum → falling progesterone/estradiol/inhibin → return to the follicular-phase pattern.

### Citation-anchor quotes
- > "After puberty, activity of the neurons increases, triggering release of GnRH in rhythmic pulses, about once per hour." (p. 1111)
- > "Pulses spaced 60 to 90 minutes apart upregulate the GnRH receptors on the gonadotrophs … However, continuous administration of GnRH (or an analog) causes downregulation of the GnRH receptors, which suppresses gonadotropin release …" (p. 1111)
- > "Although ovarian steroids feed back negatively on the hypothalamic-pituitary axis during most of the menstrual cycle, they have the opposite effect at the end of the follicular phase." (p. 1113)
- > "After the estradiol levels reach a certain threshold for a minimum of 2 days … the hypothalamic-pituitary axis reverses its sensitivity to estrogens. That is, estradiol now exerts positive feedback on the axis." (p. 1113)
- > "Inhibins inhibit FSH production by gonadotrophs, activins activate FSH production, and follistatins inhibit FSH production by binding to and thereby inhibiting activins." (p. 1114)
- > "The LH surge is an abrupt and dramatic rise in the LH level that occurs around the 13th to 14th day of the follicular phase … The LH surge peaks ~12 hours after its initiation and lasts for ~48 hours." (p. 1116)

## Section 3 — Ovarian steroids (pp. 1116–1120)

### Subsection headings
- **Starting from cholesterol, the ovary synthesizes estradiol … and progesterone** (pp. 1116–1117)
- **Estrogen biosynthesis requires two ovarian cells and two gonadotropins, whereas progestin synthesis requires only a single cell** (pp. 1117–1119)
- **Estrogens stimulate cellular proliferation and growth of sex organs** (pp. 1119–1120)

### Core claims — steroidogenesis
- All ovarian steroids derive from **cholesterol** (27 C; from diet, hepatic synthesis, or LDL uptake via LDL receptors; ovarian cells can also synthesize cholesterol de novo).
- **Rate-limiting step**: the P-450 **side-chain-cleavage enzyme (20,22-desmolase)** converts cholesterol → **pregnenolone**. Pregnenolone → progestins and (via the adrenal-androgen intermediates **DHEA** and **androstenedione**, both 19 C) → estrogens.
- **The ovarian signature**: ovaries have **P-450 aromatase** (P-450arom), which converts androstenedione → **estrone (E₁)** and testosterone → **estradiol (E₂)**, losing the 19-methyl group (estrogens have 18 C). **17β-hydroxysteroid dehydrogenase (17β-HSD)** interconverts estrone ↔ estradiol. The liver converts E₂/E₁ → the weak estrogen **estriol (E₃)**.
- **Progestins**: progesterone and 17α-hydroxyprogesterone form earlier in the pathway than the adrenal androgens; **progesterone** is the functionally dominant, higher-circulating progestin.

### Core claims — the two-cell, two-gonadotropin model
- **Estradiol requires two cells** because the enzymes are split:
  - **Theca / theca-lutein cells**: take up cholesterol, make DHEA + androstenedione, but **lack aromatase**. They are near blood vessels (LDL supply) and bear **LH receptors**.
  - **Granulosa / granulosa-lutein cells**: have **aromatase** but **lack 17α-hydroxylase / 17,20-desmolase** (one dual-function protein), so cannot make DHEA/androstenedione. In the follicle they are avascular (LDL-poor follicular fluid) and bear **both LH and FSH receptors**.
- **Six-step model** (follicular phase):
  1. LH → theca cell (adenylyl cyclase) → ↑ LDL receptors + side-chain-cleavage enzyme.
  2. Theca cell ↑ androstenedione synthesis.
  3. Androstenedione **diffuses to the granulosa cell**.
  4. FSH → granulosa cell (adenylyl cyclase) → ↑ aromatase.
  5. Aromatase: androstenedione → estrone → (17β-HSD) → **estradiol** (or androstenedione → testosterone → estradiol).
  6. Estradiol diffuses into the blood.

  $$\underbrace{\text{cholesterol}\xrightarrow{\text{LH}}\text{androstenedione}}_{\text{theca cell}}\;\xrightarrow{\text{diffusion}}\;\underbrace{\text{androstenedione}\xrightarrow[\text{FSH}]{\text{aromatase}}\text{estradiol}}_{\text{granulosa cell}}$$

- **Androgen concentration is a switch**: at *low* levels theca androgens are aromatase substrates (and enhance aromatase); at *high* levels they are diverted by **5α-reductase** to dihydrotestosterone (DHT, non-aromatizable), which also inhibits aromatase and LH-receptor formation → a high-androgen follicle makes *less* estrogen → atresia.
- **Luteal phase**: vascularization of the corpus luteum delivers LDL to granulosa-lutein cells; LH drives cholesterol uptake/processing → large **progesterone** output. Major corpus-luteum products = progesterone + 17α-hydroxyprogesterone (plus estradiol). Granulosa-lutein cells still lack 17α-hydroxylase/17,20-desmolase, so 17α-OH-progesterone and androstenedione production needs progesterone/precursor to shuttle to the **theca-lutein** cell.

### Core claims — transport, receptors, actions
- **Estradiol transport**: ~60% bound to albumin, ~38% to **SHBG** (= TeBG), only **2% free**. Estrogens (including OCPs) raise SHBG, so SHBG is ~2× higher in women than men.
- **Estrogen receptors**: nuclear **ERα and ERβ**, functioning as dimers (αα, αβ, ββ) that bind steroid-response elements → transcription → DNA synthesis → mitogenic action on target organs (uterus, breast). Nongenomic estrogen action via the GPCR **GPR30**.
- **Progesterone actions**: binds the dimeric **PR**; induces **secretory changes in the endometrium**; estrogen must first induce PR expression, so estrogen "conditions" the endometrium for progesterone (luteal phase).

### Citation-anchor quotes
- > "A P-450 enzyme … known as the side-chain-cleavage enzyme (or 20,22-desmolase) catalyzes the conversion of cholesterol to pregnenolone. This reaction is the rate-limiting step in estrogen production." (p. 1117)
- > "A unique aspect of estradiol synthesis in the ovary is that it requires the contribution of two distinct cell types: the theca and granulosa cells …" (p. 1117)
- > "Because of their unique physiological properties, neither the theca/theca-lutein cells nor the granulosa/granulosa-lutein cells can make estrogens by themselves." (p. 1117)
- > "Thus, the net effect of a high-androgen environment in the follicle is to decrease estrogen production." (p. 1117)
- > "Only 2% of total plasma estradiol circulates as the free hormone, which readily crosses cell membranes." (p. 1119)
- > "One of the most prominent actions of progesterone … is the induction of secretory changes in the endometrium." (p. 1120)

## Section 4 — The ovarian cycle: folliculogenesis, ovulation, and the corpus luteum (pp. 1120–1124)

### Subsection headings
- **Female reproductive life span is determined by the number of primordial follicles established during fetal life** (pp. 1120–1122)
- **The oocyte grows and matures during folliculogenesis** (p. 1122)
- **FSH and LH stimulate the growth of a cohort of follicles** (pp. 1122–1123)
- **Each month, one follicle achieves dominance** (p. 1123)
- **Estradiol secretion by the dominant follicle triggers the LH surge and thus ovulation** (pp. 1123–1124)
- **After ovulation, theca and granulosa cells differentiate into theca-lutein and granulosa-lutein cells of the corpus luteum** (p. 1124)
- **Growth and involution of the corpus luteum produce the rise and fall in estradiol and progesterone during the luteal phase** (p. 1124)

### Core claims — the fixed oocyte endowment
- Unlike males, the female has a **fixed, finite gamete pool** set in fetal life. Primordial germ cells migrate (~wk 4) to the gonadal ridge → **oogonia** (proliferate by mitosis). By ~8 wk: ~300,000 oogonia per ovary; some enter prophase I → **primary oocytes (4N DNA)**.
- All mitosis done by **20 wk**; germ-cell count peaks at **6–7 million**. Oogonia not in prophase I by 28–30 wk die by apoptosis. Oocytes arrest in the **diplotene/dictyotene** stage of prophase I until just before ovulation (meiosis I completes then; meiosis II completes at syngamy).
- **Primordial follicle** = dictyotene oocyte + single layer of flat pregranulosa cells (30–60 µm). Endowment trajectory: **~7 million** oogonia at midgestation → **~1 million at birth** → **~300,000 at puberty** → **virtually none at menopause**. Of ~300,000 at puberty only **400–500 ovulate** (≈12/yr × 40 yr); the rest undergo **atresia**.
- Depletion is **gonadotropin-independent** and unaffected by pregnancy or OCPs.

### Core claims — folliculogenesis stages
Folliculogenesis = (1) oocyte enlargement/maturation, (2) granulosa + theca differentiation/proliferation, (3) antral-fluid formation. Stage progression:
- **Primary follicle**: pregranulosa cells become cuboidal and proliferate; oocyte forms the **zona pellucida**.
- **Secondary follicle**: several cuboidal granulosa layers; stromal cells differentiate into **theca cells** (theca interna near granulosa + theca externa); oocyte ~80 µm, follicle 110–120 µm; theca cells acquire **LH receptors** + steroidogenic capacity; gap junctions form (oocyte↔granulosa); granulosa stays avascular (like Sertoli cells — nurse the gamete, barrier to blood).
- **Tertiary follicle (first antral stage)**: granulosa cells secrete fluid → **antrum**; **FSH** induces the preantral→antral transition.
- **Graafian (preovulatory) follicle**: antrum nearly encircles the oocyte except for the **cumulus** stalk; diameter 20–33 mm (note: text gives mm). Three granulosa subtypes: **mural** (most active, high LH receptors), **cumulus** (shed with the oocyte; cannot make estrogen), **antral** (remain → large luteal cells). The dominant follicle accelerates 5–6 d before ovulation into a surface bulge.

### Core claims — dominance, ovulation, corpus luteum
- **Dominance**: as estradiol + inhibin rise, FSH falls; less-mature follicles lose FSH-dependent aromatase → less estradiol, lower estrogen/androgen ratio → atresia. The dominant follicle has **more FSH receptors**, more aromatase, more estrogen, and a richer theca vascular supply → it outcompetes the cohort.
- **Ovulation**: rapidly rising estradiol switches feedback to positive and sensitizes the pituitary → **LH surge** (begins ~24–36 h after peak estradiol). **Ovulation ~36 h after surge onset, ~12 h after its peak.** At the surge the primary oocyte resumes meiosis I → first polar body + **secondary oocyte** (arrests in metaphase II until fertilization). LH + progesterone + prostaglandins (E, F) → proteolysis (collagenase) of the follicular wall → **stigma** forms in an avascular spot → rupture → oocyte (with corona radiata) expelled, swept by fimbriae into the ampulla. Zygote resides in ampulla ~72 h, then to the uterus where it floats 2–3 d before attaching.
- **Corpus luteum**: remaining granulosa + theca cells undergo **luteinization** under LH → **granulosa-lutein (large luteal)** cells (mainly progesterone, also estradiol) + **theca-lutein (small luteal)** cells (androgens, estradiol). Highly vascularized. Progesterone rises modestly before rupture, sharply after, peaks ~7 d; acts locally to inhibit follicle growth and is an **antiestrogen** (↓ ER expression).
- **Luteolysis**: without hCG rescue, the corpus luteum regresses ~**11 days after ovulation** (withdrawal of trophic support, and/or local prostaglandin F₂α from endometrium) → **corpus albicans** (fibrotic scar). hCG (syncytiotrophoblast) maintains it to ~9 wk gestation until the placenta takes over.

### Citation-anchor quotes
- > "Therefore, by midgestation, the female gamete endowment is established." (p. 1121)
- > "Of the ~300,000 primordial follicles present at puberty, only 400 to 500 are destined for ovulation between puberty and menopause …" (p. 1121)
- > "FSH induces the transition of preantral secondary follicles to antral tertiary follicles." (p. 1122)
- > "The dominant follicle therefore has more FSH receptors, a greater rate of granulosa-cell proliferation, more FSH-dependent aromatase activity, and more estrogen production than the less dominant follicles." (p. 1123)
- > "Ovulation usually occurs ~36 hours after onset of the LH surge, and ~12 hours after its peak." (p. 1123)
- > "If pregnancy is not established, the corpus luteum regresses ~11 days after ovulation." (p. 1124)

## Section 5 — The endometrial cycle (pp. 1124–1126)

### Subsection headings
- **The ovarian hormones drive the morphological and functional changes of the endometrium during the monthly cycle** (pp. 1124–1126)
- **The effective implantation window is 3 to 4 days** (p. 1126)

### Core claims
- The endometrial cycle has three phases driven by ovarian steroids:
  - **Menstrual phase**: falling estradiol + progesterone (corpus-luteum demise) withdraw support; spiral arteries spasm, the **functional layer** becomes necrotic and is shed with blood (~30 mL; does not clot — fibrinolysins). Day 1 of menses = day 1 of the cycle. Only the **zona basalis** + remnant glands remain.
  - **Proliferative (follicular/estrogen) phase**: by ~day 5 the basalis re-epithelializes; **estrogen** from developing follicles drives mitosis; endometrium thickens from **~0.5 mm to ~5 mm**. Estrogen ↑ ER, ↑ growth factors (IGF, TGF, EGF) acting auto/paracrine, and crucially **induces PR expression** (PR peaks at ovulation) to prime for progesterone.
  - **Secretory (luteal/progesterone) phase**: **progesterone** halts proliferation (it ↓ ER and ↑ 17β-HSD [E₂→estrone] + sulfotransferase [inactivates estrogens]) and drives glandular secretion. Glands become tortuous and engorged, glycogen ↑, vascularity ↑, thickness 5–6 mm; max secretion ~day 20–21. Stromal cells undergo **predecidualization** (→ decidual cells, ECM of laminin/fibronectin/collagen IV) forming the **zona compacta** over the **zona spongiosa** — together the **functional layer**.
  - **Late luteal / ischemic phase**: falling steroids → spiral-artery spasm-relax cycles → ischemia, lysosomal hydrolase release, prostaglandin ↑, microhemorrhage → menses.
- **Implantation window**: endometrial receptivity spans roughly **day 16–19** (≤4 days). Embryos transferred days 20–24 yield no pregnancies. Receptivity correlates with microvilli/**pinopods** and adhesive ECM (glycoproteins, laminin, fibronectin).

### Citation-anchor quotes
- > "The ovarian steroids—primarily estradiol and progesterone—control the cyclic monthly growth and breakdown of the endometrium." (p. 1124)
- > "The thickness of the endometrium increases from ~0.5 mm to as much as 5 mm during the proliferative phase." (p. 1124)
- > "Estradiol also induces the synthesis of PRs in endometrial tissue. Levels of PRs peak at ovulation … to prepare the cells for the high progesterone levels of the luteal phase …" (p. 1125)
- > "Progesterone … opposes the action of estradiol on the epithelial cells of the endometrium by inhibiting ER expression and stimulating expression of 17β-HSD and sulfotransferase." (p. 1125)
- > "The period of endometrial receptivity for implantation of the embryo is estimated to extend from as early as day 16 to as late as day 19 …" (p. 1126)

## Section 6 — Female sex act (pp. 1126–1127)

### Subsection headings
- **The female sex response occurs in four distinct phases** (pp. 1126–1127)
- **Both the sympathetic and the parasympathetic divisions control the female sex response** (p. 1127)
- **The female sex response facilitates sperm transport through the female reproductive tract** (p. 1127)

### Core claims
- **Libido** is modulated by circulating sex steroids; sexual activity frequency rises near **ovulation** (possibly the periovulatory androgen rise from the LH surge).
- **Four phases**: excitement/seduction → plateau → orgasm → resolution (Table 55-2). **Excitement + plateau** are largely **parasympathetic** (sacral fibers → clitoral/peri-introital engorgement, Bartholin/vaginal lubrication); **orgasm** is **sympathetic** (analogous to male emission), with rhythmic perineal contractions at ~0.8-s intervals via a pudendal-nerve (S2–S4) spinal reflex. **No refractory period** in women.
- **Sperm transport**: orgasm-related cervical dilation + oxytocin-driven uterine contractility aid transport. Of 150–600 million sperm deposited, ~100,000 reach the uterine cavity and only **50–100 viable sperm** reach the distal fallopian tube; sperm reach the ampulla within ~5 min — too fast for swimming alone, so uterine/tubal activity dominates.

### Citation-anchor quotes
- > "The four stages of the sex response in women are excitement or seduction, plateau, orgasm, and resolution." (p. 1126)
- > "Whereas the excitement phase is under the influence of the parasympathetic division of the ANS … orgasm seems to be related to the sympathetic division, as is the emission phase in men." (p. 1127)
- > "Although 150 to 600 million sperm cells are normally deposited in the vagina … ~100,000 reach the cavity of the uterus and only 50 to 100 viable sperm reach the distal fallopian tube where fertilization occurs." (p. 1127)

## Section 7 — Menopause (pp. 1127–1128)

### Subsection headings
- **Only a few functioning follicles remain in the ovaries of a menopausal woman** (p. 1127)
- **During menopause, levels of the ovarian steroids fall, whereas gonadotropin levels rise** (pp. 1127–1128)

### Core claims
- **Menopause (climacteric)** ends cyclic reproductive function. US averages: puberty ~12.5 yr, menopause ~51.5 yr. The driver is **depletion of ovarian follicles** by lifelong atresia (~300,000 at puberty → ~none at menopause).
- **Hormonal hallmark**: with few follicles, ovarian estradiol (and inhibin) fall → loss of negative feedback → **FSH rises** (detectable as early as 35 yr, before cycles stop), then LH. Postmenopausal FSH/LH can exceed the premenopausal midcycle surge — a "futile" attempt to drive absent follicles. The pituitary still pulses GnRH-driven gonadotropins, but the ovary cannot respond (though it responds to exogenous GnRH).
- **Levels**: menopausal estradiol generally **<30 pg/mL**, progesterone often **<1 ng/mL** — below the lowest premenopausal-cycle levels. Ovarian androstenedione is minimal; adrenal androstenedione stays normal.
- **Menopausal syndrome / changes (Table 55-3)**: vasomotor instability (hot flashes, night sweats), mood/memory/sleep changes, loss of libido; vaginal epithelial atrophy, pH and secretion changes, pelvic relaxation, cardiovascular disease, osteoporosis, Alzheimer disease. **HRT** (estrogen + progestin; progestin omitted post-hysterectomy because unopposed estrogen risks endometrial neoplasia); **SERMs** (tamoxifen, raloxifene) act as tissue-selective estrogen agonist/antagonists (Box 55-4).

### Citation-anchor quotes
- > "The loss of functional ovarian follicles is primarily responsible for menopause in primates." (p. 1128)
- > "Increased levels of FSH are seen as early as 35 years of age, even though cyclic reproductive function continues." (p. 1128)
- > "Because the output of estrogens, progestins, and inhibins from the ovaries falls to very low levels during menopause, negative feedback on the hypothalamic-pituitary-ovarian axis … becomes minimal." (p. 1128)
- > "During menopause, estradiol levels are generally <30 pg/mL, and progesterone levels are often <1 ng/mL of plasma." (p. 1128)

## Glossary

- **GnRH** — hypothalamic decapeptide; pulsatile (~1/hr); half-life 2–4 min; pulse *frequency* sets gonadal response.
- **Pulsatile vs. continuous GnRH** — pulsatile upregulates GnRHR (stimulates); continuous downregulates (suppresses; therapeutic).
- **LH / FSH** — αβ heterodimers, shared α, specificity-determining β; Gαs → cAMP.
- **Negative→positive feedback switch** — estradiol is inhibitory most of the cycle; above threshold for ≥2 days it becomes stimulatory → LH surge.
- **LH surge** — midcycle (day ~13–14), peaks +12 h, lasts ~48 h, ~3× baseline → ovulation ~36 h after onset.
- **Two-cell, two-gonadotropin model** — theca (LH → androgens) + granulosa (FSH → aromatase → estradiol).
- **Inhibin / activin / follistatin** — FSH-selective TGF-β-family peptides.
- **Folliculogenesis stages** — primordial → primary (zona pellucida) → secondary (theca + LH receptors) → tertiary (antrum, FSH-driven) → graafian (cumulus, corona radiata).
- **Dominant follicle** — most FSH receptors/aromatase/estrogen; rescues itself while cohort undergoes atresia.
- **Dictyotene arrest** — diplotene/prophase-I arrest from fetal life to just before ovulation.
- **Secondary oocyte** — post-meiosis-I (2N), arrested in metaphase II until fertilization.
- **Corpus luteum** — luteinized granulosa-lutein (progesterone) + theca-lutein (androgen/estradiol) cells; regresses ~11 d post-ovulation unless rescued by hCG → corpus albicans.
- **Endometrial phases** — menstrual / proliferative (estrogen) / secretory (progesterone); functional layer (zona compacta + spongiosa) shed, zona basalis retained.
- **Implantation window** — ~day 16–19; pinopods; adhesive ECM.
- **Menopause** — follicle depletion → ↓ estradiol/inhibin → ↑ FSH (then LH); E₂ <30 pg/mL, progesterone <1 ng/mL.
- **SHBG/TeBG** — binds ~38% of estradiol; raised by estrogens; 2× higher in women.
- **ERα/ERβ, PR, GPR30** — estrogen (nuclear dimers + membrane GPCR) and progesterone receptors; estrogen induces PR.
