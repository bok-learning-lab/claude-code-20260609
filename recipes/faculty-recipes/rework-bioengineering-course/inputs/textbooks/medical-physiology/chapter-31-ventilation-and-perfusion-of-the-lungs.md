## Section 1 — Dead space and alveolar ventilation (pp. 675–681)

### Subsection headings
- **Anatomic dead space is the volume of the conducting airways** (pp. 675–676)
- **The Fowler single-breath N₂ method measures anatomic dead space** (pp. 676–677)
- **Alveolar dead space is wasted ventilation in alveoli with no perfusion** (p. 677)
- **Physiologic dead space = anatomic + alveolar dead space; measured by the Bohr method** (pp. 677–679)
- **Alveolar ventilation is inversely proportional to alveolar PCO₂** (pp. 679–681)

### Core claims
- **Anatomic dead space (V_D,anat)** ≈ 150 mL in a typical adult — the volume of trachea, bronchi, and bronchioles that conduct but do not exchange gas. Approximately equals body weight in pounds × 1 mL.
- **Alveolar dead space (V_D,alv)** = ventilated alveoli that are not perfused (V/Q = ∞). Negligible in health; can become large with pulmonary embolism or zone-1 lung.
- **Physiologic dead space**: V_D = V_D,anat + V_D,alv.
- **Fowler method**: subject breathes 100% O₂ for one tidal breath, then exhales while N₂ concentration is recorded. The volume up to the rising portion of the N₂ tracing equals V_D,anat (geometric midpoint of the rise).
- **Bohr equation** for V_D / V_T (the dead-space fraction):

  $$\frac{V_D}{V_T} = \frac{P_{ACO_2} - P_{ECO_2}}{P_{ACO_2}}$$

  with $P_{ACO_2}$ (or arterial $P_{aCO_2}$ in the enhanced form) and the mixed expired gas $P_{ECO_2}$. Normal $V_D/V_T \approx 0.3$.
- **Alveolar ventilation equation** (the chapter's centerpiece, Eq. 31-11):

  $$\dot V_A = 0.863 \cdot \dfrac{\dot V_{CO_2}}{P_{ACO_2}}$$

  with $\dot V_A$ in L/min (BTPS), $\dot V_{CO_2}$ in mL/min (STPD), $P_{ACO_2}$ in mm Hg. The factor 0.863 converts STPD → BTPS gas-law units.
- **Consequence**: at steady state, $P_{ACO_2} \approx P_{aCO_2}$, so $P_{aCO_2}$ is inversely proportional to alveolar ventilation. Halving $\dot V_A$ at constant $\dot V_{CO_2}$ doubles $P_{aCO_2}$.

### Citation-anchor quotes
- > "The Fowler approach … measures the volume of the conducting airways." (p. 679)
- > "Physiological dead space = Anatomical dead space + Alveolar dead space." (Eq. 31-8, p. 679)
- > "Thus, in Equation 31-10 we introduce a constant k that not only indicates that alveolar V̇_CO₂ (P_A,CO₂) measured at 37 °C is proportional to the mole fraction of CO₂ in alveolar air, but also accounts for the different conditions for measuring the parameters." (p. 679)
- > "In this practice, clinicians generally measure arterial P_a,CO₂ (P_a,CO₂), and assume that alveolar and arterial P_CO₂ are identical (see p. 673)." (p. 679)
- > "Alveolar and arterial P_CO₂ are inversely proportional to alveolar ventilation." (p. 679)
- > "Increased alveolar ventilation causes a decrease in alveolar P_CO₂, even though the metabolic production of CO₂ is unchanged." (p. 679)

#### Figure 31-9 — Physiological consequences of pulmonary perfusion *(viewed)*

Three-panel figure tying gravity to perfusion distribution and to lung-zone definitions.

- **Panel A — Xenon detection of regional perfusion.** A standing subject is injected IV with ¹³³Xe (a poorly soluble γ-emitter). Multiple γ-counters arranged externally over apex, middle, and base count the Xe arriving in successive lung regions.
- **Panel B — Regional distribution of perfusion.** Plot of perfusion per unit volume (arbitrary units, y-axis) vs. distance from lung base (cm, x-axis). The curve declines steadily from a peak at the base (~200 units) to ~50 units at the apex — gravity-dependent perfusion gradient ~5:1 in an upright human at rest.
- **Panel C — Lung zones.** Coronal lung outline with four labeled zones from apex to base and accompanying alveolus-with-capillary cartoons that show the relevant pressures:
  - **Zone 1** (apex, $P_A > P_a > P_v$): blood vessels closed by alveolar pressure; negligible blood flow. *"Pressures inside capillary are all = P_A. Pressures outside high alveolar pressure tends to crush capillary along its entire length, minimizing blood flow."* Zone 1 conditions occur only when $P_a$ is low (e.g., hemorrhage) or when alveolar pressure is high (e.g., positive-pressure ventilation).
  - **Zone 2** (upper middle, $P_a > P_A > P_v$): capillary collapses near venous end; flow is sluiced and depends on $P_a − P_A$, not on $P_a − P_v$ (Starling resistor / "waterfall").
  - **Zone 3** (lower lung, $P_a > P_v > P_A$): capillary fully patent; flow is the conventional $(P_a − P_v)/R$ form. Most of an upright lung at rest is zone 3.
  - **Zone 4** (extreme base at low lung volume): extra-alveolar vessels are compressed by the surrounding parenchyma, and small regional volume → less mechanical tethering → narrower extra-alveolar vessels → reduced perfusion.

> Vision note: This is the textbook's keystone figure for V/Q gradients. Anchor any RAG query about gravity-dependent perfusion, zones of West, positive-pressure ventilation, hemorrhage-induced zone 1, or pulmonary embolism increasing alveolar dead space.

## Section 2 — Pulmonary blood flow (pp. 681–688)

### Subsection headings
- **The pulmonary circulation is a low-pressure, low-resistance, high-compliance system** (pp. 681–683)
- **Pulmonary vascular resistance falls when pulmonary arterial pressure rises** (pp. 683–684) — recruitment + distention.
- **Pulmonary vascular resistance is a U-shaped function of lung volume** (pp. 684–685)
- **Hypoxia is a strong vasoconstrictor — opposite to the systemic circulation** (pp. 685–687)
- **CO₂, autonomic nerves, and humoral agents modulate PVR** (pp. 687–688)

### Core claims
- Pulmonary arterial pressure ~25/10 mm Hg (mean ~15); PCWP ~5–10; PVR ~1/10 of systemic. Table 31-1 in source compares pulmonary and systemic circulation pressures.
- **Recruitment**: at low cardiac output, many capillaries are closed; raising PA pressure opens them.
- **Distention**: at higher cardiac output, capillaries already open widen further.
- Both mechanisms drop PVR as PA pressure rises — quintessentially Starling-resistor behavior, but the upshot is the pulmonary circulation passively accommodates exercise-induced 4–5× rise in cardiac output with minimal rise in PA pressure.
- **PVR vs. lung volume** (U-shaped):
  - **Extra-alveolar vessels** are pulled open by parenchymal traction at high lung volume and collapse at low lung volume.
  - **Alveolar vessels** (capillaries) are squeezed by alveolar wall tension at high lung volume and patulous at low lung volume.
  - Minimum PVR is at FRC; PVR rises at both very low and very high lung volumes.
- **Hypoxic pulmonary vasoconstriction (HPV)**: low alveolar PO₂ (not low arterial or venous PO₂) constricts adjacent pulmonary arterioles. Mechanism (still debated): inhibition of smooth-muscle K⁺ channels → depolarization → Ca²⁺ entry. Physiological role: shunts blood away from hypoxic alveoli to better-ventilated regions, improving V/Q matching. **Pathological at altitude or with global hypoxia**: produces pulmonary hypertension and right-heart strain.
- **CO₂ / acidosis** also constrict; alkalosis dilates.
- **Autonomic effects** are modest. **Vasoactive substances** (Table 31-2): dilators include NO, prostacyclin, bradykinin, ACh, β-agonists; constrictors include endothelin-1, thromboxane, angiotensin II, serotonin, α-agonists.

### Citation-anchor quotes
- > "Hypoxia is a strong vasoconstrictor, opposite to the effect in the systemic circulation." (p. 685)
- > "In addition to lung volume and perfusion pressure, several other factors can modulate pulmonary vascular resistance." (p. 685)
- > "The effects of changes in P_a,O₂ and pH on pulmonary vascular resistance are opposite to those observed in the systemic circulation." (p. 685)
- > "Hypoxic vasoconstriction occurs in isolated lung tissue and thus does not rely on either the nervous system or systemic factors." (p. 685)
- > "The hypoxic mechanism is presumably an oxygen-sensitive K channel." (p. 685)

## Section 3 — Regional ventilation and perfusion (pp. 688–693)

### Subsection headings
- **Gravity is the principal determinant of regional differences in pulmonary blood flow** (pp. 688–690)
- **Regional differences in alveolar ventilation also follow gravity, but less steeply than perfusion** (pp. 690–691)
- **The base of the lung has a smaller V_A/Q than the apex** (pp. 691–693)

### Core claims
- **Perfusion gradient**: gravity-driven; base > apex; ratio ~5:1 in the upright human at rest. Mechanism is the zone structure (Section 2).
- **Ventilation gradient**: also base > apex, but less steep (ratio ~1.5–2:1).
  - At FRC, the apical alveoli are more inflated than basal alveoli because pleural pressure is more negative at the apex (lung tissue weight makes intrapleural pressure more positive at the base). So apical alveoli sit on the flat top of the compliance curve and accept less ΔV per ΔP than basal alveoli, which sit on the steep middle of the compliance curve.
- Because perfusion gradient is steeper than ventilation gradient, **V̇_A/Q̇ varies from base to apex**: ~0.6 at base, ~1.0 at level of third rib, ~3.0 at apex.
- Consequences (Table 31-3):
  - Apex: high V̇_A/Q̇ → alveolar PO₂ high (~130 mm Hg), PCO₂ low (~28 mm Hg); blood leaving apical capillaries has high PO₂ but low O₂ content per mL because flow is low.
  - Base: low V̇_A/Q̇ → alveolar PO₂ lower (~90 mm Hg), PCO₂ higher (~42 mm Hg); blood leaving basal capillaries has slightly lower PO₂ but most of the total O₂ output because flow is high.
- Clinically, **TB granulomas favor the apex** because high O₂ tension favors aerobic mycobacterial growth and lymphatic clearance is poorest there.

### Citation-anchor quotes
- > "Because of gravity, regional perfusion in an upright subject is far greater near the base than near the apex of the lung." (p. 688)
- > "Most of an upright lung at rest is in zone 3." (p. 688)
- > "When it comes to perfusion … all alveoli are created equal." (p. 687)
- > "Within the lung, ventilation also exhibits regional differences in pulmonary ventilation due to gravity." (p. 691)
- > "In the upright lung, the ratio of alveolar ventilation to perfusion (V̇_A/Q̇) is higher at the apex than at the base." (p. 691)

#### Figure 31-11 — Regional V_A/Q ratio along the upright lung *(listed)*

## Section 4 — V/Q matching and the integrated O₂–CO₂ diagram (pp. 693–699)

### Subsection headings
- **The V_A/Q ratio determines the composition of alveolar gas and end-capillary blood** (pp. 693–695)
- **The O₂–CO₂ diagram displays the locus of possible alveolar compositions for varying V_A/Q** (pp. 695–696)
- **Shunt is V_A/Q = 0; alveolar dead space is V_A/Q = ∞** (pp. 696–697)
- **V_A/Q heterogeneity causes hypoxemia and CO₂ retention differently** (pp. 697–698)
- **Approach to hypoxemia: five causes, distinguished by A-a gradient and response to 100% O₂** (pp. 698–699)

### Equations

- **V_A/Q ratio at the alveolar level**:

  $$\frac{\dot V_A}{\dot Q} = \frac{8.63 \cdot (C_aO_2 - C_{\bar v}O_2)}{P_{ACO_2}}$$

  (one classic form derived from the Fick principle for O₂ uptake and the alveolar ventilation equation for CO₂).

- **Shunt fraction**:

  $$\frac{\dot Q_s}{\dot Q_t} = \frac{C_{c'}O_2 - C_aO_2}{C_{c'}O_2 - C_{\bar v}O_2}$$

  with end-capillary $C_{c'}O_2$, arterial $C_aO_2$, and mixed venous $C_{\bar v}O_2$. Normal physiologic shunt ~2–5%.

- **Alveolar gas equation** (developed in Ch 30 and reused here):

  $$P_{AO_2} = P_{IO_2} - \frac{P_{ACO_2}}{R}$$

  with $R$ the respiratory exchange ratio (typically 0.8).

- **A-a gradient**: $P_{AO_2} - P_{aO_2}$; normal at sea level on room air is <15 mm Hg (rises with age).

### Core claims — five causes of hypoxemia (Table 31-5 territory)

| Cause | $P_{aCO_2}$ | A-a gradient | Response to 100% O₂ |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Low PIO₂** (high altitude) | ↓ (hyperventilation) | normal | corrects |
| **Hypoventilation** | ↑ | normal | corrects |
| **V/Q mismatch** | normal or ↑ | widened | mostly corrects |
| **Shunt** | normal or ↓ | widened | does **not** correct |
| **Diffusion impairment** | normal | widened | corrects (with rest) |

- **V/Q mismatch** is the most common cause of clinical hypoxemia. Many alveoli with low V/Q produce blood with low O₂ content; high-V/Q alveoli cannot compensate because their blood is already saturated (sigmoidal Hb-O₂ curve plateau).
- **Shunt**: blood passes from venous to arterial side without contacting ventilated alveoli (anatomic shunt: bronchial veins, thebesian veins; physiologic shunt: V/Q = 0 alveoli, atelectasis, pneumonia, pulmonary edema, ARDS). Cannot be corrected by 100% O₂.

### Citation-anchor quotes
- > "The V_A/Q ratio determines the composition of alveolar gas and end-capillary blood." (p. 693)
- > "At one extreme, an airway can be completely obstructed: V_A/Q = 0. At the other extreme, blood flow can be completely obstructed: V_A/Q = ∞." (p. 696)
- > "Hypoxemia caused by a true shunt does not correct with 100% O₂; hypoxemia caused by V/Q mismatch does." (p. 698)
- > "Most clinical hypoxemia is caused by V/Q mismatch." (p. 697)

### Figures

#### Figure 31-13 — The O₂–CO₂ diagram *(listed)*

#### Figure 31-16 — Analyzing V/Q mismatch by administering 100% O₂ *(listed)*

## Glossary

- **Anatomic / alveolar / physiologic dead space** (V_D,anat / V_D,alv / V_D)
- **Fowler N₂ method** — anatomic dead-space measurement.
- **Bohr equation** — V_D / V_T = (P_aCO₂ − P_ECO₂)/P_aCO₂.
- **Alveolar ventilation equation** — V̇_A = 0.863 V̇_CO₂ / P_ACO₂.
- **Pulmonary vascular resistance (PVR)** — passively low; recruitment + distention.
- **Zones of West 1–4** — gravity-dependent regional perfusion.
- **Hypoxic pulmonary vasoconstriction (HPV)** — alveolar PO₂-driven; opposite of systemic.
- **V_A/Q ratio** — base ~0.6, apex ~3.0; ideal ~1.
- **O₂–CO₂ diagram** — locus of possible alveolar gas compositions.
- **Shunt** — V_A/Q = 0; anatomic vs. physiologic; not corrected by 100% O₂.
- **Alveolar dead space** — V_A/Q = ∞; wasted ventilation.
- **A-a gradient** — P_AO₂ − P_aO₂; widens with V/Q mismatch, shunt, diffusion impairment.
- **Five causes of hypoxemia** — low PIO₂; hypoventilation; V/Q mismatch; shunt; diffusion impairment.
- **Pulmonary hypertension** — chronic elevation of PA pressure; HPV-driven at altitude or in chronic hypoxic lung disease.
- **Pulmonary embolism** — produces alveolar dead space and possible HPV in surrounding lung.
- **Starling resistor / vascular waterfall** — zone-2 mechanism.
