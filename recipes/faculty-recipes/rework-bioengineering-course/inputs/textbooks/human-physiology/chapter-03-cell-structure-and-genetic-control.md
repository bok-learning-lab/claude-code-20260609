## Section 3.1 — Plasma Membrane and Associated Structures (pp. 50–55)

### Subsection headings (verbatim)
- **Structure of the Plasma Membrane** (pp. 51–53)
- **Phagocytosis** (p. 53)
- **Endocytosis** (pp. 53–54)
- **Exocytosis** (pp. 54–55)
- **Cilia and Flagella** (p. 55)
- **Microvilli** (p. 55)

### Core claims
- A cell can be divided into three principal parts: the **plasma membrane**, the **cytoplasm** with its organelles, and the **nucleus**.
- All cells share certain features (Table 3.1); no cell is "typical," but a generalized illustration (Fig 3.1) shows the major organelles.
- **Fluid-mosaic model (Singer & Nicholson, 1972):** the plasma membrane is a double layer of phospholipids with polar heads facing the aqueous extracellular and intracellular sides and nonpolar tails hidden in the middle. **Cholesterol** is interspersed in the bilayer (~stabilizes fluidity over temperature changes). Embedded **integral proteins** span the bilayer; **peripheral proteins** are loosely attached on either face. The whole mosaic is fluid — proteins drift laterally.
- **Membrane protein functions:** structural (cytoskeletal connections), enzymatic, receptors (Ch 6, 11), carriers (Ch 6), and **identity markers / antigens** for the immune system (Ch 15).
- **Glycoproteins and glycolipids** project carbohydrate "antennae" from the extracellular face — basis of blood groups, cell-cell recognition.
- **Endocytosis** is bulk inward transport:
  - **Phagocytosis** ("cell eating") — engulfs large particles (bacteria, debris); performed by macrophages and neutrophils.
  - **Pinocytosis** ("cell drinking") — nonspecific uptake of extracellular fluid in small vesicles.
  - **Receptor-mediated endocytosis** — specific uptake (LDL receptor for cholesterol, transferrin receptor for iron); concentrates ligand 1000-fold via clathrin-coated pits.
- **Exocytosis** — secretory vesicles fuse with the plasma membrane and release contents (hormones, neurotransmitters, extracellular matrix).
- **Cilia** are short, numerous, motile projections on epithelial surfaces (respiratory tract, oviducts); 9+2 microtubule arrangement. **Flagella** are long, single, motile projections — in humans only the **sperm tail**.
- **Microvilli** are non-motile finger-like extensions of the apical surface (intestinal "brush border", proximal kidney tubule) that **amplify surface area** for absorption.

## Section 3.2 — Cytoplasm and Its Organelles (pp. 55–61)

### Subsection headings (verbatim)
- **Cytoplasm and Cytoskeleton** (pp. 55–57)
- **Lysosomes** (p. 57)
- **Peroxisomes** (pp. 57–58)
- **Mitochondria** (pp. 58–59)
- **Ribosomes** (p. 59)
- **Endoplasmic Reticulum** (pp. 59–60)
- **Golgi Complex** (pp. 60–61)
- **Proteasomes** (p. 61)

### Core claims
- **Cytoskeleton:** three filament systems.
  - **Microfilaments** = actin polymers; involved in cell shape, amoeboid movement, cytokinesis cleavage furrow, muscle contraction (Ch 12).
  - **Microtubules** = α/β tubulin polymers; tracks for organelle movement (motor proteins **kinesin** = anterograde and **dynein** = retrograde); build mitotic spindle, cilia and flagella (9+2 axoneme).
  - **Intermediate filaments** = coiled rope-like proteins (keratins in epithelial cells, vimentin, neurofilaments) for mechanical strength.
- **Inclusions:** non-membrane-bound aggregates such as **glycogen granules** (liver, muscle), **melanin granules** (melanocytes), and **triglyceride droplets** (adipocytes).
- **Lysosomes** are membrane-bound organelles containing > 60 hydrolytic enzymes that function at an acidic internal pH (~5). They digest material delivered by endocytic vesicles (heterophagy) or by **autophagy** (digestion of the cell's own damaged organelles). 2016 Nobel: **Yoshinori Ohsumi** for autophagy genes.
- **Lysosomal storage diseases** (clinical box) — missing or defective lysosomal enzymes cause substrate accumulation: **Gaucher's, Tay-Sachs, Pompe, GM1 gangliosidosis** (~50 known diseases).
- **Peroxisomes** contain oxidases; remove H from organic molecules → H₂O₂. Catalase: $2\,\mathrm{H_2O_2} \to 2\,\mathrm{H_2O} + \mathrm{O_2}$. Detoxify ethanol → acetaldehyde in liver.
- **Mitochondria**: double-membrane organelles; the inner membrane is convoluted into **cristae** (site of the electron transport chain and ATP synthase); the **matrix** holds Krebs cycle enzymes. Mitochondria are the cell's principal ATP factories. They carry their own circular DNA (mtDNA), divide independently, and are **maternally inherited** (sperm mitochondria are destroyed after fertilization). Endosymbiotic origin from ancient bacteria.
- **Ribosomes** are made of ribosomal RNA + protein; each has a **small subunit** and a **large subunit**. Synthesize proteins by translating mRNA. Free ribosomes → cytosolic proteins; ribosomes on rough ER → secretory and membrane proteins.
- **Endoplasmic reticulum:** **Rough ER** (with ribosomes) synthesizes proteins for secretion or membrane insertion; **Smooth ER** synthesizes lipids and steroids, detoxifies drugs (liver), and stores Ca²⁺ (sarcoplasmic reticulum of muscle, Ch 12).
- **Golgi complex** = stacked flattened cisternae; modifies proteins from the rough ER (glycosylation, sorting), and packages them into vesicles destined for lysosomes, the plasma membrane, or secretion.
- **Proteasomes** are large cytoplasmic protein-degradation machines that hydrolyze proteins tagged for destruction by **ubiquitin** ("Ub" tag). 2004 Nobel.

## Section 3.3 — Cell Nucleus and Gene Expression (pp. 61–67)

### Subsection headings (verbatim)
- **Genome and Proteome** (p. 62)
- **Chromatin** (pp. 62–63)
- **Genetic Transcription — RNA Synthesis** (pp. 64–66)

### Core claims
- The **nucleus** is enclosed by a double-membrane **nuclear envelope** pierced by **nuclear pores** (large multi-protein complexes that regulate RNA and protein traffic). The outer nuclear membrane is continuous with the rough ER. Inside the nucleus, the **nucleolus** is a dense region where rRNA is synthesized and ribosome subunits are assembled.
- **Genome and proteome:** the human genome contains roughly **25,000 protein-coding genes**, but the **proteome** contains hundreds of thousands of distinct proteins due to alternative splicing and post-translational modification.
- **Chromatin** = DNA + histones. DNA wraps around histone octamers to form **nucleosomes** ("beads on a string"), which further coil into 30-nm fibers and higher-order chromatin. During mitosis, chromatin condenses into visible chromosomes.
  - **Euchromatin** (less condensed) is transcriptionally active.
  - **Heterochromatin** (densely condensed) is transcriptionally silent.
- **Transcription (Fig 3.16):** RNA polymerase synthesizes a pre-mRNA strand complementary to a DNA template (5′→3′), using U in place of T.
- **Pre-mRNA processing (Fig 3.17):**
  - **5′ cap** added (modified guanine).
  - **3′ poly-A tail** added.
  - **Introns removed**, **exons spliced** together by the spliceosome.
- **Alternative splicing:** the same gene can yield multiple proteins by including or skipping particular exons — expanding the proteome.
- **Genetic code:** each amino acid is specified by one or more three-nucleotide **codons**. 64 codons total — 61 specify amino acids (with redundancy), 3 are stop codons. **AUG** (methionine) is the start codon.

- > "About 25,000 genes are present in the human genome." (p. 62)
- > "The DNA in the chromatin of the cell nucleus is associated with proteins called **histones**." (p. 62)
- > "The DNA is wound around complexes of histone proteins, forming particles known as nucleosomes." (p. 62)
- > "RNA synthesis on a DNA template is called **genetic transcription**." (p. 64)

## Section 3.4 — Protein Synthesis and Secretion (pp. 67–71)

### Subsection headings (verbatim)
- **Transfer RNA** (p. 68)
- **Translation of mRNA** (pp. 68–69)
- **Functions of the Endoplasmic Reticulum and Golgi Apparatus** (pp. 69–71)
- **Protein Degradation** (p. 71)

### Core claims
- **tRNA** has a cloverleaf secondary structure (3D L-shape) carrying a specific amino acid at its 3′ end and recognizing the corresponding mRNA codon via its **anticodon** loop. Each amino acid has its own aminoacyl-tRNA synthetase that "charges" the tRNA.
- **Translation** (Fig 3.21) occurs on the ribosome:
  1. The small ribosomal subunit binds mRNA at the 5′ cap and scans to the start codon (AUG).
  2. The large subunit assembles; the initiator tRNA (Met-tRNA) occupies the P site.
  3. Each incoming aminoacyl-tRNA enters the A site with anticodon matched to codon.
  4. A **peptide bond** forms between the P-site polypeptide and the A-site amino acid.
  5. The ribosome translocates one codon; the empty tRNA leaves via the E site.
  6. Repeat until a stop codon → release factor disassembles the complex.
- A **polyribosome** = multiple ribosomes translating the same mRNA in succession.
- **Secretory proteins (Fig 3.22):** the nascent polypeptide carries a **leader (signal) sequence** that targets it (via SRP) to the rough ER membrane. The ribosome attaches; the growing chain threads into the ER lumen; the leader is cleaved off; the protein folds and is glycosylated.
- ER → Golgi → secretory vesicles: transport vesicles bud from the ER and fuse with the *cis* face of the Golgi; modified proteins exit the *trans* face in vesicles destined for lysosomes, the membrane, or exocytosis.
- **Post-translational processing example: proinsulin → insulin** (Fig 3.23). Pre-proinsulin → proinsulin in the ER → insulin (A chain + B chain held by disulfide bonds) by enzymatic cleavage in secretory granules.
- **Protein degradation:** misfolded or short-lived regulatory proteins are tagged with **ubiquitin** and digested by the **proteasome**.

### Citation-anchor quotes
- > "The translation of the mRNA codons begins with the first three nucleotides at the start codon, AUG." (p. 68)
- > "Proteins are made on ribosomes from amino acids. The ribosomes can be free in the cytosol or attached to the endoplasmic reticulum." (p. 67)
- > "Each ribosome will leave the mRNA when it reaches a stop codon, and the new polypeptide chain will then be released." (p. 69)
- > "Proteins destined for secretion or for insertion into the plasma membrane are made by ribosomes attached to the rough endoplasmic reticulum." (p. 69)

## Section 3.5 — DNA Synthesis and Cell Division (pp. 71–84)

### Subsection headings (verbatim)
- **DNA Replication** (pp. 72–73)
- **The Cell Cycle** (pp. 73–74)
- **Mitosis** (pp. 74–76)
- **Apoptosis** (pp. 76–77)
- **Telomeres and Cell Division** (pp. 77–78)
- **Cancer** (pp. 78–80)
- **Epigenetic Inheritance** (pp. 80–81)
- **Meiosis** (pp. 81–84)

### Core claims
- **DNA replication is semiconservative** (Meselson–Stahl, 1958): each parental strand templates a new daughter strand. **DNA polymerase** synthesizes 5′→3′; the **leading strand** is continuous; the **lagging strand** is built in short **Okazaki fragments** later joined by **DNA ligase**.
- **The cell cycle (Fig 3.25):**
  - **G₁** — growth, protein synthesis, organelle duplication.
  - **S** — DNA replication; each chromosome becomes two sister chromatids.
  - **G₂** — final growth, error-check.
  - **M** — mitosis + cytokinesis.
  - **G₀** — quiescent state outside the cycle (most differentiated cells).
- **Cyclins and cyclin-dependent kinases (Cdks)** drive cell-cycle transitions and were the subject of the 2001 Nobel. **Checkpoints** (G₁/S, G₂/M, spindle) halt progression if errors are detected.
- **Mitosis stages (Fig 3.27):**
  - **Prophase** — chromosomes condense; centrosomes (with centrioles) move apart; nuclear envelope breaks down; mitotic spindle forms.
  - **Metaphase** — chromosomes align at equator (metaphase plate); spindle fibers attach to kinetochores.
  - **Anaphase** — sister chromatids separate; each is pulled to opposite poles.
  - **Telophase** — chromosomes decondense; nuclear envelopes re-form around the two new nuclei; cytokinesis (cleavage furrow) divides the cell.
- **Apoptosis** = programmed cell death; cells shrink, fragment DNA, and are phagocytosed without inflammation. Mediated by **caspase** proteases. Essential for development (e.g., interdigital webbing loss), tissue homeostasis, and elimination of damaged cells.
- **Telomeres** are repetitive (TTAGGG)ₙ DNA caps at chromosome ends. They shorten with each replication (a "molecular clock"). **Telomerase** (an RNA-containing reverse transcriptase) extends telomeres; expressed in germline, stem cells, and most cancers — but suppressed in most somatic cells. Hayflick limit ≈ 50 divisions.
- **Cancer:**
  - **Oncogenes** = mutationally activated proto-oncogenes that drive proliferation (e.g., RAS, MYC).
  - **Tumor suppressors** restrain proliferation; loss of function (e.g., **p53** — the "guardian of the genome") permits oncogenesis. p53 normally arrests the cell cycle on DNA damage and triggers apoptosis if damage is irreparable.
  - **HeLa cells** (clinical box): cervical cancer cells from Henrietta Lacks (1951) — immortalized via active telomerase; first immortal human cell line.
- **Epigenetic inheritance** = heritable changes in gene expression *without* DNA sequence changes:
  - **DNA methylation** (CpG cytosines) → silences transcription. X-inactivation in females uses methylation; many tumor suppressors are epigenetically silenced in cancer.
  - **Histone acetylation** by histone acetyltransferases (HATs) → opens chromatin → ↑ transcription.
  - Some epigenetic marks are transmitted through germ cells (e.g., effects of famine on offspring metabolism), though most are erased between generations.
- **Meiosis** produces haploid gametes from diploid germ cells:
  - **Meiosis I** (reductional division): homologous chromosome pairs separate. Includes **crossing-over** in prophase I — homologous chromatids exchange segments, creating genetic recombination.
  - **Meiosis II** (equational division): sister chromatids separate (like mitosis), yielding four haploid cells from one diploid precursor.
  - Net result: one diploid (2n=46) cell → four genetically unique haploid (n=23) gametes.

### Citation-anchor quotes

## Clinical apparatus

- **Clinical Investigation: George** — opens chapter. George had a grandparent on each side with **Gaucher's disease** (the most common lysosomal storage disease). Doctor checks for inheritance. Teaching points: lysosomes, hydrolytic enzymes, lysosomal storage disease mechanism.
- **Clinical Application — Autophagy and Ohsumi (2016 Nobel):** autophagy supplies cells with nutrients during starvation and eliminates damaged organelles; defective autophagy contributes to neurodegeneration and cancer.
- **Clinical Application — HeLa cells / immortalized cell lines:** Henrietta Lacks (1951) cervical cancer cell line; immortalized by active telomerase; first continuously propagated human cell line.
- **Clinical Application — p53 / cancer:** p53 mutations are present in ~50% of human cancers; loss of the guardian permits unchecked proliferation and impaired apoptosis.
- **Clinical Application — Epigenetics and nutrition:** maternal famine (Dutch Hunger Winter, 1944–45) altered DNA methylation in offspring; metabolic disease risk in those offspring 60 years later — an early human epidemiologic demonstration of transgenerational epigenetics.

## Glossary (downstream-chunkable)

- **Plasma membrane** — selectively permeable lipid bilayer enclosing the cell.
- **Fluid-mosaic model** — Singer–Nicholson model of the membrane as a fluid bilayer with proteins embedded.
- **Integral / peripheral membrane protein** — membrane-spanning vs. surface-associated.
- **Glycoprotein / glycolipid** — carbohydrate-decorated membrane molecules; cell-cell recognition.
- **Cholesterol** — bilayer stiffener that buffers membrane fluidity.
- **Phagocytosis / pinocytosis / receptor-mediated endocytosis / exocytosis** — bulk import (large solid) / nonspecific liquid uptake / specific clathrin-coated pit uptake / vesicle fusion-release.
- **Cilia / flagella / microvilli** — short motile / long motile / non-motile surface projections.
- **Cytoskeleton** — microfilaments (actin), microtubules (tubulin), intermediate filaments (keratin/vimentin/etc.).
- **Kinesin / dynein** — motor proteins for anterograde / retrograde transport on microtubules.
- **Inclusion** — non-membrane-bound cytoplasmic aggregate (glycogen, melanin, fat droplet).
- **Lysosome** — acidic hydrolytic compartment.
- **Autophagy** — self-digestion of cellular components; autophagosome → lysosome fusion.
- **Lysosomal storage disease** — substrate accumulation from defective lysosomal enzyme (Gaucher, Tay-Sachs, Pompe, GM1).
- **Peroxisome** — H₂O₂-handling organelle with catalase; detoxifies ethanol in liver.
- **Mitochondrion** — double-membrane ATP factory with cristae, matrix, mtDNA; maternally inherited.
- **Cristae / matrix** — folded inner membrane / inside-the-inner-membrane compartment.
- **Ribosome** — protein synthesizer; small + large rRNA-protein subunits.
- **Rough / smooth endoplasmic reticulum** — ribosome-studded / ribosome-free ER.
- **Golgi complex** — modify/sort vesicles arriving from ER.
- **Proteasome** — protein-degradation barrel; recognizes ubiquitin-tagged substrates.
- **Ubiquitin** — small protein tag marking substrates for proteasomal degradation.
- **Nuclear envelope / nuclear pore** — double membrane around the nucleus / large transport channel.
- **Nucleolus** — rRNA-synthesizing region inside the nucleus.
- **Chromatin** — DNA + histones in the interphase nucleus.
- **Nucleosome** — DNA wound around histone octamer.
- **Heterochromatin / euchromatin** — condensed/silent vs. open/active chromatin.
- **Genome / proteome** — set of genes (~25,000 protein-coding in humans) / set of all expressed proteins (>10× larger).
- **Transcription** — DNA → RNA synthesis (in the nucleus).
- **Pre-mRNA processing** — 5′ cap, 3′ poly-A, splicing.
- **Intron / exon** — removed / retained segments of pre-mRNA.
- **Alternative splicing** — one gene → multiple proteins.
- **Codon / anticodon / start codon (AUG) / stop codon** — three-base genetic code unit.
- **tRNA** — adapter molecule carrying an amino acid and bearing a complementary anticodon.
- **Translation** — protein synthesis at the ribosome.
- **Polyribosome** — multiple ribosomes on one mRNA.
- **Signal (leader) sequence** — N-terminal targeting peptide that directs nascent proteins to the rough ER.
- **Semiconservative replication** — each new DNA duplex retains one parental strand.
- **Okazaki fragment / DNA ligase** — short discontinuous lagging-strand pieces / enzyme joining them.
- **Cell cycle (G₁/S/G₂/M; G₀)** — phases of cell duplication.
- **Cyclin / Cdk / checkpoint** — cell-cycle gas pedal / kinase / quality-control gate.
- **Mitosis stages** — prophase, metaphase, anaphase, telophase.
- **Cytokinesis** — cytoplasmic division following mitosis.
- **Apoptosis** — programmed, non-inflammatory cell death (caspases).
- **Telomere / telomerase** — chromosome end-cap repeats / RNA-templated extender.
- **Hayflick limit** — somatic cells' ~50-division ceiling.
- **Oncogene / proto-oncogene / tumor suppressor / p53** — proliferation activator / its precursor / proliferation restrainer / "guardian of the genome."
- **HeLa cells** — first immortal human cell line.
- **Epigenetics** — heritable gene-expression changes not encoded in DNA sequence.
- **DNA methylation / histone acetylation** — silences / activates transcription.
- **Meiosis I (reductional) / meiosis II (equational)** — halves chromosome number / separates sister chromatids.
- **Crossing-over** — recombination between homologous chromatids during prophase I.
- **Tetrad** — paired homologous chromosomes (each with two chromatids) at meiosis I.
- **Karyotype** — ordered diagram of an individual's chromosomes.
