### Core claims
- **Energy value of food**: 4 kcal/g for carbohydrate or protein; 9 kcal/g for fat. 1 kcal ("Calorie") = heat to raise 1 cm³ water by 1 °C.
- **Basal metabolic rate (BMR)** = O₂ consumption of an awake, resting, fasted (12–14 h), thermoneutral person; primarily determined by age, sex, body surface area, and **thyroid hormone secretion**. Hyperthyroidism → high BMR; hypothyroidism → low BMR. Genetic predisposition to low BMR may underlie familial obesity.
- Daily energy expenditures span 1,300–5,000 kcal/day; typical sedentary value ~2,500 for men, ~1,800 for women. **Caloric expenditure rate (kcal/min)** rises with weight and intensity (Table 19.1).
- **Positive energy balance** → excess calories stored as fat regardless of source (carbohydrate, protein, fat are interconvertible via the acetyl-CoA hub; see Ch 5 Fig 5.18). **Negative balance** → weight loss.
- **Set-point defense of weight**: subjects held at ±10% of usual weight have respectively lower or higher metabolic rates — the body resists weight change by altering both intake and expenditure.
- **Anabolic requirements**:
  - Protein turnover ~150 g/day, dietary need ~35 g/day because amino acids can be reused.
  - Fat turnover ~100 g/day; dietary fat need is minimal beyond fat-soluble vitamins and essential fatty acids.
  - **9 essential amino acids**: Lys, Trp, Phe, Thr, Val, Met, Leu, Ile, His.
  - **Essential fatty acids**: linoleic acid (18:2 ω-6) and α-linolenic acid (18:3 ω-3); humans lack the desaturases for the ω-6 or ω-3 positions.
- **Omega-3 fatty acids** (α-linolenic, EPA, DHA — from cold-water fish) may inhibit thrombus formation, atherosclerosis, and ventricular arrhythmias; American Heart Association recommends ≥ 2 fish meals per week.
- **Vitamins** = small organic molecules required in trace amounts; serve as coenzymes or perform unique functions. Two classes:
  - **Water-soluble**: thiamine (B₁), riboflavin (B₂), niacin (B₃), pyridoxine (B₆), pantothenate, biotin, folate, B₁₂, vitamin C. Many serve as coenzymes in respiration (e.g., riboflavin → FAD; niacin → NAD⁺).
  - **Fat-soluble**: A, D, E, K.
    - **Vitamin A** → retinal (visual pigment) and retinoic acid (binds nuclear receptors → gene transcription; key in embryonic development; basis of retinoid acne treatments).
    - **Vitamin D** → 1,25-(OH)₂D₃ (§19.6 — promotes intestinal Ca²⁺ and PO₄³⁻ absorption).
    - **Vitamin E** = α-tocopherol; lipid-phase antioxidant.
    - **Vitamin K** = coenzyme for synthesis of clotting factors II, VII, IX, X (Ch 13).
- **Vitamin D, A, and thyroxine receptors all dimerize with RXR** (binds 9-cis retinoic acid). This explains overlapping actions on differentiation and gene expression.
- **Free radicals** = molecules with an unpaired electron. **Reactive oxygen species (ROS)** include superoxide (·O₂⁻) and hydroxyl (·OH); produced by mitochondria, peroxisomes, NADPH oxidase, and inflammation. ~1–3% of electrons reaching the ETC leak prematurely to form superoxide.
- ROS have physiological roles (bacterial killing in neutrophils, vasodilation via NO·, cell proliferation, lymphocyte clonal expansion). **Excessive ROS** → oxidative stress → cell death, aging, cancer, neurodegeneration (Alzheimer's, Parkinson's), MS, ischemia, hypertension.
- **Antioxidant defenses**: enzymatic — **superoxide dismutase (SOD)** converts 2 ·O₂⁻ → H₂O₂ + O₂; **catalase** removes H₂O₂; **glutathione peroxidase** uses NADPH + glutathione. Nonenzymatic — **glutathione** (the major intracellular antioxidant), **vitamin C** (aqueous), **vitamin E / α-tocopherol** (lipid phase).
- **Paradox of antioxidant supplements**: large studies found *increased* cancer incidence in supplement users; ROS may help the immune system kill metastasized cells.

### Core claims
- **Circulating energy substrates** (Fig 19.2) = glucose, fatty acids, glycerol, ketone bodies, lactic acid, amino acids. Different organs have preferred substrates:
  - **Brain**: almost exclusively glucose (~60% of resting body glucose use); <50 mg/dL plasma glucose "starves" brain.
  - **Resting skeletal muscle**: preferred substrate is **fatty acids**.
  - **Liver, kidney, heart**: fatty acids and ketones during fasting; glucose during feeding.
- **White adipose tissue** is the main long-term energy store. Adipocyte development requires the nuclear receptor **PPARγ** (activated by a prostaglandin ligand) — a "master switch" of adipogenesis from preadipocytes (derived from fibroblasts).
- **Visceral vs. subcutaneous fat**: visceral (mesentery, omentum) fat is more inflammatory and insulin-resistant than subcutaneous fat; "apple shape" correlates with cardiovascular disease and diabetes risk; "pear shape" is lower risk.
- **Adipokines** (adipose-secreted hormones):
  - **Leptin** — secreted in proportion to fat stores; crosses BBB; suppresses appetite via the arcuate nucleus; also reaches helper T cells and the reproductive system (puberty, menarche, fertility).
  - **Adiponectin** — *decreases* with obesity; insulin-sensitizing, anti-inflammatory.
  - **Resistin, retinol-binding protein 4, TNFα** — *increase* with obesity; pro-inflammatory; promote insulin resistance.
- **Brown adipose tissue** holds many small fat droplets and many mitochondria. **UCP-1 (thermogenin)** uncouples electron transport from ATP synthesis → heat. Critical for neonatal thermogenesis; rediscovered in adults (supraclavicular) via FDG-PET. Activated by sympathetic norepinephrine on β₃-adrenergic receptors. There is more brown fat in lean than obese individuals, suggesting it may protect against weight gain.
- **Beige adipocytes** = recruitable UCP-1⁺ cells within white adipose tissue, induced by cold exposure via β₃-adrenergic signaling.
- **Secretin** (the original 1902 hormone) directly stimulates brown adipose tissue thermogenesis after a meal — a newly recognized postprandial thermogenic pathway.
- **Hunger control circuitry** in the arcuate nucleus (Fig 19.3):
  - **POMC neurons** → MSH (anorexigenic; activates melanocortin receptors; also stimulates BAT via spinal sympathetics).
  - **NPY / AgRP neurons** → NPY directly orexigenic; AgRP indirectly orexigenic by blocking MSH at melanocortin receptors.
  - **After a meal** (high circulating substrates): POMC activated, NPY/AgRP inhibited.
- **Hormonal signals to the arcuate nucleus**:
  - **Ghrelin** (stomach P/D1 cells) — *only* hormone that *stimulates* hunger; rises pre-meal; remains elevated in dieters who lost weight.
  - **CCK** (duodenum) — postprandial satiety.
  - **PYY** and **GLP-1** (distal small intestine) — postprandial satiety; long-term weight regulation. A long-acting GLP-1 agonist drug is approved for obesity treatment.
  - **Leptin / insulin** — long-term satiety/adiposity signals; both cross the BBB and act on the arcuate nucleus to suppress NPY/AgRP and activate POMC.
- **Obesity & leptin resistance**: obese individuals have *high* leptin levels but the leptin fails to suppress hunger adequately — a poorly understood resistance.
- **BMI formula**: $\mathrm{BMI} = \frac{w}{h^2}$ (w in kg, h in m). WHO thresholds: <19 underweight; 19–25 healthy; 25.0–29.9 overweight; ≥30 obese. Lowest mortality at BMI 22.5–24.9 (men), 22.0–23.4 (women). ~30% of Americans have BMI ≥ 30.
- **Metabolic syndrome** = central obesity (waist circumference) + ≥ 2 of: insulin resistance / impaired glucose tolerance, hypertension, dyslipidemia (high TG, low HDL, small dense LDL). Raises CV disease and stroke risk threefold.
- **Caloric expenditure** has three components: **BMR (~60%)**, **adaptive thermogenesis (~10%)** (diet-induced + cold-induced), and **physical activity** (highly variable).
- **Cold-induced thermogenesis**: cutaneous vasoconstriction → shivering thermogenesis (skeletal muscle = 40% of body weight) + sympathoadrenal activation of brown/beige fat (UCP-1) + permissive thyroxine.
- **Starvation slows the metabolic rate** by up to 40%, partly through ↓ leptin → ↓ TRH → ↓ thyroxine and ↓ sympathetic stimulation of brown fat — an energy-conservation mechanism that makes weight loss hard to maintain.
- **Hormonal balance of metabolism** (Fig 19.6): anabolic side = insulin, sex steroids, (growth hormone, thyroxine in parentheses). Catabolic side = glucagon, epinephrine, glucocorticoids, (GH, thyroxine in parentheses). GH and thyroxine have both effects.

### Core claims
- The pancreatic islets contain three main cell types: **β cells** (~60%) secrete **insulin**; **α cells** (~25%) secrete **glucagon**; **δ cells** secrete **somatostatin** (identical to the hypothalamic and intestinal somatostatin).
- **Insulin's anabolic actions**: in skeletal muscle, cardiac muscle, adipose, and liver, insulin stimulates fusion of **GLUT4-containing vesicles** with the plasma membrane → facilitated glucose entry; promotes glycogenesis, lipogenesis, protein synthesis; **inhibits lipolysis** and muscle protein breakdown.
- **β-cell glucose-stimulated insulin secretion** (Fig 19.8):
  1. Plasma glucose ↑ → glucose enters β cell via **GLUT2** (facilitated diffusion).
  2. Glycolysis + oxidative phosphorylation → ↑ cytosolic **ATP/ADP** ratio.
  3. ATP closes **K-ATP channels** on the β-cell membrane.
  4. K⁺ accumulation → membrane depolarization → opens **voltage-gated Ca²⁺ channels**.
  5. Ca²⁺ influx → vesicle exocytosis → insulin secretion.
- **Glucagon secretion** is inverse: low glucose / low cellular ATP → α cells secrete glucagon. **Insulin secretion + low blood glucose** also directly inhibit α cells.
- **Fasting plasma glucose**: 65–105 mg/dL. Postprandial: 140–150 mg/dL. **Insulin & glucagon normally prevent blood glucose from rising above 170 mg/dL postprandially or falling below ~50 mg/dL between meals**.
- **Amino acids** in dietary protein stimulate *both* insulin and glucagon. A high-protein meal alone (without carbohydrate) → both hormones up.
- **Autonomic effects**: parasympathetic vagal stimulation (during meals) promotes insulin secretion; sympathoadrenal stress → ↓ insulin + ↑ glucagon → "stress hyperglycemia" during fight-or-flight.
- **Somatostatin paracrine brake** within the islet: glucose → β cells secrete both insulin and **urocortin 3** → δ cells secrete somatostatin → inhibits further insulin secretion — preventing hypoglycemic overshoot.
- **Incretins** (GLP-1 from ileum, GIP from duodenum) → preprandial rise in insulin in anticipation of glucose absorption (Ch 18). Oral glucose is a more potent insulin stimulus than IV glucose.
- **Absorptive state** (post-meal): high insulin → uptake of glucose by skeletal muscle (the largest sink), liver glycogenesis, adipose lipogenesis; suppressed lipolysis (low free fatty acids). A nonobese 70-kg man has ~10 kg fat (~82,500 kcal) — a 40-day fuel reserve. Glycogen stores: ~100 g liver (~400 kcal), ~375–400 g muscle (~1,500 kcal).
- **Postabsorptive (fasting) state**: glucagon ↑, insulin ↓. Effects (Fig 19.9):
  - Liver glycogenolysis (glycogen → glucose 6-P → free glucose, via **glucose 6-phosphatase** — found only in liver, not muscle).
  - Liver gluconeogenesis from amino acids (sourced from muscle proteolysis), glycerol (from adipose lipolysis), lactate.
  - Adipose **hormone-sensitive lipase** liberates free fatty acids and glycerol.
  - Liver ketogenesis converts acetyl-CoA → ketone bodies (acetoacetate, β-hydroxybutyrate, acetone) → alternative fuel for muscle, kidney, heart, eventually brain.
- **The brain still uses glucose during fasting**; muscle/heart/kidney use fatty acids and ketones to "spare" glucose for the brain.

### Citation-anchor quotes
- > "About 60% of each islet consists of beta cells … The alpha cells form about 25% of each islet and secrete the hormone glucagon. The least numerous cell type, the delta cells, produce somatostatin." (p. 678)
- > "Insulin is the major hormone that maintains homeostasis of the plasma glucose concentration, with glucagon playing an important supporting role." (p. 678)
- > "Insulin secretion increases more rapidly when glucose is taken by mouth than when glucose is injected intravenously." (p. 680)
- > "Only the liver has this enzyme [glucose 6-phosphatase], and therefore only the liver can use its stored glycogen as a source of additional blood glucose." (p. 681)
- > "Through the stimulation of lipolysis (the breakdown of fat) and ketogenesis (the formation of ketone bodies), the high glucagon and low insulin levels during fasting provide circulating energy substrates for use by the muscles, liver, and other organs." (p. 681)

## Section 19.4 — Diabetes Mellitus and Hypoglycemia (pp. 682–687)

### Subsection headings (verbatim)
- **Type 1 Diabetes Mellitus** (p. 683)
- **Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus** (pp. 683–686)
- **Hypoglycemia** (pp. 686–687)

### Core claims
- **Diabetes mellitus** = chronic hyperglycemia from insufficient insulin secretion or action. "Mellitus" (Latin "honeyed") from glucose spilling into urine. "Diabetes" (Greek "siphon") = polyuria from osmotic diuresis. Elevated glucagon adds to the hyperglycemia.
- **Type 1 diabetes mellitus** (~5–10%):
  - **Autoimmune** β-cell destruction (MHC-linked, sibling concordance ~6%, identical-twin concordance ~65%); environmental triggers (esp. viruses).
  - **Rapid symptom onset**, ketoacidosis common, autoantibodies present.
  - Treated by **subcutaneous insulin injection** (finger-stick monitoring or continuous-glucose-monitor + pump).
  - Stem-cell-derived β-cell transplantation is in development but limited by the autoimmunity (the immune system attacks the new β cells unless protected).
  - **Uncorrected insulin deficiency** (Fig 19.11): ↑ hepatic glycogenolysis + gluconeogenesis + ketogenesis + lipolysis → hyperglycemia + hyperketonemia → osmotic diuresis → dehydration, hypotension, **metabolic acidosis** → coma/death.
- **Type 2 diabetes mellitus** (~90–95%):
  - Insulin **resistance** + relative β-cell failure. Beta cells not destroyed; insulin levels often *high*.
  - Strong heritable component (~70% identical-twin concordance; ~70% lifetime risk if both parents affected). Obesity multiplies risk; BMI ≥ 30 carries 5× the risk of type 2 diabetes.
  - **Visceral and ectopic fat** (in muscle, liver) drive insulin resistance through free fatty acids, diacylglycerol, and pro-inflammatory adipokines (**TNFα, IL-1, resistin**). Adiponectin (insulin-sensitizing) is decreased.
  - Hyperglycemia arises from (1) ↑ hepatic glycogenolysis + gluconeogenesis (insulin resistance + α-cell resistance → high glucagon) and (2) ↓ glucose uptake into muscle/adipose/liver.
  - **Prediabetes / impaired glucose tolerance**: repeatable fasting plasma glucose 100–125 mg/dL with elevated insulin (insulin resistance). Diagnostic threshold for diabetes: fasting ≥ 126 mg/dL or random ≥ 200 mg/dL.
  - **HbA1c (glycated hemoglobin)**: ~5% normal; 5.7–6.4% prediabetic; ≥6.5% diabetic. Treatment target ≤ 7%. Reflects ~3-month average glucose.
  - **Complications**: leading cause of kidney failure, blindness, lower-limb amputation; accelerated atherosclerosis; metabolic syndrome.
- **Antidiabetic drugs**:
  - **Metformin** (Glucophage) — inhibits hepatic gluconeogenesis. First-line for type 2.
  - **Sulfonylureas** — close β-cell K-ATP channels → mimic glucose-driven depolarization → stimulate insulin secretion.
  - **SGLT2 inhibitors** — block proximal-tubule glucose reabsorption → glucosuria + glucose excretion.
  - **Thiazolidinediones** (pioglitazone, rosiglitazone) — PPARγ activators in adipocytes and macrophages → reduce inflammation and insulin resistance.
  - **GLP-1 agonists** (incretin-mimetic) — approved for obesity and type 2 diabetes (long-acting forms).
- **Type 2 management**: weight reduction (shrinks visceral adipocytes), high-fiber diet (SCFA production by microbiota improves insulin sensitivity), and exercise (insulin-independent translocation of GLUT4 to muscle plasma membrane during contraction; improved muscle insulin sensitivity). One study: weight loss + fiber + exercise → 58% lower 4-year diabetes incidence.
- **Reactive hypoglycemia** (Fig 19.13): excessive β-cell insulin response to a carbohydrate meal drives blood glucose below normal within 1–5 h. Diagnostic criterion: glucose < 70 mg/dL during symptoms, relieved by carbohydrate. Most common in adults genetically predisposed to type 2 diabetes. The oral glucose tolerance test is *no longer* used because it can trigger dangerous symptoms.

## Section 19.5 — Metabolic Regulation by Adrenal Hormones, Thyroxine, and Growth Hormone (pp. 687–691)

### Subsection headings (verbatim)

  - **Regulation of Growth Hormone Secretion** (p. 689)

  - **Effects of Growth Hormone on Metabolism** (p. 690)
  - **Effects of Growth Hormone on Body Growth** (pp. 690–691)

### Core claims
- **Catecholamines (epinephrine, norepinephrine)** from the adrenal medulla and sympathetic nerves act similarly to glucagon: stimulate hepatic glycogenolysis & gluconeogenesis and adipose lipolysis. Both glucagon and epinephrine signal through Gs/adenylate cyclase → cAMP → protein kinase A → enzyme phosphorylation cascades (Fig 19.14).
- **Sympathetic nerves act on β₃-adrenergic receptors in brown (and beige) adipose tissue** to drive UCP-1 mediated nonshivering thermogenesis.
- **Glucocorticoids (cortisol)** are ACTH-driven (Ch 11) and rise in any prolonged stress including fasting and prolonged exercise. They:
  - Stimulate hepatic enzymes for gluconeogenesis.
  - Stimulate adipose lipolysis and hepatic ketogenesis.
  - Stimulate hepatic protein synthesis (gluconeogenic enzymes) while *breaking down muscle protein* → amino-acid substrate for gluconeogenesis.
- **Cushing's syndrome** (excess glucocorticoids) and chronic steroid therapy → osteoporosis (↑ bone resorption, ↓ deposition).
- **Thyroxine (T₄)** is a **prehormone**; targets convert T₄ → T₃ intracellularly. Acting via T₃:
  - Stimulates cellular respiration in almost all cells by lowering ATP (which would otherwise inhibit respiration).
  - Activates Na⁺/K⁺ ATPase and Ca²⁺ SERCA pumps → ATP consumption.
  - Promotes mitochondrial inner-membrane proton leak (uncoupling) → less efficient oxidative phosphorylation → more heat.
- Thyroxine "sets" the BMR component that responds to thyroid hormones. Hypothyroid BMR ~30% below normal; hyperthyroid BMR up to 50% above normal.
- **Thyroxine is required for proper CNS development**; hypothyroidism in children → **cretinism** (Ch 11).
- Thyroxine is **permissive** for brown-fat thermogenesis in response to sympathetic stimulation. T₄ doesn't rise in response to a cold meal or shivering but *falls* in starvation, slowing the metabolic rate.
- **Growth hormone (GH / somatotropin)** is secreted by the anterior pituitary; the most abundantly secreted pituitary hormone. Secretion is:
  - **Stimulated by GHRH** from the hypothalamus; **inhibited by somatostatin**.
  - **Pulsatile**, with 85% of daily secretion at night (circadian).
  - **Stimulated by ↑ plasma amino acids** (protein meals) and **↓ plasma glucose** (fasting).
- **IGFs (insulin-like growth factors) / somatomedins (IGF-1, IGF-2)** are GH's mediators. Liver-derived IGF-1 acts as an endocrine hormone; chondrocyte-derived IGF-1 acts as an autocrine. IGF-1 drives most of GH's anabolic effects (chondrocyte mitosis, bone growth, protein synthesis). Lipolytic and anti-glucose-utilization effects of GH appear **direct** (not IGF-mediated).
- **GH metabolic profile**: anabolic for protein (mediated by IGF-1) but **catabolic for fat** (lipolysis) and **anti-glucose** (↓ glucose utilization, ↑ blood glucose) → "**diabetogenic**". The post-meal pattern (high AAs, low/normal glucose, high GH) supports protein-building from dietary AAs while sparing carbohydrate.
- **Body growth**: GH/IGF-1 stimulate mitosis in **epiphyseal growth plates** of long bones. Growth stops when the plates ossify after puberty.
- **Excess GH**: in children → **gigantism** (proportional growth, up to 8 ft); in adults (after plate closure) → **acromegaly** with thickened jaw, hands, feet, and coarsened skin (Fig 19.17).
- **GH deficiency**: dwarfism. **Laron dwarfism** = mutation in GH receptor (insensitivity to GH). Most cases of dwarfism are **achondroplasia** (autosomal dominant FGFR3 mutation, not GH).
- **Recombinant human GH** has replaced cadaver-derived pituitary GH; FDA-approved for idiopathic short stature (<1st percentile); adds 3–7 cm to adult height. Long-term disease risk is uncertain.
- **Kwashiorkor** (childhood protein malnutrition) → low IGF-1 despite elevated GH → stunted growth, muscle atrophy, hypoproteinemia → edema (ascites, "distended belly"). Reversible with adequate protein.

## Section 19.6 — Regulation of Calcium and Phosphate Balance (pp. 691–698)

### Subsection headings (verbatim)
- **Bone Deposition and Resorption** (pp. 691–693)
- **Hormonal Regulation of Bone** (pp. 693–695)
  - **Parathyroid Hormone and Calcitonin** (pp. 693–694)
  - **Estrogen and Testosterone** (p. 694)
  - **Effects of Other Hormones** (pp. 694–695)
- **1,25-Dihydroxyvitamin D₃** (pp. 695–696)
- **Negative Feedback Control of Calcium and Phosphate Balance** (pp. 696–698)

### Core claims
- **Hydroxyapatite** Ca₁₀(PO₄)₆(OH)₂ is the calcium-phosphate mineral of bone. **Osteoblasts** secrete collagen matrix and deposit hydroxyapatite (bone formation). **Osteoclasts** dissolve hydroxyapatite and digest the protein matrix (bone resorption).
- **Osteoclast development requires M-CSF + RANKL**: osteoclast progenitors express **RANK**; osteoblasts secrete **RANK ligand (RANKL)** that activates RANK → mature osteoclasts. Osteoblasts also secrete **osteoprotegerin**, a "decoy receptor" that traps RANKL and *blocks* osteoclast formation.
- **Bone resorption mechanism** (Fig 19.18): osteoclast forms a "ruffled membrane" against bone; an apical **H⁺-ATPase** acidifies the seal to **pH 4.5** (Cl⁻ follows H⁺; HCO₃⁻ exits the back via basolateral exchange), dissolving CaPO₄. Then **cathepsin K** digests the collagen matrix.
- Peak bone mass occurs in the 30s; resorption begins to exceed deposition by 50–60.
- **Plasma calcium** is maintained at ~2.5 mM (5 mEq/L). Ca²⁺ is required for blood clotting, second-messenger signaling, neurotransmitter release, and muscle contraction. **Hypocalcemia → increased nerve and muscle excitability → tetany**.
- **Parathyroid hormone (PTH)**, secreted from the parathyroid glands in response to falling plasma Ca²⁺, raises blood Ca²⁺ via three mechanisms (Fig 19.19):
  1. **Bone**: stimulates osteoblasts to secrete RANKL → ↑ osteoclasts → bone resorption releases Ca²⁺ + PO₄³⁻ (primary mechanism).
  2. **Kidneys**: ↑ Ca²⁺ reabsorption; ↓ PO₄³⁻ reabsorption (the latter prevents serum phosphate rising and depositing as bone mineral, which would lower Ca²⁺).
  3. **Kidneys**: ↑ activity of **1α-hydroxylase** → ↑ formation of **1,25-(OH)₂D₃**, which promotes intestinal Ca²⁺ and PO₄³⁻ absorption.
- **Calcitonin** from thyroid parafollicular cells inhibits osteoclasts and renal Ca²⁺/PO₄³⁻ reabsorption when plasma Ca²⁺ is high. Calcitonin is physiologically minor in humans but pharmacologically used for osteoporosis and Paget's disease.
- **Estrogen and testosterone** are required for epiphyseal-plate sealing in *both* sexes (in men, estrogen is locally aromatized from testosterone in bone). They promote osteoclast apoptosis and suppress osteoblast apoptosis → net deposition. Postmenopausal estrogen withdrawal → osteoporosis.
- **Insulin → osteocalcin loop**: insulin inhibits osteoblast osteoprotegerin → ↑ osteoclasts. The acidic resorptive environment activates **osteocalcin**, which then *stimulates* β-cell insulin secretion (positive feedback). Osteocalcin also stimulates testicular Leydig cells (Ch 20) and supports the acute stress response by suppressing parasympathetic activity.
- **1,25-(OH)₂D₃ formation** (Fig 19.21):
  - **Skin**: 7-dehydrocholesterol → **vitamin D₃** under UV-B (or absorb dietary D₃).
  - **Liver**: **25-hydroxylase** → 25-hydroxyvitamin D₃ (storage form).
  - **Kidney**: PTH-stimulated **1α-hydroxylase** → **1,25-(OH)₂D₃**, the active hormone.
- **1,25-(OH)₂D₃ actions**:
  - **Intestine** (primary): ↑ active transcellular Ca²⁺ and PO₄³⁻ transport across duodenal/jejunal epithelium.
  - **Kidney**: ↑ distal-tubule Ca²⁺ and PO₄³⁻ reabsorption.
  - **Bone**: when dietary Ca²⁺ is *adequate* → supports mineralization. When dietary Ca²⁺ is *deficient* → stimulates osteoblast RANKL → ↑ osteoclasts → resorption that maintains plasma Ca²⁺ at the expense of bone.
  - **Autocrine/paracrine** roles in skin, breast, colon, prostate, and immune cells — differentiation, anti-proliferation (possible cancer protection), antimicrobial defense.
- **Osteomalacia / rickets** = bone-softening from vitamin D deficiency (inadequate sun + inadequate diet, or malabsorption in Crohn's / celiac disease).
- **Hyperparathyroidism** → osteitis fibrosa cystica with hypercalcemia and kidney stones.
- **Osteoporosis** is ~10× more common in postmenopausal women than in age-matched men; treatments include estrogen replacement, **bisphosphonates** (Fosamax, Boniva — bind hydroxyapatite, disrupt osteoclast ruffled border), **denosumab** (anti-RANKL antibody), **raloxifene (SERM)**, **teriparatide** (PTH derivative — paradoxically anabolic with intermittent dosing), and calcitonin.
- **Negative feedback** (Figs 19.22, 19.23): low Ca²⁺ → PTH → ↑ bone resorption + ↑ kidney Ca²⁺ reabsorption + ↓ kidney PO₄³⁻ reabsorption + ↑ 1,25-(OH)₂D₃ → ↑ intestinal absorption. High Ca²⁺ → calcitonin → ↓ osteoclasts + ↑ renal Ca²⁺/PO₄³⁻ excretion.

## Glossary (downstream-chunkable)

- **BMR (basal metabolic rate)** — resting O₂ consumption (12–14 h fasted, thermoneutral).
- **Adaptive thermogenesis** — diet- and cold-induced heat production (~10% of expenditure).
- **Kilocalorie (Calorie)** — food energy unit; 4 kcal/g for CHO + protein, 9 kcal/g for fat.
- **BMI** — weight (kg) / height² (m²); ≥30 = obese.
- **Essential amino acids / fatty acids** — must come from diet (9 AAs; linoleic ω-6 + α-linolenic ω-3).
- **Free radical / ROS / RNS** — molecules with unpaired electron; ·O₂⁻, ·OH, NO·.
- **SOD / catalase / glutathione peroxidase** — antioxidant enzymes.
- **Glutathione / vitamin C / vitamin E** — major nonenzymatic antioxidants.
- **Circulating energy substrates** — glucose, fatty acids, glycerol, ketones, lactate, amino acids.
- **PPARγ** — adipogenic master-switch nuclear receptor.
- **White / brown / beige adipose** — long-term storage / heat-producing (UCP-1) / inducible brown-like.
- **UCP-1 (thermogenin)** — uncouples ETC from ATP synthesis in brown fat → heat.
- **Leptin** — adipokine; fat-store signal to arcuate nucleus; suppresses appetite.
- **Adiponectin** — anti-inflammatory, insulin-sensitizing adipokine.
- **Ghrelin** — only hunger-stimulating gut hormone; from stomach P/D1 cells.
- **PYY / GLP-1 / CCK** — satiety gut hormones.
- **POMC / NPY-AgRP neurons** — arcuate-nucleus anorexigenic vs. orexigenic populations.
- **Insulin / glucagon / β cell / α cell / δ cell** — islet hormones / cells; insulin is dominant anabolic.
- **GLUT2 / GLUT4** — β-cell glucose sensor / insulin-recruited transporter (muscle, fat, heart).
- **K-ATP channel** — β-cell ATP-sensitive K⁺ channel that triggers depolarization & Ca²⁺-driven insulin exocytosis.
- **Incretin** — GIP, GLP-1; potentiate glucose-stimulated insulin release.
- **Glucose 6-phosphatase** — liver enzyme that releases free glucose into blood; absent in muscle.
- **Hormone-sensitive lipase** — glucagon/epinephrine-activated lipase that mobilizes adipose TGs.
- **Ketogenesis** — hepatic conversion of acetyl-CoA → acetoacetate, β-hydroxybutyrate, acetone.
- **Type 1 vs. type 2 diabetes** — autoimmune β-cell destruction vs. insulin resistance + relative β-cell failure.
- **HbA1c (glycated hemoglobin)** — 3-month average glucose marker.
- **Metabolic syndrome** — central obesity + insulin resistance + hypertension + dyslipidemia.
- **Reactive hypoglycemia** — postprandial overshoot insulin → <70 mg/dL.
- **Metformin / sulfonylurea / SGLT2 inhibitor / thiazolidinedione / GLP-1 agonist** — five drug classes for T2DM.
- **Catecholamine** — epi/NE; cAMP-mediated glycogenolysis + lipolysis.
- **Glucocorticoid (cortisol)** — stress hormone; promotes gluconeogenesis, muscle proteolysis, lipolysis.
- **Thyroxine (T₄) / T₃** — prehormone / active hormone; sets BMR; permissive for thermogenesis.
- **Growth hormone (GH / somatotropin)** — anterior pituitary; pulsatile, nocturnal, anabolic for protein, lipolytic, diabetogenic.
- **IGF-1 / IGF-2 (somatomedins)** — liver/chondrocyte-derived mediators of GH growth effects.
- **Acromegaly / gigantism / dwarfism / achondroplasia / Laron** — GH excess (adult/child), GH/IGF deficiency, FGFR3 mutation, GH-receptor mutation.
- **Hydroxyapatite** — Ca₁₀(PO₄)₆(OH)₂; bone mineral.
- **Osteoblast / osteoclast / osteocyte** — bone-forming / bone-resorbing / quiescent matrix-embedded.
- **RANK / RANKL / osteoprotegerin** — osteoclast-formation receptor / ligand / decoy.
- **Cathepsin K** — osteoclast-secreted collagen-digesting enzyme.
- **Parathyroid hormone (PTH)** — raises Ca²⁺ by bone resorption + renal Ca²⁺ reabsorption + 1α-hydroxylase activation.
- **Calcitonin** — thyroid parafollicular hormone; lowers Ca²⁺ pharmacologically (minor physiologic role).
- **1,25-(OH)₂D₃** — active vitamin D; intestinal Ca²⁺/PO₄³⁻ absorption.
- **Osteomalacia / rickets / osteoporosis** — bone undermineralization (adult/child) vs. loss of matrix density.
- **Bisphosphonate / denosumab / raloxifene / teriparatide** — osteoporosis drugs (hydroxyapatite-binding / anti-RANKL Ab / SERM / PTH derivative).
- **Osteocalcin** — bone-derived hormone that stimulates β-cell insulin secretion (positive feedback) and Leydig-cell testosterone.
