### Core claims
- An **atom** has a nucleus of protons (+) and neutrons (neutral) surrounded by electrons (−) in orbital shells. The number of protons = the **atomic number**; protons + neutrons = the **mass number**. Electron mass is negligible.
- **Atoms commonly in organic molecules (Table 2.1):** H, C, N, O, Na, Mg, P, S, Cl, K, Ca.
- **Isotopes** of an element have identical chemistry but different mass numbers (different neutron counts). Hydrogen: ¹H (protium), ²H (deuterium, 1 proton + 1 neutron), ³H (tritium, 1 proton + 2 neutrons, radioactive — used in research and clinical assays).
- **Valence electrons** in the outermost shell determine bond capacity. Atoms bond to complete the outer shell (2 for H, 8 for C, N, O, etc.).
- **Covalent bonds** = sharing of electron pairs. Equal sharing → **nonpolar** (e.g., H₂, C–H). Unequal sharing (between atoms of different electronegativity) → **polar** (e.g., O–H in water). **Double bonds** share two pairs (4 electrons).
- **Ionic bonds** form when an atom transfers electrons to another (e.g., Na → Cl, forming Na⁺Cl⁻); these compounds dissociate in water into hydrated ions.
- **Water is a polar molecule:** the O end is δ⁻; the H ends are δ⁺. Water dissolves polar substances and ions but excludes nonpolar ("hydrophobic") molecules, which then aggregate (the hydrophobic effect).
- **Hydrogen bonds** are weak electrostatic links between an H bonded to N/O and a nearby electronegative atom. They cohere liquid water, give water its high surface tension and high specific heat, anneal the DNA double helix, and stabilize protein secondary structure.
- **Acids** dissociate to release H⁺ (proton donors); **bases** accept H⁺ (often by releasing OH⁻ that combines with H⁺ to form water). The Brønsted–Lowry definition.
- **The pH scale:** $\mathrm{pH} = -\log_{10}[\mathrm{H}^+]$. Each pH unit is a 10-fold change in [H⁺]. Pure water at 25 °C: [H⁺] = 10⁻⁷ M → pH 7. Blood pH is tightly maintained near **7.40** (range 7.35–7.45). pH below 7.35 = **acidosis**; above 7.45 = **alkalosis**.
- **Common acids and bases (Table 2.2):** HCl (gastric), H₂CO₃ (blood buffer), H₂SO₄, lactic acid; NaOH, NaHCO₃, NH₄OH.
- **Organic molecules** are carbon-based, with C–H backbones built into chains and rings (cyclic; aromatic = ring with alternating double bonds). Carbon's tetravalence permits enormous structural diversity.
- **Functional groups** confer characteristic chemistry: hydroxyl (–OH), carbonyl (>C=O), carboxyl (–COOH, ionizes to –COO⁻ + H⁺), amino (–NH₂), sulfhydryl (–SH), phosphate (–OPO₃²⁻).
- **Stereoisomers** (geometric isomers): same molecular formula and bond connectivity but different 3D arrangement.
  - **Cis/trans isomers** — restricted rotation around a double bond.
  - **Optical isomers (enantiomers)** — non-superimposable mirror images, distinguished as **D-** and **L-** (or R/S). Only **D-sugars** and **L-amino acids** are used biologically.
  - Clinically relevant: **thalidomide** has two enantiomers; one is sedative, the other teratogenic — and the molecule **racemizes in vivo**, so administering a single enantiomer does not prevent birth defects.

  - **Phospholipids** (p. 38)

### Core claims
- **Carbohydrates** are aldehydes or ketones with multiple hydroxyl groups, formula ≈ $(CH_2O)_n$. Three classes:
  - **Monosaccharides** — simple sugars: glucose, fructose, galactose (all C₆H₁₂O₆ hexoses), ribose (C₅H₁₀O₅, pentose).
  - **Disaccharides** — two monosaccharides linked: maltose (glucose+glucose), sucrose (glucose+fructose), lactose (glucose+galactose).
  - **Polysaccharides** — long polymers: **glycogen** (animal storage, branched, in liver and muscle), **starch** (plant storage), **cellulose** (plant cell wall, β-1,4-linked glucose, indigestible by humans → dietary fiber).
- **Dehydration synthesis** joins two molecules and releases one water; **hydrolysis** is the reverse — water cleaves the bond. Both require enzymes in vivo.
- **Lipids** are heterogeneous, defined by hydrophobicity rather than a common skeleton.
  - **Triglycerides** (= triacylglycerols) = glycerol + 3 fatty acids. The body's main long-term fuel store; densely packed (9 kcal/g vs. ~4 kcal/g for carbohydrates and proteins).
  - **Fatty acids**: long hydrocarbon chains with terminal –COOH. **Saturated** (no C=C) or **unsaturated** (one or more C=C). Cis double bonds bend the chain (lower melting point — oils); trans double bonds keep it straight (artificial **trans fats** raise LDL and CV risk).
  - **Essential fatty acids:** linoleic (ω-6) and α-linolenic (ω-3) — not synthesized by humans.
  - **Ketone bodies:** acetoacetic acid, β-hydroxybutyric acid, acetone — produced by liver when carbohydrate is scarce (fasting, untreated diabetes); elevated levels → **ketoacidosis**, breath acetone, ketonuria.
  - **Phospholipids** (e.g., **lecithin / phosphatidylcholine**) are amphipathic — polar phosphate-choline head + two nonpolar fatty-acid tails. Self-assemble into bilayers and form the basis of cell membranes.
  - **Steroids** share a four-fused-ring skeleton: cholesterol (membrane lipid and hormone precursor), the **steroid hormones** (cortisol, aldosterone, testosterone, estrogen, progesterone), and **bile acids** (lipid emulsifiers).
  - **Prostaglandins** are 20-carbon **eicosanoids** derived from arachidonic acid; local (paracrine) signaling molecules involved in inflammation, fever, vasomotion, and platelet aggregation.

## Section 2.3 — Proteins (pp. 40–44)

### Subsection headings (verbatim)
- **Structure of Proteins** (pp. 40–43)
  - **Primary structure**
  - **Secondary structure**
  - **Tertiary structure**
  - **Quaternary structure**
  - **Denaturation**
- **Functions of Proteins** (pp. 43–44)

### Core claims
- **Amino acids** have a central α-carbon bonded to: an amino group (–NH₂), a carboxyl group (–COOH), a hydrogen, and a variable side chain (**R group**). The R group determines whether the AA is **nonpolar, polar uncharged, acidic** (e.g., glutamate, aspartate), or **basic** (e.g., lysine, arginine, histidine).
- **20 standard amino acids** are used in protein synthesis; 9 are **essential** (must come from diet) in humans.
- **Peptide bonds** join the α-COOH of one AA to the α-NH₂ of the next via dehydration synthesis; many AAs strung together form a **polypeptide chain**.
- **Four levels of protein structure (Fig 2.28):**
  - **Primary** — the linear sequence of amino acids encoded by DNA.
  - **Secondary** — local backbone folding into **α-helix** (right-handed coil stabilized by intra-helical H-bonds between backbone C=O and N–H atoms) or **β-pleated sheet** (parallel/antiparallel strands H-bonded side-by-side).
  - **Tertiary** — the overall 3D fold of one polypeptide; stabilized by hydrogen bonds, ionic bonds, hydrophobic / van der Waals interactions, and the strong covalent **disulfide (S–S) bond** between cysteine residues.
  - **Quaternary** — assembly of two or more polypeptide subunits into a functional multi-subunit protein (e.g., hemoglobin = 4 subunits, insulin = 2 chains held by disulfide).
- **Denaturation:** disruption of secondary/tertiary/quaternary structure (heat, pH extremes, organic solvents) without breaking peptide bonds. Egg white frying is the canonical demonstration. Denaturation is usually irreversible in vivo and abolishes function.
- **Functions of proteins:** structural (collagen in tendons, keratin in epidermis), enzymes, antibodies, membrane receptors, membrane carriers (transporters and channels), hormones (e.g., insulin), oxygen carriers (hemoglobin, myoglobin), motility (actin, myosin).
- **Conjugated proteins:** **glycoproteins** (protein + carbohydrate, e.g., membrane proteins, hormones), **lipoproteins** (protein + lipid, e.g., HDL/LDL/VLDL), **chromoproteins** (protein + pigment — hemoglobin, cytochromes).

### Citation-anchor quotes
- > "Proteins are composed of subunits called amino acids that are joined together by peptide bonds." (p. 40)
- > "The primary structure of a protein refers to the sequence of amino acids in the polypeptide chain." (p. 41)
- > "Hydrogen bonds give rise to the secondary structure of a protein, which can be either an alpha helix or a beta pleated sheet." (p. 41)
- > "The tertiary structure of a protein is held in place by a variety of bonds. These include relatively weak bonds, such as hydrogen bonds, ionic bonds, and van der Waals (hydrophobic) forces, as well as the strong covalent disulfide bonds." (p. 43)
- > "Denatured proteins retain their primary structure (the peptide bonds are not broken) but have altered chemical properties." (p. 42)
- > "Because of their tremendous structural diversity, proteins can serve a wider variety of functions than any other type of molecule in the body." (p. 43)

## Section 2.4 — Nucleic Acids (pp. 44–47)

### Subsection headings (verbatim)
- **Deoxyribonucleic Acid** (pp. 44–46)
- **Ribonucleic Acid** (pp. 46–47)

### Core claims
- **Nucleotides** = sugar + phosphate + nitrogenous base. They polymerize via 5′→3′ phosphodiester bonds into a sugar-phosphate backbone with bases as side chains.
- **DNA (deoxyribonucleic acid):**
  - Sugar = **deoxyribose** (lacks 2′-OH).
  - Bases = **adenine (A), thymine (T), cytosine (C), guanine (G)**. A and G are **purines** (two-ring); C and T are **pyrimidines** (one-ring).
  - **Chargaff's rule:** A pairs with T (2 hydrogen bonds), G pairs with C (3 hydrogen bonds). So [A] = [T], [G] = [C].
  - DNA is a **right-handed double helix** with antiparallel strands joined by H-bonds between complementary bases. Watson & Crick (1953).
  - Stored in the cell nucleus; encodes genes that direct protein synthesis (Ch 3).
- **RNA (ribonucleic acid):**
  - Sugar = **ribose** (has 2′-OH).
  - Bases = A, **U (uracil)**, C, G — U replaces T.
  - Usually single-stranded.
  - Three classes:
    - **Messenger RNA (mRNA)** — gene transcript that carries the protein-coding sequence to the ribosome.
    - **Transfer RNA (tRNA)** — adapter that brings amino acids to mRNA codons.
    - **Ribosomal RNA (rRNA)** — catalytic structural backbone of the ribosome.
- Even with massive base-sequence diversity across genes, every cell in a person carries identical DNA copies; the mechanisms of replication and partition are taken up in Ch 3.

### Citation-anchor quotes
- > "Both DNA and RNA are composed of long chains of nucleotides." (p. 44)
- > "DNA consists of two such chains that are wrapped about each other to form a double helix." (p. 45)
- > "Hydrogen bonds can form between guanine and cytosine and between thymine and adenine." (p. 45)
- > "RNA consists of long chains of nucleotides joined together by sugar-phosphate bonds. However, RNA differs from DNA in three important respects." (p. 46)

### Equations / quantitative
- **Base-pairing arithmetic:** In any DNA sample [A] = [T] and [G] = [C]; %GC + %AT = 100%. Used in Test Your Quantitative Ability.

## Glossary (downstream-chunkable)

- **Atom** — smallest unit of an element; nucleus of protons + neutrons orbited by electrons.
- **Atomic number / mass number** — # protons; # protons + neutrons.
- **Isotope** — same element, different neutron count.
- **Chemical element** — set of all isotopes of a given atomic number.
- **Valence electron** — outer-shell electron available for bonding.
- **Covalent bond** — atoms share electron pair(s). Nonpolar if equal sharing; polar if unequal.
- **Ionic bond** — electron transfer producing cation + anion (e.g., NaCl).
- **Hydrogen bond** — weak attraction between H bonded to N/O and a nearby electronegative atom.
- **Polar molecule** — asymmetric distribution of charge (e.g., water).
- **Hydrophilic / hydrophobic** — water-loving / water-fearing.
- **Acid / base** — proton donor / acceptor.
- **pH** — $-\log_{10}[\mathrm{H}^+]$.
- **Acidosis / alkalosis** — blood pH < 7.35 / > 7.45.
- **Organic molecule** — carbon-based molecule with C–H bonds.
- **Functional group** — characteristic chemistry: –OH, –COOH, –NH₂, –SH, –OPO₃²⁻, >C=O.
- **Stereoisomer** — same connectivity, different 3D arrangement.
- **Enantiomer / D-, L-** — non-superimposable mirror images.
- **Cis / trans** — orientations across a double bond.
- **Monosaccharide / disaccharide / polysaccharide** — 1 / 2 / many sugar units.
- **Dehydration synthesis / hydrolysis** — bond formation with H₂O release / bond cleavage with H₂O addition.
- **Glycogen** — branched glucose polymer; animal storage form in liver and muscle.
- **Starch / cellulose** — plant glucose polymers; only starch is digestible by humans.
- **Triglyceride** — glycerol + 3 fatty acids; main fat storage.
- **Saturated / unsaturated / cis / trans fatty acid** — by C=C presence and geometry.
- **Essential fatty acid** — linoleic, linolenic — required from diet.
- **Ketone body** — acetoacetic acid, β-hydroxybutyric acid, acetone; ↑ in fasting/diabetes.
- **Ketoacidosis / ketonuria** — metabolic acidosis from ketone accumulation; ketones in urine.
- **Phospholipid** — amphipathic lipid; membrane bilayer precursor.
- **Lecithin (phosphatidylcholine)** — major mammalian phospholipid; choline is also an ACh precursor.
- **Amphipathic** — molecule with polar and nonpolar regions.
- **Steroid** — cholesterol-based four-ring molecule; class includes cortisol, sex hormones, bile acids, vitamin D.
- **Prostaglandin** — 20-carbon eicosanoid local signaling molecule.
- **Amino acid** — α-C with amino, carboxyl, H, and R; 20 standard in proteins; 9 essential.
- **Peptide bond** — amide linkage between AA carboxyl and amine.
- **Polypeptide / protein** — chain of amino acids; functional protein once folded.
- **α-helix / β-pleated sheet** — two principal secondary structures.
- **Tertiary / quaternary structure** — 3D fold of one polypeptide / assembly of multiple polypeptides.
- **Disulfide bond** — S–S between cysteine residues; covalent stabilizer of tertiary/quaternary structure.
- **Denaturation** — loss of higher-order protein structure with primary structure intact.
- **Glycoprotein / lipoprotein / chromoprotein** — protein conjugated to carbohydrate / lipid / pigment.
- **Hemoglobin / myoglobin** — O₂-transport / O₂-storage hemoproteins.
- **Nucleotide** — sugar + phosphate + base.
- **Purine / pyrimidine** — two-ring (A, G) / one-ring (C, T, U) bases.
- **DNA / RNA** — deoxyribose, A/T/C/G, double-stranded / ribose, A/U/C/G, single-stranded.
- **Chargaff's rule** — [A]=[T], [G]=[C] in DNA.
- **Double helix** — right-handed antiparallel duplex DNA (Watson & Crick).
- **mRNA / tRNA / rRNA** — protein-coding transcript / amino-acid adapter / ribosome RNA.
