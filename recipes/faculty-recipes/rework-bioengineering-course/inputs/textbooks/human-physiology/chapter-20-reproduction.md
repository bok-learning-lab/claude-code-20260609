## Section 20.1 — Sexual Reproduction (pp. 703–709)

### Subsection headings (verbatim)
- **Sex Determination** (pp. 703–706)

  - **Alleles and Genomic Imprinting** (pp. 704–705)

- **Development of Accessory Sex Organs and External Genitalia** (pp. 706–707)

### Core claims
- Each diploid (2n = 46) human cell has 22 autosomal homologous pairs + one sex-chromosome pair (XX female, XY male). Meiosis halves to a haploid (n = 23) gamete; fertilization restores 2n.
- **X chromosome** (~1,090 genes) is much larger than **Y chromosome** (~80 genes). The Y has unusual long **palindromes** (one up to 2.9 Mbp) that allow intra-chromosomal "gene conversions" — a substitute for the missing inter-homologue crossing-over.
- **X-linked diseases**: 168 known diseases caused by mutations in 113 X-linked genes; males are at higher risk because their single X is unpaired (no recessive masking).
- **X-inactivation**: in each female cell one X chromosome becomes a condensed **Barr body** (or a "drumstick" in neutrophil nuclei). Which X is silenced is random, making females cellular mosaics. About 15% of inactive-X genes escape inactivation.
- **Sex determination cascade** (Fig 20.4): chromosomal sex (XX vs. XY) → gonadal sex (testis vs. ovary) → phenotypic sex (accessory organs and genitalia).
- **SRY** (sex-determining region Y) gene on the short arm of the Y chromosome codes for a 204-AA DNA-binding protein = the **testis-determining factor**. SRY translocation to the X chromosome produces XX males; loss of SRY from the Y produces XY females.
- **Embryonic gonad timing**:
  - Day 19: primordial germ cells migrate from yolk sac.
  - Day 25–30: Wolffian (mesonephric) ducts begin.
  - Day 44–48: Müllerian (paramesonephric) ducts begin.
  - Day 50–52: urogenital sinus + tubercle.
  - Day 53–60: seminiferous tubules + Sertoli cells appear; Müllerian regression begins.
  - Day 60–75: Leydig cells appear; testosterone production begins.
  - Day 105: ovarian follicle development begins (third trimester).
- **Embryonic male endocrine program**:
  - **Sertoli cells** secrete **anti-Müllerian hormone (AMH / Müllerian inhibition factor)** → Müllerian ducts regress.
  - **Leydig (interstitial endocrine) cells** secrete **testosterone** → Wolffian ducts develop → epididymis, ductus deferens, seminal vesicles, ejaculatory duct.
  - **Testosterone is converted by 5α-reductase to dihydrotestosterone (DHT)** intracellularly → develops penis, spongy urethra, scrotum, prostate.
  - In the absence of testes (and therefore AMH and testosterone), Müllerian ducts develop into uterus + uterine tubes; the genital tubercle becomes clitoris; labioscrotal swellings become labia majora.
- **Testicular descent into scrotum** is testosterone-driven; **cryptorchidism** (undescended testes) occurs in 2–9% of full-term male births and impairs spermatogenesis because scrotal T° (~35 °C) is 3 °C below body temperature.
- **Disorders of embryonic sexual development**:
  - **Klinefelter syndrome** (47, XXY) — male phenotype but infertile, low testosterone (nondisjunction).
  - **Turner syndrome** (45, X) — female phenotype but infertile (single X from mother).
  - **Hermaphroditism / ovotestis** — extremely rare; some cells inherit the SRY-bearing fragment, others do not.
  - **Pseudohermaphrodites**: either testes or ovaries but with inappropriate internal/external genitalia.
  - **Congenital adrenal hyperplasia** — female pseudohermaphroditism; excessive adrenal androgens masculinize XX fetus.
  - **Testicular feminization (androgen insensitivity)** — XY with non-functional androgen receptors → female external genitalia + breasts at puberty (peripheral aromatization), testes retained, no uterus (AMH still secreted), no menses, infertile.
  - **5α-reductase deficiency** — XY with normal androgen receptors but no DHT → female-looking external genitalia at birth, masculinization at puberty.

## Section 20.2 — Endocrine Regulation of Reproduction (pp. 709–713)

### Subsection headings (verbatim)
- **Interactions Among the Hypothalamus, Pituitary Gland, and Gonads** (pp. 710–711)
- **Onset of Puberty** (pp. 711–712)
- **Pineal Gland** (p. 713)
- **Human Sexual Response** (p. 713)

### Core claims
- The hypothalamic-pituitary-gonadal axis (HPG):
  - **Hypothalamus** → **GnRH (gonadotropin-releasing hormone)** secreted in **pulsatile** fashion into the hypothalamo-hypophyseal portal system.
  - **Anterior pituitary** → **FSH + LH** (= ICSH in males).
  - **Gonads** → sex steroids (testosterone or estradiol/progesterone) and the polypeptide **inhibin** (Sertoli cells in males; granulosa cells in females) — selectively inhibits FSH without affecting LH.
- **Steroid biosynthesis chain** (Fig 20.8): cholesterol → progesterone (ovaries, placenta) → androstenedione (adrenal cortex) → testosterone (testes) → **estradiol-17β** (ovaries, placenta; via aromatase).
- **Pulsatile GnRH is required** to prevent target-receptor desensitization and downregulation. Continuous GnRH (or long-acting GnRH agonists) paradoxically *suppress* gonadotropin secretion — the basis of GnRH-analogue therapy for endometriosis and prostate cancer.
- **Three primary actions of LH + FSH on gonads**: (1) stimulate spermatogenesis / oogenesis; (2) stimulate sex-steroid secretion; (3) maintain gonadal structure.
- Castration → loss of negative-feedback steroids → ↑ FSH + LH.
- **In adult males**: testosterone and sex steroids exert *negative* feedback throughout life; secretion is **continuous** (not cyclic).
- **In adult females**: secretion is **cyclic**; just before ovulation, estradiol exerts *positive* feedback on LH → LH surge → ovulation.
- **Puberty**: gonads are not deficient in steroidogenic ability — they lack stimulation. The hypothalamus + anterior pituitary become *less* sensitive to negative feedback from sex steroids → ↑ GnRH amplitude and frequency → ↑ gonadotropins → puberty.
- **Leptin** as a gating signal for puberty: leptin from adipose tissue is required to permit puberty onset; thin adolescent girls delay puberty (Ch 19).
- **Kisspeptins** (encoded by KISS1) are recently discovered hypothalamic neuropeptides that drive GnRH neurons and may integrate metabolic + neural signals controlling puberty.
- **Pineal gland**: secretes **melatonin** (a tryptophan derivative) at night; antigonadotropic in many seasonally breeding mammals; role in human reproduction remains controversial.
- **Human sexual response** (Masters & Johnson): four phases — **excitation, plateau, orgasm, resolution** — common to both sexes. Male resolution includes a refractory period during which a new orgasm cannot occur; women lack an obligate refractory period.

### Citation-anchor quotes
- > "Although these two hormones are named according to their actions in the female, the same hormones are secreted by the male's pituitary gland." (p. 710)
- > "Studies have shown that secretion of GnRH from the hypothalamus is pulsatile rather than continuous, stimulating a similar pulsatile secretion of FSH and LH from the anterior pituitary." (p. 710)
- > "This pulsatile secretion is needed to prevent desensitization and downregulation of the target glands." (p. 710)
- > "Inhibin is secreted by the Sertoli cells of the seminiferous tubules in males and by the granulosa cells of the ovarian follicles in females. This hormone specifically inhibits the anterior pituitary's secretion of FSH without affecting the secretion of LH." (p. 710)
- > "The secretion of testosterone is relatively constant rather than cyclic." (p. 711)
- > "During one phase of the female cycle — shortly before ovulation — estrogen exerts a positive feedback effect on LH secretion." (p. 710)

## Section 20.3 — Male Reproductive System (pp. 713–723)

### Subsection headings (verbatim)

- **Male Accessory Sex Organs** (pp. 719–720)
- **Erection, Emission, and Ejaculation** (pp. 720–722)

### Core claims
- **Two compartments of the testis**:
  - **Seminiferous tubules** — site of spermatogenesis; contain germ cells + **Sertoli cells**; FSH receptors are on Sertoli cells (only). Sertoli cells secrete inhibin and androgen-binding protein (ABP), form the **blood-testis barrier** via tight junctions, secrete FAS ligand to apoptose intruding T cells, and engulf residual spermatid cytoplasm.
  - **Interstitium** — contains **Leydig cells**; LH receptors are on Leydig cells. Leydig cells secrete testosterone in response to LH.
- **Negative feedback** in the adult male:
  - **LH** is inhibited by testosterone (and its conversion products: 5α-reduced androgens and estradiol).
  - **FSH** is inhibited by **inhibin** (Sertoli-derived polypeptide).
- **Androgens are anabolic steroids**: promote bone and muscle growth, sebaceous gland activity, secondary sex characteristics. Use of high-dose anabolic-androgenic steroids has serious adverse effects (acne, liver damage, ↑ LDL, suppression of HPG axis, gynecomastia from aromatization).
- **Spermatogenesis** (Fig 20.15):
  - **Spermatogonia** (2n) at the basement membrane undergo mitosis: one daughter remains a stem cell, the other becomes a **primary spermatocyte** (2n).
  - Primary spermatocytes enter **meiosis I** → 2 **secondary spermatocytes** (n).
  - **Meiosis II** → 4 **spermatids** (n) per primary spermatocyte; the spermatids remain interconnected by cytoplasmic bridges.
  - **Spermiogenesis**: spermatids develop acrosome + flagellum → **spermatozoa**; cytoplasm pinched off as "residual bodies" and phagocytosed by Sertoli cells.
- **Sperm structure** (Fig 20.18): head with nucleus + **acrosome** cap (enzymes for fertilization); midpiece with mitochondria around the "9 + 2" axoneme; principal piece + end piece (flagellum tail).
- Sperm in seminiferous tubules are **non-motile**; they acquire motility during epididymal transit.
- **Hormonal control of spermatogenesis**:
  - **LH-stimulated testosterone** initiates and maintains spermatogenesis.
  - **FSH** is not absolutely required (men with non-functional FSH receptors still produce sperm) but enhances spermatogenesis and supports Sertoli-cell proliferation — required for maximal fertility.
  - **Estrogen** produced in testes (testosterone → estradiol via aromatase in Sertoli cells) may also be required.
- **Male accessory sex organs** (Fig 20.19): epididymis (5 m if uncoiled; sperm maturation + storage) → **ductus (vas) deferens** → ampulla → ejaculatory duct (joined by seminal vesicle) → prostatic urethra → membranous urethra → spongy (penile) urethra. **Seminal vesicles** contribute 45–80% of semen volume including **fructose** (sperm fuel). **Prostate** adds citric acid, calcium, and vesiculase (coagulant). **Bulbourethral (Cowper's) glands** secrete pre-ejaculatory lubricant.
- **Erection** (Fig 20.21): parasympathetic nerves release nitric oxide (NO·) onto corpora cavernosa smooth muscle. NO· activates **guanylate cyclase** → **cGMP** → closes plasma-membrane Ca²⁺ channels → smooth-muscle relaxation → vasodilation → engorgement. Venous outflow is occluded as cavernous bodies swell.
- **Phosphodiesterase-5 (PDE5) inhibitors** (sildenafil/Viagra, tadalafil/Cialis) block cGMP breakdown → sustained erection.
- **Emission** (sympathetic) = movement of semen into urethra by peristaltic contractions of ductus deferens, seminal vesicles, prostate.
- **Ejaculation** (sympathetic) = expulsion by rhythmic contraction of bulbospongiosus and ischiocavernosus muscles at the base of the penis. Sexual function requires synergistic parasympathetic (erection) + sympathetic (ejaculation) activity.
- **Semen analysis** (Table 20.5): volume 1.5–5.0 mL; sperm count 40–250 M/mL; motility ≥ 70% at 1 h; pH 7.2–7.8; fructose 150–600 mg/100 mL. **Oligospermia** = < 20 M/mL → reduced fertility.
- **Vasectomy** (Fig 20.22): surgical interruption of the vas deferens. Does not affect testosterone secretion. Sperm accumulate in crypts and are phagocytosed; ~70% of vasectomized men develop antisperm antibodies → low reversal success.
- **Benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH)**: DHT-driven; treated with α₁-blockers + 5α-reductase inhibitors (also used for androgenic alopecia / male-pattern baldness).

## Section 20.4 — Female Reproductive System (pp. 723–729)

### Subsection headings (verbatim)
- **Ovarian Cycle** (pp. 725–727)
- **Ovulation** (pp. 727–728)
- **Hypothalamic-Pituitary-Ovarian Axis** (pp. 728–729)

### Core claims
- **Female anatomy** (Fig 20.23): two ovaries → fimbriae → uterine (fallopian) tubes → uterus (perimetrium / myometrium / endometrium = stratum basale + stratum functionale) → cervix (cervical mucus plug) → vagina → vulva (labia majora, labia minora, clitoris, vaginal orifice, urethral orifice).
- **Uterine fibroids (leiomyomas)** are benign smooth-muscle tumors of the myometrium; estradiol- and progesterone-stimulated; affect 70–80% of women by age 50; cause heavy menses, miscarriage, pelvic pain. Most common reason for hysterectomy.
- **Female germ-cell timeline**:
  - **5 months gestation**: ovaries contain ~6–7 million **oogonia**. Most undergo apoptosis.
  - **Birth**: ~2 million **primary oocytes** (already arrested at **prophase I**) within **primordial follicles**.
  - **Puberty**: ~400,000 oocytes remain.
  - **Reproductive lifetime**: only ~400 actually ovulate; the rest atretic.
  - **Menopause**: oogenesis ceases.
- **Follicle development** (Fig 20.26):
  - **Primordial follicle** = primary oocyte + single flat layer of follicular cells.
  - **Primary ovarian follicle** = primary oocyte + cuboidal granulosa cells (single layer).
  - **Secondary follicle** = multiple granulosa layers + fluid vesicles + outer theca interna.
  - **Graafian (mature) follicle** = single antral cavity; secondary oocyte + corona radiata + zona pellucida + cumulus oophorus.
  - Primary follicles secrete **anti-Müllerian hormone (AMH)** in adult women — a useful clinical marker of ovarian reserve before IVF.
- **Oocyte meiotic arrests** are released by hormonal cues:
  - **Arrest 1**: prophase I; held by cGMP passed through gap junctions from granulosa cells.
  - **LH surge** → granulosa cGMP falls → oocyte resumes meiosis I → **secondary oocyte** (n) + first polar body. Cytoplasm distribution is unequal: secondary oocyte gets nearly all the cytoplasm.
  - **Arrest 2**: metaphase II; held until fertilization.
  - Fertilization → meiosis II completes → ovum + second polar body.
- **Hormonal follicle development**:
  - **FSH** stimulates follicular growth and granulosa-cell **aromatase** activity.
  - **LH** stimulates **theca interna** cells to make testosterone (parallel to Leydig cells).
  - **Aromatase** in granulosa cells converts theca-derived testosterone to **estradiol** — the **two-cell two-gonadotropin model**.
- **Ovulation** (Fig 20.29):
  - One follicle becomes dominant (graafian); others become atretic.
  - The LH surge causes the follicle to rupture and extrude the secondary oocyte + corona radiata + zona pellucida into the uterine tube; fimbriae sweep it in.
  - The empty follicle is reorganized into the **corpus luteum** (LH-driven), which secretes both estradiol and progesterone.
  - Without pregnancy, the corpus luteum regresses to a **corpus albicans**.

### Citation-anchor quotes
- > "The germ cells that migrate into the ovaries during early embryonic development multiply, so that by about five months of gestation the ovaries contain approximately 6 million to 7 million oogonia." (p. 725)
- > "By the time a girl reaches puberty, the number of oocytes and follicles has been reduced to 400,000. Only about 400 of these oocytes will ovulate during the woman's reproductive years." (p. 726)
- > "The cells of the theca interna of the ovarian follicles produce testosterone in response to LH stimulation, similar to the response of Leydig cells to LH in the testes of males. In females, the testosterone produced by the theca diffuses to the granulosa cells of the ovarian follicles, which have the enzyme aromatase to convert testosterone to estradiol." (p. 726)
- > "After ovulation, the empty follicle is stimulated by LH to become a new structure — the corpus luteum." (p. 731)

## Section 20.5 — Menstrual Cycle (pp. 729–735)

### Subsection headings (verbatim)
- **Phases of the Menstrual Cycle: Cyclic Changes in the Ovaries** (pp. 729–731)
  - **Follicular Phase** (p. 730)

- **Effects of Pheromones, Stress, and Body Fat** (pp. 733–734)

- **Menopause** (p. 735)

### Core claims
- The **menstrual cycle** is described in terms of **ovarian** (follicular / ovulation / luteal) phases and **endometrial** (menstrual / proliferative / secretory) phases (Fig 20.33).
- **Follicular phase (days 1–14)**:
  - FSH from anterior pituitary → growth of secondary follicles → granulosa cells secrete **estradiol** (rising).
  - Inhibin from granulosa cells suppresses FSH selectively → only the dominant follicle survives (others go atretic).
  - Early estradiol exerts **negative feedback** on FSH/LH.
- **LH surge (day 13)**: rapidly rising estradiol flips to **positive feedback** at the hypothalamus + pituitary → rapid LH (and smaller FSH) surge.
- **Ovulation (day 14)**: LH surge triggers follicular rupture → secondary oocyte released into the uterine tube.
- **Luteal phase (days 14–28)**:
  - LH converts the ruptured follicle into the corpus luteum, which secretes **progesterone + estradiol**.
  - These two together exert *negative* feedback on FSH/LH → prevent new follicle development.
  - The corpus luteum also secretes inhibin → further suppression of FSH.
  - Without pregnancy, the corpus luteum regresses around day 24–26 → progesterone and estradiol plummet → menstrual bleeding.
- **Endometrial phases**:
  - **Menstrual (days 1–4)**: shedding of stratum functionale due to plummeting progesterone/estradiol.
  - **Proliferative (days 5–14)**: estradiol-driven endometrial regrowth.
  - **Secretory (days 15–28)**: progesterone-driven gland coiling, glycogen accumulation, vascular changes; primes endometrium for blastocyst implantation.
- **Body temperature** rises ~0.5 °C after ovulation due to progesterone — the basis of basal-body-temperature contraception (Fig 20.36; not reliable enough for standalone use).
- **Menarche** typically ages 11–13; **menopause** at age 45–55. Average ovulatory cycle = 28 days but commonly 24–35 days.
- **Effects of pheromones, stress, body fat**: stress and very low body fat (e.g., athletes, anorexia) → amenorrhea via hypothalamic GnRH suppression. The McClintock-effect synchronization of menstrual cycles among women living together is now controversial.
- **Polycystic ovarian syndrome (PCOS)**: anovulatory cycles, multiple small ovarian cysts, hyperandrogenism (acne, hirsutism, alopecia), insulin resistance, metabolic-syndrome risk.
- **Contraceptive methods**:
  - **Combined oral contraceptive pills** (estrogen + progestin) → suppress GnRH/FSH/LH → no follicle development, no ovulation.
  - **Progestin-only pills, depot, implants** → thicken cervical mucus, alter endometrium.
  - **IUDs** (copper or progestin-releasing) → local effects on sperm transport, endometrium.
  - **Emergency contraception** (Plan B / ulipristal) → delay ovulation.
  - **Sterilization** (tubal ligation / vasectomy).
  - **Mifepristone (RU-486)** = progesterone-receptor antagonist; pharmacologic abortifacient.
- **Menopause**: depletion of ovarian follicles → ↓ estradiol → ↑ FSH/LH (loss of negative feedback). Symptoms: vasomotor (hot flashes), atrophy of urogenital epithelium, osteoporosis (Ch 19), cardiovascular risk shift. **Hormone replacement therapy** (estrogen ± progestin) helps symptoms but carries breast-cancer and thromboembolic risk.

### Core claims
- **Fertilization** normally occurs in the upper third of the **uterine tube** within ~24 h of ovulation. Of the 60–150 million sperm per ejaculate, only a few hundred reach the oocyte. Sperm must undergo **capacitation** in the female tract (~4–6 h) before they are competent to fertilize.
- **Acrosomal reaction** (Fig 20.38): on contact with the **zona pellucida**, sperm release **acrosomal enzymes** (hyaluronidase, acrosin) that digest a path through the corona radiata and zona. Fusion of sperm and oocyte plasma membranes triggers a **Ca²⁺ wave** that:
  - Completes the oocyte's meiosis II → second polar body extruded.
  - Triggers the **cortical reaction** = exocytosis of cortical granules → hardens the zona pellucida → **block to polyspermy**.
- The fertilized **zygote** undergoes mitotic **cleavage** without growth: 2-cell → 4-cell → 8-cell → **morula** (~day 3) → **blastocyst** (~day 5; **inner cell mass + trophoblast + blastocyst cavity**).
- **Implantation** (~day 6–7) (Fig 20.43): trophoblast adheres to the endometrium and differentiates into:
  - **Cytotrophoblast** — inner cellular layer.
  - **Syncytiotrophoblast** — multinucleated outer mass that erodes endometrial tissue and creates lacunae filled with maternal blood.
- **hCG (human chorionic gonadotropin)** secreted by the syncytiotrophoblast from ~day 6–10 onward acts on the mother's corpus luteum (LH-like) → continued progesterone and estradiol → endometrium maintained.
  - hCG is the analyte detected by urine and serum pregnancy tests (monoclonal antibody against the β subunit).
  - hCG also may help suppress immunological rejection of the conceptus and has TSH-like activity.
- The **placenta** takes over progesterone and estradiol production by ~week 10 (the corpus luteum regresses).
- **Ectopic pregnancy** = implantation outside the uterus, usually in the fallopian tube; can rupture and cause life-threatening hemorrhage.
- **Embryonic stem (ES) cells** are isolated from the inner cell mass of a cultured blastocyst; they are **pluripotent** and can form all three germ layers (ectoderm, mesoderm, endoderm). **Reproductive cloning** uses somatic-cell nuclear transfer to derive a totipotent zygote-like cell.
- **Adult stem cells** are **multipotent** and form a limited variety of related cell types within their lineage.
- **iPS cells** (Yamanaka 2006) — adult differentiated cells reprogrammed to pluripotency by 4 transcription factors.
- **Three primary germ layers** (ectoderm / mesoderm / endoderm) are established during **gastrulation** in the third week and give rise to all body tissues.
- **Extra-embryonic membranes** (Fig 20.45): amnion, chorion, yolk sac, allantois. The **placenta** forms from the **chorion frondosum** of the embryo + **decidua basalis** of the mother. The **umbilical cord** carries two umbilical arteries (deoxygenated → placenta) and one umbilical vein (oxygenated ← placenta) (Fig 20.48).
- **Placental exchange**:
  - **Gases** (O₂, CO₂) diffuse passively.
  - **Nutrients** (glucose by facilitated diffusion / amino acids by active transport).
  - **Waste** (urea, creatinine) diffuses to maternal blood.
  - **Maternal IgG antibodies** cross placenta (passive immunity).
  - **Alcohol, nicotine, cocaine, opioids, THC, many drugs** cross placenta → fetal harm (fetal alcohol syndrome).
- The placenta is metabolically very active (~⅓ of maternal-side O₂ and glucose; protein synthesis rate higher than liver).
- **Pregnancy hormones from the placenta** (Table 20.7):
  - **hCG** — maintains corpus luteum for first ~10 weeks.
  - **Chorionic somatomammotropin (hCS / hPL)** + **placental growth hormone (PGH / hGH-V)** — diabetic-like effects on the mother (↑ lipolysis, glucose-sparing, maternal hyperglycemia) → ensures fetal glucose supply; can precipitate **gestational diabetes**.
  - **Progesterone** — relaxes myometrium, maintains endometrium, suppresses LH/FSH.
  - **Estrogens** (esp. **estriol** in late pregnancy) — sensitize myometrium for labor.
  - **CRH** (in humans and great apes only) — drives onset of labor.
- The placenta is an **incomplete endocrine gland**: it cannot synthesize cholesterol de novo (uses maternal supply) and cannot convert progesterone to androgens (relies on fetal adrenal cortex, especially the **fetal adrenal zone**, to provide DHEAS).
- **Fetal-placental unit**: fetal DHEAS → placenta → estriol → maternal urine. Urinary estriol can assess placental health.
- **Onset of labor** (Fig 20.50): in humans the cascade involves:
  1. Fetal hypothalamus → CRH → ACTH from fetal anterior pituitary → fetal adrenal cortex secretes cortisol + **DHEAS**.
  2. Cortisol → positive feedback on placental CRH (key driver of timing in humans).
  3. DHEAS → placenta → estriol.
  4. Estriol acts on the maternal myometrium: ↑ oxytocin receptors, ↑ prostaglandin (PGF₂α, PGE₂) receptors, ↑ gap junctions.
  5. Oxytocin (from maternal posterior pituitary) and prostaglandins (from uterus) → contractions of myometrium → labor.
- **Oxytocin + cervical stretch** is a positive-feedback loop (the classic textbook example of physiological positive feedback) that ends only with delivery of the baby.
- Cortisol also matures fetal lungs (pulmonary surfactant production) — Ch 16.
- After delivery, oxytocin promotes uterine involution (1 kg → 60 g by 6 weeks) and reduces hemorrhage.
- **Lactation** (Figs 20.52, 20.53):
  - **Mammary gland** development during pregnancy requires estrogen + progesterone + **prolactin** + insulin + cortisol + thyroxine.
  - **Prolactin** is held in check during pregnancy by high progesterone/estrogen; after delivery, progesterone falls → prolactin stimulates **milk production** by alveoli.
  - **Suckling** triggers a neuroendocrine reflex:
    - Hypothalamus inhibits **prolactin-inhibiting hormone (PIH = dopamine)** → ↑ prolactin → more milk.
    - Hypothalamus stimulates posterior pituitary → **oxytocin** release → myoepithelial cell contraction → **milk-ejection reflex (let-down)** through lactiferous ducts.
  - **Bromocriptine** (dopamine agonist) is given to suppress lactation when not desired.
- **Lactational amenorrhea**: prolactin inhibits GnRH → suppressed ovulation; natural birth-spacing (more effective with frequent, day-and-night nursing).
- **Colostrum** (the first milk, ~5 days postpartum) is rich in **IgA antibodies**, cytokines, growth factors, lymphocytes. Mature milk also contains casein, lactalbumin, lactose, vitamins, minerals.
- **Maternal antibody protection** (Fig 20.54): **IgG** crosses placenta in utero → passive immunity for 3–12 months; **IgA** from milk supplements through breastfeeding period; the baby's own active immunity develops over the first year.
