# Mini Quiz — Boron Ch 1: Foundations of Physiology

_Five questions, ~five minutes. Source: [chapter-01-foundations-of-physiology.md](../../../inputs/textbooks/medical-physiology/chapter-01-foundations-of-physiology.md) (Boron & Boulpaep, *Medical Physiology* 3e, ch. 1, pp. 2–5)._

## 1. Claude Bernard's *milieu intérieur* refers to…

A. the external environment surrounding the organism.
B. the **extracellular fluid** that bathes tissues and cells and is held to a controlled composition.
C. the cytoplasm inside individual cells.
D. the lumen of the gastrointestinal tract.

## 2. According to Boron's "topological insight," which of the following is **not really inside the body** — i.e., is continuous with the *milieu extérieur*?

A. Plasma in a peripheral vein.
B. Interstitial fluid around a hepatocyte.
C. **Fluid inside the lumen of the small intestine.**
D. Cytosol of a renal tubular epithelial cell.

## 3. Boron describes the eukaryotic negative-feedback loop as having four required elements. In order, they are…

A. effector → comparator → set-point → sensor.
B. **sensor → comparator (vs. set-point) → gain stage → effector.**
C. gain stage → sensor → effector → comparator.
D. set-point → effector → gain stage → comparator.

## 4. The chapter argues that modern physiology must reach into molecular biology because…

A. cellular and molecular events are downstream of organ-level regulation and need not be modeled directly.
B. DNA encodes the proteins that perform the organ-level functions physiology studies, so physiological genomics "closes the circle" between organs and genes.
C. molecular biology has replaced physiology as a discipline.
D. organ-level regulation is unaffected by individual genetic variation.

## 5. A **knockout mouse** that lacks an "essential" gene shows no obvious phenotype. The chapter's framing is that…

A. the knockout was technically unsuccessful.
B. the gene is dispensable in mammals.
C. **understanding the unexpected phenotype (or its absence) requires re-evaluating the whole mouse's physiology** — compensatory regulation by other pathways must be considered.
D. the gene is paralog-redundant in mice but not humans.

---

## Answer key (instructor)

1. **B.** The *milieu intérieur* = the **extracellular fluid (ECF)**. Bernard described it as "the organic liquid that circulates and bathes all the anatomic elements of the tissues, the lymph or the plasma." (§3, p. 3.)
2. **C.** Topologically the GI lumen, sweat ducts, and renal tubule lumen are continuous with the outside world — Boron makes this exact point. (§3, p. 3.)
3. **B.** Sensor (measures parameter) → comparator (compares to set-point; outputs error signal) → gain stage (multiplies error) → effector (acts to oppose disturbance). (§4, p. 4.)
4. **B.** Physiological genomics is the discipline-bridging move — DNA encodes the proteins that organ physiology depends on. The chapter literally uses the phrase "closes the circle." (§2, p. 2.)
5. **C.** The chapter's exact framing: an apparently essential gene that produces no phenotype on knockout forces the physiologist to study the whole mouse and uncover compensatory regulation. (§2, "Knockout mouse" passage.)
