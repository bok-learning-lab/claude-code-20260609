# Mini Quiz — Boron Ch 5: Transport of Solutes and Water

_Five questions, ~five minutes. Source: [chapter-05-transport-of-solutes-and-water.md](../../../inputs/textbooks/medical-physiology/chapter-05-transport-of-solutes-and-water.md) (Boron & Boulpaep, *Medical Physiology* 3e, ch. 5, pp. 102–140)._

## 1. In a typical 70-kg adult, total body water is approximately **42 L**. Of that, intracellular fluid (ICF) is about…

A. 13 L.
B. 3 L.
C. **25 L** (≈ 60% of TBW).
D. 42 L (all of TBW).

## 2. The **principal ion** at high concentration in the **intracellular fluid** is…

A. Na⁺ (~140 mM).
B. **K⁺ (~120 mM).**
C. Cl⁻ (~115 mM).
D. HCO₃⁻ (~25 mM).

## 3. **Channels** and **carriers** can be distinguished kinetically because…

A. channels show saturation kinetics; carriers do not.
B. **carriers show saturation kinetics (Michaelis–Menten, half-maximal at $K_m$, plateau at $V_{\max}$); channels typically do not — their throughput is much higher (10⁶–10⁸ ions/s vs. 10²–10⁴ for carriers).**
C. channels require ATP; carriers do not.
D. carriers move ions, channels move only water.

## 4. The **Na⁺/glucose cotransporter SGLT1** on the apical brush border is an example of…

A. primary active transport.
B. **secondary active transport** (the Na⁺ gradient — maintained by the basolateral Na/K pump — drives uphill glucose uptake).
C. simple diffusion through the bilayer.
D. facilitated diffusion of glucose without coupling to Na⁺.

## 5. The **Na⁺/Ca²⁺ exchanger (NCX)** is an antiporter with a 3:1 stoichiometry. The functional consequence is that NCX…

A. moves 3 Ca²⁺ out for every 1 Na⁺ in.
B. **moves 3 Na⁺ in for every 1 Ca²⁺ out** — using the Na⁺ gradient to extrude Ca²⁺ uphill.
C. requires direct ATP hydrolysis.
D. is electrically silent (transports no net charge).

---

## Answer key (instructor)

1. **C.** TBW ≈ 42 L (60% of 70 kg). ICF is 60% of TBW ≈ 25 L. ECF is the other ~17 L (interstitial ~13 L + plasma ~3 L + transcellular ~1 L). The traps (13 L and 3 L) are real ECF subcompartment values placed as distractors. (§1; Fig 5-1.)
2. **B.** K⁺ ~120 mM in ICF vs. ~4.5 mM in ECF. Na⁺ ~15 mM in ICF vs. ~145 mM in ECF. These reciprocal gradients are maintained by the Na/K pump and are foundational to every later chapter. (§1, composition table.)
3. **B.** Saturation is the signature of carrier-mediated transport; channels are aqueous pores with linear throughput vs. substrate. (§2, channels-vs-carriers table; Fig 5-6.)
4. **B.** SGLT1 is the textbook example of secondary active transport — the energetic driver is the Na⁺ electrochemical gradient, not direct ATP. (§2; Fig 5-12.)
5. **B.** 3 Na⁺ in / 1 Ca²⁺ out — a net inward positive charge, so NCX is **electrogenic** and its rate depends on membrane voltage. Critical for cardiac repolarization (downstream chapter). (§2, antiport examples.)
