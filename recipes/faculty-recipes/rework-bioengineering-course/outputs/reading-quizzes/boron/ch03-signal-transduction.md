# Mini Quiz — Boron Ch 3: Signal Transduction

_Five questions, ~five minutes. Source: [chapter-03-signal-transduction.md](../../../inputs/textbooks/medical-physiology/chapter-03-signal-transduction.md) (Boron & Boulpaep, *Medical Physiology* 3e, ch. 3, pp. 47–72)._

## 1. Acetylcholine released by a motor nerve terminal onto the nicotinic AChR of a skeletal-muscle endplate is, by Boron's classification, an example of…

A. endocrine signaling.
B. **paracrine signaling** (the nerve terminal releases a chemical that acts on a neighboring cell).
C. autocrine signaling.
D. juxtacrine signaling.

## 2. Which of the following correctly orders the **timescales** of these receptor classes (fastest → slowest)?

A. Nuclear receptor < GPCR < ligand-gated ion channel.
B. **Ligand-gated ion channel (ms) < GPCR (s) < RTK (min) < nuclear receptor (h).**
C. RTK < ligand-gated ion channel < nuclear receptor < GPCR.
D. GPCR < ligand-gated ion channel < nuclear receptor < RTK.

## 3. **Receptor tyrosine kinases (RTKs)** typically signal by…

A. opening a ligand-gated ion channel in milliseconds.
B. activating a heterotrimeric G-protein in the cytosol.
C. **ligand-induced dimerization followed by trans-autophosphorylation** on tyrosine residues, creating docking sites for downstream effectors (Ras-MAPK, PI3K-Akt, PLCγ).
D. binding DNA directly as a ligand-activated transcription factor.

## 4. The IP₃/DAG pathway, triggered by GPCRs coupled to **Gαq → PLCβ**, releases **Ca²⁺** from intracellular stores because…

A. DAG opens plasma-membrane Ca²⁺ channels directly.
B. **IP₃ binds receptors on the endoplasmic reticulum**, opening Ca²⁺-release channels.
C. PKA phosphorylates SERCA, reducing Ca²⁺ reuptake.
D. cAMP activates ryanodine receptors directly.

## 5. **Nuclear receptors** (glucocorticoid, thyroid, vitamin D, estrogen) bind ligands that can…

A. only act after binding a plasma-membrane GPCR.
B. **cross the plasma membrane (lipid-soluble)** — their receptors are cytosolic/nuclear and function as ligand-activated transcription factors.
C. only bind extracellular receptors, like peptide hormones.
D. only act on ion channels, never on transcription.

---

## Answer key (instructor)

1. **B.** Boron is explicit: ACh at the NMJ is paracrine — "the nerve terminal releases a chemical that acts on a neighboring cell." (Fig. 3-2 caption, p. 48.) The trap is calling it juxtacrine, but the signal diffuses across a synaptic cleft, not via direct membrane-to-membrane contact.
2. **B.** ms (ion channels) → s (GPCRs) → min (catalytic receptors / cytokine R) → h (nuclear receptors). (§2, Table 3-1.)
3. **C.** RTKs dimerize on ligand binding, trans-autophosphorylate, and create docking sites for SH2-domain effectors. (§5, p. 60.)
4. **B.** PLCβ cleaves PIP₂ into IP₃ + DAG; IP₃ binds IP₃ receptors on the ER and releases stored Ca²⁺. DAG (not IP₃) activates PKC. (§4, "phosphoinositide breakdown.")
5. **B.** Lipid-soluble hormones cross the membrane; their receptors are intracellular and act as ligand-activated transcription factors. (§6, p. 67.)
