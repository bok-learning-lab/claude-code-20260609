# Mini Quiz — Boron Ch 2: Functional Organization of the Cell

_Five questions, ~five minutes. Source: [chapter-02-functional-organization-of-the-cell.md](../../../inputs/textbooks/medical-physiology/chapter-02-functional-organization-of-the-cell.md) (Boron & Boulpaep, *Medical Physiology* 3e, ch. 2, pp. 8–46)._

## 1. The phospholipid bilayer is **extremely impermeable to charged molecules** because…

A. the head groups are too tightly packed for ions to slip between them.
B. the **hydrophobic acyl-chain core** is energetically prohibitive for charged species (high partition cost).
C. cholesterol blocks transmembrane ion movement.
D. ions are too large to diffuse through the bilayer.

## 2. Cholesterol's effect on bilayer fluidity is best described as…

A. it makes all bilayers more fluid at all temperatures.
B. it makes all bilayers less fluid at all temperatures.
C. **it rigidifies fluid bilayers and fluidizes gel-phase bilayers — a fluidity buffer.**
D. it has no effect on bilayer fluidity in animal cells.

## 3. Which of the following best distinguishes an **integral** from a **peripheral** membrane protein?

A. Integral proteins span both leaflets; peripheral proteins span only one.
B. **Integral proteins are released only by detergents that disrupt the bilayer; peripheral proteins are released by milder treatments (high salt, pH change).**
C. Integral proteins are glycosylated; peripheral proteins are not.
D. Integral proteins are always enzymes; peripheral proteins are always receptors.

## 4. A typical transmembrane α-helical segment in a single-pass membrane protein is approximately…

A. 5–10 hydrophilic residues.
B. **20–25 hydrophobic residues** — the length that matches the bilayer thickness, with backbone H-bonds satisfied internally by the α-helix.
C. 40 charged residues stabilized by salt bridges.
D. 100 residues forming a β-barrel.

## 5. Phosphatidylserine (PS) is normally restricted to the **cytosolic** leaflet of the plasma membrane. Its **exposure on the outer leaflet** is biologically significant because it…

A. increases bilayer fluidity in the outer leaflet.
B. is required for receptor-tyrosine-kinase dimerization.
C. **signals apoptosis** — flagging the cell for recognition and clearance by phagocytes.
D. is needed for cholesterol uptake from circulating LDL.

---

## Answer key (instructor)

1. **B.** The hydrophobic interior of the bilayer is the energetic barrier to charged species — the partition cost dominates. (§1, "Phospholipid bilayer membranes are impermeable to charged molecules.")
2. **C.** Cholesterol is a **fluidity buffer**: rigidifies fluid bilayers, fluidizes gel-phase. This dual action is the chapter's key teaching. (§1, "physical characteristics of a lipid bilayer.")
3. **B.** The operational definition is extraction behavior — peripheral come off with mild treatments; integral require detergent. (§2, "Membrane proteins can be integrally or peripherally associated…")
4. **B.** ~20–25 hydrophobic residues matching the ~3 nm hydrophobic bilayer thickness, with the polar backbone H-bonded internally by the helix. (§2, "membrane-spanning portions of transmembrane proteins are usually hydrophobic α helices.")
5. **C.** Outward-facing PS is an "eat me" signal flagging apoptotic cells for phagocyte clearance. (§1, PS table entry.)
