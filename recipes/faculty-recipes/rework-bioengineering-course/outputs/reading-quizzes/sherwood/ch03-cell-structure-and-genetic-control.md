# Mini Quiz — Sherwood Ch 3: Cell Structure and Genetic Control

_Five questions, ~five minutes. Source: [chapter-03-cell-structure-and-genetic-control.md](../../../inputs/textbooks/human-physiology/chapter-03-cell-structure-and-genetic-control.md) (Fox & Rompolski, *Human Physiology* 16e, ch. 3, pp. 49–85)._

## 1. **Receptor-mediated endocytosis** is distinguished from pinocytosis primarily by…

A. the size of the engulfed material.
B. its requirement for ATP.
C. its **specificity** — a receptor concentrates a particular ligand (e.g., LDL, transferrin) before the membrane invaginates via a clathrin-coated pit.
D. its irreversibility once the vesicle has internalized.

## 2. Which two cytoskeletal elements form the **9 + 2 axoneme** found in cilia and flagella?

A. Microfilaments and intermediate filaments.
B. Actin and tubulin.
C. Microtubules (α/β tubulin polymers) only.
D. Intermediate filaments and keratin.

## 3. **Heterochromatin** differs from euchromatin in that it is…

A. less condensed and transcriptionally active.
B. more condensed and transcriptionally **silent**.
C. found only in the nucleolus.
D. composed of RNA rather than DNA.

## 4. Mitochondria are **maternally inherited** because…

A. paternal mitochondria are excluded from the sperm before fertilization.
B. paternal mitochondria are destroyed after fertilization.
C. the zygote synthesizes new mitochondria de novo using maternal mRNA only.
D. mitochondrial DNA is encoded in the X chromosome.

## 5. The human genome contains approximately **25,000 protein-coding genes**, yet the proteome contains hundreds of thousands of distinct proteins. The textbook attributes this gap chiefly to…

A. very high mutation rates in human cells.
B. **alternative splicing** of pre-mRNA and post-translational modification.
C. duplication of every gene multiple times.
D. translation of intronic sequences.

---

## Answer key (instructor)

1. **C.** The receptor is what makes it specific — and clathrin-coated pits concentrate the ligand ~1000-fold before internalization. Phagocytosis is large-particle; pinocytosis is non-specific bulk fluid uptake. (§3.1, "Endocytosis.")
2. **C.** The 9+2 axoneme is built entirely of microtubules — nine outer doublets surrounding a central pair. Actin is microfilaments (cytokinesis, amoeboid motion), not motile cilia. (§3.2 "Cytoplasm and Cytoskeleton.")
3. **B.** Heterochromatin is densely packed and transcriptionally silent; euchromatin is less condensed and transcriptionally active. (§3.3, Chromatin.)
4. **B.** Sperm mitochondria are **destroyed after fertilization** — the text uses this exact phrasing. (§3.2, "Mitochondria.") Worth flagging for follow-up about MELAS and mitochondrial inheritance patterns.
5. **B.** Alternative splicing (and post-translational modification) explains the gene/proteome gap — the same gene produces many distinct proteins. (§3.3, "Genome and Proteome.")
