# Mini Quiz — Sherwood Ch 1: The Study of Body Function

_Five questions, ~five minutes. Source: [chapter-01-the-study-of-body-function.md](../../../inputs/textbooks/human-physiology/chapter-01-the-study-of-body-function.md) (Fox & Rompolski, *Human Physiology* 16e, ch. 1, pp. 1–23)._

## 1. Walter Cannon's term *homeostasis* is best described in this chapter as…

A. perfect constancy of every internal variable, maintained by the autonomic nervous system.
B. **dynamic** constancy — internal values oscillate around a set point, defended by negative feedback.
C. constancy of the external environment, in which homeostatic mechanisms shield the body.
D. the absence of positive-feedback mechanisms in healthy physiology.

## 2. Which of the following is the textbook's prototypical example of **positive feedback as a component of an otherwise homeostatic process**?

A. Body temperature regulation via sweating vs. shivering.
B. Insulin secretion in response to rising blood glucose.
C. The blood-clotting cascade — each factor activates many others until the bleed is stopped.
D. The baroreceptor reflex restoring blood pressure when standing from supine.

## 3. The textbook describes "control by antagonistic effectors" (e.g. sweating vs. shivering; insulin vs. glucagon) as…

A. mathematically equivalent to a single switching effector.
B. "push–pull" — affording finer regulation than switching one effector on and off.
C. a feature unique to endocrine, not neural, regulation.
D. the reason positive feedback can be tolerated in homeostasis.

## 4. In a phase **III** clinical trial, the drug is…

A. tested on a small group of healthy volunteers to establish toxicity.
B. tested for the first time on the target patient population.
C. tested in a large, multicenter study including sufficient numbers of both sexes and different demographic groups.
D. surveilled in the post-marketing period for late or rare adverse effects.

## 5. The chapter introduces the **set-point arithmetic** used in the Review Activities: given a temperature oscillating between $T_{\min}$ = 36.6 °C and $T_{\max}$ = 37.7 °C, the **set point** and the **normal range** are…

A. set point 36.6 °C; range 37.7 °C.
B. set point 37.15 °C; range 1.1 °C.
C. set point 37.7 °C; range 36.6 °C.
D. set point 37.15 °C; range 74.3 °C.

---

## Answer key (instructor)

1. **B.** Cannon's homeostasis is **dynamic** constancy — the textbook flags this explicitly ("Homeostasis is best conceived as a state of **dynamic constancy** in which conditions are stabilized above and below the set point," p. 6). A is the predictable wrong-answer (over-strict reading of "constancy"). (§1.2.)
2. **C.** The blood-clotting cascade is the textbook's clearest positive-feedback-inside-a-homeostatic-loop example: amplification of clotting halts bleeding, which then closes a larger negative loop. Other positive-feedback examples named are estrogen→LH surge and oxytocin→parturition. (§1.2 "Positive Feedback.")
3. **B.** The text uses the exact phrase "push–pull" — antagonistic effectors afford finer control than on/off switching. (§1.2, p. 7.)
4. **C.** The phase ladder: I = small healthy-volunteer toxicity; II = target patient population; III = large, multicenter, diverse demographics; IV = post-marketing surveillance. The trap is mixing II and III, since both involve patients. (§1.1, p. 4.)
5. **B.** Set point = average of extremes = (36.6 + 37.7)/2 = 37.15 °C; normal range = $T_{\max} - T_{\min}$ = 1.1 °C. The chapter walks this example explicitly. (§1.2, Review Activity arithmetic.)
