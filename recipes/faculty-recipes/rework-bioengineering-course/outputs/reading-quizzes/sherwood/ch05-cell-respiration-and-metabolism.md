# Mini Quiz — Sherwood Ch 5: Cell Respiration and Metabolism

_Five questions, ~five minutes. Source: [chapter-05-cell-respiration-and-metabolism.md](../../../inputs/textbooks/human-physiology/chapter-05-cell-respiration-and-metabolism.md) (Fox & Rompolski, *Human Physiology* 16e, ch. 5, pp. 104–127)._

## 1. The **net** yield of glycolysis from one molecule of glucose is…

A. 4 ATP, 2 NADH, 2 pyruvate.
B. **2 ATP (net), 2 NADH, 2 pyruvate.**
C. 2 ATP, 2 FADH₂, 1 pyruvate.
D. ~30 ATP per glucose.

## 2. Red blood cells are forced to rely on **anaerobic glycolysis (the lactate pathway)** because they…

A. have an unusually high mitochondrial density.
B. **lack mitochondria.**
C. cannot transport pyruvate across the inner mitochondrial membrane.
D. cannot regenerate NAD⁺ from NADH.

## 3. In the citric acid cycle, **each turn** of the cycle produces…

A. 1 ATP (or GTP), 3 NADH, 1 FADH₂, and 2 CO₂.
B. 2 ATP, 2 NADH, 1 FADH₂, and 1 CO₂.
C. 1 ATP, 4 NADH, 0 FADH₂, and 3 CO₂.
D. 0 ATP — all energy is captured downstream as NADH.

## 4. The role of **oxygen** in aerobic respiration is as…

A. the source of carbon for the cycle's intermediates.
B. the proton pumped across the inner mitochondrial membrane to build the gradient.
C. **the final electron acceptor** of the electron-transport chain; without it, NADH/FADH₂ cannot be reoxidized and aerobic respiration halts.
D. the high-energy bond that drives ATP synthase.

## 5. Compared with full aerobic respiration (~30 ATP per glucose), purely **anaerobic** metabolism of glucose to lactate yields approximately…

A. ~30 ATP per glucose.
B. ~15 ATP per glucose.
C. **2 ATP per glucose** — a ~15-fold loss vs. aerobic.
D. 0 ATP per glucose; lactate is a metabolic dead end.

---

## Answer key (instructor)

1. **B.** 2 ATP invested, 4 ATP produced → **net** 2 ATP, plus 2 NADH and 2 pyruvate per glucose. (A) is the gross ATP, not the net — the predictable trap. (§5.1; Fig 5.3.)
2. **B.** No mitochondria → no Krebs cycle or ETC → must regenerate NAD⁺ by lactate fermentation. The chapter calls this out explicitly. (§5.1.)
3. **A.** Per-turn yield: 1 GTP (≡ ATP), 3 NADH, 1 FADH₂, 2 CO₂. One glucose → 2 pyruvates → 2 turns. (§5.2; equation block.)
4. **C.** O₂ is the *final* electron acceptor at complex IV: ½O₂ + 2 e⁻ + 2 H⁺ → H₂O. Without it the ETC backs up, NADH/FADH₂ can't be reoxidized, the citric acid cycle halts, and only anaerobic glycolysis remains. (§5.2, "Function of Oxygen.")
5. **C.** Aerobic ~30 ATP vs. anaerobic ~2 ATP — a roughly 15-fold gain from going aerobic. The chapter uses this number as the central energetic punchline. (§5.1–5.2; Table 5.1.)
