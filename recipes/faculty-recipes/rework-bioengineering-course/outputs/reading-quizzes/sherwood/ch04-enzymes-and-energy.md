# Mini Quiz — Sherwood Ch 4: Enzymes and Energy

_Five questions, ~five minutes. Source: [chapter-04-enzymes-and-energy.md](../../../inputs/textbooks/human-physiology/chapter-04-enzymes-and-energy.md) (Fox & Rompolski, *Human Physiology* 16e, ch. 4, pp. 86–103)._

## 1. Enzymes increase reaction rate by…

A. raising the free-energy change (ΔG) of the reaction.
B. lowering the **activation energy** required for reactants to reach the transition state.
C. shifting the equilibrium constant toward products.
D. supplying ATP directly to the substrate.

## 2. A blood test for a patient with chest pain shows elevated **troponin I**. The chapter's framework explains this as…

A. an inborn error of metabolism affecting cardiac muscle.
B. an isoenzyme of LDH released from skeletal muscle.
C. **leakage of an intracellular enzyme** from damaged myocardial cells into plasma.
D. a coenzyme deficiency caused by low niacin intake.

## 3. **Pepsin** functions optimally at a pH of approximately…

A. 2 (in the stomach lumen).
B. 7.4 (in blood).
C. 8 (in the small intestine).
D. 9.5 (in alkaline saliva).

## 4. A drug binds to the **active site** of an enzyme and competes with the natural substrate. Increasing substrate concentration largely **reverses** the drug's effect. The drug is best classified as a(n)…

A. allosteric activator.
B. **competitive** inhibitor.
C. allosteric inhibitor (its effect is independent of substrate concentration).
D. coenzyme analog acting at a separate regulatory site.

## 5. Phenylketonuria (PKU) is caused by a defect in **phenylalanine hydroxylase**. The downstream consequences in the textbook's pathway diagram include all of the following EXCEPT…

A. accumulation of phenylalanine in the blood.
B. shunting of phenylalanine to phenylpyruvic acid.
C. decreased downstream production of tyrosine via the normal route.
D. accumulation of homogentisic acid (the alkaptonuria phenotype).

---

## Answer key (instructor)

1. **B.** Enzymes don't change ΔG or the equilibrium constant — they lower the activation hump so a much larger fraction of reactant molecules can react at body temperature. Fig 4.1 is the canonical figure. (§4.1.)
2. **C.** The chapter's diagnostic principle: plasma elevations of normally-intracellular enzymes (troponin I/T for MI, AST/ALT for hepatitis, amylase/lipase for pancreatitis) signal cell damage. (§4.1 + Table 4.1.)
3. **A.** Pepsin's optimum is ~pH 2 (gastric). Trypsin/chymotrypsin (small intestine) ~ pH 8. The text lists pH optima in Table 4.3. (§4.2.)
4. **B.** Competitive inhibitors bind the active site and mimic substrate; raising [S] outcompetes them. Allosteric inhibitors bind elsewhere and **cannot** be overcome by raising [S]. (§4.2, "Allosteric vs. competitive inhibitors.")
5. **D.** PKU shunts phenylalanine to phenylpyruvic acid (A & B), starves the downstream tyrosine pathway (C), but does **not** affect homogentisic acid — that's alkaptonuria, a different inborn error of metabolism in the same family of disorders. The trap is conflating two diseases in the same figure cluster. (§4.2, Fig 4.10–4.11.)
