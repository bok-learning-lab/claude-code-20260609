## 5. Which complementary base pair in DNA is held together by **three hydrogen bonds**?

A. A–T
B. G–C
C. A–U
D. T–G

---

## Answer key (instructor)

1. **B.** pH = −log[H⁺]; at pH 7.40, [H⁺] = 10⁻⁷·⁴ ≈ 4 × 10⁻⁸ M. The trap is C (pH-7 water, the wrong pH) and D (mistaking the exponent). (§2.1, pH equation; worked example.)
2. **B.** Secondary structure (α-helix, β-sheet) is stabilized by backbone H-bonds. Primary is covalent peptide bonds; tertiary uses side-chain interactions; quaternary is subunit assembly. (§2.3 "Proteins"; Fig 2.28.)
3. **A.** The chapter explicitly flags that thalidomide racemizes in vivo, which is the failure mode of giving "just the safe enantiomer." (§2.1, Stereoisomers; Clinical Investigation Brian.)
4. **B.** Cis double bonds introduce a bend; trans double bonds (industrial hydrogenation byproducts) are straight and pack like saturated fats, which is why they elevate cardiovascular risk. (§2.2, Lipids; Fig 2.19.)
5. **B.** G–C: three hydrogen bonds. A–T: two hydrogen bonds. The text uses the exact pairing pattern (G–C triple, T–A double) in Fig 2.32 / 2.33. A–U is RNA, not DNA. (§2.4; Fig 2.32–2.33.)
