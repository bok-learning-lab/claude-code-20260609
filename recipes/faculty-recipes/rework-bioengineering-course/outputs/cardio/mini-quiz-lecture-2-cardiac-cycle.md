# Mini Quiz — Cardio Lecture 2: Cardiac Cycle & Wigger's Diagram

_Five questions, ~five minutes. Source: [ES53_Sept24_Lecture_Cardio2_2025.md](../../inputs/course-pack/ES%2053%20Lectures%202025/Cardio/ES53_Sept24_Lecture_Cardio2_2025.md) and the [Cardio Learning Objectives handout](../../inputs/course-pack/ES%2053%20Lectures%202025/Cardio/ES53_Handout_Cardio_LearningObjectivesandExercises_2025.md)._

## 1. During isovolumetric contraction, which of the following best describes the state of the four cardiac valves?

A. Mitral and aortic open; tricuspid and pulmonic closed.
B. All four valves closed.
C. Mitral and tricuspid open; aortic and pulmonic closed.
D. Aortic and pulmonic open; mitral and tricuspid closed.

## 2. A patient has EDV = 130 mL, ESV = 65 mL, and HR = 80 bpm. What is the ejection fraction (rounded)?

A. 35%
B. 50%
C. 65%
D. 80%

## 3. Using the same patient (EDV 130, ESV 65, HR 80), what is the cardiac output?

A. 3.9 L/min
B. 5.2 L/min
C. 7.8 L/min
D. 10.4 L/min

## 4. A patient's blood pressure is 132 / 78 mmHg. Their mean arterial pressure is closest to…

A. 80 mmHg
B. 96 mmHg
C. 105 mmHg
D. 120 mmHg

## 5. The first heart sound ("lub") is produced by…

A. opening of the aortic and pulmonic valves at the start of ejection.
B. closing of the mitral and tricuspid valves at the start of isovolumetric contraction.
C. blood striking the closed aortic valve during isovolumetric relaxation.
D. atrial contraction at the end of diastole.

---

## Answer key (instructor)

1. **B.** Isovolumetric contraction is, by definition, the interval when LV pressure has risen above LA pressure (mitral closed) but is still below aortic pressure (aortic still closed) — and the same is true on the right side. No blood moves. (Lecture & objectives, "Cardiac Cycle.")
2. **B.** SV = 130 − 65 = 65 mL. EF = SV/EDV = 65/130 = 0.50 = 50%. The trap is reading "65" as the EF, but 65 is the SV in mL. (Equations: SV, EF.)
3. **B.** CO = SV × HR = 65 mL × 80 bpm = 5200 mL/min = 5.2 L/min. (Equation: CO.)
4. **B.** MAP ≈ DP + (1/3)·PP = 78 + (1/3)(132−78) = 78 + 18 = 96 mmHg. The diastole-weighted formula matters because diastole is roughly twice as long as systole at resting heart rates — averaging 132 and 78 (which gives 105) is the predictable wrong answer. (Equation: MAP.)
5. **B.** "Lub" = AV valves slamming shut at the start of isovolumetric contraction. "Dub" = semilunar valves closing at the start of isovolumetric relaxation. (Lecture; objectives §II.)
