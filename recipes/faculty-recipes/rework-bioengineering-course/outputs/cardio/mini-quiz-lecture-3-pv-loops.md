# Mini Quiz — Cardio Lecture 3: Pressure–Volume Loops, Preload, and Afterload

_Five questions, ~five minutes. Source: [ES53_Sep26_Lecture_Cardio3_2025.md](../../inputs/course-pack/ES%2053%20Lectures%202025/Cardio/ES53_Sep26_Lecture_Cardio3_2025.md) and the [Cardio Learning Objectives handout](../../inputs/course-pack/ES%2053%20Lectures%202025/Cardio/ES53_Handout_Cardio_LearningObjectivesandExercises_2025.md)._

## 1. On a left-ventricular pressure–volume loop, the aortic valve opens when…

A. LV pressure reaches its peak (~120 mmHg).
B. LV pressure rises to equal aortic diastolic pressure (~80 mmHg).
C. LV volume reaches EDV.
D. the mitral valve closes.

## 2. A patient's pre-load suddenly increases (e.g. rapid IV fluid bolus). Holding contractility and after-load constant, you would expect…

A. EDV to rise, SV to rise (Frank–Starling), and the PV loop to widen.
B. EDV to rise, SV to fall, and the PV loop to narrow.
C. ESV to fall, SV to rise, and the PV loop to narrow.
D. EDV to stay constant, SV to rise, and the PV loop to shift upward.

## 3. The area enclosed by the left-ventricular PV loop represents…

A. cardiac output per minute.
B. ejection fraction.
C. stroke work done by the left ventricle per beat.
D. total peripheral resistance.

## 4. A patient develops severe aortic stenosis. Compared to baseline, you would expect…

A. lower after-load and a wider PV loop.
B. higher after-load, a taller and narrower PV loop, increased ESV, and decreased SV.
C. lower pre-load and decreased contractility.
D. an unchanged PV loop but a faster heart rate.

## 5. The Frank–Starling Law of the Heart states that…

A. heart rate rises proportionally to mean arterial pressure.
B. stroke volume falls when the ventricle is overfilled, due to actin–myosin overlap loss.
C. an increase in venous return (and thus EDV) increases the force of the next contraction by stretching sarcomeres toward optimal overlap, increasing stroke volume.
D. cardiac output is determined entirely by sympathetic nervous-system tone.

---

## Answer key (instructor)

1. **B.** The LV must build pressure above aortic *diastolic* pressure (≈80 mmHg) for the aortic valve to open and ejection to begin. Peak LV pressure (120 mmHg) is reached *during* ejection, not at its onset. This is the most-missed feature when students draw a PV loop. (Lecture & objectives §III, Exercise 3.)
2. **A.** Increased pre-load → increased EDV → longer sarcomere length → more cross-bridge cycling → larger SV (Frank–Starling). The PV loop widens to the right (larger EDV, similar or slightly lower ESV). (Objectives §III; Exercise 4a.)
3. **C.** The area enclosed by the PV loop equals stroke work per beat: SW ≈ SV × mean aortic pressure. (Objectives §III; equation: SW.)
4. **B.** Aortic stenosis raises the pressure the LV must generate to open the aortic valve (after-load). The PV loop becomes taller (higher pressure during ejection) and narrower (more residual volume → higher ESV → smaller SV). (Objectives §III; Exercise 4b.)
5. **C.** Frank–Starling is an intrinsic, beat-by-beat mechanism: ↑venous return → ↑filling → ↑pre-load → ↑sarcomere length → ↑force → ↑SV. It does not require neural input. (Objectives §III; "Frank-Starling Law of the Heart.")
