# Mini Quiz — Cardio Lecture 1: Anatomy, Conduction, and the ECG

_Five questions, ~five minutes. Source: [ES53_Sept22_Lecture_Cardio1_2025.md](../../inputs/course-pack/ES%2053%20Lectures%202025/Cardio/ES53_Sept22_Lecture_Cardio1_2025.md) and the [Cardio Learning Objectives handout](../../inputs/course-pack/ES%2053%20Lectures%202025/Cardio/ES53_Handout_Cardio_LearningObjectivesandExercises_2025.md)._

## 1. The AV node delay (roughly 100 ms between atrial and ventricular depolarization) is essential because it…

A. allows the ventricles to repolarize fully before the next atrial signal arrives.
B. lets the atria empty into the ventricles before ventricular contraction begins.
C. synchronizes the SA node with the bundle branches.
D. permits the Purkinje fibers to reach their threshold potential.

## 2. A patient's ECG shows P waves at a regular 75 / min and QRS complexes at a regular 38 / min, with no fixed relationship between them. The most likely conduction abnormality is…

A. atrial fibrillation.
B. first-degree AV block.
C. complete (third-degree) AV block, with a ventricular escape rhythm.
D. ventricular tachycardia.

## 3. The S–T segment of a normal ECG corresponds in time to…

A. atrial depolarization.
B. atrial repolarization.
C. the plateau phase of the ventricular action potential.
D. ventricular repolarization.

## 4. Cardiac muscle cannot be tetanized. The underlying reason is that…

A. the cardiac action potential has an unusually long absolute refractory period that lasts for most of the contraction.
B. cardiac muscle lacks the troponin–tropomyosin system that allows summation in skeletal muscle.
C. the SA node fires too slowly for the contractions to fuse.
D. the AV delay prevents successive depolarizations from reaching the ventricles in time.

## 5. The electrocardiogram records…

A. the action potential of the SA node.
B. the mechanical contraction of the ventricles measured at the chest wall.
C. body-surface voltage produced by waves of depolarization moving through cardiac tissue.
D. the change in intracellular Ca²⁺ during the cardiac cycle.

---

## Answer key (instructor)

1. **B.** The AV delay's functional purpose is mechanical: it allows atrial systole (the final ~20% of ventricular filling) to complete before the ventricles contract. (A) confuses the AV delay with the absolute refractory period. (Lecture, "Conduction pathways of the heart"; Objectives §3.)
2. **C.** AV dissociation — independent atrial and ventricular rhythms with the ventricular rate consistent with a junctional or ventricular escape (~20–40 bpm) — is the signature of complete heart block. AFib would show no P waves at all; first-degree block preserves 1:1 conduction with a long PR. (Objectives §5; "Arrhythmias detected by the ECG.")
3. **C.** The S–T segment is the isoelectric stretch between QRS and T — when ventricular myocytes are all depolarized (plateau phase) and no net current is flowing across the body surface. (Objectives §4; "Waves and intervals you should know.")
4. **A.** The long plateau-driven refractory period of the cardiac AP outlasts the contraction itself, so no second AP can fire before the contraction ends — fusion of contractions is mechanically impossible. (Objectives §7, "Refractory period.")
5. **C.** The ECG does **not** record action potentials or mechanical contraction directly — it picks up the body-surface voltage that results from waves of depolarization moving through tissue. This is the clarifying line the handout flags explicitly. (Handout: "Does not record action potentials, but results from waves of depolarization.")
