# **Course Description**

This course invites students to explore current events and evolving media landscapes across the Francophone world through a multimodal and intercultural lens. We will examine how identity, current events, and representation circulate in traditional and digital media \- from print journalism and podcasts to memes, reels, and AI-generated content. Grounded in multiliteracies pedagogy and experiential learning, this course integrates the four core language skills (listening, speaking, reading, and writing) with digital storytelling, journalistic writing, podcast production, and media critique. We will engage with materials from diverse Francophone regions \- Francophone North America, the Caribbean, Sub-Saharan Africa, France, and beyond \- while developing critical awareness of voice, authorship, and ethics in the age of generative media. Students will produce original multimodal work, including podcast interviews, digital editorials, and a final multimodal project. Readings include journalistic and literary texts, while assignments foreground collaboration, intercultural analysis, and design thinking. No prior experience with digital tools is required \- only curiosity, empathy, and creativity.

**Course Notes**: Conducted in French. May not be taken Pass/Fail, but may be taken Sat/Unsat by graduate students. Not open to auditors. This course is taught by members of the Department. 

# **Course Objectives** 

By the end of the course, you will be able to:

- analyze contemporary Francophone media using critical frameworks of identity, language, and representation,  
- interpret diverse media formats (written press, social media, audiovisual storytelling, AI-generated content) with attention to form, purpose, and sociocultural context,  
- express nuanced ideas in French through spontaneous dialogue, structured presentations, and journalistic writing,  
- employ responsible and ethical digital practices, including the use of generative tools and multimedia publishing platforms,  
- compare how current events are perceived and represented across Francophone regions, incorporating intercultural perspectives,  
- create and revise multimodal compositions in French, including audio journalism, short-form writing, and visual storytelling,  
- evaluate the authority, creation process, and value of Francophone information sources across print and digital formats, including state-sponsored cultural initiatives, commercial platforms, library collections, and AI-generated content, and   
- contribute responsibly to Francophone media conversations as both critical readers and ethical creators.

**No Late Work is Accepted**

**Class Participation and Homework (15%)**

Students are expected to come to class prepared, having completed the assigned readings, listening, writing, and other preparatory work before each class session. Participation includes active engagement in French during class discussions, pair work, group activities, and other in-class tasks. Because this is a language course, regular preparation and sustained effort are essential to progress.

**Media Journals (10%)**

Students will complete short media journal entries throughout the semester in response to assigned Francophone media. Most journal entries will be written in class and will emphasize reflection, observation, and careful language use. These assignments are designed to strengthen reading comprehension, vocabulary acquisition, and written expression while encouraging students to engage critically with authentic media.

**Podcasts (10%)**

Students will create three podcast assignments over the course of the semester. These recordings will allow students to practice oral fluency, pronunciation, organization, and extended spoken expression in French. Each podcast will respond to a course theme and will increase in complexity over the semester. One podcast will be completed as part of the final multimodal project, but will be counted in this category.

**Writing Assignments (15%)**

Students will complete three major writing assignments in French during the semester. Rédactions 1 and 2 will be journalistic compositions submitted in draft and final form, while Rédaction 3 will be a reflective capstone writing assignment submitted without a draft. These assignments will be evaluated for clarity, grammatical accuracy, vocabulary, organization, content development, and attention to audience and genre. Because the three assignments serve different rhetorical purposes, the specific expectations for each will be provided on the assignment sheets. These assignments should not use AI-generated nor translated texts; however, the use of dictionaries and thesauri is encouraged.

**Exams (30%)**

There will be three in-class exams during the semester, each approximately 30 minutes in length. Exams will assess students' ability to understand and use the vocabulary, grammar, and communicative structures developed in the course. They may include listening comprehension, reading comprehension, grammar and vocabulary exercises, and short writing or speaking tasks.

**Final Multimodal Media Project (20%)**

The final project will ask students to synthesize the course's language, media, and cultural work in a polished multimodal composition. The project will include a final presentation, supporting materials, and a reflective component in French. Students will be evaluated on the quality of their content, organization, language use, originality, visual and/or audio design, and ability to respond thoughtfully to questions during the presentation.

**Information Literacy and the Library Partnership**  
This course treats information literacy as inseparable from language learning. As you analyze, produce, and publish Francophone media in French, you will be asked to evaluate where they come from, how they were made, whose voices are speaking, and what interests shape their circulation. Our approach draws on the Association of College and Research Libraries' Framework for Information Literacy for Higher Education, with particular attention to four threshold concepts:

- **Authority Is Constructed and Contextual:** recognizing that authority in the Francophone media ecosystem is claimed and contested differently by state ministries, commercial platforms, community media, and diasporic creators.  
- **Information Creation as a Process:** understanding that the podcast, the print journal, the Instagram carousel, and the AI-generated image each emerge from distinct creation processes that shape meaning.  
- **Information Has Value:** examining how Francophone information circulates as commodity, cultural good, and civic resource, and how some voices have more discoverability within these systems.  
- **Searching as Strategic Exploration:** developing the disposition to seek expert guidance, browse with purpose, and treat research as an ongoing process.

   
We are fortunate to collaborate with [Odile Harter](https://library.harvard.edu/staff/odile-harter), Head of Library Engagement and Experience (FAS Libraries) and liaison librarian to Romance Languages and Literatures. Odile co-designs and leads our Widener Library session at the end of Unit 4, where you will work hands-on with Francophone print collections and archives. You are encouraged to consult her as a source of expert guidance throughout the semester; seeking help from librarians is itself a hallmark of information-literate practice.  
   
**Digital Tools and Platforms**  
This course emphasizes the responsible, creative, and ethical use of digital technologies to support French language acquisition, critical thinking, and media literacy. You will be introduced to a suite of tools that help you plan, design, produce, and reflect on media across different formats. 

No previous experience with these tools is required, and workshops will support your use of these tools and practice with them in French:

- You will receive hands-on tutorials and walkthroughs in the Learning Lab at the Bok Center, and will participate in a brief AI & Digital Ethics workshop covering authorship, transparency, and responsible engagement in creative media production.  
- You will be asked not only to use these tools, but to reflect on how and why you use them. What does it mean to collaborate with AI as a language learner? How do we preserve authenticity in digital storytelling? These questions will guide our creative journey throughout the semester.

| 01 | Enquêter : qui, quoi, où, quand, pourquoi?*Investigating: Who, What, Where, When, Why, How?*  Linguistic Functions: Asking and answering questions, expressing facts and uncertainty, reporting observed events, expressing curiosity and initial reactions  Grammar Focus: Formes interrogatives, verbes déclaratifs et d'opinion, expressions de nécessité (il faut que), subjonctif avec l'émotion et le doute  Sustainable Development Goals: SDG 16 \- Paix, justice et institutions efficaces ; SDG 4 \- Éducation de qualité  Core Skills: How can students formulate questions and report basic facts with accuracy and clarity? What strategies help distinguish between verified information and speculation? How can students begin expressing emotion and perspective while maintaining journalistic objectivity?  Overview: This unit introduces the fundamentals of inquiry and reporting in French. Students explore basic media formats, develop skills for asking clear and relevant questions, and learn how to convey factual information alongside personal reactions. Through a series of short listening, speaking, and writing tasks \- including interviews and article analysis \- students begin to engage with core journalistic practices. The unit also introduces podcasting as a tool for oral expression, culminating in a brief recorded report on a current topic of interest. Students also begin to explore how journalistic practices and press freedoms differ across Francophone regions, thinking critically about media within diverse sociocultural contexts.  Geographies: Afrique de l'Ouest, France, Amérique du Nord, Caraïbes (vue d'ensemble) |

| 02 | **Raconter des lieux qui changent : le journalisme local et l'environnement *Talking about Places That Are Changing: Local Journalism and the Environment* Linguistic Functions:** Narrating past events, describing people and places, organizing information chronologically and thematically, developing clarity and cohesion in written language  **Grammar Focus:** Passé composé, imparfait, plus-que-parfait, adjectifs qualificatifs, articles définis/indéfinis, marqueurs temporels et connecteurs logiques  **Sustainable Development Goals:** SDG 13 \- Action climatique ; SDG 14 \- Vie aquatique ; SDG 11 \- Villes et communautés durables  **Core Skills:** How do Francophone journalists structure and convey local stories about environmental change? What language and stylistic choices help readers visualize places and understand the stakes of climate reporting? How can students develop descriptive and narrative writing skills through analysis and imitation?  **Overview:** This unit focuses on written narration and description as practiced in local Francophone environmental journalism. Students explore how narrative structure, detail, and tone contribute to a clear and engaging presentation of change over time. By reading journalistic and institutional texts across three Francophone geographies, students analyze the use of past tenses, descriptive vocabulary, and temporal sequencing. They apply these techniques in their own writing, producing a short journalistic article that follows genre conventions. The unit emphasizes precision, coherence, and the effective use of language to describe places, people, and environmental events. Students also connect local stories to global frameworks through the UN Sustainable Development Goals.  **Geographies:** France (Paris), Sahel, Pacifique francophone |
| 03 | **Les enjeux \- le monde diplomatique et économique *What's at Stake: The Diplomatic and Economic World*  Linguistic Functions:** Expressing and supporting opinions, constructing logical arguments, using conditionals, responding to differing viewpoints, analyzing economic and diplomatic discourse in Francophone media  **Grammar Focus:** Conditionnel présent et passé, subordonnées causales et concessives, structures d'opinion (je trouve que, il est important que…), connecteurs logiques de l'argumentation (car, donc, cependant)  **Sustainable Development Goals:** SDG 8 \- Travail décent et croissance économique ; SDG 10 \- Inégalités réduites ; SDG 17 \- Partenariats pour la réalisation des objectifs  **Core Skills:** How do Francophone journalists cover economic and diplomatic stories, and how does that differ from political or environmental reporting? What is the relationship between the French language and international economic institutions? How can students analyze and argue about resource allocation, partnership, and equity across Francophone contexts?  **Overview:** In this unit, students examine how Francophone media cover economic and diplomatic issues, with a focus on the relationship between language, power, and resource allocation. Taking on the role of interns at the Représentation de la Francophonie at the United Nations, students analyze three Francophone models of sustainable development \- Paris, Montréal, and Brazzaville \- and produce journalistic and analytical texts that argue a position on questions of economic justice and international partnership. Through editorials, structured debates, and podcast production, students learn to identify argument structures and rhetorical strategies in economic and diplomatic media, and to deploy them in their own writing and speech. The unit emphasizes clarity, logic, and the ability to argue persuasively across registers \- journalistic, diplomatic, and civic.  **Geographies:** France (Paris), Canada (Québec, Montréal), République du Congo (Brazzaville) |
| 04 | **Créer \- Culture, identité et engagement *Creating: Culture, Identity and Engagement*  Linguistic functions:** Comparing styles and periods; reporting what others say (and identifying who is speaking for whom); expressing a cultural preference; analyzing an image; taking a position in a debate on culture and authenticity.  **Grammatical points:** The comparative and superlative; indirect speech.  **Sustainable Development Goals:** SDG 12 \- Consommation et production responsables ; SDG 10 \- Inégalités réduites ; SDG 4 \- Éducation de qualité  **Core Skills:** How do Francophone creators use culture (fashion, music, digital media) as a form of civic and political engagement? What responsibilities do content creators have in ethical communication across digital platforms? How can students use creative media to inform, challenge, and engage rather than manipulate?  **Overview:** In this unit, students examine how culture, identity, and creative expression function as forms of engagement in Francophone spaces, from the sapeurs of Brazzaville to digital influencer culture across the francophone world. Through podcast production, peer presentations, and multimodal critique, students explore the persuasive power of visual and audio storytelling and its ethical limits. They analyze tone, emotional appeal, and audience while practicing diplomatic disagreement, constructive commentary, and ethical influence. The unit connects cultural production to questions of representation, authenticity, and responsibility in Francophone media, culminating in a creative media project that puts students' own voices in conversation with the traditions they have studied.  **Geographies:** République du Congo (Brazzaville), \+ géographies au choix des étudiant.e.s |
| 05 | **La voix engagée : journalisme audio dans la Francophonie *The Engaged Voice: Francophone Audio Journalism*  Linguistic Functions:** Formulating interview questions that invite substantive response; reporting a source's words faithfully and without distortion; presenting a francophone subject to a general audience through a scripted introduction; reacting spontaneously to peer questions by reformulating and nuancing in real time.  **Grammar Focus:** Structures interrogatives et de relance (*qu'est-ce qui, dans quelle mesure, comment se fait-il que* \+ subjonctif) ; discours indirect libre au passé (concordance des temps) ; verbes introducteurs comme choix éditorial (*affirmer, souligner, déplorer, reconnaître*) ; conditionnel de suggestion et d'hypothèse ; subjonctif de nécessité (rappel).  **Sustainable Development Goals:** SDG 10 \- Inégalités réduites ; SDG 16 \- Paix, justice et institutions efficaces ; SDG 4 \- Éducation de qualité. **Core Skills:** How do francophone journalists choose an angle, find sources, and present a subject in audio and how do those choices reflect ethical responsibilities toward the people they cover? What does it mean for a francophone cultural voice to be undiscoverable, and what institutional, legal, and journalistic responses exist? How can students produce audio journalism in French that centers a francophone source faithfully, in a register that invites response rather than closing it?  **Overview:** This unit introduces students to the craft of francophone audio journalism, taking up the question of cultural *découvrabilité* : who gets heard in the francophone world, and why. Working as interns at a francophone audio outlet of their choice (France Culture, RFI, Radio-Canada Première, or another), students analyze two authentic francophone texts: a policy brief from the Organisation internationale de la Francophonie on the algorithmic invisibility of francophone content, and a transcribed extract from a francophone audio broadcast.  Through close reading, active listening, and structured production work, students develop the skills to ask questions that open rather than close a conversation, to report a source's words with precision and respect, and to script and record their own voice in French. The unit's production arc builds directly from the Widener Library visit that closes Unit 4: students identify a francophone subject (archival, cultural, political, or artistic) that merits coverage, and develop it into a 3-4 minute audio piece with a scripted introduction, an interview-driven or source-constructed central segment, and a closing that leaves the listener with an opening rather than a conclusion.  A workshop at the Bok Center Learning Lab gives students the experience of hearing their own recorded voices in French and revising their register accordingly. The unit culminates in the *Projet final multimodal* \- a public audio presentation with a visual companion and written source annotation \- in which students present their piece to the class and respond to peer questions using the concession and reformulation structures developed across the semester. The unit asks not only what stories are told in francophone media, but who gets to tell them, in what register, and with what responsibility toward the voices they carry.  **Geographies:** La Francophonie globale ; Canada (CRTC, Radio-Canada) ; France (OIF, France Culture) ; géographies au choix des étudiant.e.s. |
