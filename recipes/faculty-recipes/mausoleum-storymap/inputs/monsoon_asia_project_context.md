# Monsoon Asia: Art, Architecture, and Environment — Final Project Context

## Course Overview

**Course focus:** South and Southeast Asia (5th–16th centuries)
**Core question:** How were artistic means harnessed to conceptualize, cope with, and tame environmental challenges posed by monsoons and long-distance travel in pre-modern times?

The course examines the common patterns and traits of art and architecture across Monsoon Asia — the region where climates are determined or profoundly affected by the monsoon, stretching from the Himalayas to the islands of the Indian Ocean and the Japanese archipelago.

Key thematic threads include:
- Water management and water symbolism
- Temples as markers of auspicious arrivals
- Itinerant objects (ivories, amulets) moving with travelers across Monsoon Asia
- Ports as nodes connecting hidden narratives of long-distance travel
- Environmental challenge and artistic response

The course explicitly challenges the **diffusionist model** of influence in understanding trans-regional interactions, instead foregrounding interconnected, networked histories — explored through digital tools.

## Final Project: "Mapping Visual and Material Culture of Monsoon Asia"

### Platform
**Scalar** (current) — a collaborative digital humanities publishing platform used to compile data and present findings, including annotated maps and research pages.

> **Note for prototype work:** This platform choice is under review. A new version of this assignment may migrate to or integrate other tools.

### Project Tracks (choose one)
- **a. Objects / Motifs** — identify recurring visual motifs or object types; locate on map
- **b. Sites / Monuments** — identify built heritage sites; locate and document in geographic context
- **c. Routes / Traded Goods** — trace movement of goods along maritime/overland routes

Students go on a **"treasure hunt"** — finding examples in their chosen track using digital museum and library collections, and pinning them to a map with annotations.

### Deliverables and Weights

| Component | Weight | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Group concept-note (choice of motifs/objects/sites/routes) | 5% | Collaborative |
| Anatomy of the Tamil Buddha at MFA | 10% | 600–800 words; individual close-looking exercise |
| Entries (using provided template) | 40% | Core research output |
| Reflection essay | 5% | 500–600 words |

## Context for Prototype / Redesign Work

This document provides background for developing a **new version of the final assignment**, potentially using Claude Code to rework the project infrastructure. Key considerations:

- The **three-track structure** (objects/motifs, sites/monuments, routes/traded goods) is a keeper
- The **treasure hunt + map annotation** model is central to the pedagogy
- The **Scalar platform** is the current solution but is open to revision
- The **Tamil Buddha at MFA close-looking exercise** anchors individual analytical skill-building
- Digital tool literacy (annotated maps, database entries, digital art history methods) is a stated learning goal — any new version should preserve and ideally deepen this
- The course explicitly resists diffusionist frameworks — any organizational or visualization logic should reflect **networked/relational** rather than hierarchical or origin-based models
