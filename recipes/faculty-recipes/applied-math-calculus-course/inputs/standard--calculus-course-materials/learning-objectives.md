# Math 1b — Learning Objectives by Unit

These objectives describe what a student should be able to *do* at the end of each unit. They are intended to guide studying and self-assessment.

---

## Unit 1: Integration Techniques

- Apply integration by parts to products of functions, including repeated application and the tabular method.
- Evaluate trigonometric integrals involving powers of sine, cosine, tangent, and secant.
- Use trigonometric substitution to evaluate integrals containing √(a²−x²), √(a²+x²), and √(x²−a²).
- Decompose a rational function into partial fractions and integrate each term.
- Determine convergence or divergence of improper integrals using limits; apply the comparison test.
- Select an appropriate integration technique given an unfamiliar integral, and explain that choice.

---

## Unit 2: Applications of Integration

- Set up and evaluate integrals to find areas between curves using both vertical and horizontal slicing.
- Compute volumes of solids of revolution using the disk/washer method and the shell method.
- Calculate arc length and surface area of revolution.
- Set up integrals for physical quantities: work done by a variable force, hydrostatic force, center of mass.
- Interpret a probability density function; compute probabilities and expected values using integration.

---

## Unit 3: Sequences and Series

- Determine whether a sequence converges, and find its limit when it does.
- Apply the Monotone Convergence Theorem to establish convergence of bounded monotone sequences.
- Recognize and sum geometric and telescoping series.
- Apply the following convergence tests correctly, including verifying all hypotheses: divergence test, integral test, direct comparison, limit comparison, ratio test, root test, alternating series test.
- Distinguish absolute convergence from conditional convergence and state consequences of each.
- Choose an appropriate convergence test for a given series and justify the choice.

---

## Unit 4: Power Series and Taylor Series

- Find the radius and interval of convergence of a power series using the ratio test; check endpoints separately.
- Differentiate and integrate a power series term-by-term within the interval of convergence.
- Write the Taylor series for a function centered at a given point from scratch using the definition.
- Reproduce from memory the Maclaurin series for eˣ, sin x, cos x, ln(1+x), 1/(1−x), and (1+x)^k.
- Derive new series by substituting into, differentiating, integrating, or multiplying known series.
- Apply Taylor's theorem to bound the error in a Taylor polynomial approximation.
- Use series to evaluate limits and integrals that resist other techniques.

---

## Unit 5: Differential Equations

- Sketch and interpret a direction field; describe the qualitative behavior of solutions.
- Apply Euler's method to approximate the solution to an IVP numerically.
- Identify and solve separable first-order ODEs; check solutions by substitution.
- Set up and solve exponential growth/decay, logistic growth, and Newton's Law of Cooling problems.
- Solve a linear first-order ODE using the integrating factor method.
- Set up and solve mixing/compartment problems using first-order linear ODEs.
- Find the general solution to a second-order linear homogeneous ODE with constant coefficients in all three cases (distinct real, repeated, and complex roots).
- Apply the method of undetermined coefficients to solve non-homogeneous second-order equations.
- Interpret the spring-mass (harmonic oscillator) model in terms of underdamping, overdamping, critical damping, and resonance.

---

## Cross-Cutting Skills

Throughout the course, students develop the following transferable abilities:

- **Successive approximation:** Replace a hard problem with a sequence of easier approximations; quantify and control error.
- **Multiple representations:** Move fluidly between symbolic, graphical, and numerical descriptions of the same object.
- **Mathematical modeling:** Translate a real-world situation into a mathematical problem, solve it, and interpret the solution in context.
- **Justification and proof:** Write clear, logically complete arguments; distinguish a computation from an explanation.
