# Math 1b — Single-Variable Calculus II: Syllabus

## Course Overview

Math 1b continues the study of single-variable calculus begun in Math 1a. The course develops three major topics: integration techniques, infinite series, and differential equations. A unifying theme is **successive approximation**: representing complicated objects as limits of simpler ones, controlling error, and building models of real-world phenomena.

**Prerequisites:** Math 1a or equivalent (derivatives, basic integration, Fundamental Theorem of Calculus).

---

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

1. Apply advanced integration techniques to evaluate definite and indefinite integrals.
2. Use integrals to compute geometric quantities (area, volume, arc length) and physical quantities (work, center of mass).
3. Determine convergence or divergence of sequences and series using standard tests.
4. Represent functions as power series and Taylor series, and estimate truncation error.
5. Formulate, solve, and interpret first- and second-order differential equations arising in scientific models.
6. Connect symbolic, graphical, and numerical representations when reasoning about each topic.

## Course Schedule (14 Weeks)

### Unit 1: Integration Techniques (Weeks 1–3)

| Week | Topics |
|------|--------|
| 1 | Review: FTC, substitution; integration by parts |
| 2 | Trigonometric integrals and trigonometric substitution |
| 3 | Partial fractions; improper integrals |

### Unit 2: Applications of Integration (Weeks 4–5)

| Week | Topics |
|------|--------|
| 4 | Areas between curves; volumes by slicing and shells |
| 5 | Arc length; work and physical applications; probability density functions |

### Unit 3: Sequences and Series (Weeks 6–9)

| Week | Topics |
|------|--------|
| 6 | Sequences: limits, monotone convergence theorem |
| 7 | Series: geometric and telescoping; divergence test; integral test |
| 8 | Comparison, limit comparison, ratio, and root tests |
| 9 | Alternating series; absolute vs. conditional convergence |

### Unit 4: Power Series and Taylor Series (Weeks 10–11)

| Week | Topics |
|------|--------|
| 10 | Power series: radius and interval of convergence; differentiation and integration of power series |
| 11 | Taylor and Maclaurin series; Taylor's theorem with remainder; applications and approximation |

### Unit 5: Differential Equations (Weeks 12–14)

| Week | Topics |
|------|--------|
| 12 | Introduction to ODEs; separable equations; exponential growth and decay |
| 13 | Linear first-order equations; mixing and population models |
| 14 | Second-order linear equations with constant coefficients; harmonic oscillators; review |
