# Math 1b — Reading List and Resources

## Primary Textbook

**Stewart, James.** *Calculus: Early Transcendentals.* Cengage, 9th ed. (2020).
- Ch. 7: Techniques of Integration
- Ch. 8: Further Applications of Integration
- Ch. 11: Infinite Sequences and Series
- Ch. 9: Differential Equations

This is the standard reference for all lecture topics. Students should read the relevant sections before each lecture.

---

## Free Online Textbooks

**Boelkins, Matthew.** *Active Calculus Single Variable.* (freely available at activecalculus.org)
- Emphasizes conceptual understanding through activities and preview exercises
- Excellent complement to Stewart for students who want a more inquiry-based presentation

**Strang, Gilbert, and Edwin Herman.** *Calculus Volume 2.* OpenStax (free at openstax.org/books/calculus-volume-2)
- Covers all course topics with worked examples and conceptual explanations
- Good alternative reference for students who find Stewart's presentation too terse

---

## Supplementary Reading by Topic

### Integration Techniques
- Stewart §7.1–7.5, 7.8
- Boelkins §5.3–5.5
- Paul's Online Math Notes (tutorial.math.lamar.edu): "Integration Techniques" — concise worked examples

### Series and Convergence
- Stewart §11.1–11.8
- Apostol, Tom M. *Calculus, Vol. 1*, Ch. 10 — more rigorous treatment for students wanting proof-based foundations
- 3Blue1Brown, "Taylor Series" (YouTube) — outstanding visual intuition

### Taylor Series and Approximation
- Stewart §11.9–11.11
- Hamming, R. W. *Methods of Mathematics Applied to Calculus, Probability, and Statistics*, Ch. 1–2 — motivates series from the standpoint of numerical computation

### Differential Equations
- Stewart §9.1–9.5
- Strogatz, Steven. *Infinite Powers: How Calculus Reveals the Secrets of the Universe.* Houghton Mifflin Harcourt, 2019.
  - Accessible narrative on calculus and differential equations; recommended for motivation and context
- Blanchard, Devaney, and Hall. *Differential Equations*, 4th ed. — more advanced; useful for students planning to take ODE courses

---

## Practice and Problem Resources

- **MIT OpenCourseWare 18.01SC** (Single Variable Calculus): problem sets and exams with solutions, covering all Math 1b topics
- **Paul's Online Math Notes** (tutorial.math.lamar.edu/Classes/CalcII/CalcII.aspx): comprehensive notes and practice problems
- **Wolfram Alpha** — for checking indefinite integrals and series sums (use for verification, not substitution for work)
- **Desmos** (desmos.com) — graphing tool for visualizing series partial sums, slope fields, and solution curves

---

## For Students Seeking Greater Depth

- **Spivak, Michael.** *Calculus.* 4th ed. Publish or Perish, 2008. — rigorous, proof-based; bridges calculus and real analysis
- **Courant, Richard, and Fritz John.** *Introduction to Calculus and Analysis, Vol. 1.* Springer. — classic treatment with applications to physics
- **Rudin, Walter.** *Principles of Mathematical Analysis.* 3rd ed. McGraw-Hill. — real analysis text; appropriate for students planning to major in mathematics
